# Cheatsheet

All rules and numbers are the BIS authors'. Dates matter: B120 = January 2026, QR box = March 2026, B140 = October 2026.

## Who owns, who lends, who loses (B140, Sec 2)

| Role | Party | Claim or exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Owns the asset | Data centre vehicle | Site, building, chips |
| Pays rent | Hyperscaler (IG). Neocloud or AI lab (unrated or sub-IG). | Lease or offtake, sometimes short initial term with renewal options |
| Holds the debt | NBFIs: insurers, private credit funds | Privately placed bond or ABS |
| Funds construction | Banks | Funding lines to the vehicle |
| Levers the holders | Banks | Loans to private credit funds |
| Backs the residual | Hyperscaler, chip vendor | Capped, contingent, recorded at little or no value |
| Bears loss past the cap | Insurers, funds, then their bank lenders | Delayed by opacity |

## Decision rules
- If the debt is investment grade but the lease is shorter than the bond, then check the renewal option and the guarantee cap. The vehicle's protection is "only in part".
- If one firm is tenant or guarantor on several vehicles, then add those to its bond exposure. Lenders "may hold larger exposures to a single firm than they realise".
- If a supplier sells to, owns, buys from and guarantees the same customer, then count four loss channels, not one.
- If collateral is chips, then expect falling value with each generation and correlated resale.
- If the question is funding-run risk, then the authors see it as "less likely" because the debt is long term. Look at valuation freezes and slow loss recognition instead.
- If the question is whether the boom is good or bad, then the authors say it is too early. Credit paths do not separate the two in real time.
- If loan spreads for a sector equal the market average while its equity implies outsized returns, then one market is mispricing (B120).
- If AI supply news hits, then the measured effect so far is small and gone in two days. Do not treat that as a constant.

## Tells and smells
- Leases "disclosed but not recorded as debt".
- Guarantees "recorded at little or no value until payments become likely".
- Off-balance sheet commitments above reported debt.
- A vehicle rated one notch below its tenant that pays more than twice the tenant's spread.
- Neoclouds borrowing against chips at floating rates.
- CDS of lower-rated hyperscalers rising well ahead of higher-rated ones (QR box).
- Five firms taking a quarter of long-dated corporate issuance.

## What the event study can and cannot say

| It can say | It cannot say |
|---|---|
| Term premium rose about 1.5 bp on 18 AI news days relative to other days | That AI supply caused the rise |
| The rise reversed within two days | What happens at higher issuance |
| IG index spreads did not move significantly | Anything on single names or private vehicle debt |
| The response is larger at long maturities | A threshold for when spillovers become large |

## Stress path (B140 Sec 2, QR box)
1. Tenant does not renew or terminates early.
2. Vehicle seeks a new tenant or sells assets while similar capacity is on offer.
3. Residual value guarantee pays up to its cap.
4. Remaining loss falls on insurers and funds.
5. Bank lenders to those funds and to the vehicle take the next loss.
6. Opacity delays recognition and can freeze the market for the debt.
