# Glossary

Section numbers refer to the files in `chapters/`. Sec 1 to 3 are BIS Bulletin No 140. Sec 4 is BIS Bulletin No 120. Sec 5 is the BIS Quarterly Review box of March 2026.

**Adjusted term premium** — the daily change in the 10-year term premium after removing the fitted constant, FOMC statement days and the change in the two-year yield (Sec 3)

**AI-financing-related news day** — one of 18 sessions in 2026 with hyperscaler results, a jumbo bond deal, chip vendor results or chip vendor financing news (Sec 3)

**AI firms** — in Bulletin 120 Graph 2: Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle. In Bulletin 120 Graph 3: firms in the PitchBook verticals "Artificial Intelligence", "Big Data" and "Cloud Tech" (Sec 4)

**AI hyperscalers (AIHs)** — Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle (Sec 1, Sec 5)

**AI labs** — firms that, with hyperscalers and neoclouds, support the build-out. Mostly unrated or below investment grade (Sec 1, Sec 2)

**Bad boom** — a credit boom that ended in a systemic banking crisis, after Gorton and Ordoñez (2020) (Sec 1)

**Capacity backstop** — the chip vendor's commitment to buy computing capacity from some neoclouds (Sec 2)

**Capacity offtake agreement** — a long-term commitment to purchase or reserve power and compute capacity (Sec 5)

**Chip vendor** — the hardware seller that also holds equity in some customers, backs some lease obligations and may support residual values (Sec 2)

**Circular financing** — multiple financial links among the same AI firms. Named, not developed. See BIS Bulletin No 137 (Sec 2, Sec 4)

**Closed-end structure** — a private credit fund that locks in capital for about four to eight years (Sec 4)

**Concentration risk** — lease commitments and guarantees rest on a small number of large firms (Sec 2)

**Correlated exposures** — the long-dated claims that duration investors absorb share the same AI risk (Sec 3)

**Data centre vehicle** — the special purpose vehicle that "owns the site, building and chips" and issues private debt (Sec 2)

**Debt-equity pricing gap** — AI loans priced like average loans while AI equity implies outsized returns (Sec 4)

**Duration risk** — the risk of holding long-dated bonds. The term premium is its compensation (Sec 3)

**Free cash flow** — operating cash flow minus capex (Sec 4)

**Fund leverage** — bank credit to the private credit funds that buy vehicle debt (Sec 2)

**General purpose technology** — the authors' label for AI. Gains arrive once it is embedded in capital and processes (Sec 1)

**Good boom** — a credit boom that generated enough returns to repay the debt (Sec 1)

**Hidden leverage** — leverage moved off the balance sheet. "leverage does not disappear by being out of sight" (Sec 4)

**Hidden single-name exposure** — a lender's total exposure to one ultimate guarantor across bonds and several vehicles (Sec 2)

**Hyperion** — the Meta and Blue Owl joint venture data centre, financed in part by $27.3 billion of A+ bonds (Sec 2)

**Jumbo bond deal** — an issue of $20 billion or more (Sec 3)

**NBFIs** — non-bank financial institutions, such as insurers and private credit funds. They hold the debt (Sec 2, Sec 3)

**Neoclouds** — a smaller set of newer cloud providers. They also borrow against chips, often at floating rates (Sec 1, Sec 2)

**Offtake** — a contract to take capacity from the vehicle (Sec 2)

**Opacity** — leases not yet started are disclosed but not recorded as debt, and guarantees carry little or no recorded value (Sec 2)

**Private credit** — non-bank credit from specialised funds, negotiated directly and held to maturity (Sec 4)

**Private placement** — debt sold directly to a few institutional investors (Sec 2, Sec 5)

**Rating headroom** — room under a firm's rating that it keeps by leaving debt off its balance sheet (Sec 2)

**Residual value guarantee** — support for the value of the assets after the lease. Typically capped (Sec 2)

**Ring-fencing** — placing the data centre in a vehicle so lenders hold a secured claim on it (Sec 2)

**Shadow borrowing** — obligations economically akin to debt that sit largely outside corporate balance sheets (Sec 5)

**Term premium** — the part of a long yield that compensates for duration risk. Here the Adrian, Crump and Moench (2013) 10-year estimate (Sec 3)

**Vendor backstop** — support from the chip vendor for its customers' financing (Sec 1, Sec 2)
