# Cheatsheet: A Template for Understanding Big Debt Crises

All rules and numbers are the book's. Averages are rough and vary widely by case.

## First decision

| If... | Then... | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Debt is in a currency the policy makers print | Expect a deflationary depression that can be managed well | Losses can be spread over time (Ch 1) |
| Much debt is in foreign currency, or the currency is pegged to gold | Expect harder choices and possibly an inflationary depression | That debt cannot be monetized (Ch 5, Ch 8) |
| The external burden cannot be defaulted on, cut or financed | Expect printing and risk of hyperinflation | No other lever is left (Ch 6, Ch 7) |

## Decision rules

- When debt service is being paid with new borrowing, treat the trend as unsustainable (Ch 1, Ch 2).
- When inflation and growth look fine but leveraged asset prices are soaring, expect the central bank to feed the bubble (Ch 2, Ch 8, Ch 9).
- When the curve is flat or inverted late in a leveraged boom, expect a move to cash and slower credit (Ch 2).
- When prices fall after a bubble before earnings do, do not treat it as cheap (Ch 2, Ch 8).
- When rates are near 0 percent and deleveraging has begun, expect a "D", not an "R", and expect printing (Ch 3, Ch 9).
- When a policy announcement produces a rally, ask whether total money and credit changed. If not, expect the rally to fade (Ch 8).
- When a systemic institution faces a run, provide liquidity and guarantees first and sort blame later (Ch 3, Ch 9).
- When QE or stimulus is slowed or ended, treat it as a tightening (Ch 9).
- When a central bank defends a currency with reserves and rate rises, expect it to fail in about six months (Ch 5).
- When currency holders have had negative returns for long and the currency still falls, expect an inflationary spiral (Ch 5).
- In a hyperinflation: short the currency, move money out, hold gold and commodities, avoid bonds, distrust local equity gains (Ch 6).
- After a devaluation, watch the central bank before going long (Ch 5).

## Beautiful or ugly?

| Test | Beautiful | Ugly deflationary | Ugly inflationary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominal growth versus nominal rate | Above | Below | Far above, with a run on debt assets |
| Dominant levers | Balanced | Austerity and defaults | Money printing and devaluation |
| Debt/income | Falling | Rising | Inflated away |

## Thresholds and typical magnitudes

| Deflationary archetype (21 cases) | Value |
|---|---|
| Leveraging in the bubble | 20 to 25% of GDP over about 3 years |
| Debt growth vs income growth in the bubble | 32% vs 13% |
| Fastest tightening | About 5 months before the stock top |
| Equity fall in depression | Around 50% |
| Stimulus arrives | 2 to 3 years in, after activity is down about 10% and unemployment is 10 to 15% |
| Money creation at the turn | About 4% of GDP a year (range 1 to 9%) |
| FX decline vs gold | -44% in the table, "around 50 percent" in the prose |
| Peak fiscal deficit | About 6% of GDP |
| Activity back to peak / stocks back to highs | 5 to 10 years / about a decade |

## Tells

- New lending channels outside the regulated banks, described as safe (Ch 2, Ch 8, Ch 9).
- New buyers with no experience in the market (Ch 2, Ch 7, Ch 8).
- Debt-funded consumption instead of investment (Ch 9).
- Low measured volatility with high leverage (Ch 9).
- A country borrowing in foreign currency because lenders refuse its own (Ch 7).
- Shop shelves emptying and local stocks soaring while the currency falls: flight from money (Ch 7).
- Forward rate falling ahead of spot (Ch 5).
- Falling average debt maturity (Ch 6).

For the stage-by-stage well managed versus poorly managed tables, read ch04 (deflationary) and ch05 (currency crises).
