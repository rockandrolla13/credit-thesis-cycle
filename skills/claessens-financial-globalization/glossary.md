# Glossary

Terms from "A Cross-Country Perspective on the Causes of the Global Financial Crisis"
(Claessens, Dell'Ariccia, Igan, Laeven). Chapter numbers refer to this skill's chapter files.

**Adverse feedback loop** — rising foreclosures push house prices down, which causes more foreclosures (Ch 2)

**Adverse selection (interbank)** — lenders cannot separate healthy from unhealthy institutions and stop lending to all (Ch 2)

**Agency problems** — weaker due diligence and monitoring when the loan originator sells the loan on (Ch 2)

**Amplification mechanism** — a feedback process, such as fire sales, that enlarges an initial shock (Ch 3)

**Announced or pledged amounts** — the basis of Table 48.1 columns A, B, C and E, as opposed to actual uptake (Ch 4)

**Asset grab** — see Ring-fencing (Ch 4)

**Asset-backed commercial paper (ABCP)** — short-term paper whose issuance contracted sharply in the turmoil (Ch 3)

**Beggar-thy-neighbor effects** — one country's guarantee forces others to follow or lose deposits and funding (Ch 4)

**Big 5** — five major advanced-economy banking crises used as the house price benchmark: Spain 1977, Norway 1987, Finland 1991, Sweden 1991, Japan 1992 (Ch 1)

**"Broke the buck"** — a money market fund's net asset value falling below one dollar (Ch 4)

**Burden-sharing mechanism** — an agreed rule for splitting the cost of a failing cross-border bank, which was absent (Ch 4)

**Catalyst** — the vulnerable condition behind a crisis, here the overextended US housing and mortgage markets (Ch 3)

**Common lender effects** — spillover when a distressed intermediary sells other assets and cuts lending elsewhere (Ch 2)

**Commonalities with previous crises** — asset price bubbles, credit booms, marginal loans and systemic risk, failure of regulation and supervision (Ch 1)

**Contingent costs** — hard-to-quantify fiscal costs such as asset insurance schemes and higher deposit insurance limits (Ch 4)

**Credit boom** — a sustained episode of rapid credit growth, with output, consumption and investment above trend in the buildup and below trend in the unwinding (Ch 1)

**Debt service ratio** — debt service relative to disposable income, at a US historical high before the crisis (Ch 1)

**Direct distortion** — support of financial institutions in nonmarket ways (Ch 4)

**Direct exposures** — holdings of subprime-related assets, the phase 1 spillover channel (Ch 3)

**Exit** — withdrawal of guarantees, enlarged deposit insurance, state ownership and asset acquisitions (Ch 4)

**Fire sales** — forced asset sales in a cycle of falling prices and further sales (Ch 2, Ch 3)

**Forced deleveraging** — balance sheet contraction compelled by losses, funding loss or mark-to-market rules (Ch 2, Ch 3)

**Foreign currency lending** — domestic credit denominated in euros, Swiss francs or yen, the Eastern European form of the marginal loan (Ch 1)

**Gross cross-border positions** — external assets plus liabilities, which rose massively among OECD countries (Ch 2)

**Household debt-to-assets ratio** — the authors' measure of household leverage, which fell in Japan's boom (Ch 2)

**Household debt-to-income ratio** — household indebtedness relative to income, which rose sharply in the US, UK, Spain and Ireland (Ch 2)

**Indirect distortion** — skewed resource allocation in the real sector caused by support measures (Ch 4)

**Interbank spread** — in Figure 48.13, 3-month LIBOR minus a 3-month borrowing rate (Ch 3)

**Marginal loans / marginal assets** — assets whose viability relied on continued favourable macroeconomic conditions (Ch 1)

**Negative equity** — mortgage debt above the home's value, which gives an incentive to "walk away" (Ch 2)

**New dimensions of the crisis** — opaque instruments, interconnectedness with the US at the core, financial institution leverage, the central role of households (Ch 2)

**Nonconforming mortgages** — loans outside GSE underwriting standards, more than 70% securitized by 2007 (Ch 2)

**Opaqueness** — complexity of instruments and balance sheets that blocks valuation and price discovery (Ch 2)

**Originate-to-distribute model** — lending for onward sale, also written "originate-and-distribute" in the source (Ch 1, Ch 2)

**Phases of spillover** — direct exposures, then asset markets, then solvency concerns after Lehman Brothers (Ch 3)

**Ring-fencing** — a jurisdiction seizing or isolating the local assets of a failing cross-border entity (Ch 4)

**Shadow banking system** — finance companies, merchant banks, investment banks and off-balance-sheet vehicles operating, to varying degrees, outside banking regulation (Ch 1)

**Subprime borrowers** — borrowers with limited credit and employment histories (Ch 1)

**Sudden stop** — abrupt scarcity or reversal of foreign funding for emerging economies (Ch 2)

**Systemic risk** — risk to the financial system as a whole, which authorities underestimated by focusing on individual institutions (Ch 1)

**Trigger** — the event that starts a crisis, here the turnaround in US house prices (Ch 3)

**Upfront government financing** — gross support needing upfront government outlays, before any recovery from asset sales (Ch 4)

**Wealth, collateral and confidence effects** — the routes by which household balance sheet stress cut consumption (Ch 2)

**Zombie firms** — inefficient firms kept alive by public support, which may drive out efficient firms (Ch 4)
