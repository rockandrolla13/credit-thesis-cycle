# Glossary: The Event-Driven Edge in Investing

Definitions follow the book's wording and scope. (Ch N) marks where the term is used.

**10b5-1 plan** — a pre-set trading plan for an insider, fixing price, amount and dates, set up before the insider holds material non-public information. The author says such plans can be gamed. (Ch 3)

**8-K (current report)** — the SEC filing for major events, due within four business days. Item 5.02 covers departures, appointments and pay arrangements. (Ch 6, Ch 7)

**Accelerated share repurchase (ASR)** — a buyback in which an investment bank buys large blocks for the company and delivers shares quickly. (Ch 4)

**Annualized return** — the deal spread scaled to a year by the expected time to close, without compounding. 4% in four months is 12%. (Ch 2)

**Arbitrage tourist** — an investor drawn to a delayed deal by its large spread. (Ch 2)

**Carve-out** — a parent sells some or all of a subsidiary through an IPO. (Ch 6)

**CFIUS** — the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States. It reviews cross-border deals for national security risk. (Ch 2)

**Cluster purchase** — purchases of one company's stock by more than one insider at the same time. (Ch 3)

**Collar** — a deal term that changes the exchange ratio with the acquirer's share price inside a set range. (Ch 2)

**Community-adjusted EBITDA** — WeWork's metric, which excluded design, development, marketing and other basic costs. (Ch 5)

**Conglomerate discount** — a holding company trading below the value of its underlying businesses. (Ch 6)

**Contingent value right (CVR)** — part of the merger consideration paid later if set milestones are met. Greenblatt's "merger securities". (Ch 2)

**Dark side** — the author's label for each strategy's failure modes. (all strategy chapters)

**Definitive merger agreement** — the signed contract that sets price, timing, approvals and termination terms. (Ch 2)

**De-SPAC** — the company after a SPAC merges with an operating business. (Ch 5)

**Double Dipper** — the author's screen for companies buying back stock while insiders buy on the open market. (Ch 4)

**Enterprise value** — market cap plus net debt. The author adds capitalized leases for WeWork. (Ch 4, Ch 5)

**Event-driven investing** — investing in companies experiencing a specific event. Also called special situations investing. (Preface, Ch 1)

**Flip-flopper** — an insider who was selling and starts buying, or the reverse. (Ch 3)

**Forced selling** — selling for reasons other than value: arbitrage funds after a deal breaks, funds that cannot hold a spinoff, a large holder liquidating. (Ch 2, Ch 3, Ch 6)

**Form 4** — the insider transaction filing, due within two business days. Codes: P purchase, S sale, M option exercise, F tax payment, G gift. Form 4/A is an amendment. (Ch 3)

**Form 10-12B (Form 10)** — the spinoff filing, with pro-forma financials for the new company. (Ch 6)

**Free cash flow (owner's earnings)** — cash left after all expenses and reinvestment. The author prefers it to EBITDA. (Ch 5)

**Go shop** — a period, usually 25 to 45 days, in which a target may seek a competing bid. (Ch 2)

**HSR filing** — the pre-merger notification under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Act. It starts a 30-day clock. The 2023 threshold is $111.4m. (Ch 2)

**Insider** — management down to VP level, directors, and any holder of more than 10%. (Ch 3)

**Issuer tender offer** — a tender offer by a company for its own shares, bonds or preferred shares. (Ch 4)

**Lock in the spread** — short the acquirer's stock at the exchange ratio in a stock deal. (Ch 2)

**Material Adverse Change (MAC)** — what an acquirer must prove to exit a signed agreement. (Ch 2)

**Merger arbitrage (risk arbitrage)** — buy the target below the deal price and wait for the close. (Ch 1, Ch 2)

**Negative spread** — the target trades above the deal price. (Ch 2)

**Net Promoter Score (NPS)** — promoters minus detractors on a 0 to 10 survey question. (Ch 7)

**Odd lot clause** — a tender offer term that accepts holdings under 100 shares in full. (Ch 4)

**Outside date** — the deadline in a merger agreement for completing the deal. It can be extended. (Ch 2)

**Pay ratio** — CEO pay divided by median employee pay, from the proxy statement. (Ch 7)

**Poison pill** — a defence that lets a company issue many shares to dilute a hostile acquirer. (Ch 2)

**Proration** — partial acceptance of tendered or elected shares when demand exceeds the amount on offer. (Ch 2, Ch 4)

**Proxy statement** — the SEC filing with the Summary Compensation Table. (Ch 7)

**Quiet period** — a company-set window around earnings in which insiders may not trade. (Ch 3)

**Realized compensation** — stock awards counted at vesting and options at exercise. (Ch 7)

**Rebound trade** — buying a target after a deal breaks, once forced selling ends. (Ch 2)

**Redemption** — a SPAC holder's right to hand back shares for about $10 plus interest at the vote. (Ch 5)

**Return on assets (ROA)** — net income divided by total assets. (Ch 7)

**Return on equity (ROE)** — net income divided by shareholder equity. The author screens for 15% or more. (Ch 7)

**Reverse merger** — a private company merging into a public shell. (Ch 5)

**Reverse Morris Trust** — a tax-efficient spinoff combined with a merger into another company. (Ch 6)

**Rollup** — a company that grows by a series of acquisitions in a fragmented market. (Ch 5)

**SAMR** — China's State Administration for Market Regulation. "Where deals often go to die." (Ch 2)

**Second request** — the US agency's demand for more information after an HSR filing. (Ch 2)

**Secondary offering** — any sale of new shares after the IPO. (Ch 1, Ch 3)

**Sell/buy ratio** — aggregate weekly insider selling divided by aggregate weekly insider buying. Mean 30.10 and median 19.03 for August 2010 to July 2022. (Ch 3)

**Short-Swing Profit rule** — Section 16(b) of the 1934 Act. An insider must return profit from a purchase and sale within six months. (Ch 3)

**SPAC** — Special Purpose Acquisition Company. A blank check company that raises money first and looks for a business second. (Ch 1, Ch 5)

**Specific performance** — the clause that lets a target sue to force the acquirer to close. (Ch 2)

**SpinCo** — the new company created by a spinoff. (Ch 6)

**Spinoff** — holders of the parent receive shares in a newly independent company. (Ch 1, Ch 6)

**Spinsider** — the author's screen for spinoffs with insider buying. (Ch 6)

**Split-off** — holders choose subsidiary shares in place of parent shares. (Ch 6)

**Sponsor** — the party that forms a SPAC. Typically owns 20% of the combined company. (Ch 5)

**Spread** — the gap between the target's price and the deal price. (Ch 1, Ch 2)

**Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP)** — valuing each business in a holding company and comparing the total with its market value. (Ch 6)

**Termination fee** — a payment owed when a deal ends under stated conditions. (Ch 2)

**Uber cannibals** — Pabrai's term for the five companies that repurchased the most stock in the prior year, after his filters. (Ch 4)

**Unit** — the SPAC IPO security, usually $10, made of a share and a warrant or part of one. (Ch 5)

**Variant perception** — a view of a stock that differs from the market's. (Ch 3)

**Warrant** — a right to buy shares at a set price, often lasting five to 15 years. (Ch 5)
