# Cheatsheet: The Event-Driven Edge in Investing

All rules are the author's. Chapter cites in brackets.

## Decision rules
| When | Do | Because |
|---|---|---|
| A deal offers a small spread and a short timeline | Annualize before rejecting it | 4% in four months is 12% a year [Ch 2] |
| The spread is wide and widening | Treat it as a warning | Failed deals start wide and widen before failure [Ch 2] |
| A deal waits on SAMR, or is in banking, insurance or utilities | Avoid, or expect a long delay | Approvals can take 12 to 15 months [Ch 2] |
| The spread narrows to about 1% | Consider taking the exit | First Horizon fell from near $25 to under $10 [Ch 2] |
| A deal breaks | Wait some days, then look at a rebound trade | Arbitrage funds are forced sellers [Ch 2] |
| An insider "buys" | Read the Form 4 footnotes first | It may be a secondary, a plan, or a contract [Ch 3] |
| An industry is in crisis and its insiders buy heavily | Research the strongest companies | Energy 2020, regional banks March 2023 [Ch 3] |
| Several insiders sell as the price falls | Revisit the thesis | First Solar, Carvana [Ch 3] |
| A buyback is announced | Check the share count later | Announcements are not purchases [Ch 4] |
| A leveraged company buys back stock | Default answer is no | Franchise Group [Ch 4] |
| A cyclical company buys back with peak cash | Ask if supply is being held back | Shipping against frackers [Ch 4] |
| A SPAC target looks weak | Redeem and keep the warrant | Floor near $10 plus interest [Ch 5] |
| A spinoff is announced | Find where the debt goes | Viatris against the new Biohaven [Ch 6] |
| A founder steps down fully | Revisit the thesis | [Ch 7] |
| Executives leave suddenly or in a cluster | Sell signal for holders | [Ch 7] |

## Deal type quick guide [Ch 2]
- All cash → buy and wait.
- All stock → buy the target, short the acquirer at the ratio.
- Cash plus stock → short only the stock portion.
- Cash or stock → expect proration. Payout form is uncertain.
- Collar → recalculate the spread as the acquirer moves.
- CVR attached → worth it only if the cash spread stands alone.

## Insider signal sorter [Ch 3]
- Keep: open-market, discretionary, rare for that insider, joined by others, long tenure, investment background, founder or cycle-tested chair.
- Drop: same price across a cluster, same-day matched trades, late filings, new-hire or annual or contractual buys, exercise-and-sell, code F tax sales.
- Suspect: a purchase announced by press release.

## Tells and smells
- "One-time" charges that recur each quarter [Ch 5].
- Free cash flow well below net income year after year [Ch 5].
- Stock-based compensation equal to the net loss [Ch 7].
- A buyback finished faster than planned while sales fall [Ch 4].
- Bad news filed on a Friday evening or before a holiday [Ch 7].
- A departure notice that stresses it has nothing to do with financial reporting [Ch 7].
- Debt issued by the spinoff with the proceeds paid to the parent [Ch 6].
- A CEO "cheering the stock on" [Introduction].

## What the author will not claim
- No screen is a buy signal. Each is an idea source [Ch 3, Ch 4, Conclusion].
- The sell/buy ratio is not a timing tool [Ch 3].
- The strategies are not crisis-proof. "Almost all correlations go to one" [Ch 2].
