# Cheatsheet

Decision aids from Hayford and Malliaris, "Asset Prices and the Financial Crisis of 2007-09". Every number is from the paper. Most come from one episode or one cited study, so treat them as reference points.

## Decision Rules

| When you see | Conclude or do | Because | Ch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial liberalization plus credit expansion | Flag crisis set-up; look for a bubble in stocks, real estate, or both | These precede most crises in the cited samples | 2 |
| Set-up factors but no bubble-and-bust cycle | Do not call a crisis | The crisis is conditional on the cycle occurring | 2 |
| Price rises that raise expected future prices | Classify as a bubble spiral | This is the authors' definition | 2 |
| New buyers running out of credit access or belief | Expect the peak | The boom needs both | 2 |
| Rising collateral values with looser bank lending | Assume a leverage cycle, and model the reverse loop | Collateral works in both directions | 2 |
| A bust in an asset the financial sector financed | Expect large real-economy impact | Propagation depends on financial-sector involvement | 1, 2 |
| A bust with little financial-sector financing | Expect limited real impact | Earlier episodes "had little impact on the real economy" | 1 |
| Stock and real estate collapse with overexposed banks | Expect a banking crisis in about a year, then a recession of about a year and a half | Kaminsky-Reinhart averages | 2 |
| Agency spread leaving its 15-25 bp range | Read as flight to quality starting | August 2007 pattern | 3 |
| Rate cuts fail to restore normal conditions | Diagnose risk and credit shortage; expect unconventional actions | Fall 2007 to winter 2008 | 3 |
| Large expected bank losses | Expect credit contraction | Loans are capped at a multiple of capital | 3 |
| Many institutions making the same bet | Treat as high systemic risk | Macro-prudential rule | 4 |
| A bailout of too-big or too-interconnected firms | Expect more risk taking unless regulation tightens | Moral hazard | 4 |
| Low macro volatility with a growing financial sector | Do not infer safety | Table 1, 2000-2008 | 1 |

## Stage Classifier

- Is there liberalization or credit expansion?
  - No: outside this framework's set-up.
  - Yes: are asset prices feeding expectations of further rises?
    - No: set-up only. Monitor.
    - Yes: bubble (Minsky "euphoria"). Has "scary news" or buyer exhaustion arrived?
      - No: boom continues while credit and belief hold.
      - Yes: bust (Minsky "panic"). Did the financial sector finance it?
        - No: limited propagation.
        - Yes: balance sheet hits, funding withdrawal, forced sales, market freeze, reduced lending, recession.

## Tells and Smells

- Economists and policy makers "congratulating themselves": consensus risk (Ch 1).
- Everyone "betting that real estate prices will continue to rise": crowded trade (Ch 4).
- Leverage rising with asset prices: pro-cyclical leverage (Ch 3).
- Lenders refusing to roll over bank debt: propagation has started (Ch 2).
- A near-riskless spread moving: flight to quality (Ch 3).
- Financial sector resisting regulation: expected, and a reason reform may fall short (Ch 4).
- Unsettled cause: Taylor says easy Fed policy fueled housing in 2004-2006. Bernanke disputes it (Ch 3).
