# Patterns

Analytical techniques and causal mechanisms from Hayford and Malliaris. Each is stated so it can be applied to a new episode. Chapter references point to the source of each element.

## Three-Part Crisis Anatomy
**When to use**: first pass on any suspected financial instability (Ch 2).
**How**: assign the episode to a part. (1) Initial causal factors: financial liberalization, credit expansion. (2) Asset price bubble and bust, in stocks, real estate, or both. (3) Propagation from the financial to the real economy.
**Trade-offs**: the set-up factors are not sufficient. A crisis follows only if a bubble-and-bust cycle occurs.

## Kaminsky-Reinhart Sequence Template
**When to use**: to put rough timing on what follows an asset market collapse (Ch 2).
**How**: liberalization and credit expansion, then stock prices rising about 40% per year above normal times, then collapse of stock and real estate markets, then a banking crisis about a year later, then a recession of about a year and a half.
**Trade-offs**: these are averages from 20 countries (5 industrial, 15 emerging). The banking crisis step holds "in many cases", where intermediaries were overexposed.

## Minsky Five-Stage Credit Cycle
**When to use**: to label where a boom stands (Ch 2).
**How**: displacement, boom, euphoria, profit taking, panic. Map displacement to liberalization and credit expansion, euphoria to the bubble, panic to the pop.
**Trade-offs**: the paper names the stages but gives no observable criteria for each. Profit taking is not described.

## Bubble Sustainability Test
**When to use**: to judge whether a price spiral can continue (Ch 2).
**How**: check two conditions together: new buyers are still available with access to credit, and they believe prices will keep rising. The bubble pops when all so-inclined buyers have bought.
**Trade-offs**: the paper gives no measure of the remaining buyer pool.

## Leverage Cycle (Geanakoplos)
**When to use**: when rising asset prices and easier lending appear together (Ch 2).
**How**: up-leg: prices up, collateral value up, banks lend more, demand up (most optimistic borrowers first), prices up. Down-leg: "scary news", sudden selling, wealth of leveraged buyers falls, forced sales, banks tighten standards.
**Trade-offs**: the trigger is news, so timing is not predictable from the loop itself.

## Collateral Amplification (Kiyotaki-Moore)
**When to use**: to explain why a small shock produced a large effect (Ch 2).
**How**: shock lowers asset prices, collateral value falls, borrowing capacity falls, asset prices fall again.
**Trade-offs**: stated qualitatively in the source, with no magnitudes.

## Financial-Sector Involvement Test
**When to use**: to size the real-economy impact of a bust (Ch 2, Ch 1).
**How**: ask how much the financial sector financed the bubble. More involvement means bigger balance sheet hits, doubt about bank solvency, withdrawals and refusal to roll over debt, forced sales, then a price spiral or market freeze.
**Trade-offs**: perceived solvency can fall without actual insolvency because of asymmetric information.

## Financial Accelerator (Bernanke-Gertler)
**When to use**: to reason from tighter lending standards to output (Ch 2).
**How**: identify borrowers who depend on bank credit. A loss of bank financing, or a wealth shock that removes access to it, amplifies and propagates the original shock. Sectors dependent on external finance, developing countries, and countries with less access to foreign finance are hit harder.
**Trade-offs**: the supporting evidence is cited without named authors in the extracted text.

## Shared-versus-Unique Decomposition
**When to use**: when comparing a current episode with history (Ch 3).
**How**: list features shared with past crises (for 2007-09: global liquidity creation, global saving glut). List features unique to this one (rating agency business model flaws, pro-cyclical leverage, privately rational but socially inefficient disintermediation).
**Trade-offs**: the unique list comes from one source (Buiter 2007).

## Reinhart-Rogoff Historical Benchmark
**When to use**: to judge whether a housing run-up is crisis-sized (Ch 3).
**How**: compare the pre-crisis housing price run-up with the eighteen bank-centered post-war crises. The U.S. run-up exceeded the sample.
**Trade-offs**: the paper reports the comparison's result, not the numbers.

## Agency Spread Stress Gauge
**When to use**: early detection of flight to quality (Ch 3).
**How**: track agency minus Treasury yields at equal maturity. Typical 15-25 bp. Above 40 bp was the August 2007 reading. Above 90 bp was March 2008.
**Trade-offs**: one episode. The levels are descriptive of 2007-08, not tested thresholds.

## Bank Capital Channel
**When to use**: to translate expected losses into credit supply (Ch 3).
**How**: loans cannot exceed a regulatory multiple of capital. Losses cut capital, and lending capacity contracts.
**Trade-offs**: no multiple is given in the source.

## Conventional-Tools-Failed Signal
**When to use**: to diagnose the type of stress (Ch 3).
**How**: if discount rate cuts and funds rate cuts do not restore normal conditions, treat the problem as risk and credit shortage. In 2007-08 the Fed then cut the discount premium from 100 to 50 to 25 bp, extended terms from overnight to 30 then 90 days, and created TAF.
**Trade-offs**: drawn from a single episode.

## Bailout Moral Hazard Chain
**When to use**: to project behavior after a rescue (Ch 4).
**How**: social compact, then bailout of too-big or too-interconnected institutions, then expected future cover, then risk taking with taxpayer-borne losses, then larger future crises unless regulation responds.
**Trade-offs**: the authors assert the chain. They do not test it.

## Crowded-Trade Macro-Prudential Rule
**When to use**: when many institutions hold the same position on the same assumptions (Ch 4).
**How**: treat the common bet as high systemic risk. The regulatory response is more capital, which means less leverage, for the firms involved.
**Trade-offs**: reduces the sector's profitability, size and political influence, so expect resistance.

## Four-Solution Policy Menu
**When to use**: to frame policy scenarios after a crisis (Ch 4).
**How**: (1) break the social compact; (2) bailouts, no new regulation; (3) bailouts plus more regulation; (4) Minsky-style public policy agenda.
**Trade-offs**: authors rate 1 as very unlikely, 2 as leading to bigger crises, 3 as practical. They do not detail 4.
