# Glossary

Terms from "Asset Prices and the Financial Crisis of 2007-09" (Hayford and Malliaris). Definitions follow the source's usage.

**Agency spread** — average yield difference between U.S. government agency securities (Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and similar) and Treasuries of equivalent maturity; used as an early indicator of risk taking. Typical range 15-25 bp (Ch 3)

**Asset price bubble** — a rise in asset prices that raises expected future prices, which raises current demand and prices again in a spiral (Ch 2)

**Asymmetric response** — doing nothing during an asset price boom and cutting rates during the bust; the core of the Greenspan doctrine (Ch 1)

**Bank capital (net worth)** — the base to which bank lending is tied; regulators cap loans at a multiple of it (Ch 3)

**Collateral amplification** — Kiyotaki and Moore's (1997) result that small shocks have large effects because lower asset prices lower collateral value and borrowing (Ch 2)

**Contagion** — spread of distress across financial institutions, which interventions aim to prevent (Ch 2)

**Counter-cyclical leverage** — leverage regulation that leans against the cycle; first part of micro-prudential regulation (Ch 4)

**Credit cycle model (Minsky)** — five stages: displacement, boom, euphoria, profit taking, panic (Ch 2)

**Credit expansion** — one of the two key initial causal factors of financial crises (Ch 2, Ch 3)

**Displacement** — first Minsky stage; can occur through financial liberalization and credit expansion (Ch 2)

**Epidemic of beliefs** — Akerlof and Shiller's (2009) idea that belief in rising prices spreads from investor to investor (Ch 2)

**Euphoria** — Minsky stage the authors equate with the asset price bubble (Ch 2)

**Financial accelerator** — Bernanke and Gertler's (1989) model in which credit market conditions amplify and propagate shocks when borrowers lose access to bank credit (Ch 2)

**Financial disintermediation** — loss of bank intermediation; the channel by which a financial crisis hits the real economy (Ch 2, Ch 3)

**Financial innovation** — development and marketing of new financial instruments; credited for the Great Moderation, then cited as a cause of the crisis (Ch 1)

**Financial liberalization** — financial deregulation; one of the two key initial causal factors of crises (Ch 2)

**Flight to quality** — investors and institutions shunning everything except U.S. Treasuries (Ch 3)

**Forced sales** — asset sales by leveraged buyers after wealth losses, or by institutions facing withdrawals (Ch 2)

**Global saving glut** — excess saving from high-saving entrants to the global economy (notably China) and commodity exporters (Ch 3)

**Great Moderation** — the post-early-1980s period of two long expansions, two mild recessions and lower volatility of inflation and real GDP growth (Ch 1)

**Great Recession / 2nd Great Contraction** — the authors' names for the downturn after the 2007-08 crisis (Ch 1)

**Greenspan doctrine** — pre-crisis consensus that central banks respond to asset price booms asymmetrically (Ch 1)

**Leverage cycle** — Geanakoplos's (2009) loop linking asset prices, collateral values, bank lending and optimistic borrowers (Ch 2)

**Macro-prudential monitoring** — regulators watching the trades of financial institutions for common bets that create systemic risk (Ch 4)

**Micro-prudential regulation** — institution-level rules: counter-cyclical leverage and compensation that makes managers bear bad outcomes (Ch 4)

**Moral hazard** — incentive to take risks because taxpayers are expected to bear bad outcomes after bailouts (Ch 4)

**Panic** — final Minsky stage; the bubble pops (Ch 2)

**Pro-cyclical leverage** — leverage that moves with the cycle; a unique feature of the 2007-09 crisis (Ch 3, Ch 4)

**Propagation mechanism** — third part of a crisis: transmission from the financial to the real economy (Ch 2)

**"Scary news"** — Geanakoplos's trigger for sudden selling at the top of a leverage cycle (Ch 2)

**Social compact (post-WWII)** — government responsibility for dampening the business cycle, which induces bailouts (Ch 4)

**Systemic risk** — risk to all institutions when many make the same trade and it goes bad (Ch 4)

**Taylor rule** — named as the statement of the standard goals of price stability and maximum sustainable growth (Ch 4)

**Term Auction Facilities (TAF)** — facilities created among the Fed's less conventional actions (Ch 3)

**Too big to fail / too interconnected to fail** — criteria for the institutions governments bail out (Ch 4)

**Value added of financial sector as a share of GDP** — size measure in Table 1; 4.0% in the 1960s, 8.4% in 2000-2008 (Ch 1)
