# Cheatsheet: Investing Amid Low Expected Returns (Ilmanen)

**Stance**: "humble forecasts and bold diversification". Expected returns being low is a fact. Realized returns being low is an opinion. (Ch 1)

## Decision rules
- When quoting a historical average, strip the valuation windfall first, then compare today's yield with the sample average. (Ch 2)
- When cash is the cause of low returns, do not treat cash as the refuge. (Ch 1, Ch 18)
- When a spread or yield looks attractive, ask whether the expected capital gain is negative. It is for credit, volatility selling, emerging currency carry and possibly real estate. (Ch 10)
- When pricing an illiquid asset, assume the realized premium is the fair premium less what investors pay for smoothing. In calm times assume it may be zero. (Ch 5)
- When a private asset class is new, extrapolate from older illiquid classes, not from its own short IRR record. (Ch 5)
- When a factor has lost for three years, check whether it cheapened. Cheapening is not crowding. (Ch 9)
- When worried about crowding, look at valuations and at who holds the position, not at flows. (Ch 8)
- When a valuation signal says "rich", wait for trend confirmation or delay 6-12 months. (Ch 16)
- When equity risk dominates and drawdown threatens survival, add trend before puts. (Ch 13)
- When an optimizer loves private assets or ARP, cap them top-down. That is a model error, not a finding. (Ch 12)
- When a manager's fee looks low, compute the fee per unit of active risk. (Ch 15)
- When in doubt between more risk and less, keep a similar portfolio and lower spending or expectations. (Ch 18)

## Which premia to trust (the author's ranking logic)
| Return source | Evidence length | Author's confidence | Main catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equity premium | 120+ years | Highest | Already dominates portfolios. Not cheap |
| Term, credit, commodity premia | about a century | High | Low starting yields. Narrow spreads in 2021 |
| Style premia, diversified | about a century, many assets | High | Needs leverage and shorting. Bad stretches |
| Illiquidity premia | 30-40 years, smoothed data | Low | Smoothing offsets it. High fees |
| Manager alpha | short, biased samples | Low | Scarce, decaying, bundled with beta |
| Tactical timing | long but weak | Low | Early, low breadth |

## Style quick reference (Ch 6)
| Style | Pays when | Hurts when | Equity tail behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Value | over-extrapolation corrects | structural change. 2018-2020 | mixed, near zero beta on average |
| Momentum | news diffuses slowly | sharp market turns | good in bear markets, crash at the turn |
| Trend | large persistent moves | whipsaws. The 2010s | strong in slow bear markets |
| Carry | conditions stay unchanged | risk-off unwinds | credit, FX and volatility carry lose. Others benign |
| Defensive | leverage aversion persists | junk rallies | dollar-neutral versions gain. BAB about 50/50 |

## Tells and smells
- A return series with high autocorrelation and low volatility is smoothed, not safe. (Ch 5)
- Half the funds claim top quartile. (Ch 5)
- An IG bond fund that beat its index and correlates 0.6-0.7 with equities is long credit beta. (Ch 4, Ch 7)
- A hedge fund with 0.8 equity correlation is selling beta at alpha fees. (Ch 7)
- A new signal with a better backtest than an old one has a shorter history and more freedom to fit. (Ch 7, Ch 9)
- A quintile chart of valuation against future return hides how long the signal sat in one bucket. (Ch 16)
- A reallocation proposed after three to five bad years is return chasing at a reversal horizon. (Ch 17)
- Spreads narrow, index quality lower, duration longer: reach-for-yield is offsetting fundamentals. (Ch 4)
- Large dry powder: capital is chasing too few deals. (Ch 5)

## Six principles (Ch 18)
Select return sources thoughtfully. Size risks appropriately. Invest strategically and patiently. Diversify boldly. Execute cost-effectively. Govern well.
