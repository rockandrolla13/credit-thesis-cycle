# Chapter 1: Introduction

## Core Idea
Low starting yields and rich valuations in almost every asset class point to low expected returns. Investors should accept that fact, then control what they can: return sources, diversification, risk, costs and their own patience. The foreword (Cliff Asness, November 2021) frames three options: take more risk, accept lower returns, or add other return sources.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Serenity Prayer versus St. Augustine's prayer** (1.1): accept what cannot change (low market yields), change what can (process, costs, diversification). Most investors instead follow Augustine: "make me chaste – but not yet". They add risk and delay spending cuts.
- **Borrowed returns**: windfall gains from falling yields "brought future returns forward". Strong realized returns after 2008 came with lower expected returns.
- **Fact versus opinion**: "That expected returns are low is a fact. That realized returns will be low in the rest of the 2020s is merely an opinion."
- **Outcome bias** (also "resulting fallacy"): judging a decision by its outcome and not its process. Luck dominates skill "over months and even years". Statisticians may need decades to tell them apart.
- **Three "knows"** (1.2): know your history (Part I), know your opportunity set (Part II), know how to assemble the parts (Part III).
- **Base-rate information**: treat the long-run premia evidence as the base rate. Overlay specific views on top. Do not neglect it.
- **Apple harvesting parable** (Figure 1.3): investors reach for expected returns and overlook portfolio construction, risk management and costs. The author calls these "alpha beyond expected returns".
- **The author's investment beliefs** (1.3), stated as his own leanings:
  1. Diversification over concentration. The test is willingness to use leverage to harness several rewarded factors.
  2. More belief in style premia than in illiquidity premia.
  3. Portfolio perspective over narrow framing.
  4. Strategic diversification over bold tactical timing.
  5. Portfolios resilient across macro scenarios, not portfolios that win only if one view is right.
  6. Survival first in risk management.
  7. Probabilistic thinking over stories.
  8. Systematic over discretionary.
  9. Summary: "humble forecasts and bold diversification".

## Key Concepts
- **Expected versus realized return**: expected returns cannot be observed. They are estimated from current yields, from historical averages, from theory, or from discretionary views.
- **Sharpe ratio (SR)**: average excess return over cash divided by volatility. The book uses it because it is scale-invariant for long/short strategies.
- **Slow pain / fast pain**: low coupons and dividends for decades, or quick capital losses as valuations revert (developed in Ch 2).
- **Rearview-mirror expectations**: extrapolating strong past returns.

## Mental Models
- Think of nine premia as the menu with the longest evidence: four asset class premia (equity, term, credit, commodity) and five styles (value, momentum, carry, defensive, trend). Illiquidity premia and manager alpha lack near-century evidence.
- Treat cash as no refuge when record-low cash rates are the cause of the problem.
- Judge a manager or strategy on process. A "long-run positively rewarded strategy" can have a losing decade.

## Anti-patterns
- **Reaching for yield as the only response**: it "helps only so far".
- **Wishful thinking**: leaving return assumptions, investment plans and spending plans all unchanged.
- **Chasing strategies**: "Inconsistently chasing different long-run successful strategies would likely turn out worse than sticking with one mediocre strategy."
- **Market timing from cash**: a timer "will have to be very lucky".

## Key Takeaways
1. The low-return problem covers all long-only assets because they share one riskless discount rate.
2. Style premia numbers in Figure 1.2 are before trading costs and fees. The author says wide awareness may lower future returns.
3. The book gives no investment advice. It says so explicitly.
4. The book avoids meme stocks, bitcoin, NFTs and SPACs by design.
