# Glossary

Terms as the contributors use them. Chapter in brackets.

**AQR (Asset Quality Review)** — the ECB's 2014 review of bank legacy assets, feeding a stress test on an adjusted CET1 ratio (Ch 12)
**Bad equilibrium** — self-fulfilling fear of sovereign payment difficulty that raises yields and causes a liquidity crisis (Ch 6)
**Bail-in** — converting unsecured bank creditors into equity holders by seniority, before failure (Ch 12, Ch 13)
**Blue debt / red debt** — mutualized debt up to 60% of GDP and national debt above it (Ch 8)
**BRRD** — Bank Recovery and Resolution Directive, the EU-wide resolution rule book (Ch 13)
**CAC (collective action clause)** — clause letting a defined majority of bondholders amend terms for all (Ch 5, Ch 10)
**Competitiveness-adjusted debt ratio** — headline debt/GDP multiplied by the country's GDP deflator relative to a benchmark since a base year (Ch 2)
**Conversion risk** — spread component from fear of euro exit or break-up (Ch 1)
**Country-of-origin risk** — funding premium tied to a firm's or bank's member state, not its fundamentals (Ch 13)
**CRA** — credit rating agency (Ch 4)
**CSRs** — country-specific recommendations of the European Semester (Ch 2)
**Cyclical transfer mechanism** — rule-based payments from members above the area-average cycle to those below it (Ch 9)
**Debt deflation** — falling prices or nominal income raising the real debt burden, with feedback to demand (Ch 2)
**Debt inflation (relative)** — the gain to a country whose prices must rise relative to partners (Ch 2)
**Debt overhang / legacy debt** — debt stock from the crisis large enough to deter new voluntary lending (Ch 10)
**Denominator effect** — the rise in debt/GDP caused by falling GDP (Ch 3)
**Disintermediation** — replacing bank loans with capital-market funding (Ch 11)
**Distance-to-default (DTD)** — standard deviations between the market value of a bank's assets and its liabilities (Ch 12)
**EB / TB consolidation** — expenditure-based and tax-based fiscal adjustment (Ch 1)
**Efficient market theory** — the view that yields are the best estimate of default risk (Ch 6)
**ELA** — Exceptional Liquidity Assistance from a national central bank, as named in the text (Ch 3, Ch 5)
**ESM / EFSF** — the permanent and temporary euro-area financing mechanisms (Ch 5, Ch 8, Ch 13)
**European Semester** — annual EU policy coordination cycle (Ch 2, Ch 8)
**Financial trilemma** — financial stability, cross-border integration and national financial policy cannot all hold (Ch 13)
**Fiscal effort** — actual fiscal balance minus the debt-stabilizing balance (Ch 1)
**Fiscal headwind** — change in the cyclically adjusted primary balance, the demand withdrawn by consolidation (Ch 3)
**Fiscal Pact** — treaty rule of a structural deficit below 0.5% of GDP (Ch 8)
**Fiscal solvency equation** — regression of the primary balance on lagged debt and the output gap (Ch 10)
**Forecast breakdown** — a significant widening between two competing sovereign default probability estimates (Ch 4)
**HRADF** — Hellenic Republic Asset Development Fund, the Greek privatization fund (Ch 7)
**Holdouts** — creditors who stay out of a debt exchange (Ch 5, Ch 10)
**Home-country control** — prudential supervision by the member state that licensed the bank (Ch 13)
**Implied probability to default** — default probability backed out of bond spreads, CDS spreads or ratings (Ch 4)
**Internal devaluation** — fall in domestic wages and prices relative to partners (Ch 1, Ch 2, Ch 13)
**Leverage ratio** — core Tier 1 capital over total unweighted IFRS assets (Ch 12)
**Lender of last resort** — central bank backstop for banks and, in the secondary market, for sovereigns (Ch 6, Ch 8)
**MIP** — Macroeconomic Imbalance Procedure (Ch 8)
**Multiple equilibria** — coexistence of a good and a bad outcome for a sovereign without a liquidity backstop (Ch 6)
**Neglected risk** — improbable states left out of the pricing of "safe" securities (Ch 10)
**No-bailout (official / private)** — the Treaty ban on public-to-public rescue, and the policy of not repaying private creditors with official money (Ch 10)
**OCA (optimal currency area)** — an area meeting criteria of trade integration, symmetric shocks, labour flexibility and mobility, and fiscal federalism (Ch 1)
**OMT** — the ECB's conditional secondary-market government bond purchase programme (Ch 5, Ch 6, Ch 8)
**OSI** — official sector involvement, easing of official loan terms (Ch 5, Ch 10)
**Policy hubris** — officially declaring unsustainable debt sustainable (Ch 4)
**PSI** — private sector involvement in sovereign debt relief (Ch 5)
**Redemption fund** — proposed jointly guaranteed fund for debt above 60% of GDP, repaid over 25 years (Ch 8)
**Relative pessimism** — ranking of risk forecasts by asymmetry of forecast loss (Ch 4)
**SMP** — the ECB's 2010-2012 Securities Market Programme (Ch 5)
**Sovereign cocos** — sovereign bonds whose terms ease automatically when the debt ratio crosses a threshold (Ch 10)
**SRM / SRF** — Single Resolution Mechanism and Fund (Ch 13)
**SSM** — Single Supervisory Mechanism led by the ECB (Ch 12, Ch 13)
**Stability gap** — primary balance minus the debt-stabilizing primary balance (Ch 8)
**Sudden stop** — abrupt end of external financing for a sovereign or a bank-dependent firm (Ch 1, Ch 11)
**Target 2** — settlement system whose balances finance national banking systems through the ESCB (Ch 8)
**Troika** — the EU, ECB and IMF as official creditors (Ch 7)
**War of attrition** — prolonged, reactive crisis management where no party accepts losses first (Ch 10)
