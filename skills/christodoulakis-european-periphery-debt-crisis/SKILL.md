## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. How periphery stress builds (Ch 1 d'Arvisenet; Ch 12 Blundell-Wignall and Roulet)
- Chain: common low rates -> real rates far below growth in the south -> private borrowing binge -> construction or public-spending boom -> unit labour cost divergence -> external deficits -> sudden stop.
- A euro member borrows as if in a foreign currency. The euro removed exchange-rate speculation, not speculation in debt.
- Screen with nominal growth minus interest rate, private credit growth, construction share and current account. The fiscal balance misleads in a boom (Spain had a surplus).
- OCA checklist: trade integration, symmetric shocks, labour flexibility, labour mobility, fiscal federalism. D'Arvisenet says only the first was met.
- Blundell-Wignall and Roulet add the real-shock view: the north supplied emerging markets, the south competed with them.

### 2. Pricing sovereign spreads (Ch 1; Ch 6 De Grauwe; Ch 4 Christodoulakis)
- D'Arvisenet's panel model (to 2010 Q4): `S = -0.78 + 0.02 R x DS^2 - 0.09 FE - 0.09 Y - 0.102 L + 0.25 R + 0.15 DS`, R2 0.67. Use the residual as a stress gauge. It reached 750 bp for Greece.
- De Grauwe: without a liquidity backstop, sovereign markets have multiple equilibria. Fear alone can force default. Use this when a spread move outruns fundamentals.
- Christodoulakis: compare default probabilities implied by bonds, CDS and ratings. Their pessimism ranking rotates in regimes (RR1, RR2, RR3) after events, differently for the Hellenic, Latin and Celtic "clubs".

### 3. Debt arithmetic that changes the verdict
- Debt-stabilizing primary balance: `sp = d(t-1) x (r - g)` (Ch 1). Full identity with nominal growth in Ch 12.
- Competitiveness-adjusted debt ratio (Ch 2 Gros and Alcidi): headline ratio x relative GDP deflator since 1999. Greece 177 -> 190, Germany 80 -> 72. A successful internal devaluation worsens the headline ratio.
- Denominator effect (Ch 3 Anderson and Stallings): Greek consolidation added about 11 points a year to debt/GDP in 2010-2012.
- Stability gap (Ch 8 Mathieu and Sterdyniak): primary balance minus the stabilizing balance. In 2007 the euro area was 1.8 points above it.
- Fiscal solvency regression (Ch 10 Mody): the euro area's primary-balance response to debt in 2008-13 was 0.14, against -0.01 outside.

### 4. Adjustment paths: Greece versus Ireland (Ch 3)
- Decompose growth into export effect, fiscal headwind and other.
- Fiscal headwind = change in cyclically adjusted primary balance x multiplier. Greece: 5.6% of GDP a year x 1.0. Ireland: 1.7% x 0.4.
- Export effect scales with domestic value-added in exports: about 55% of GDP in Ireland, 15% in Greece.
- Prefer a slower, back-loaded consolidation with public investment when the multiplier is high.
- Contrast: Ch 1 reports evidence that spending-based consolidation is less contractionary. Ch 8 and Ch 10 argue multipliers were underestimated.

### 5. Restructuring: when and how
- Xafa (Ch 5): the 2012 Greek exchange gave 53.5% face-value loss, net relief of 46% of GDP with the buyback, yet debt/GDP ended higher because GDP fell. She doubts an earlier haircut was feasible or sufficient.
- Mody (Ch 10): delay and ad hoc handling are the costly choices. Official loans subordinate private debt, then official debt becomes junior in effect once its terms are eased. His event study finds the Deauville effect small and transient.
- Christodoulakis (Ch 4): "policy hubris" delayed restructuring.
- Use net relief accounting (gross haircut minus domestic bank recapitalization) and check the creditor mix afterwards.

### 6. Proposed instruments, by proponent
- Mutualization plus a small counter-cyclical central budget (Ch 1).
- Cyclical transfer scheme balanced each period, triggered by output-gap or short-term unemployment deviations (Ch 9 Bernoth and Engler).
- Sovereign cocos: automatic maturity extension when lagged debt/GDP crosses a contracted threshold (Ch 10).
- Full ECB or eurobond guarantee tied to negotiated policy coordination. Rejects fiscal federalism, market discipline and numerical rules (Ch 8).
- ECB yield cap or QE, bank clean-up, 5% leverage ratio, bank separation, long-run full fiscal union (Ch 12).
- Banking union: SSM, SRM, bail-in (Ch 13 Tsibanoulis and Kounadis).
- Privatization as a reform tool, "not a panacea" (Ch 7 Christodoulakis).
- Ch 8 reviews and criticizes the redemption pact, eurobond agency, blue/red bonds, EMF and ESDRR proposals.

### 7. Political and legal constraints
- Karlsruhe (Ch 6): the German court's objections to OMT rest on efficient markets and on central banks needing equity. De Grauwe rejects both. ECB losses are a redistribution of interest among treasuries.
- Two "no-bailouts" (Ch 10): the Treaty ban on official rescue and the policy choice on private creditors. Keeping the first requires enforcing the second.
- Troika negotiation (Ch 7): nothing agreed until all agreed, substance fixed, time on the creditor's side. The Greek privatization target went from EUR 3 bn to EUR 50 bn against a government estimate of EUR 15 bn.
- "No crisis, no reform" (Ch 1). Vague country recommendations that strong states ignore (Ch 2).
- Financial trilemma (Ch 13): stability, integration and national control cannot all hold.

### 8. Banks, firms and credit
- Judge banks by unweighted leverage and distance-to-default above 3 standard deviations (Ch 12).
- Zero risk weights and high sovereign yields crowd out SME loans (Ch 12).
- Debt deflation hits domestic-services SMEs and their banks first (Ch 2).
- Rated firms kept market access. Insolvencies rose eightfold in Spain against 2006 (Ch 11 Ganguin).
- Funding cost follows the country of incorporation while resolution and deposit insurance stay national (Ch 13).

### 9. Where the contributors disagree
- Fiscal federalism: for (Ch 1, Ch 9, Ch 12), against (Ch 8), unavailable (Ch 10).
- Safe sovereign debt by guarantee (Ch 8) versus risky debt with automatic restructuring (Ch 10).
- Internal devaluation: unavoidable (Ch 2) versus failed (Ch 13).
- Rating agencies: perverse incentives (Ch 4) versus bridge for information gaps (Ch 11).
Do not reconcile these. Report both with their chapters.
