# Cheatsheet

Decision rules from the volume. Each rule is one contributor's judgment. Text dates from 2013-2014.

## Decision rules
- When real rates sit far below nominal growth and credit goes to construction or current public spending, expect external deficits and a later sudden stop, because the tax base and spreads look sound until financing stops (Ch 1).
- When a member still has a price gap to close, multiply debt/GDP by the relative deflator before judging sustainability, because internal devaluation lowers nominal GDP (Ch 2).
- When consolidation is fast and the economy is closed with a large output gap, expect the debt ratio to rise, because the denominator effect dominates (Ch 3).
- When bonds, CDS and ratings disagree, track which is most pessimistic. CDS led bonds early. Agencies topping the ranking was the Greek end-state (Ch 4).
- When valuing a restructuring, net out domestic bank recapitalization and check who holds what remains (Ch 5).
- When a sovereign has no central bank backstop, treat a spread spike as a possible self-fulfilling run, not only as news on solvency (Ch 6).
- When a programme raises its privatization target sharply, read it as a failed macro baseline (Ch 7).
- When structural balances drive a policy call, recompute them under a second potential-output assumption (Ch 8).
- When output gaps of two members are weakly correlated, a single rate destabilizes both, and transfers between them would be large (Ch 9).
- When official loans replace private debt, expect the remaining private debt to be repriced as junior, then favoured again once official terms are eased (Ch 10).
- When sovereign yields are near or above bank loan rates and sovereigns carry a zero risk weight, expect SME credit to keep shrinking (Ch 12).
- When deposit insurance and resolution stay national, expect funding costs to follow the country, not the firm (Ch 13).

## Identities
- Debt-stabilizing primary balance: `sp = d(t-1) x (r - g)` (Ch 1).
- Debt change: `-pb + [(i - g) / (1 + g)] x d(t-1)` (Ch 12).
- Adjusted debt ratio: `(D/Y) x (P_country / P_benchmark)` (Ch 2).
- Fiscal drag on growth: change in cyclically adjusted primary balance x multiplier (Ch 3).
- Net relief: face tendered x haircut - bank recapitalization + (buyback face - cost) (Ch 5).

## Tells
- Corporate cash hoarding in one region: funding doubt (Ch 11).
- Each crisis case declared "exceptional" or "not a template": no rule exists (Ch 10).
- New bonds trading far below par after an exchange: a second default is priced (Ch 5).
- Rated-firm defaults normal but insolvencies multiplying: bank-dependent small firms are failing (Ch 11).
- Potential output revised down after the fact: structural deficits are being created by estimation (Ch 8).
- Youth emigration of the highly qualified: adjustment through depopulation (Ch 12).
