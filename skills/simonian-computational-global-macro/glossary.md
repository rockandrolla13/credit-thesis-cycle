# Glossary: Computational Global Macro

**Assurance problem** — the trust risk in a Stag Hunt: the lone cooperator takes the "sucker" payoff (Ch 3)
**ATE / CATE / ITE** — average, conditional average and individual treatment effects (Ch 5)
**Attack-Defense Game** — one player wants to change the status quo, the other to preserve it (Ch 2)
**Backdoor adjustment** — conditioning on common causes of treatment and outcome to isolate the causal effect (Ch 5)
**Bargaining power** — a player's weight in the Myerson value, a function of economic size, market access and political and military influence (Ch 3)
**Battle of the Sexes** — coordination game where players want to act together but prefer different joint actions (Ch 3)
**Bayesian game** — game of incomplete information in which players have types (Ch 2)
**Black-Litterman (BL)** — Bayesian allocation model that blends benchmark-implied returns with manager views (Ch 6)
**Blocking pair** — a lesser and greater power that would both prefer each other to their current match (Ch 3)
**Bounded rationality** — limits on computing and executing the rational act (Ch 1)
**Causal Markov Condition** — given its direct causes, a node is independent of everything except its direct causes and effects (Ch 5)
**Causal proximity** — closeness of one candidate causal graph to the others (Ch 5)
**CMAB** — Contextual Multi-Armed Bandit, a bandit whose arms are evaluated in contexts (Ch 2)
**Collider** — node where two incoming edges meet on a path (Ch 5)
**Colonel Blotto game** — zero-sum game of splitting a budget across battlefields (Ch 5)
**Commitment** — action taken independently of its envisaged consequences (Ch 1)
**Computational global macro** — the book's approach: game theory, machine learning and causal inference applied to thematic macro (Preface)
**Confounding variable** — variable influencing both treatment and outcome (Ch 5)
**Constrained maximizer** — agent who maximizes subject to constraints, plans and commitments (Ch 1)
**Context** — the state of the world in a CMAB, representing a player's type (Ch 2)
**Coordination penalty (δ)** — payoff loss for deviating from the other central bank's action (Ch 4)
**Correlated equilibrium** — equilibrium in which players condition on a shared signal (Ch 3)
**Correlating device** — the signal that coordinates players without communication (Ch 3)
**Counterfactual distance (d_C)** — share of possible counterfactual dependencies on which two graphs disagree (Ch 5)
**d-separation** — graphical test of conditional independence in a DAG (Ch 5)
**DAG** — directed acyclic graph of causal dependencies (Ch 5)
**Diachronic utility** — longer-term utility, often bought with short-term sacrifice (Ch 1)
**do operator** — notation for externally setting a variable, do(X = x) (Ch 5)
**Epistemic Shapley value** — Shapley procedure with propositions as players, used to aggregate probabilities coherently (Ch 6)
**Evolutionarily Stable Strategy (ESS)** — strategy that rivals cannot easily displace (Ch 2)
**Exploration vs exploitation** — trying unknown options versus choosing known good ones (Ch 2, 4)
**External solution** — a fix to the Prisoner's Dilemma that changes the rules or payoffs (Ch 2)
**Focal point** — salient choice players converge on with no signal (Ch 3)
**Free-rider problem** — incentive to consume a public good without contributing (Ch 1)
**Gale-Shapley algorithm** — stable-matching procedure, used here for alliance formation (Ch 3)
**GAN** — Generative Adversarial Network: Generator against Discriminator (Ch 6)
**Geopolitical risk** — potential detriment to a portfolio's positions from political and political-economic actions within and among states and non-state actors (Ch 1)
**Granger causality** — x precedes y and predicts it better than a naive rival (Ch 5)
**Ideal value** — the average of managers' estimates for a proposition (Ch 6)
**Internal solution** — spontaneous, voluntary cooperation with no change in the game (Ch 2)
**Kullback-Leibler divergence** — information lost when one distribution approximates another (Ch 6)
**Leak probability** — chance of an effect when no modeled cause is active (Ch 5)
**Levels of analysis** — individual, state, interstate (Ch 1)
**Lexicographic beliefs** — beliefs that an opponent acts in an order of priority (Ch 4)
**Liberalism** — worldview in which states seek absolute gains through institutions (Ch 1)
**Markov Decision Process (MDP)** — states, actions, transition probabilities and rewards (Ch 4)
**Markov equivalence** — two DAGs sharing the same d-separations (Ch 5)
**Maximizing difference** — giving up absolute payoff to worsen the opponent's position (Ch 2)
**Myerson value (MV)** — allocation weighted by bargaining power, aimed at stability (Ch 3)
**Nash equilibrium** — each action is a best response to the others (Ch 2)
**Noisy-OR (NOR)** — probabilistic OR that combines independent causes (Ch 5)
**Ordinal Black-Litterman (OBL)** — Black-Litterman with rank-based views (Ch 6)
**Pareto optimal** — no one can be made better off without making another worse off (Ch 2)
**Prisoner's Dilemma (PD)** — game where the dominant act conflicts with the cooperative outcome (Ch 2)
**Probabilistic distance (d_P)** — share of possible conditional independencies entailed by only one of two graphs (Ch 5)
**Proportional navigation** — pursuit rule that sets velocity from the line-of-sight rate (Ch 4)
**Pursuit-Evasion game** — one player closes a gap, the other avoids contact (Ch 4)
**Q-learning** — reinforcement learning that updates Q(s, a) from the reward and the best next Q-value (Ch 4)
**Realism (Defensive, Offensive)** — worldview of states seeking security or power, valuing relative gains (Ch 1)
**Reservation value** — the minimum offer a player accepts (Ch 3)
**rBL** — robust Black-Litterman with multiple priors over the covariance matrix (Ch 6)
**Run-Pass Game** — an Attack-Defense game, a variant of Matching Pennies (Ch 2)
**SARSA** — on-policy reinforcement learning that updates with the action actually taken next (Ch 5)
**Satisficer** — agent who accepts the first "good enough" option (Ch 1)
**Shapley value (SV)** — average marginal contribution across all player orderings (Ch 3, 6)
**Signal quality** — clarity of a central bank's communication, from 0 to 1 (Ch 4)
**Signaling game** — an informed sender transmits a costly signal to a receiver (Ch 4)
**Social role** — set of norms binding whoever occupies the role (Ch 1)
**Stackelberg game** — leader moves first, follower responds (Ch 4)
**Stag Hunt (SH)** — choice between a certain small payoff and an uncertain large joint one (Ch 3)
**Structural causal model (SCM)** — a DAG plus structural equations (Ch 5)
**Thematic global macro** — discretionary macro focused on developments with the broadest market impact (Preface)
**Thompson Sampling** — bandit rule that samples each arm's reward probability from a Beta posterior (Ch 5)
**Tit-for-tat** — cooperate first, then copy the opponent's last move (Ch 2)
**Total distance score** — sum of a causal scheme's probabilistic and counterfactual distances to the others (Ch 5, 6)
**Type** — a player's character, expressed as a distinct payoff matrix (Ch 2)
**UCB** — Upper Confidence Bound rule: pick the arm with the highest mean plus exploration bonus (Ch 2)
**War of Attrition (WA)** — sequential conflict where each player decides when to retreat (Ch 4)
**Worldview** — the theoretical orientation fixing actors' goals and the nature of the system (Ch 1)
