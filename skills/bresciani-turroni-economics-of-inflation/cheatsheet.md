# Cheatsheet: The Economics of Inflation (Bresciani-Turroni)

## Decision rules
- When a currency falls and officials blame the balance of payments, check the deficit and the note issue first, because a non-monetary fall is self-limiting. (Ch 2, Ch 2a)
- When real money balances are very low, do not call policy tight. Low real balances mean flight. (Ch 4)
- When the central bank discounts below the rate of depreciation, expect its credit to finance the attack on the currency. Price credit, do not ration it. (Ch 2)
- When the exchange is supported with reserves while issues continue, expect the support to fail. (Ch 2, Ch 3)
- When prices start to include expected depreciation, expect home prices to overshoot world prices and exports to stall. (Ch 3, Ch 6)
- When unemployment rises during depreciation, the stimulus phase is over. (Ch 5, Ch 8)
- When stabilising, stop State borrowing from the central bank on a fixed date and cap the new issue. Do not wait for a trade surplus, a foreign loan or a settled external debt. (Ch 9, Ch 11)
- After stabilising, restrict credit as soon as the free-market exchange weakens. Currency stability comes before support for business. (Ch 9, Ch 10)
- When a post-inflation slump shows dear money plus idle heavy industry, treat it as a capital-structure problem. Do not meet it with central bank credit. (Ch 10)
- When a recovery runs on short-term foreign money, treat it as unstable. (Ch 12)

## Which phase is this? (German reference pattern)

| Sign | Early | Middle | Late | Terminal |
|------|------|------|------|------|
| Money against prices | Money ahead | Exchange ahead of prices, prices ahead of money | Prices follow exchange quickly | All move together |
| Velocity index (1913 = 1) | Below 1 | 1 to 2 | 2 to 10 | Above 10 |
| Divergence D | About 1 | 1.5 to 3.25 | 1.5 to 2.4 | Near or below 1 |
| Unemployment against depreciation | Falls | Falls | Rises | Mass unemployment |
| Shares in gold | Hold | Track the dollar at a low level | Collapse (2.72) | Spike as last hedge |
| Real wages | Fall slowly | Fall, jobs rise | Fall, then swing wildly | Swing wildly |
| Budget effect of depreciation | Lowers debt service | Neutral | Widens deficit | Taxes cover under 1% |

The bands above are grouped from the author's tables. He does not state them as thresholds.

## Thresholds and reference numbers
- Peak divergence: 3.25 (February 1920). (Ch 3)
- Real value of the note issue: about 6 milliard gold marks pre-war; under 100 million on some days in August 1923. (Ch 4)
- Velocity index, wholesale basis: 0.43 (June 1919) to 17.79 (October 1923). (Ch 4)
- Share index in gold, 1913 = 100: 8.47 (February 1920), 2.72 (October 1922), 39.36 (November 1923). (Ch 7)
- Miners' real wages, 1913 = 100: 47.6 to 86.2 within 1923. (Ch 8)
- Unemployed union members: 0.6 per cent (mid-1922) to 28.2 per cent (December 1923). (Ch 8)
- Taxes covered about 15 per cent of Reich spending, 1914 to October 1923. (Ch 2)
- Rentenmark cap: 2,400 million, half for the State. Stabilisation rate: 4,200 milliard paper marks per dollar. (Ch 9)
- Revaluation: mortgages 25 per cent, debentures 15 per cent, public loans 2.5 per cent of nominal (12.5 for drawn bonds of old holders). (Ch 8)
- One-month money at 72 per cent a year, spring 1924. (Ch 10)
- Purely real exchange shocks: author's rough bound 15 to 30 per cent. (Ch 2)

## Trade-off matrix: holding wealth through the German inflation

| Asset | Outcome | Why |
|------|------|------|
| Government bonds, mortgages, deposits | Near-total loss | Nominal claims |
| Shares held from 1913 | About three-quarters lost in gold | Dilution, low payouts, forced sales |
| Shares bought in 1920, sold in 1921 | Real value kept | Dollar-linked phase |
| Houses | Low price, low yield | Rent control |
| Farm land, industrial plant with debt | Gain | Debt erased, prices follow exchange |
| Foreign exchange | Best store of value | But access was restricted |
| Stable-value bonds | Held value | Indexed interest |

## Tells and smells
- Officials say "there is no inflation, money is scarce". (Ch 4)
- Bankruptcies fall to near zero. (Ch 5)
- Bank staff and current accounts multiply while output is below normal. (Ch 5)
- The currency falls first on the home bourse, not abroad. (Ch 2)
- Prices differ by means of payment. (Ch 3)
- Retail prices rise faster than wholesale. (Ch 3)
- Bad political news lifts share prices. (Ch 7)
- Shares bounce while liquidation has barely begun: a false barometer. (Ch 12)
- Short-notice deposits grow relative to long ones. (Ch 12)
