# Glossary: The Economics of Inflation (Bresciani-Turroni)

Chapter codes: Ch 2a is the Appendix to Chapter II. Ch 12 is the Appendix on 1924-31. Ch 13 is the statistical appendix.

**Abnormal demand for foreign exchange** — demand for hoarding, internal payments, speculation and hedging, on top of normal trade demand (Ch 2)
**Aggregates and systems** — industrial groups formed for financial motives, against groups with an economic logic (Ch 5)
**Apparent profits (Scheingewinne)** — paper profits that exist only because costs are booked at historical values (Ch 7)
**Balance of payments theory** — the "German" view that the passive balance and reparations drove the exchange down and that inflation followed (Ch 2)
**Barter terms of trade** — units of export given per unit of import (Ch 2a)
**Billion, trillion** — long scale: 1,000,000 squared and 1,000,000 cubed (Ch 1, Ch 13)
**Coefficient of divergence (D)** — actual exchange rate of the gold mark over the price parity; equally, world gold prices over German gold prices (Ch 3, Ch 13)
**Coefficient of elasticity** — a bank's liquid assets over its sight liabilities (Ch 12)
**Coefficient of inertia** — Messedaglia's term for the slowness of prices in following the exchange (Ch 3)
**Constant value (depreciation) clause** — obligation to repay a loan at its original real value (Ch 2, Ch 9)
**Conversion of circulating capital into fixed capital** — over-investment in plant and higher-order goods at the expense of the flow of consumption goods (Ch 10)
**Crisis of returns** — the theory that weak profit prospects caused the 1929-31 depression (Ch 12)
**Dilution** — repeated share issues below market price that turn gold capital into paper capital (Ch 7)
**"English" theory** — the view that the budget deficit and note issue caused the depreciation; the author's own (Ch 2)
**Equation of foreign exchanges** — exchange-rate index = ratio of purchasing powers in export prices times the index of the barter terms of trade (Ch 2a)
**Ertragswert** — earning-capacity value of a firm's assets (Ch 7)
**Exchange dumping (Valutadumping)** — exporting below world gold prices because the exchange has fallen faster than home costs (Ch 6)
**External value** — the currency's purchasing power in gold or foreign goods (Ch 3)
**Field of circulation** — the range of transactions still settled in the paper currency (Ch 4)
**Flight from the mark** — conversion of marks into foreign exchange, goods and shares out of distrust (Ch 2, Ch 4)
**Floating debt** — Treasury bills outstanding; the author's gauge of the deficit (Ch 1, Ch 2, Ch 13)
**Forced saving** — real resources taken from wage earners and fixed incomes by rising prices and put at entrepreneurs' disposal (Ch 5, Ch 10, Ch 12)
**Gold balance sheet** — compulsory restatement of company accounts in gold marks, decree of 28 December 1923 (Ch 7)
**Gold mark** — the pre-war mark at gold parity; unit of account for real values (Ch 1)
**Goldanleihe** — Gold Loan of August 1923, issued in small denominations and used as money (Ch 9)
**Governmental and private inflation** — note creation for the State and note creation for business (Ch 2)
**Income-velocity** — number of times a unit of money becomes income in a period (Ch 4)
**Inflation profiteers** — those enriched by borrowing marks and buying real assets (Ch 8)
**Instrumental goods** — producers' goods, as against goods for direct consumption (Ch 5, Ch 10)
**Internal value** — the currency's purchasing power over goods at home (Ch 3)
**Limitation of quantity** — Ricardo's principle that the quantity of paper money must be limited for prices to be stable (Ch 11)
**Mark = mark** — legal doctrine that a debt is discharged by its nominal amount (Ch 8)
**Multiplier and basic price** — late-1923 pricing: a gold base price times an index tied to the exchange (Ch 3)
**Notgeld** — emergency money issued by public bodies and firms (Ch 4, Ch 9)
**Plural-vote shares** — shares with many votes, issued to insiders (Ch 7, Ch 8)
**Premium against the risk of depreciation** — a mark-up in prices for the loss expected before paper receipts can be converted (Ch 3)
**Price parity** — ratio of German paper prices to US prices (Ch 3)
**Purchasing Power Parity** — the doctrine that the exchange equals the ratio of price levels; valid for the long run, per the author (Ch 2a)
**Rationalisation** — post-1924 reorganisation of industry to raise productivity (Ch 10, Ch 12)
**Real value of the circulation** — nominal circulation deflated by prices or by the gold mark rate (Ch 4, Ch 9)
**Reichsmark** — the currency from 11 October 1924; 1 reichsmark = 1 billion paper marks (Ch 9)
**Rentenmark** — the currency issued from 16 November 1923 by the Rentenbank; issue capped at 2,400 million (Ch 9)
**Revaluation** — partial legal restoration of old paper-mark claims, 1924-25 (Ch 8)
**Scale of sensitiveness** — ranking of taxes and expenses by how fast they follow depreciation (Ch 2)
**Sophism of the real value of the circulation** — the claim that low gold value of the note issue proves there is no inflation (Ch 4)
**Stabilisation crisis** — the shortage of working capital and the reversal of demand that follow the end of an inflation (Ch 10, Ch 12)
**Subsistence fund** — the flow of consumption goods that supports workers during production (Ch 10)
**Substanzwert** — "intrinsic" or replacement value of assets (Ch 7)
**Valorization** — charging taxes or debts at gold value as of the date the obligation arose (Ch 2)
**Valutapapiere** — securities in foreign currency; loosely, shares of firms with foreign earnings (Ch 7)
**Vicious circle** — exchange falls, prices rise, notes are issued, exchange falls again (Ch 2)
**Wertbeständiges Notgeld** — emergency money "with a constant value" (Ch 9)
**Working capital** — liquid means to buy materials and pay wages (Ch 7, Ch 10)
**Zwangswirtschaft** — the system of maximum prices and controls kept after the war (Ch 3)
