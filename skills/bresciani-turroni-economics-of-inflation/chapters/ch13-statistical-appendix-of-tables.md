# Appendix of Tables (Statistical Appendix): Index and Checked Extracts

## Core Idea
The book ends with thirteen numbered statistical tables on pp. 437-457, which hold the monthly series the chapters argue from. This file is a finding aid, not a transcription. It lists every appendix table with its book page and PDF page, says which were read from the page image, and gives short checked extracts of the core series. In the text extract this part of the book (lines 6991 to 7806) is mostly unreadable, because many tables are printed sideways. Go to the PDF for any number not given here.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **How to cite a number from this book**
  - When to use: before putting any figure from this book into a model or a memo.
  - How: (1) Look for it in a chapter file marked "checked against PDF p. N". (2) If it is not there, find the table below and open the PDF page. (3) Do not take digits from the extract's version of the appendix. (4) PDF page = book page + 1 throughout.
- **Units and scale**: the book uses the long scale. A milliard is 1,000 million. A billion is 1,000,000 squared. A trillion is 1,000,000 cubed. The 1923 columns switch units inside a column, marked by footnote signs. Read the footnote before reading the number.
- **Gold-mark conversion caveat**: the author warns under Table I that the 1923 gold figures "are somewhat uncertain", because of the averaging problem set out in the Appendix to Chapter III.

## Key Concepts
- **Table I**: the Reich's income, expenditure and increase in floating debt in gold marks. The author's chosen gauge of the deficit (Ch II).
- **Table II**: Treasury bills, the fiscal source of the note issue.
- **Table III**: Reichsbank note circulation.
- **Table IV**: paper marks per gold mark, monthly average. The book's standard deflator.
- **Table V**: wholesale and cost-of-living indices, in paper and in gold.
- **Table VI**: "price parities", German paper prices over US prices.
- **Table VII**: coefficients of divergence between internal and external value (Ch III).
- **Tables X and XI**: unemployment and wages (Ch VIII, Ch X).
- **Table XII**: share price indices (Ch VII).
- **Table XIII**: consumption statistics (Ch X).

## Mental Models
- Use Tables III, IV and V (a) together to rebuild the argument of Chapters I and IV. Circulation over the wholesale index gives the real value of the note issue. The wholesale index over circulation, with 1913 = 1, gives the velocity index.
- Use Table IV over the price parity to get Table VII. A reader can recompute the divergence for any month from Tables IV and V and a US price index.
- Use Table I's third column as the deficit. The author prefers it to any gold conversion of the budget.

## Anti-patterns
- **Taking appendix digits from the text extract**: the sideways tables came out as noise.
- **Mixing units in the 1923 columns**: the same column moves from plain index numbers to millions and milliards.
- **Treating the 1923 gold figures as precise**: the author says they are uncertain.
- **Reading Table II's heading literally**: see the note under that table.

## Worked Example
**Cross-checking the text against the appendix, and what does not reconcile.**
1. Gold mark rate. Chapter II says the gold mark was 3.40 paper marks in July 1919, 10.8 in December and 23.58 in February 1920. Table IV shows 3.59, 11.14 and 23.6. Two of three differ.
2. Coefficient of divergence. Chapter III gives 1.36 for October 1918, 3.25 for February 1920 and 1.18 for June 1918. Table VII (a) agrees. Chapter III gives 1.71 for June 1920. Table VII (a) shows 1.64.
3. Circulation. Chapter II gives 1,280 milliards at end 1922, then 1,984, 3,513, 5,518 and 6,546 for January to April 1923. Table III agrees exactly.
4. Floating debt in 1923. Chapter IX gives increases of 906, 1,506 and 860 million gold marks for August to October 1923. Table I shows 914.7, 1,564.1 and 860.1. Chapter IX also gives 1,560 as September's borrowing.
5. Deficit for 1923. Chapter II's Table XI gives 6,538.3 for April to December 1923. Table I's 4,690.1 covers April to October only. These are different periods, not a conflict.
6. Gold reserve. Chapter I's footnote gives 1,253 million marks for 31 July 1914. Table VIII gives 1,356.8 for 23 July 1914. Year-end figures from 1914 to 1918 agree after rounding.
7. Trade volumes. Chapter VI gives exports of 3.2, 12.0 and 27.6 million quintals for January, August and December 1919. Table IX shows 323, 1,202 and 2,762 thousand tons, which agree. Chapter VI's import figures (1.7, 10.2 and 8.2 million quintals for January, July and December 1919) do not match Table IX (200, 1,298 and 1,081 thousand tons).
8. Wholesale index. Chapter I gives 16.85 for February 1920 and 13.08 for May 1921. Table V (a) shows 16.8 and 13.1.
9. Rule drawn from this: where text and appendix differ, cite both and say so.

## Key Takeaways
1. Six appendix tables carry the book's monetary argument: I, II, III, IV, V and VII.
2. Eleven of the nineteen table parts listed above were read from the page image for this skill.
3. Not opened: V (b), V (c), V (d), VI, VII (b) and the Reparation Commission table. XIII (d) to (f) were seen but not reproduced.
4. The appendix and the chapter text disagree in a handful of places. The Worked Example lists those found.
5. The main-text tables (I to XL, plus many unnumbered footnote tables) are listed in the book's own "List of Tables" on pp. 15-17 and are covered chapter by chapter in the other files.

## Extraction Notes
- The extract of this part of the book contains about 360 table rows, most of them unusable. Tables II, III, IV, V, VII and XII are printed sideways and came out as scattered characters.
- Tables I, X, XI and XIII are printed upright and are partly readable in the extract, but columns are merged or shifted (Table I for 1921 to 1923 loses its expenditure and debt columns). All extracts above come from the PDF pages, not from the extract.
- In Table II and Table III the footnote signs that mark billions and trillions in 1923 are small on the page. The units given above follow the table footnotes as read.
- The book's index (pp. 459 onwards) was not processed.
- The book also has thirty diagrams (I to XXX), listed on pp. 19-20 of the book. All are image placeholders in the extract. Only Diagram VI and Charts I and II of the Appendix to Chapter II were seen in passing, and no data is taken from any of them.
