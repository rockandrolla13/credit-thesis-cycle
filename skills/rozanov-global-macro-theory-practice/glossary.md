# Glossary: Global Macro: Theory and Practice (ed. Rozanov)

**Absolute loss control** — a cap on loss of capital over a set interval; at the limit all positions are closed (Ch 7)
**Anthropomorphic stop-loss** — a stop built into a trading system for human comfort, not for statistical reasons (Ch 4)
**Balance sheet leverage** — financing long positions with the cash proceeds of short sales (Ch 10)
**Base and growth portfolio** — Mercer's split: a defensive, liability-matching part and a risk-seeking part (Ch 11)
**Behavioural macro strategy** — forecasting markets by understanding the reaction function of powerful participants; distinct from Kahneman-Tversky behavioural finance (Ch 5)
**Canonical currency crisis model** — a peg with finite reserves, open capital markets and a money-financed deficit, ending in a speculative attack (Ch 5)
**Capital utilisation ratio** — (margin requirements + net option premium cost) ÷ portfolio equity (Ch 10)
**Competence drift** — a macro fund moving into markets where it lacks expertise or infrastructure (Ch 12)
**Convergence versus divergence** — mean-reversion capture versus breakout capture; macro tends to the second (Ch 2)
**Cross-product margining** — setting margin on the net risk of offsetting positions across products (Ch 9)
**Crowded trade** — speculative interest concentrated in one position; a source of episodic illiquidity (Ch 7)
**CTA / managed futures** — commodity trading advisor; systematic, mostly trend-following futures trading (Ch 1, 3, 4)
**Derivatives-based leverage** — leverage inherent in the instrument; nothing is borrowed (Ch 10)
**Derivatives intermediation** — trading OTC swaps in a prime broker's name; "a credit enhancement transaction masquerading as an operational function" (Ch 9)
**Drawdown control** — a halt triggered by the fall from peak cumulative return; implies that profits belong to the fund (Ch 7)
**Emerging market "situation"** — a country-driven case where markets deepen or disintegrate fast and tails are extreme, whatever the country's label (Ch 6)
**Emotional empathy** — Weber's term, used for studying the herd once an actor's framework has failed (Ch 5)
**Expected tail loss (ETL, CVaR)** — the average loss given that the loss exceeds VaR (Ch 13)
**Factor shock** — the assumed move in a core market price in a stress scenario (Ch 7)
**Five Stages of Empire** — Murrin's cycle: regionalisation, ascension, maturity, overextension, decline and legacy; not a quantitative model (Ch 8)
**Fundamental macro strategy** — the view that economic variables drive prices over the long run (Ch 5)
**Good and bad correlations** — correlation that helps in a sell-off versus correlation that hurts (Ch 14)
**Gross notional leverage** — total notional ÷ NAV; "a flawed risk indicator" for macro funds (Ch 10)
**GTAA** — global tactical asset allocation; systematic, originally relative-value and valuation-based (Ch 4, 11, 14)
**Impossible trinity** — a fixed exchange rate, an open capital account and independent monetary policy cannot coexist (Ch 5)
**Instrumentalist view** — a model is a tool of convenience judged by usefulness, not by truth (Ch 7)
**Intellectual empathy** — understanding a policymaker's framework without having to agree with it (Ch 5)
**Investable index** — a hedge fund index built only from open funds with capacity (Ch 12)
**KIKO / TARN** — knock-in knock-out options and target accrual redemption notes sold to corporates; the hidden positioning behind 2008 currency collapses (Ch 6)
**Liquidity black hole** — many large speculators exiting one position at once (Ch 7)
**Liquidity drift** — a macro fund moving into esoteric, illiquid markets (Ch 12)
**Macro investing versus macro trading** — long-horizon, contrarian, unleveraged versus short-horizon, leveraged (Ch 1)
**Margin leverage** — borrowing from the prime broker (Ch 10)
**Margin-to-equity ratio** — margin posted ÷ NAV; about 20% for the average macro fund per the author (Ch 10)
**Niche betas** — value, carry, sentiment and similar systematic return sources (Ch 11)
**Original sin** — an emerging market's inability to issue much debt in its own currency (Ch 6)
**Overlay** — a derivatives programme run on top of a client's portfolio (Ch 4, 14)
**Price takers** — participants insensitive to price: central banks, corporates, hedgers, index investors (Ch 11, 14)
**Reaction function** — how a participant or policymaker responds to a stimulus (Ch 5)
**Reflexivity** — Soros's idea that self-reinforcing biases can change fundamentals (Ch 1)
**Regime credibility** — whether a government's macro promise (a peg, a rate path, a bank rescue) can be kept; what macro funds bet on (Ch 6)
**Reverse stress test** — a search for the set of shocks that maximises portfolio loss (Ch 7)
**Risk allocation** — sizing by contribution to risk, not by capital (Ch 14)
**Roach motel** — a market that is easy to enter and impossible to exit (Ch 7)
**Serial correlation** — correlation of returns with their own lag; a sign of smoothing from illiquid holdings (Ch 13)
**Short-volatility profile** — steady gains and rare large losses; shows as strongly negative skew with high kurtosis (Ch 10, 12)
**Staying power** — the capacity to hold a long-horizon valuation trade through adverse momentum (Ch 14)
**Synthetic equity finance** — the financier owns the asset and swaps the return to the client, allowing leverage beyond Regulation T (Ch 9)
**Themes** — a manager's macro framework, used as a compass for the presumed direction of markets (Ch 2)
**"Thin air"** — Frieda's label for 2H 2011: small gains take great effort and downside is large (Ch 6)
**Triffin dilemma** — supplying a reserve currency requires deficits that undermine its backing (Ch 5)
**Unsmoothing** — removing serial correlation from hedge fund returns before analysis (Ch 13)
**Value trap** — a valuation signal that keeps adding against a persistent trend (Ch 4)
**Verstehen** — Weber's "interpretative understanding"; the root of behavioural macro strategy (Ch 5)
