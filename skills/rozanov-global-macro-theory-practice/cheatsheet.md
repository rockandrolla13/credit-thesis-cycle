# Cheatsheet: Global Macro: Theory and Practice (ed. Rozanov)

An edited volume from 2012. Each rule belongs to the chapter's author.

## Decision rules
- When price moves against the flow of news, hedge, cut or close. (Ch 7)
- When you must reduce risk, exit. Hedge only if you cannot, and lift the hedge fast. (Ch 2, Ch 7)
- When a leg carries a factor outside the thesis, replace the leg. (Ch 7)
- When you forecast direction, forecast volatility too. Err high. A low volatility forecast is "quite dangerous". (Ch 5)
- When a dominant actor's framework is failing, drop valuation as a timing tool and study the actor's balance sheet. (Ch 5)
- When an imbalance looks unsustainable, ask whether global liquidity lets it persist. (Ch 6)
- When official intervention suppresses volatility, expect leverage to build behind it. (Ch 6)
- When spreads are tight and volatility is low, treat risk as high. De-risk or buy protection. Do not add leverage. (Ch 14)
- When you feel the need to hedge an asset, first ask why you hold it. (Ch 14)
- When a strategy's returns are not positively auto-correlated, a stop-loss tends to cost. (Ch 4)
- When a systematic manager overrides the model often, treat the fund as discretionary. (Ch 4, Ch 12)
- When a macro fund adds illiquid books, expect gates in a crisis. (Ch 1, Ch 12)
- When margin-to-equity jumps, meet the manager. (Ch 10)
- When a strategy shows a high Sharpe ratio, check skew, kurtosis and serial correlation before believing it. (Ch 12, Ch 13)
- When someone quotes "macro returns", ask which index. (Ch 12)
- One to three years after a dislocation, the averages favoured moving from trend followers to discretionary macro. (Ch 12)

## Loss control choice (Ch 7)
| Want | Use | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Cap loss of capital | Absolute loss limit | Lower |
| Protect earned profit | Drawdown limit | Higher. Deters risk-taking after gains |

## Leverage read (Ch 10)
1. How is it sourced: short proceeds, broker loan, or embedded in derivatives?
2. Margin-to-equity and capital utilisation ratio.
3. Volatility of what the notional sits in.
4. Liquidity. Two times in illiquid credit outranks four times in G7 rates.
5. Do the offsetting correlations hold in stress?

## Tells and smells
- Smooth returns, high notional, illiquid holdings: short-option profile. (Ch 10, Ch 12)
- Serial correlation near 0.5: smoothed marks. (Ch 13)
- Persistent beta in a macro manager: not macro. Episodic beta is normal. (Ch 2)
- A view with no matching position, or the reverse: inexperience. (Ch 2)
- A market that ignores good news or overreacts to bad: crowded positioning. (Ch 6)
- A currency move with no macro imbalance behind it: look for hidden derivative exposure. (Ch 6)
- A back-test that halves when the start date moves: not robust. (Ch 4)
- A hedge whose writer may not pay in the scenario hedged: no hedge. (Ch 9)
- A model that explains the last crisis perfectly: over-fitted. (Ch 4)
