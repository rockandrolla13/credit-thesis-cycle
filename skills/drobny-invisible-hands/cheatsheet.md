# Cheatsheet: The Invisible Hands (Drobny)

Interviews from 2009. Every rule is the named manager's own. Check the chapter before relying on a number.

## The author's three principles (Ch 1)
1. Risk-adjusted targets, not return targets.
2. Look forward, not backward. Macro first.
3. Rethink liquidity. Horizons are shorter than believed.

## Decision rules
- When a credit product's spread barely exceeds what risk-free liquidity provision pays, treat the market as a bubble, whatever the borrowers look like. (House, Ch 4)
- When protection is cheap because risk is priced near zero, buy it in option-like form and plan the exit around who can pay you. (House, Ch 4. Leitner, Ch 3)
- When a "risky asset is priced with a small risk premium", do not own it. Return follows price, not risk. (House, Ch 4)
- When the market prices one outcome as certain, ask whether a second reasonable school exists. If yes, trade the skew. (Philosopher, Ch 5)
- When the reason for a trade fails, exit, even at a profit. (Philosopher, Ch 5. Commodity Hedger, Ch 10)
- When the portfolio is down and no single theme is falsified, assume wrong sizing or hidden correlation. Cut everything. (Philosopher, Ch 5)
- When both legs of a hedge make money, it is one position. Cut gross on both. (Plasticine, Ch 13)
- When volatility quadruples, halving gross is not a risk cut. (Philosopher, Ch 5)
- When correlations start to rise, cut gross and net. Do not add hedges. (Commodity Investor, Ch 9)
- When you cannot quantify the risk of a policy decision, go flat and react afterwards. (Equity Trader, Ch 11)
- When funding stress shows in LIBOR, sell anything that can become illiquid. (Philosopher, Ch 5)
- When bank results are bad together and getting worse, the trajectory matters more than the size. Raise cash. (Predator, Ch 12)
- When a liquidity crisis is under way, drop valuation. Hold cash or government bonds. (Predator, Ch 12)
- When you are in cash and good companies' bonds reach "absurdly cheap prices", buy credit before equity. He bought bonds in late November 2008 and stocks in March 2009. (Predator, Ch 12)
- When interbank rates and CDS levels fall back and the rate of decline in the data slows, look for the turn. (Predator, Ch 12)
- When assets are expensive, cash is cheap to hold. When assets are cheap, cash is expensive. (Leitner, Ch 3)
- When everything is expensive, diversification gives nothing. (Leitner, Ch 3)
- When a strategy has worked for ten years and been copied, move half into something different. (Predator, Ch 12)
- When an allocation feels comfortable and nobody objects, step back. (Plasticine, Ch 13)
- When leverage is banned, look for it off balance sheet. It will be in private equity. (Pensioner, Ch 14)
- When a fund depends more on investment returns for its budget, it should take less risk, not more. (Drobny citing Bacow, Ch 2)
- When someone forecasts a bond rout, ask for the inflation view. Without inflation, higher real yields bring buyers. (Drobny, Ch 2)
- When an investment boom has a low marginal return and high sentiment, use the currency, not equities. (Professor, Ch 7)
- When a commodity's inventory is near its stocks-to-use threshold, expect a non-linear price move. (Commodity Investor, Ch 9)
- When in doubt, do a third. (Plasticine, Ch 13)

## Tells and smells
- A fund's volatility looks "too low": ask what instruments produce it. (Bond Trader, Ch 6)
- "Alpha" that is really equity, illiquidity, short volatility or credit. All four fail together. (Pensioner, Ch 14)
- A manager who has never had a significant drawdown. (Commodity Trader, Ch 8)
- A portfolio with "marginal" trades in it. (Commodity Trader, Ch 8)
- A stop the trader does not believe in: he re-enters the next day. (Philosopher, Ch 5)
- A stock that rises on bad news: expectations are low and shorts are crowded. (Equity Trader, Ch 11)
- Liquid managers being redeemed because illiquid ones cannot be. (Pensioner, Ch 14)
- An extreme forecast that nobody challenges: the crowd already holds it. (Plasticine, Ch 13)
- Deal sizes and liquidity offered far above prior years. (Bond Trader on 1998, Ch 6)
- A leveraged firm raising leverage and buying back stock into stress. (Predator on Lehman, Ch 12)
- Being the only possible buyer of what you own. (Predator, Ch 12)

## Loss arithmetic (Ch 1)
10% needs 11%. 20% needs 25%. 30% needs 43%. 40% needs 67%. 50% needs 100%. 60% needs 150%.
