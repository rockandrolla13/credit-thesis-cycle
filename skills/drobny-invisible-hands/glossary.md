# Glossary: The Invisible Hands (Drobny)

Terms as the book or the named speaker uses them. The chapter shows who said it.

**A-B-D ("Anything but the dollar")** — the 2009 consensus stance behind inflation bets, per the Plasticine Macro Trader (Ch 13)
**Accounting leverage** — leverage that shows on the fund's own balance sheet, such as repo or derivatives (Ch 14)
**Alpha** — excess return from skill. Zero-sum before costs (Ch 4). Includes macro, and is not tactical beta trading (Ch 5)
**Analytical Sharpe versus trading Sharpe** — the gap between the quality of ideas and the profit captured from them (Ch 9)
**Backwardation** — spot price above the futures price (Ch 8)
**Basic Principles** — the Professor's list of macroeconomic priors used to estimate fair value (Ch 7)
**Beauty contest** — Keynes's image of guessing what average opinion expects average opinion to be (Ch 5)
**Beta-plus** — "hard-to-access beta": private equity, distressed real estate and other illiquids. Cheap only while hard to access (Ch 5)
**Black swan (Popper's sense)** — an observation that falsifies a hypothesis, not an unforeseeable event (Ch 5)
**Bond vigilantes** — bond investors who push yields up in protest at inflationary policy (Ch 2)
**Break-even inflation** — nominal yield minus the real yield on a matching inflation-linked bond (Ch 2)
**Centipede portfolio** — hundreds of positions expressing one view, so legs can be cut without stopping (Ch 13)
**Client risk** — all investors leaving at once because they fear the others will (Ch 12)
**Cockroach mandate** — "being a survivor no matter what" (Ch 13)
**Commodity super cycle** — a long demand upswing from emerging market growth against finite resources (Ch 8)
**Contango** — futures price above spot (Ch 8)
**Convenience yield** — the premium for prompt physical delivery. It moves inversely to inventory (Ch 9)
**Corner solution** — bankruptcy, default or draconian spending cuts, which an optimization with only a budget constraint ignores (Ch 2)
**Cyclical versus secular** — cyclical analysis concerns microeconomic imbalances, secular analysis macroeconomic ones (Ch 9)
**Denominator effect** — an illiquid holding's share rises when the rest of the fund falls (Ch 1)
**Diffusion** — how far a fundamental event spreads across a supply chain (Ch 9)
**Dollar notional thinking** — allocating by dollar value instead of by risk (Ch 14)
**Dry powder** — cash or cash-like reserves kept for crises (Ch 4)
**Economic leverage** — leverage carried inside another entity the fund owns, such as a levered company or fund (Ch 14)
**Endowment Model (Yale Model)** — a diversified, equity-oriented portfolio that seeks an illiquidity premium (Ch 1)
**Export land model** — commodity producers consuming more of their own output, leaving less to export (Ch 9)
**Favorite trade** — the Drobny Global format: one best idea, defended to peers, with instrument and horizon (Ch 2)
**Fracture** — the Predator's word for the market break of fall 2008 (Ch 12)
**Franchise risk** — a long-only firm's fear of losing more than peers (Ch 13). Also counterparty-driven business risk in 2008 (Ch 9)
**Fundamentals to market / markets to fundamentals** — the Philosopher's two sources of trade ideas (Ch 5)
**Great Macro Experiment** — aggressive reflation to overcome debt deflation after a bubble (Ch 2)
**Gross derivative book** — notional derivatives held with bank counterparties (Ch 6)
**Headline versus core inflation** — core excludes volatile items, usually food and energy (Ch 8)
**House, the** — a book of many small, independent bets with a slight edge, like a casino owner (Ch 4)
**Hypothesis falsified** — the Philosopher's replacement for "stop loss": exit when the reason for the trade is disproved (Ch 5)
**Illiquidity premium** — extra return for giving up the option to sell. "A short put option on opportunity cost" (Ch 14)
**Kink** — the point past which losses force an institution to change behaviour (Ch 1)
**Lay board** — a pension board with non-professional plan members (Ch 14)
**LIBOR-OIS spread** — LIBOR minus the overnight indexed swap rate, a gauge of bank funding stress (Ch 5, Ch 7)
**Liquidity (Predator)** — days needed to sell a position or the portfolio, and who else holds it (Ch 12)
**Liquidity (Bond Trader)** — the cost to exit over a given period (Ch 6)
**Liquidity provider** — a long-term investor paid for pockets of illiquidity (Ch 4, citing Scholes)
**Macro overlay** — index shorts, puts and cash decisions laid over a stock portfolio (Ch 12)
**Macro prejudice** — a manager's own standing bias, stated so it can be controlled (Ch 13)
**Maximum Sharpe Ratio (MSR) portfolio** — the mix with the best return per unit of risk (Ch 14)
**Minsky moment** — the point at which debt-fuelled speculation unravels and no bid is found (Ch 13)
**Native volatility** — a portfolio's volatility before leverage (Ch 14)
**Negative externality (of illiquidity)** — one department earns the premium and the whole plan bears the cost (Ch 14)
**Nominal anchor** — a fixed reference, such as a currency conversion rate, that pins a trade's value (Ch 7)
**Noneconomic actors** — governments, reserve managers and hedgers whose goals are not profit (Ch 7)
**Over-commitment** — committing up to twice the target allocation to private funds (Ch 1)
**Paradox of perfection** — central bank skill encouraging excess risk taking (Ch 4)
**Plumbing** — money markets, funding and collateral flows that carry the financial system (Ch 5)
**Real macro** — structural, country-specific bets on policy and fundamentals over months (Ch 7)
**Real money** — fully funded, long-only, unlevered institutional investors (Ch 1)
**Reflexivity** — Soros's feedback loop between participants' beliefs and the situation (Ch 4)
**Rehypothecation** — a broker pledging customers' collateral for its own borrowing (Ch 5)
**Resource nationalism** — producers keeping commodities and processing at home (Ch 9)
**Risk budget** — a maximum amount of risk assigned to each manager by a central system (Ch 14)
**Risk collar** — sell an out-of-the-money call to buy an out-of-the-money put on a long, near zero cost (Ch 10)
**Risk reversal** — the same structure as a collar, in the House's words (Ch 4)
**Risk units** — position sizes set by tolerated daily swing, so trades are comparable (Ch 9)
**Roll yield** — return from rolling futures in a backwardated market (Ch 8)
**Second derivative** — how a data point changes holders' positions (Ch 11). Also the slowing of a rate of decline (Ch 12)
**Siegel's Paradox** — the author's label for the asymmetry of losses and gains (Ch 1)
**Single point volatility** — commodity markets gapping faster since speculative flows grew (Ch 9)
**Stochastic volatility** — volatility treated as a random process, not a constant (Ch 14)
**Threshold (stocks-to-use)** — the inventory level past which price moves become non-linear (Ch 9)
**Titanic scenario / Titanic funnel** — the Professor's catastrophe loss limit and the screen that sizes every trade against it (Ch 7)
**Tradable float** — commodity supply actually available to the market (Ch 9)
**Triangulated conviction** — agreement across macro, micro and asset classes before taking size (Ch 9)
**True believer** — someone for whom price has lost its importance (Ch 5)
**Vintage macro** — trade-by-trade stops anchored on a year-to-date loss limit (Ch 7)
**Vision macro** — seeking a single truth about how the world works. The Philosopher rejects it (Ch 5)
**Zones of value** — price bands from the Commodity Hedger's model, used for entry and collar strikes (Ch 10)
