# Patterns: The Invisible Hands (Drobny)

Each pattern belongs to the manager named. The managers disagree with each other in places. Those conflicts are listed at the end.

## Hypothesis falsification (the Philosopher, Ch 5)
**When to use**: any liquid directional trade.
**How**: Write what would make you believe the whole rationale is wrong. Work backwards to the loss you accept and so to size. Exit when price or fundamentals falsify the hypothesis, even at a profit and even before a stop.
**Trade-offs**: needs liquid markets. Accepts a hit ratio near 50 percent and relies on a payoff near three to one.

## Range of reasonable opinions (the Philosopher, Ch 5)
**When to use**: when the market prices one outcome as near certain.
**How**: Check that a second reasonable school of thought exists. If so, sentiment can move to it. Argue the neglected side and look for a cheap expression. Look for cases where a small change in probability flips the reported consensus.
**Trade-offs**: says nothing about which outcome is true. Horizon is one to three months.

## Titanic funnel (the Professor, Ch 7)
**When to use**: sizing every trade in a macro book without trade-level stops.
**How**: Fix a hard cash loss for the fund in a catastrophe (his: 20 percent). Run each trade through historical and invented disaster scenarios. Reject trades whose scenario loss breaks the limit. Size up trades that gain in disasters. Pair a wanted risky trade with an "antirisk" trade.
**Trade-offs**: in 2008 it missed vanishing liquidity and a hedge with hidden credit risk.

## Trade deconstruction (the Professor, Ch 7)
**When to use**: before any position, above all carry trades.
**How**: List every risk the instrument carries (rate, currency, default). Strip the ones you do not want. Ask what is left and whether it is a hidden short put on a bad state of the world.
**Trade-offs**: hedging the extras can remove the return.

## Investment-boom test (the Professor, Ch 7)
**When to use**: a country with very high investment relative to GDP.
**How**: Compare the growth bought per unit of investment. High marginal return: use equities. Low marginal return with euphoric sentiment: use the currency, which he says tends to do well in boom and bust.
**Trade-offs**: he cites the rule that sustained investment above 50 percent of GDP has always ended in a bust, and still leans against applying it to China.

## Price-against-default-cost check (the House, Ch 4)
**When to use**: judging whether credit is in a bubble.
**How**: Take market pricing. Calculate the cost of likely defaults over cycles. Compare the remaining risk premium with what risk-free liquidity provision pays. Treat weak borrowers and easy terms as backup signs only.
**Trade-offs**: tells you the price is wrong, not when it corrects. He was early by about two years.

## Option-like bubble short (the House, Ch 4. Leitner, Ch 3)
**When to use**: when protection is cheap because risk is priced near zero.
**How**: Buy protection with a small fixed annual cost and a large capped payout (index tranches, CDS, index spread wideners). Add as it gets cheaper. Plan to exit before the seller's ability to pay is in doubt.
**Trade-offs**: negative carry for years. Counterparty risk grows as the trade works.

## Levered bond overlay (Leitner, Ch 3)
**When to use**: an equity-heavy portfolio that needs tail protection with positive carry.
**How**: Fund government bonds at the repo rate on top of the existing portfolio.
**Trade-offs**: fails if bonds fall with stocks in a run on the country. His back tests on 1940 to 1980 were inconclusive. He suggests testing foreign bonds.

## Valuation-driven cash (Leitner, Ch 3)
**When to use**: setting cash and cash-like weights.
**How**: Raise cash as risk assets get expensive on stated yardsticks. Lower it when they are cheap. Count liquid government bonds as cash-like.
**Trade-offs**: reversion timing is unknown. Very large funds cannot shift weights without heavy slippage.

## Balance sheet as an asset (the House, Ch 4)
**When to use**: a long-horizon, creditworthy fund.
**How**: Lend securities in demand, repo special bonds, and buy low-risk illiquid paper such as guaranteed bank bonds. Avoid illiquid risky assets.
**Trade-offs**: needs correct counterparties and haircuts. Mark-to-market losses must be tolerable.

## Risk level first, then gearing (the House, Ch 4. the Pensioner, Ch 14)
**When to use**: building a real money portfolio.
**How**: Build the most diversified mix by risk. Measure its volatility. Scale it up or down to the risk level the mandate allows.
**Trade-offs**: needs a risk system that works with leverage. The Pensioner says most pension funds lack one.

## Liquidity limit beside the risk budget (the Pensioner, Ch 14)
**When to use**: any multi-department fund.
**How**: Measure illiquidity centrally. Give each manager a liquidity limit as well as a risk budget.
**Trade-offs**: valuation inputs for the illiquidity premium are "very hard to estimate".

## Risk collar on full positions (the Commodity Hedger, Ch 10)
**When to use**: large directional positions held through shocks.
**How**: Sell out-of-the-money calls to buy out-of-the-money puts, two weeks to two months out, at strikes set by short-term value zones. Layer it over a 2 percent per trade stop, a 5 percent theme limit and a 10 percent portfolio limit.
**Trade-offs**: caps short-term upside. The House calls the same structure "not always smart" because calls are often cheaper than puts.

## Gross exposure as the control (the Plasticine Macro Trader, Ch 13. the Commodity Investor, Ch 9)
**When to use**: when fear rises or correlations converge.
**How**: Cut gross and net exposure. Do not add hedges, which raise gross and add basis risk. If unsure, do a third.
**Trade-offs**: gives up the position's upside.

## Triangulated conviction and thresholds (the Commodity Investor, Ch 9)
**When to use**: commodity and supply-chain trades.
**How**: Put supply in a demand context. Find products near a stocks-to-use threshold. Take size only when macro, micro and other asset classes agree. Scale in.
**Trade-offs**: long periods with little exposure.

## Asymmetric structure with options (the Commodity Trader, Ch 8)
**When to use**: a strong fundamental view with unknown path and timing.
**How**: Buy options when volatility is cheap. Keep 20 or more positions of equal volatility-adjusted risk. Trade the back of the curve.
**Trade-offs**: time decay if the view is wrong too often.

## Stop per decision maker and trading the P&L (the Equity Trader, Ch 11)
**When to use**: a multi-manager book.
**How**: Give every portfolio manager a hard loss limit (his: 7 percent). Cut size as losses build. Switch from fundamentals to price action when the P&L shows fundamentals are not working.
**Trade-offs**: sells good long-term ideas at bad prices.

## The zero test and the actors (the Predator, Ch 12)
**When to use**: before and during a liquidity crisis.
**How**: Mark every asset at an absurdly low price and check you survive without forced sales. For each position name the other holders and when each will let go.
**Trade-offs**: he says a liquidity crisis cannot be hedged. Only cash or government bonds work.

## Trend-confirmed contrarian (the Plasticine Macro Trader, Ch 13)
**When to use**: a contrarian macro view.
**How**: Hold the view. Wait for a positive price trend before buying. Turn down the weight of pundits whose extreme view has become accepted.
**Trade-offs**: always late.

## Extremes scan (the Commodity Hedger, Ch 10)
**When to use**: idea generation.
**How**: List the highest and lowest rates, currencies, markets, and commodity prices against cost of production. Ask whether each extreme is justified. Fund the cheap high yielder with the expensive low yielder.
**Trade-offs**: needs weeks of checking after the first idea.

## Where the managers disagree
| Question | One view | Opposing view |
|---|---|---|
| Trade-level stops | Mandatory: Commodity Hedger (Ch 10), Equity Trader (Ch 11) | Counter-productive: the Professor (Ch 7). Use falsification instead: the Philosopher (Ch 5) |
| Buying options as insurance | Always pay for some: the House (Ch 4) | Only when cheap: the Philosopher (Ch 5). Cut gross instead: Plasticine (Ch 13) |
| Passive commodity indices | Enhanced indices are acceptable: Ch 8, Ch 9 | "Practically guarantee that you overpay": Commodity Hedger (Ch 10) |
| Do index flows move commodity prices | Only briefly: Commodity Trader (Ch 8) | Yes, materially: Commodity Investor (Ch 9), Commodity Hedger (Ch 10) |
| Oil over ten years | Long: Ch 8, Ch 9 | $50 to $80 or lower: Ch 10 |
| Inflation or deflation (2009) | Inflation more likely over time: Ch 2, Ch 5 (US), Ch 7, Ch 8, Ch 9 | Deflation: Ch 12, Ch 13. Unknown: Ch 4, Ch 6 |
| Decoupling of emerging markets | Real: Ch 2, Ch 8, Ch 10 | "A joke": Ch 13. Limited: Ch 7, Ch 9 |
| Positioning as an input | Central: Ch 4, Ch 11, Ch 12 | Minor: the Bond Trader (Ch 6) |
| Diversification | "One of the few free lunches": the House (Ch 4). The core method: the Pensioner (Ch 14) | Not a drawdown tool: the Philosopher (Ch 5). "Diworsification": Ch 10. None left: Ch 13 |
| Tactical allocation for real money | Yes: Ch 3 (small funds), Ch 9, Ch 10 | Only at inflection points: Ch 12. Undecided: Ch 14 |
