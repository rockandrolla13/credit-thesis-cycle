# Cheatsheet: Inside the Fed (Axilrod)

## Regime by chairman
| Chairman (term) | Operating target | Inflation guide | Author's verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Martin (1951-1970) | Free reserves and money-market conditions | Judgement, market contacts | Sound process builder. Too cautious in the late 1960s |
| Burns (1970-1978) | Funds rate, with money ranges and a proviso clause | Money ranges, not believed | Contained, did not suppress. Lost credibility |
| Miller (1978-1979) | Same as Burns | Same | No leadership. Dollar crisis |
| Volcker (1979-1987) | Nonborrowed reserves to late 1982, then borrowed reserves | Money, then fading | The essential man. Two recessions |
| Greenspan (1987-2006) | Funds rate, announced from 1994 | Real slack | Good until the late years. Guidance bred moral hazard |
| Bernanke (2006- ) | Funds rate, plus balance-sheet programmes | Slack plus inflation expectations | Late, then effective |

## Decision rules
- When a central bank targets a rate, treat money as demand-determined. When it targets a quantity, expect the rate to take "the full brunt".
- When the real policy rate is negative or flat while inflation rises, call the stance easy, whatever the nominal rate or the money target says.
- When a money target is met but a broader aggregate is accelerating, suspect a demand shift, not restraint.
- When targets are rebased to outcomes, ignore the targets.
- When policy is late on the tightening side, expect a hard landing. When late on the easing side, expect easing to fail and fiscal policy to be needed.
- When the central bank promises a rate path, expect leverage in short-funded long positions to build.
- When officials warn about an asset market and do nothing, expect the market to read permission.
- When easing coincides with a visible rescue, expect a "put" to be priced.
- When a dollar-defence package has no monetary change, expect it to fail.
- When a forecast is shared with the administration, discount it for political content.
- When judging a rescue, ask who the creditors are. They are the ones rescued.
- When a procedure is called automatic, find the judgemental adjustment and who clears it.

## Will the Fed change regime? (all needed)
1. The public sees the cost of the current path.
2. The gains from the new policy reach voters (small savers could earn high market rates by the late 1970s).
3. No regulated sector breaks at once (thrifts under rate ceilings blocked it in the 1970s).
4. A chairman who performs on a public stage.
5. A ready procedure with intellectual standing.

## The asset-bubble dilemma
| Option | Cost | Author's view |
|---|---|---|
| Tighten earlier than the economy needs | Risk of weakening growth | Do it "to an extent" |
| Wait and clean up | Larger collapse later | "Worse alternative". Chosen in practice |
| Early prudential signal | Small direct effect | Use it. The signal changes attitudes |

## Tells
- "We are not far apart": a chairman building cohesion, not a description.
- Chairman urges action by other branches of government: he will not carry the fight himself.
- Officials call long rates a "quandary": the market is following their guidance.
- "Experiment" said of a new regime: credibility at risk.
- Several money measures published at once: hoping one lands in range.
- Fed looks passive beside the Treasury in a crisis: independence in doubt, stature falling.
- Audit powers and governance limits moving toward the Fed: institutional credibility is eroding.
- Only the chairman's words move policy. Give other speeches "some attention" only.
