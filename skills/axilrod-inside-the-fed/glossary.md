# Glossary: Inside the Fed (Axilrod)

Terms are the author's or the Fed's own. Chapter numbers point to the chapter files. "App" is the appendices file (ch10).

**Accord (1951)** — agreement that freed the Fed from supporting the government bond market at low wartime rates (Ch 2)
**Artistic bent** — a chairman's ability to sense the times, act a persuasive role, and show nerve and vision (Ch 1)
**Base drift** — Henry Wallich's term for rebasing a money target on the actual outcome, which forgives each overshoot (Ch 3)
**Beige book** — the public collation of regional reports, first given a red cover under Burns (Ch 3)
**Bills-only policy** — confining open market operations to Treasury bills so long rates stay market-set (Ch 2)
**Blue book** — staff document of policy alternatives that quantifies money-market conditions, begun about 1965 (Ch 2)
**Borrowed-reserves target** — the operating target from late 1982, close to a funds-rate target "but literally was not" (Ch 5)
**Carter bonds** — United States government bonds in foreign currency, part of the 1978 dollar package (Ch 4)
**Case of the missing money** — the 1975-1976 shortfall of M1 against money-demand equations (Ch 3)
**Considerable period** — 2003 FOMC wording that accommodation would be maintained (Ch 6, App)
**Credit controls (March 1980)** — controls imposed at Carter's request and dropped by early summer (Ch 5)
**Deposit rate ceilings** — regulatory caps on bank and thrift deposit rates, gone by the mid-1980s (Ch 3)
**Deus ex machina** — the central bank as the lender that stands outside the financial system (Ch 2, Ch 7)
**Dodd-Frank Act (2010)** — reform law that recast emergency lending, added the Financial Stability Oversight Council and a vice chairman for supervision (Ch 1, Ch 8, Ch 9)
**Eurodollars** — dollar deposits in banks abroad, mostly free of reserve requirements (Ch 4)
**Even-keel policy** — holding policy steady around large Treasury financings (Ch 2)
**Extended period** — FOMC wording from January 2009 on exceptionally low rates (Ch 7)
**Fed watching** — the profession of forecasting Fed actions, raised by the 1979 procedure (Ch 5)
**Federal funds rate** — overnight interbank rate, "the pivotal rate" of the rate structure (Ch 1, Ch 6)
**FOMC** — Federal Open Market Committee: seven governors, the New York Fed president and four rotating reserve bank presidents (Ch 1)
**Free reserves** — excess reserves minus borrowing from the Fed. The operating guide of the Martin era (Ch 2, App)
**Green book** — staff numeric economic projections (Ch 2)
**Greenspan put** — market belief that losses could be put to the Fed (Ch 6)
**House Concurrent Resolution 133** — 1975 resolution that led to annual money targets (Ch 3)
**Humphrey-Hawkins Act** — Full Employment and Balanced Growth Act of 1978, which set calendar-year targets and twice-yearly reports (Ch 3, Ch 8)
**Independent within the government** — Martin's formula for the Fed's independence (Ch 1)
**Institutional credibility** — the Fed's reputation for honest, expert administration of all its duties (Ch 8)
**Irrational exuberance** — Greenspan's December 1996 warning to the stock market (Ch 6)
**Lagged reserve requirements** — required reserves based on deposits two weeks earlier (Ch 2, Ch 5)
**Louvre Accord (February 1987)** — agreement to stabilise exchange markets after the dollar's fall (Ch 5)
**LTCM** — Long Term Capital Management, the hedge fund whose 1998 workout the Fed convened (Ch 6)
**M1, M2** — narrow money (currency and demand deposits) and a broader measure adding time and savings deposits (Ch 2, Ch 3)
**Margin requirements** — the Fed-set down payment on stock bought with credit, long held at 50 percent (Ch 6, Ch 9)
**Measured pace** — 2004 FOMC wording on the removal of accommodation (Ch 6, App)
**Membership problem** — banks leaving or avoiding Fed membership in the 1960s and 1970s (Ch 2)
**Monetary base** — currency plus bank reserve and clearing balances (Ch 1)
**Monetary Control Act of 1980** — put nearly all depository institutions under Fed reserve requirements (Ch 2, App)
**Moral hazard, creditor** — the risk that lenders skip due diligence because they expect a rescue (Ch 7)
**Moral hazard, market-wide** — risk taking induced by assurances about the future policy path (Ch 6)
**Morning call** — daily call among the account manager, a reserve bank president and Board staff (Ch 2)
**Neutral real funds rate** — the rate that neither holds growth below potential nor encourages inflation (Ch 9)
**Nonborrowed reserves** — reserves supplied through open market operations. The 1979-1982 operating target (Ch 5, App)
**Plaza Accord (September 1985)** — five-country agreement to bring the dollar down (Ch 5)
**Practical monetarism** — Volcker's term for the 1979-1982 approach (Ch 5)
**Proviso clause** — directive wording that let the funds rate move a little if money left its range (Ch 3)
**Quadriad** — joint forecasting group of the Fed, Council of Economic Advisers, Budget Bureau and Treasury (Ch 2)
**Real funds rate** — nominal funds rate minus the concurrent rise in consumer prices (Ch 3, Ch 9, App)
**Recognition lag** — delay in seeing the severity of a developing problem (Ch 3, Ch 9)
**Risk-management approach** — Greenspan's policy of insuring against a low-probability, very costly outcome (Ch 6)
**Swap network** — reciprocal currency lines with foreign central banks (Ch 4, Ch 7)
**TAF** — Term Auction Facility, auction of longer-term discount-window funds from late 2007 (Ch 7)
**TALF** — Fed facility lending against asset-backed securities (Ch 7)
**TARP** — Treasury programme proposed for troubled assets and used for capital injections (Ch 7)
**Taylor rule** — John Taylor's rule for adjusting the policy rate. Useful "only as background music" (Ch 2, Ch 9)
**TSLF** — Term Securities Lending Facility, March 2008 (App)
