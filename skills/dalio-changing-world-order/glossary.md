# Glossary: The Changing World Order (Dalio, 2020 serialized draft)

**Archetype** — the typical case the author builds from many cases, used as a template to compare a specific case against (Intro)
**Balance of power dynamic** — five steps: alliances form, the sides fight, winners fight each other (purge), peace and prosperity, rising conflict (Ch 8)
**Big Cycle** — the mutually reinforcing rise and decline of an empire's strengths in three phases: ascent, top, decline (Ch 1)
**Boutique countries** — rich countries too small to be empires, such as Switzerland and Singapore (Ch 1)
**Calculated wealth vs actual wealth** — wealth at market prices against the real things owned. Money creation raises the first, not the second (Ch 2)
**Capital war** — cutting an opponent off from capital, or attacking a reserve currency's status. Modern term "sanctions" (Ch 5a, Ch 7)
**Century of Humiliation** — China's decline from about 1840 to 1949 (Ch 6)
**Classic toxic mix** — bad finances, large income/wealth/values gaps, and a severe economic shock together (Ch 9)
**Comparability** — the point at which a rising power's strength approaches the leader's. War risk rises (Ch 4, Ch 7)
**Convertibility** — the right to change a currency into gold or another currency. Often suspended before a devaluation (Ch 3, Ch 4)
**Debt eats equity** — debt is senior, so unserviceable debt forces sale or seizure of the asset (Ch 2)
**Debt monetization** — the central bank printing money to buy government debt. "Quantitative easing" is the less threatening name (Ch 2)
**Decadence** — late-cycle spending on luxuries over investment, often debt-financed (Ch 9)
**Decoupling** — the US and China reducing dependence on each other. "Compartmented", not broad-based (Ch 7)
**Destruction/reconstruction period** — the 10-to-20-year phase of depression, revolution and war between orders (Ch 1)
**Dialectical materialism** — Marx's method as the author summarizes it: contradictions of opposites produce struggles whose resolution is progress (Ch 6)
**Eight measures of power** — education, competitiveness, technology, economic output, share of world trade, military, financial center, reserve currency (Ch 1)
**Exorbitant privilege** — a reserve issuer's ability to borrow more than others (Ch 1)
**Fascism (author's definition)** — autocratic, capitalist and collectivist (Ch 5a)
**Fiat money** — Type 3 money, linked to nothing, printable without limit (Ch 2)
**Guns and butter** — financing military and domestic spending together by borrowing (Ch 2, Ch 4, Ch 5b)
**Hard money** — money with intrinsic value, such as gold and silver. Type 1 (Ch 2)
**Helicopter money** — direct payments from government to citizens, an MP3 tool (Ch 2)
**Hollowing-out** — the rich leave, revenue falls, taxes rise, more leave (Ch 1, Ch 8, Ch 9)
**Inflationary depression** — monetary inflation with a deflating real economy (Ch 2)
**Internal order** — a country's system for governing how people deal with each other and divide wealth and power (Ch 8)
**Iron rice bowl** — China's guaranteed pay and benefits without merit pay (Ch 6)
**Linked currency system** — Type 2: paper claims convertible into hard money at a fixed rate (Ch 2)
**Long-term debt cycle** — about 50 to 75 years (Ch 2. "50-100" in the Intro), from low debt and full stimulus capacity to high debt and none
**Mandate of Heaven** — the Chinese idea that a dynasty rules while its approach works (Ch 6)
**Monetary inflation** — a fall in the value of money because its supply rose relative to demand for it (Ch 2)
**MP1** — Monetary Policy 1: interest-rate changes (Ch 2, Ch 5b)
**MP2** — Monetary Policy 2: printing money and buying financial assets (Ch 2, Ch 5b)
**MP3** — Monetary Policy 3: government borrowing and directed spending funded by central bank purchases (Ch 2, Ch 5b)
**Populism** — politics appealing to ordinary people who feel elites ignore them. Of the left or the right (Ch 1, Ch 9)
**Prisoner's dilemma** — two comparable powers each have reason to strike first unless trust or mutual protection exists (Ch 7, Ch 8)
**Purge** — fighting among the winners and the mopping-up of the old side after a change of regime (Ch 8, Ch 9)
**Real economy vs financial economy** — goods and services against money and credit, each with its own supply and demand (Ch 2)
**Red flags (index)** — economic warning signs whose count the author maps to odds of severe internal conflict (Ch 9)
**Red lines / trip wires** — limits whose crossing signals a step change in a conflict (Ch 7)
**Reserve currency** — a currency accepted worldwide for transactions and savings (Intro, Ch 2)
**Revolution (within the system)** — a revolutionary change in wealth and power that keeps the order, such as the New Deal (Ch 9)
**Run** — holders of claims demand conversion faster than the bank or central bank can meet (Ch 2, Ch 3, Ch 4)
**Self-funding debt growth** — debt that does not accelerate against the income that services it without central bank subsidy (Ch 5b)
**Short-term debt cycle** — the business cycle, about eight years, give or take a few (Ch 2)
**Six stages** — new order, building, peace and prosperity, excess, bad finances and conflict, civil war (Ch 9)
**Stage 5** — bad financial conditions and intense conflict. Where the author put the US in December 2020 (Ch 9)
**Sterling Area** — the UK and Commonwealth states that pegged to and held sterling (Ch 4)
**Stimulant in the bottle** — the central bank's remaining capacity to create credit growth that raises real activity (Ch 2)
**Storehold of wealth** — the author's term for a store of value (Ch 2)
**Systemically beneficial / destructive devaluation** — one that restarts credit against one that breaks the capital allocation system (Ch 3)
**Triangular debt** — Chinese state banks lending to weak state firms under an implied central guarantee (Ch 6)
**Type 1 / Type 2 / Type 3 monetary systems** — hard money / claims on hard money / fiat (Ch 2)
**War with ourselves** — the author's name for internal weakness as the main contest (Ch 7)
**World order** — the debt, monetary, domestic and international systems taken together (Ch 2)
