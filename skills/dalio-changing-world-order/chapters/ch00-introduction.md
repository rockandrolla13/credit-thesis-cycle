# Introduction (published 29 March 2020)

## Core Idea
Three forces are meeting for the first time in the author's lifetime: a late long-term debt cycle with near-zero rates, large internal wealth and political gaps, and a rising power (China) challenging the leading power (the US). The author says the closest analogue is 1930-45. He studies 500 years of empires to build an archetype and compare the present with it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The archetype method** (section "My Approach"). The author's stated steps:
  1. Collect as many important cases of one type of event as possible, "like a doctor studies many cases of a particular type of disease".
  2. Form a picture of the typical case. He calls it an **archetype**.
  3. Compare each specific case with the archetype. Explain the differences.
  4. Turn the cause-effect relationships into "if/then" decision rules: "if X happens, then make Y bet".
  5. Watch events against the template. If events stay on track, keep betting on what typically comes next. If they deviate, find out why and correct.
  - When to use: any big event that did not happen in your lifetime but happened before.
- **"Another one of those"**. Classify an event by type, as a biologist names a species, then apply the principles for that type.
- **The three big forces** (section "This Study & How I Came to Do It"):
  1. **The long-term money and debt cycle.** The author gives its typical length as about 50-100 years.
  2. **The domestic wealth and power cycle.** Wealth, values and political gaps.
  3. **The international wealth and power cycle.** A rising power against the existing one.
- **The upward corkscrew.** Most things "evolve upward" through learning and productivity, with cycles around the trend. Debt cycles drive activity up and down around it.

## Key Concepts
- **Reserve currency**: "a currency that is accepted around the world for transactions and savings". The author says all past reserve currencies ceased to be reserve currencies.
- **Transition phase**: a roughly 10- to 20-year period between big cycles that last about 50-100 years.
- **Empire life span**: "roughly 250 years, give or take 150 years", with economic, debt and political cycles of about 50-100 years inside it.
- **Scope of the study**: three reserve-currency empires (Dutch, British, US) in depth. Six others less closely (Germany, France, Russia, Japan, China, India). China back to the year 600.

## Mental Models
- Treat a new event as a species, not as a one-off.
- Hold two views at once: the detail and the big picture over long periods.
- Treat the author's conclusions as "theories rather than facts". He calls his outlook "very low-confidence" and says he values "diversification of my bets above all else".

## Anti-patterns
- **Relying on lifetime experience.** The author says his biggest career mistakes came from missing moves that had not happened in his lifetime.
- **Reading economic averages.** He split the US into income quintiles because averages hid the gaps ("The Top 40% and the Bottom 60%").
- **Studying one force alone.** He says none of the three forces can be understood without the other two.

## Worked Example
**15 August 1971.** The author was a 22-year-old clerk on the NYSE floor. Nixon ended the dollar's convertibility into gold on a Sunday night. The author expected stocks to fall on Monday. The market rose "about 4 percent". He then found that past devaluations had similar effects on stocks. Lesson he draws: study all the big moves of the last 100+ years in all major countries.

**2008-09 against 1929-32** (the author's parallel):
| Step | 1929-32 | 2008-09 |
|---|---|---|
| Debt and economic crisis | yes | yes |
| Interest rates hit 0% | yes | yes |
| Central banks print and buy financial assets | yes | yes |
| Financial asset prices rise, wealth gap widens | yes | yes |
| Populism of left and right | yes | yes |
| Rising power challenges the leader | Germany, Japan | China |

Data point: at the start of 2020 "more than $10 trillion of debt was at negative interest rates".

## Key Takeaways
1. Build the archetype first. Read the present as a deviation from it.
2. The three forces interact. Analyse them together.
3. Printing money and buying financial assets widens the wealth gap, according to the author, because the wealthy hold more financial assets.
4. The author states that the short-term debt cycle was already late before the pandemic.
5. The author flags his own low confidence. Carry that flag forward.
