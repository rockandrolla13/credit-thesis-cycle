# Cheatsheet: The Changing World Order (Dalio, 2020 serialized draft)

Everything here is the author's rule or number. Dates of judgment: March to December 2020.

## Decision rules
- When debt is in a currency the borrower prints, expect printing and devaluation, not default. (Ch 2, 5b)
- When debt is in a currency the borrower cannot print, expect austerity, default and exit of the rich. (Ch 2, 8, 9)
- When rates are at zero and asset purchases stop reaching the economy, expect MP3. (Ch 2)
- When a central bank must choose between higher real rates and printing, expect printing. (Ch 3)
- When net reserves fall and convertibility is limited, expect devaluation to follow. (Ch 3, 4)
- When capital controls appear with a domestic debt problem, the author says leave the currency. (Ch 6)
- When central banks make a lot of cheap money, the author says be "more aggressive in owning assets". (Ch 5b)
- When the richest country borrows from poorer ones, read it as an early sign of a wealth shift. (Ch 1)
- When a rival is cut off from essential imports or technology, read it as a major escalation. (Ch 7)
- When taxes rise and spending is cut amid wide gaps and a bad economy, read it as a leading indicator of upheaval. (Ch 9)
- When buyers of government debt are mainly its own central bank, the Stage 5 marker is on. (Ch 9)
- When people die in political fighting, Stage 6 has begun. (Ch 9)
- When in doubt, get out. Doors close for people and money. (Ch 8, 9)

## Late long-term debt cycle: three-part test (Ch 2, 3)
1. Debt high relative to income and to what backs it
2. Rates cannot be lowered enough
3. Money creation raises asset prices more than activity

## Losing reserve status: three preconditions (Ch 3)
1. A rising rival has taken economic and political primacy
2. Large debts are monetized
3. A run that cutbacks cannot stop

## Deleveraging levers (Ch 3)
| Lever | Price effect | Political cost |
|---|---|---|
| Austerity | Deflationary | High, short-lived |
| Default/restructure | Deflationary | High for both sides |
| Transfers (tax) | Not stated | Tolerable |
| Print | Lowers the value of money | Lowest |

## Tells
- Looks rich, income below expenses → going broke. (Ch 2)
- Feeling safest in debt assets → closest to the blowup. (Ch 2)
- Assets seen as "good" because they rose → Stage 4 bubble. (Ch 9)
- Stocks rise on a devaluation or default day → reflation is priced as relief. (Intro, Ch 5b)
- Reserve share far above trade and GDP share → status is lagging fundamentals. (Ch 2, 5b, 7)
- Media trust collapsing, moderates shrinking → late Stage 5. (Ch 9)

## Author's placements (2020)
- US: about 75% through its Big Cycle, +/- 10% (Ch 5b). Internal Stage 5 (Ch 9).
- China: rising, comparable in trade, military, technology. Lags in reserve currency and financial center (Ch 6).
- Dollar, euro, yen: late in their long-term debt cycles (Ch 3).
