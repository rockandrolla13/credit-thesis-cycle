## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. The archetype method (Introduction)
Collect many cases of one event type. Average them into an **archetype**. Compare the present case with it. Write "if X happens, then make Y bet" rules. Track and correct. Treat each event as "another one of those".

### 2. Three big forces (Introduction, Ch 8)
1. The long-term **money and debt cycle**.
2. The **internal** wealth, values and political gap cycle.
3. The **external** cycle of a rising power challenging the leader.
The author says they met in 2020 for the first time since 1930-45. Analyse them together.

### 3. The Big Cycle and the eight measures of power (Ch 1)
- Phases: **ascent**, **top**, **decline**.
- Measures: education, competitiveness, technology, economic output, share of world trade, military, financial center, reserve currency.
- **Education leads. Reserve currency lags.**
- Six seeds of decline at the top: costly labour, being copied, softer later generations, over-borrowing through the "exorbitant privilege", an empire that costs more than it earns, wealth gaps.
- "When the richest get into debt by borrowing from the poorest, it is a very early sign of a relative wealth shift."
- Timing: peace 40-80 years, destruction and restructuring 10-20 years, empires about 250 years give or take 150.

### 4. Money, credit and the long-term debt cycle (Ch 2)
- "Debt eats equity, money feeds the hunger of debt, and central banks can produce money."
- Watch the **real economy** and the **financial economy** separately. Monetary inflation can coexist with a deflating real economy.
- Six stages: hard money → claims on hard money → more debt → crisis, default, devaluation → fiat → flight back to hard money.
- **Type 1** hard, **Type 2** linked, **Type 3** fiat.
- Cycle length about 50-75 years. Short-term cycle about 8 years.
- **End-of-cycle test**: debt high, rates cannot fall enough, money creation lifts asset prices more than activity.
- **MP1** rates. **MP2** print and buy assets. **MP3** fiscal spending funded by the central bank.
- Fork: debt in a currency you print gets **devalued**. Debt in a currency you cannot print gets **defaulted**.

### 5. Devaluation and loss of reserve status (Ch 3, Ch 4)
- Four levers: austerity, default/restructuring, transfers, printing. Printing is "the most expedient".
- Pattern: reserves fall first → convertibility is limited → a rate defence fails → devaluation → rate cuts.
- Three preconditions to lose reserve status: a rising rival, monetized debt, an unstoppable run.
- Since 1850 interest-earning cash returned 1.2% a year on average and gold 1.3%. Since 1912, in real terms: bills -0.2%, gold 2.2%.
- Cases: guilder collapsed 1780-95 after the Bank of Amsterdam funded the Dutch East India Company. Sterling: 30% devaluation in 1949, 14% in 1967.

### 6. Economic war before shooting war (Ch 5a)
Three techniques: asset freezes/seizures, blocking capital market access, embargoes/blockades. The 1940-41 US steps against Japan cut 80% of its oil and preceded Pearl Harbor. Wartime policy: controls on production, prices, capital, and access to one's own assets.

### 7. The US position (Ch 5b)
- Seven-step cycle from post-war productivity to wars over a new order. The author put the US "roughly 75% through", +/- 10%.
- Test: is debt growth **self-funding**, or does it need central bank subsidy?
- His 1982 lesson: debt crises in a printable currency "can be well-managed". "The value of assets is the reciprocal of the value of money and credit."

### 8. Five wars plus two (Ch 7)
Trade/economic, technology, geopolitical, capital, military. Plus the culture war and "the war with ourselves".
- "Whoever wins the technology war will probably also win the economic and military wars."
- Escalation marker: cutting off **essential imports or technology**. Semiconductors are his main example.
- Timing: "it pays to fight early if one's relative power is declining and fight later if it's rising."
- Four reasons dollar debt is less attractive: oversized foreign holdings, fast-rising supply, negative real yield, wartime risk. The US is "testing the limits".
- No alternative reserve currency is needed for a currency to be sold.

### 9. Four forces of internal change (Ch 8)
Class struggle over wealth and power. The balance of power dynamic (alliance → fight → **purge** → peace → conflict). Short-term enjoyment over long-term health. Failure to learn from history. "When in doubt get out."

### 10. The six stages of the internal order (Ch 9)
1. New order, power consolidated. 2. Institutions built. 3. Peace and prosperity. 4. Excess. 5. Bad finances and intense conflict. 6. Civil war or revolution.
- **Classic toxic mix**: bad finances + large gaps + a severe shock.
- "The single most reliable leading indicator of civil war/revolution is bankrupt government finances."
- Stage 5 marker: more government debt than buyers other than its own central bank will take.
- Red flags: 60-80% present → about 1 in 6. Over 80% → about 1 in 3. The author put the US in the 60-80% bucket and in Stage 5 (December 2020).
- Line into Stage 6: people dying in the fighting.
- Policy test: easy or tight, then **what the money is spent on**.

## Use for Credit and Macro Questions

- **Credit spreads**: nothing direct. The text has no spread data and no spread method. It gives a regime frame only: who can print (devaluation) and who cannot (default, austerity).
- **Debt-financed investment booms**: a qualitative test. Productive debt earns more than it costs and is "self-funding" (Ch 5b, Ch 9). Stage 4 is debt growth above future cash flows, with assets judged "good rather than expensive" (Ch 9). The Dutch East India Company is the bubble-company case (Ch 4).
- **Inflation and Fed regimes**: strong on sequence. MP1/MP2/MP3, the 1933 and 1971 breaks, 1933-51 rate caps, Volcker 1979-82, and the 2008 and 2020 shifts (Ch 2, Ch 5b). No reaction-function detail.
- **Issuer screening**: only the generic income and balance sheet test (Ch 2) and the sub-sovereign cannot-print rule for US states and cities (Ch 9). Nothing at company level.
- **Sovereign and currency risk**: the main use. Reserve-run markers, capital controls, sanctions as capital war (Ch 3, Ch 4, Ch 7).
- `dalio-big-debt-crises` and `dalio-how-countries-go-broke` cover debt cycles in more depth.
