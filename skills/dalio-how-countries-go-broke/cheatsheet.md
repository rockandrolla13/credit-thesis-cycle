# Cheatsheet — How Countries Go Broke: The Big Cycle

## Decision rules
- When a sovereign's debt is in a currency it can print, expect devaluation, not default, because "the central bank always prints." (Ch 1)
- When debt is in a currency it cannot print, expect default, austerity and depression. (Ch 2, 13)
- When judging sovereign burden, divide by government revenue, not GDP. (Ch 3)
- When the interest rate exceeds income growth, expect the debt ratio to compound even with no primary deficit. (Ch 3)
- When private debt falls fast while government debt rises fast, treat it as a short leading indicator. (Ch 5)
- When existing holders sell government bonds, treat it as the big red flag. (Ch 1, 4, 19)
- When a monetizing central bank dominates the bond market, do not own the bonds. (Ch 16)
- When rates hit zero in a debt contraction, expect a bad contraction until policy becomes very stimulative. (Ch 12)
- When a deficit must be cut, cut it in good times and ease money alongside. (Ch 18)
- When a belief is universal, assume it is priced. (Ch 19)

## Which path does a debt crisis take?
- Debt in own currency?
  - Yes, creditors and debtors mostly domestic: monetize and devalue. Beautiful deleveraging is possible.
    - Reserve currency: more room, slower loss, privilege tends to be abused.
    - Not a reserve currency: less room, currency is the release valve.
  - No: currency defense, reserve loss, default and restructuring, ugly deleveraging.
- Fixed exchange rate? Pressures build, then break at once. Floating? The loss comes gradually.

## Central bank's choice once demand for debt falls short
| | Do not monetize | Monetize |
|---|---|---|
| Interest rates | rise | held down |
| Cash | very valuable | loses value |
| Stocks and hard assets | fall | rise |
| Prices | deflation | inflation |
| Defaults | many | minimized |
| Sequence | "typically happens first" | "typically happens eventually" |

## Tells and smells
- Rising borrowing used to pay debt service: "a sure sign of moving toward a debt crisis."
- Long rates up faster than short rates while the currency falls: stage 4.
- Government shortening its debt maturities: early-stage red flag.
- Guarantees, policy banks, forced domestic buyers, patriotic bond drives: financing stress being papered over.
- Central bank holdings of government debt rising with total debt: early-stage red flag.
- Liquidity rising with no pickup in activity and an inverted curve: pushing on a string.
- Top tax rates and inheritance taxes rising with capital controls: stage 8.
- Renewed QE to force real rates down, or government control of the central bank: the two US red flags.
- Stocks up on a devaluation: normal. It happened in 1933 and 1971.
