# Glossary — How Countries Go Broke: The Big Cycle

**Austerity** — spending less. The first of four debt-reduction levers, and a mistake when used first because one person's spending is another's income (Ch 2, 3)
**Beautiful deleveraging** — deflationary debt reduction (restructuring) balanced with inflationary debt reduction (monetization) so nominal growth exceeds nominal rates without unacceptable deflation or inflation (Ch 1, 2, 7)
**Big Debt Cycle** — the long-term debt cycle of about 80 years, give or take 25, formed by successive short-term cycles (Ch 1, 2)
**Borrower-debtors / lender-creditors** — the two sides of every debt, whose opposing needs on interest rates must both be met (Ch 2)
**Breakeven inflation rate** — nominal bond yield minus the real yield on inflation-indexed bonds, the inflation the market is betting on (Ch 10)
**Broken-promise principle** — when debt is too large relative to money, governments raise money and credit, reduce debt, and/or restrict ownership and movement of hard money (Ch 9, 11, 19)
**Bubble indicator** — the author's gauge of debt-financed speculation: 18% in 2010, 75% at end 2020, 35% after the tightening (Ch 1, 14)
**Central bank going broke** — the central bank cannot meet debt service without printing. It does not default (Ch 1, 4, 6)
**Death spiral (doom loop)** — rising rates worsen credit risk, which cuts demand for debt, which raises rates or forces printing (Ch 1, 3, 5)
**Debt ex-CB** — government debt net of central bank holdings (Ch 16)
**Debt monetization** — the central bank creating money to buy debt. "Essentially the same thing" as quantitative easing (Ch 6, 13)
**Debt service** — interest plus principal due in a year (Ch 3)
**Debt squeeze** — free-market demand for government debt falls short of supply (Ch 4, 5)
**Demagogue** — Plato's term for the leader who moves a democracy to autocracy (Ch 8, 19)
**Five big forces** — debt/money/economy, internal order, external order, acts of nature, technology (Ch 2, 8, 19)
**Fixed vs floating cases** — the split of the 35 crises by exchange rate regime. Fixed cases break abruptly, floating ones adjust gradually (Ch 4, 5)
**Hard money** — a medium of exchange and storehold of wealth whose supply cannot easily be increased, such as gold (Ch 1)
**Lost decade** — the 1980s inflationary depressions in over-indebted emerging countries (Ch 12)
**M0** — base money, which rises when the central bank buys bonds (Ch 3, 7)
**Monetary inflation / monetary deflation** — inflation in currencies a debtor can print, deflation in currencies it owes and cannot print (Ch 12)
**MP0** — linked (hard) monetary system. US 1945 to 1971 (Ch 1, 11)
**MP1** — fiat money managed by interest rates. US 1971 to 2008 (Ch 1, 12)
**MP2** — fiat money with debt monetization. US 2008 to 2020 (Ch 1, 13)
**MP3** — coordinated large fiscal deficits monetized by the central bank. US from 2020 (Ch 1, 14)
**Net IIP** — assets owned abroad minus liabilities owed to the rest of the world (Ch 5)
**Nine stages of the final crisis** — the archetypical sequence from deep indebtedness to return to equilibrium (Ch 4 to 7)
**Order** — a way of operating (monetary, domestic political, geopolitical) that changes when the system breaks down (Introduction, Ch 8)
**Overall Big Cycle** — the cycle of peace and prosperity then conflict and depression produced by the five forces (Ch 8)
**Point of no return** — debt and debt service levels that cannot be reduced without great losses to debt investors (Ch 1, 17)
**Popular meme** — the belief almost everyone holds, already in the price and "bound to be wrong in some way" (Ch 1, 19)
**Primary deficit / surplus** — revenue minus spending excluding interest (Ch 3)
**Pushing on a string** — late-cycle failure of stimulus to raise spending as savers hoard cash (Ch 1, 15, 16)
**Real bond yield** — the inflation-free, default-free return. Long-run average about 2%. "The most important number to watch" (Ch 10)
**Reserve currency status** — wide acceptance as medium of exchange and storehold of wealth, a great risk mitigator that tends to be abused (Ch 4, 17)
**Run** — turning in debt assets for real money the holder of the liability lacks (Ch 1, 8)
**Short-term debt cycle** — about 6 years, give or take 3. The US was about two-thirds through its 13th since 1945 (Ch 1, 2, 19)
**Stage 5** — the late Big Cycle stage before depression and war. Analogues: 1905-14 and 1933-38 (Ch 8, 19)
**Stagflation** — stagnation with inflation, as in 1971 to 1982 (Ch 12)
**3% 3-part solution** — cut the US deficit to 3% of GDP using spending cuts, tax increases and lower interest rates (Ch 18)
**Three arrows** — Japan's 2013 policy: more money, more fiscal spending, structural reform (Ch 16)
**Total return** — yield plus price change, the basis on which all investments compete (Ch 2)
**Transaction-based pricing (P = $/Q)** — price equals total spending divided by total quantity sold (Ch 2)
**Twin deficit** — current account deficit plus fiscal deficit (Ch 5)
**Ugly deleveraging** — debt-to-GDP rising during adjustment because austerity fails and GDP falls (Ch 13)
**Unbacked money** — money stock not covered by reserves, as a percent of GDP (Ch 17)
**Zombie institutions** — banks and firms carrying unrestructured debt (Ch 16)
