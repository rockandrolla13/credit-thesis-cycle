# Chapter 7: The Prior Big Debt Crisis Recedes, a New Equilibrium Is Reached, and a New Cycle Can Begin (Stages 7-9)

## Core Idea
Debt is cut mostly by inflation and real growth from aggressive stimulation, less by default. Government debt devalues against gold, commodities and often equities. Once five conditions are met, the country's currency and debt become among the best things to hold.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Stage 7: debts are restructured and devalued.** Remaining holders of currency and debt take big real losses until a new monetary system has enough credibility.
  - Sequence in the archetype: (1) government debt rises first while money growth is unchanged, and slows during the currency defense, (2) debt/M0 rises, forcing the private sector to absorb more debt per unit of base money, which pushes rates up, (3) the central bank gives up, lets the currency go and monetizes, (4) debt/M0 falls and inflation lifts nominal income relative to debt.
  - Reserves/debt falls first (debt rising, then reserves sold) and rises after the devaluation, because local-currency debt shrinks against hard-currency assets and competitiveness improves.
- **Attribution of the archetypical decline in government debt-to-GDP** (recovered from EPUB images f0157-01.jpg and f0158-01.jpg; the first set is also stated in the text):
  | Component | All cases | Ex-ongoing cases |
  |---|---|---|
  | Starting debt, % GDP | 89% | 90% |
  | Central bank purchases | −7% | −2% |
  | Inflation | −38% | −49% |
  | Real growth | −26% | −35% |
  | Primary surplus | −16% | −28% |
  | Debt restructuring or default | −8% | −8% |
  | Interest cost | +76% | +92% |
  | Ending debt, % GDP | 70% | 59% |
  - How to use: when projecting a sovereign's exit path, expect inflation plus real growth to do most of the work and interest cost to undo most of it.
  - Caveat from the text: averages with large ranges. For own-currency debt "the debt problems never go away as much as they remain a manageable burden."
- **Stage 8: extraordinary taxes and capital controls.** Marginal income tax rates for top earners and inheritance tax rates both rise by about 10% in the years into the devaluation (data: US, UK, Japan, Germany, France only). Controls follow to stop flight, but "the economic pressure to leave ... is too great for governments to stop the bleeding."
- **Stage 9: the five classic steps to a stable currency**:
  1. Restructure debts to manageable levels: reserve assets cover a substantial portion of liabilities and debt service no longer exceeds revenue growth. Foreign-currency debts and some local-currency debts are defaulted and restructured.
  2. A deep, painful fiscal adjustment so finances hold without monetization. The primary deficit improves before interest costs fall.
  3. Obtain enough reserves to defend the currency or back a new one: devaluation revalues reserves, exports improve, state assets are sold, official creditors lend.
  4. High real interest rates that more than compensate for the risk of holding the currency.
  5. Limits on what the central bank can do that would undermine the new stable money.
  - When these are met: "these are among the best times to hold the country's currency and debt."

## Key Concepts
- **Excess return**: return over cash, the basis of the asset table.
- **Vol-matched**: positions scaled to equal volatility (the table uses 15% volatility) before comparing assets against bonds.
- **Debt/M0**: government debt relative to base money. Archetype chart axis runs 600% to 1200% (Description 36).
- **Official vs market exchange rate**: a control technique. Controls "create market distortions and do more harm than good."
- **Capital controls** (footnote definition): meaningful restrictions on investors moving money to and from other countries and assets, excluding single-country measures such as sanctions.

## Mental Models
- Treat the devaluation as the mechanism of repair. It cuts local debt against hard assets, restores competitiveness and raises nominal income.
- After the write-down "it doesn't take much to back up the debt." Very tight money and a very high real rate then punish borrower-debtors and reward lender-creditors until buying returns.
- Expect fixed-rate and fiat cases to differ sharply in asset returns around the break.
- "Perhaps this time, digital currencies like Bitcoin will benefit." The author flags this as a possibility, not a finding.

## Anti-patterns
- **Foreign exchange controls and dual rates to manage the squeeze**: more harm than good.
- **Holding nominal bonds through the write-down**: average excess return −5%, with −99% in Weimar and −81% in Mexico 1982.
- **Assuming equities always hedge**: median equity excess return is only 3%. US equities fell 44% in the 1971 devaluation window.

## Worked Example
The archetypical debt reduction, all cases:
1. Debt stands at 89% of GDP at the devaluation.
2. Interest keeps accruing and adds 76 points.
3. Inflation removes 38 points and real growth removes 26. Both come from "aggressive stimulations."
4. Primary surpluses remove 16 points.
5. Central bank purchases remove 7 and default or restructuring removes 8.
6. Debt ends at 70% of GDP. Excluding cases still playing out, it goes from 90% to 59%, with larger contributions from inflation (49), growth (35) and primary surpluses (28).

The lesson for the investor is in step 3. The holder of the debt pays through lost purchasing power, not through missed coupons.

## Key Takeaways
1. On average gold beats local-currency cash by roughly 60% from the start of the devaluation until the currency bottoms.
2. In vol-matched terms gold beat bonds by 94% on average and 71% at the median.
3. Bonds did well in cases without a deep devaluation (USA 2008 +55%, Japan post-bubble +48%).
4. Tax increases and capital controls are common enough to plan for.
5. Buy the currency and debt after the five steps are visible, when real rates are very high against prospective inflation and depreciation.
