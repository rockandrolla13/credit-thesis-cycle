# Cheatsheet: Geopolitical Alpha (Papic)

**Maxim**: preferences are optional and subject to constraints. Constraints are neither optional nor subject to preferences. (Ch 2)

## Decision rules
- When a leader states a goal, audit the six political-capital factors before pricing it. (Ch 4)
- When rhetoric is loudest, ask who holds the speaker back. Loud can mean constrained. (Ch 2)
- When a statement pays the speaker whether or not they act, treat it as noise. (Ch 3)
- When insiders all agree, bet on funneling risk, not on the insiders. (Ch 3)
- When a legal rule is your fulcrum, "you are probably wrong". (Ch 7)
- When no legal constraint exists, the outcome rests on preference. Flag it as a risk. (Ch 7)
- When a crisis threatens the system, assume red lines and treaty limits give way. (Ch 5, Ch 7)
- When something blows up, the median 12-month S&P 500 return after past events was positive (10.92%). The author names 1973 as the exception. (Ch 2)
- When the system is multipolar, expect trade to continue between rivals. (Ch 5)
- When a new leader inherits a crisis, expect reform. When an incumbent hits one, expect loss. (Ch 4)
- When reform is absent at peak political capital, read that as the median voter's veto. (Ch 9)
- When panic sets the narrative, trade tactically with it and strategically against it. (Ch 8)
- When your view equals consensus, there is no trade. (Ch 11)

## Process
1. Net assessment → 2. Fulcrum constraint → 3. Data-stream checklist → 4. Prior (probability or bias) → 5. Compare with market → 6. Trade the gap → 7. Update. (Ch 9, Ch 11)

## Which tool
| Situation | Tool |
|---|---|
| Single leader, this term | Political capital audit |
| Policy direction over years | Median voter read |
| Country allocation | Cyclical net assessment, output a bias |
| Sudden event | Off-the-shelf net assessment plus decision tree |
| Two actors bargaining | Gameboard: power, risk tolerance, credibility, win-sets |
| Consensus "gospel" | Around-the-curve net assessment |

## Tells and smells
- Consultant drops three names before a breath: stop listening. (Ch 3)
- High conviction with no scenarios or probabilities: overstated. (Ch 3)
- Forecaster prescribes policy: discount the forecast. (Ch 3)
- Public polling goes dark: something is wrong. (Ch 3)
- Analysis rests on one technical acronym: doom-hunting. (Ch 5)
- Result in country A used to price country B: stereotype, not analysis. (Ch 11)
- Chart mixes bracket widths or shows counts as rates: manipulation. (Ch 8)
- Your friends are all savers: your sample of the median voter is skewed. (Ch 12)

## Limits the author states
- Not scientific, not quantitative, "not a foolproof method". (Ch 2, Ch 12)
- Cannot time when panic ends. (Ch 8)
- Requires country knowledge the framework does not supply. (Intro)
