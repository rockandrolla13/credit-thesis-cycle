# Patterns: Geopolitical Alpha (Papic)

## Constraint-over-preference forecast
**When to use**: any forecast of what a policymaker will do. (Ch 2)
**How**: (1) Read one biography or history to learn the actor's preferences, once. (2) List the material constraints in hierarchy order: political, economic, financial, geopolitical, then legal. (3) Ask which hypotheses each constraint eliminates. (4) Forecast the path of least resistance.
**Trade-offs**: tells you what, not when. Needs real knowledge of the country.

## Diagnosticity filter
**When to use**: sorting news, statements and expert input. (Ch 2, Ch 3)
**How**: for each item ask whether it would look the same under every hypothesis. If the speaker gains from saying it whether or not they act, discard it. Keep only evidence that eliminates a hypothesis.
**Trade-offs**: throws away most headlines. That is the point.

## Political capital audit (six factors)
**When to use**: within-term calls on whether a leader can do a stated thing. (Ch 4)
**How**: score popularity, time in power, legislative math, economic context, special interest support, global momentum. Mark each as constraining or not. Name the fulcrum.
**Trade-offs**: ad hoc and not comparable across countries. Valid for one term only.

## Median voter read
**When to use**: multi-year policy direction. (Ch 4)
**How**: (1) Identify the central issue. (2) Locate the median voter on it from iterative polling. (3) Expect every party to converge there over time. Check the opposition's positioning as a second reading. In a one-party state use middle-class support.
**Trade-offs**: policy can deviate in the short run. Fails where there is no accountability. Fails during mass panic (Ch 8).

## Macro fundamentals check-up
**When to use**: building a country assessment. (Ch 5, Ch 9)
**How**: run the five diagnostic questions (growth drivers, productivity, structural imbalances, current account, creative destruction). One red flag is routine. Several mean dig for the root cause.
**Trade-offs**: needs current economics knowledge. Screen data alone misses structure.

## Dependence mapping
**When to use**: breakup, exit, sanction or trade-war scenarios. (Ch 5, Ch 6)
**How**: ask who depends on whom for exports, energy, finance. Check who holds the claims. With a large imbalance the lender has most to lose. In a multipolar system add who captures the lost trade.
**Trade-offs**: dependence can be misjudged if only bilateral flows are counted.

## Force-requirement test
**When to use**: a feared military scenario. (Ch 6, Ch 9)
**How**: estimate the troops, aircraft, logistics and occupation effort needed. Compare with the actor's capability, terrain and local population. If the requirement overcommits the actor, lower the probability.
**Trade-offs**: relies on military knowledge the analyst may need to buy.

## Legal analysis last
**When to use**: only when stronger constraints leave hypotheses standing. (Ch 7)
**How**: (1) Check for a *missing* legal constraint, which means preference alone decides. (2) Check whether a procedure removes a political constraint. (3) In a crisis assume legal limits yield.
**Trade-offs**: tedious. Easy to misremember rule history.

## Net assessment
**When to use**: before any event, for each lens (discrete, cyclical, around the curve). (Ch 9)
**How**: net the constraints. Output three things: a prior (probability or bias), the fulcrum constraint, and a checklist of data streams. Update the prior when the streams move.
**Trade-offs**: research-heavy. It starts the work and does not finish it.

## Decision tree with conditional probabilities
**When to use**: discrete events with sequential moves. (Ch 9)
**How**: lay out steps and branches. Assign a probability to each branch. Multiply along paths to get scenario probabilities that sum to 100%. Add a timeline.
**Trade-offs**: branch weights are subjective. The discipline usually lowers gut estimates (15% to 4% in the author's no-deal Brexit case).

## Gameboard before game
**When to use**: two or more actors facing off. (Ch 10)
**How**: do a net assessment of each actor. Score material balance of power, risk tolerance, credibility. Add domestic win-sets. Rewrite each payoff, the crash payoff above all, from each actor's constraints.
**Trade-offs**: precision can pass for accuracy. The author rarely uses it.

## Bet the spread
**When to use**: turning a view into a position. (Ch 11)
**How**: compare your prior with market-implied odds or valuations. Trade only if out of consensus. Add valuation, sentiment and carry. Record the trade and let P&L audit the view.
**Trade-offs**: needs a tactician to structure. Exposed to timing.

## Relative sentiment trade
**When to use**: two markets priced on leaders' agendas, both constrained against those agendas. (Ch 11)
**How**: go long the over-sold one against the over-bought one. You need the spread to narrow, not the underdog to win.
**Trade-offs**: both legs carry macro beta. Check it lines up.

## Tactical narrative, strategic constraints
**When to use**: panics driven by terror, pandemic or social media. (Ch 8)
**How**: trade the narrative in the short run. Hold the constraint view for the long run. Ask what raises the Unit Cost of Fear and when debtor households feel it.
**Trade-offs**: the end date of a panic is not forecastable.

## Expert triage
**When to use**: buying outside research. (Ch 3)
**How**: prefer academics, second-tier technocrats and ex-regulators. Require scenarios, probabilities and monitoring variables. Frame questions around constraints. Ask who else has the same insight.
**Trade-offs**: experts give knowledge, not market calls.
