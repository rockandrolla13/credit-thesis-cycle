## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. The maxim (Introduction, Ch 2, Ch 11)
"Preferences are optional and subject to constraints, whereas constraints are neither optional nor subject to preferences."
Forecast "not on what policymakers want to do, but on what they must do given their material reality". Don't study the Prince. Study his constraints.

### 2. The three pillars (Ch 2)
1. **Materialist dialectic** (Marx): start in the material world. Ideas derive from it.
2. **Diagnosticity** (Heuer, CIA): keep only evidence that eliminates a hypothesis. Preferences are not diagnostic because acting on them is optional. Constraints are.
3. **Person versus situation** (Ross and Nisbett): the situation predicts behaviour better than character. Avoid the fundamental attribution error.

Formal version (recovered from the PDF, see ch02):
- Preference model: `P(Y) = p_X(y) · p_y(Y)`
- Constraint model: `P(Y) = Σ_Z p_X(y|Z) · p_y(Y|Z) · p_Z(Z)`
- "Hold me back": when the restraint Z is present the actor wants to fight and cannot. When it is absent he could and does not want to. Rhetoric is anticorrelated with outcome.

### 3. The hierarchy of constraints (Ch 2, Ch 4-8)
The author arranges them "from the most salient to the least":
1. **Political** — "power is the most important constraint". Start every forecast here. (Ch 4)
2. **Economic** and 3. **Financial/market** — distinct but reflexively linked. (Ch 5)
4. **Geopolitical** — real, but "analytical fast food" when used alone. (Ch 6)
5. **Constitutional/legal** — "the bottom rung". A constraint-preference hybrid. "If these are the fulcrum constraints of your forecast, you are probably wrong." (Ch 7)
- **Wild cards**: terrorism and pandemics, which expose the framework's **time** blind spot. (Ch 8)

### 4. Political constraints: two tools (Ch 4)
- **Political capital audit** for within-term calls. Six factors: popularity, time in power, legislative math, economic context, special interest support, global momentum. Reform follows a **J curve** with a "danger zone".
- **Median Voter Theorem** for multi-year direction. Politicians (1) identify the central issue, (2) find the median voter's position, (3) approach it asymptotically. "The median voter is the price maker in the political marketplace. Politicians are price takers." Policy may deviate short-term, not long-term. In a one-party state use the **median citizen** (middle-class support).
- **Fulcrum constraint**: the one constraint "so powerful that it bends all other factors to it". Find it, then watch it.

### 5. Economic and market constraints (Ch 5)
- Ask who depends on whom. Germany's export dependence on the EU was its fulcrum against exit. With large imbalances "the lender - not the debtor - has the most to lose" (TARGET2).
- Polarity sets trade behaviour. Bipolar: relative gains dominate. Unipolar: the hegemon gives "bad deals". **Multipolar: trade lost to third powers is the fulcrum that prevents all-out trade war.**
- Three misuses: outdated knowledge, assuming a 1:1 constraint-policymaker link, missing structural subtlety.

### 6. Geopolitics and law in their place (Ch 6, Ch 7)
- US grand strategy: "Prevent any one power from dominating Eurasia." It caps the Trump Doctrine (transactionalism, mercantilism, nationalism).
- A weak sphere-of-influence offer predicts coercion and also limits it (Russia in Ukraine, 2014-2015).
- Use law last. Check for a *missing* legal constraint (US presidential tariff powers) and for procedure that removes a political constraint (budget reconciliation, 2017).

### 7. The time constraint (Ch 8, Ch 12)
"Fear creates its own reality." A panicked median voter is itself a material constraint, and its end date cannot be forecast. Rule: **"React as a tactical investor to the narrative and as a strategic investor to the constraints."** Watch the **Unit Cost of Fear**: debtor households desensitize before savers.

### 8. Operationalization (Ch 9-11)
1. **Net assessment** in one of three lenses: *Discrete Event* (reactive), *Cyclical* (12-18 months, by country), *Around the Curve* (themes).
2. Output a **Bayesian prior** (a probability or a deliberate bias), the **fulcrum constraint**, and a checklist of **data streams**. Prior → new information → posterior → new prior.
3. For discrete events build a **decision tree with conditional probabilities** (Iran-US tree, May 2018: 36 / 24 / 20 / 5 / 15).
4. For multi-actor contests **set the gameboard** first: material balance of power, risk tolerance, credibility, plus domestic win-sets. Then rewrite the payoffs.
5. **Bet the spread, not the winner.** Compare your prior with market pricing. Trade only if out of consensus. Let the market check the work.

### 9. Using experts (Ch 3)
Five limits of the intelligence model: not statistically significant, wrong for complex systems, funneling, reflexivity (officials "talk their own book"), false experts. Demand scenarios, probabilities and monitoring variables. Prefer academics, second-tier technocrats and ex-regulators.
