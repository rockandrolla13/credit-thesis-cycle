# Chapter 9: The Art of the Net Assessment

## Core Idea
A net assessment nets out all the constraints of Ch 4-8 into a Bayesian prior, either a probability or a bias. Its main output is the fulcrum constraint and the data streams to watch. "The net assessment is not where constraint-based analysis ends, but where it begins."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Net assessment**: the term comes from the US Defense Department's Office of Net Assessment (ONA). "A net assessment nets out the conclusions of competing analytical approaches." Here the things netted are the material constraints.
- **Three lenses of geopolitical forecasting**, each with its own preparation step:

  | Lens | Horizon and nature | What to prepare |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Discrete Event** | Reactive, tactical, at a moment's notice (the Soleimani killing) | Each January, write the five risks most likely to need a reactive net assessment. Keep off-the-shelf assessments. End in scenarios with probabilities, ideally a decision tree. |
  | **Cyclical** | The next 12-18 months | Keep a geopolitical calendar: elections, summits, referenda, budget deadlines, fiscal cliffs, notable military exercises. Build a constraint-based net assessment of each major economy you hold or want, starting with the G20. Monitor "the mutability of constraints". |
  | **Around the Curve** | Long-term themes, not countries | Write down five assumptions "taken as gospel by most of your peers" and test each. Focus on dormant themes: "Their expiration date may be closer than the consensus thinks." |

- **The Bayesian prior**: the net assessment sets "the subjective process through which the analyst assigns the probability of an event occurring based not on the historical record but a thorough net assessment".
- **The Bayesian process (Figure 9.1, recovered from PDF p. 228; book p. 208)**: a three-box cycle.
  1. **PRIOR**: "Probability of an event occurring based on subjective analysis or historical frequency."
  2. **NEW INFORMATION**: "Probability is updated based on DATA STREAMS."
  3. **POSTERIOR**: "New information updates probabilities and becomes the *new* PRIOR."
- **Data streams and the checklist**: the net assessment "produces a fulcrum constraint on which to focus" and names "the critical pieces of data to monitor". Keep them on a checklist. "If the data flowing through those streams change, probability should change with it."
- **Decision tree with conditional probabilities**: for discrete events. It "formalizes choices available to policymakers" and shows "the constraint-defined path of least resistance". Conditional probability depends on earlier events. Subjective probability is the forecaster's judgment.
- **Prior probability versus prior bias**: for a cyclical country assessment, probabilities may not help. The India assessment "sets my prior bias". For a discrete event, end in scenarios with probabilities.
- **How to brief a consultant under this method**: frame the question around constraints. "Yes, we all know that the Quds force are ideologues who hate America, but what can they do about it, given X, Y, and Z?"

## Key Concepts
- **Frequentist inference (rejected for one-off events)**: the sample of comparable referenda is too small and too dissimilar. A frequentist would have put Brexit at 10%, "as only 1 in 10 noniterative referenda go against the established, consensus view".
- **Fulcrum constraint**: the one constraint the whole forecast turns on (Ch 4).
- **Mean reversion thesis (India)**: India and China were the two largest economies for most of two millennia. The thesis is a return to that share (Figure 9.2).
- **Round-tripping**: Indian firms invoice exports to Mauritius, pay no tax, and bring some proceeds back as "FDI". Mauritius was 30% of India's 2020 FDI.
- **Premature deindustrialization (Dani Rodrik, 2015)**: developing economies now peak in manufacturing at lower income, which blocks the main route to convergence.
- **Test-taker reform**: improving ranked indicators without fixing the underlying problem. India rose 30 places in the World Bank's Doing Business report.

## Mental Models
- Boil everything known about an economy down to the fulcrum constraint. "From there, it is simple to monitor the data streams of that one constraint."
- Treat the *absence* of a reform at peak political capital as evidence. The BJP held 21 of 29 state legislatures (17 since December 2019) and still did not move on land reform.
- Draw the tree before trusting a gut number. Conditioning each branch usually lowers a headline probability.
- "Net assessments are meant to be changed."

## Anti-patterns
- **"Putting too much faith in the man and his preferences"**: investors on Modi. "No matter Modi's preferences, he is constrained by the Indian median voter."
- **Buying on valuation against a fulcrum constraint**: "investors should not invest in India yet. Regardless of valuations."
- **Using base rates for unique political events** without checking similarity.
- **Calling "geopolitical" anything that is not market or macro**: the label hides what the analysis can and cannot do.

### India: evidence behind the underinvestment fulcrum
| Channel | Finding |
|---|---|
| Household taxes | Only 53 million people, 5.6% of the population, pay personal income tax. Wealthy families keep as much as 70% of assets abroad. |
| Corporate taxes | Cut to 25% for SMEs in 2016 and 22% for other firms in 2019. Effective rate about 25%. About 100 firms (0.012% of 800,000) pay over 40% of corporate tax. |
| FDI | Stock nearly quadrupled in a decade, but the pace slowed after 2008. India stays among the most restrictive on the OECD index. |
| Savings | Gross national savings 28.5% against China's 45.8%. Banking assets to GDP are half those of other Asian economies. |
| Labor law | Firms with more than 100 employees need government approval to dismiss one worker. |
| Land law | A state prerogative, hard to amend. |
| Labor market | Of 1.34 billion people, 500 million are employed and 300 million of working age are not. Close to a million reach working age each month. Real rural wages "grew" at -0.3% between FY2015 and FY2018. A railway posting for 63,000 jobs drew 19 million applicants. |
| Trade | Exports are 11% of GDP. Manufacturing share of GDP and share of global exports stagnated or fell after 2014, despite rising Chinese labor costs and the 2019 trade war. |

Three conditions behind the East Asian miracle, used as the benchmark: (1) high domestic savings turned into high gross capital formation, (2) high productivity growth, (3) a geopolitical environment friendly to export-led industrialization. India is short on all three.

## Worked Example: Iran-US conflict decision tree (produced May 2018)
Figure 9.16, recovered from PDF p. 252 (book p. 232). The extraction kept the scenario text but lost the branch structure.

Phases along the top: *Physical loss of Iranian exports* → *Risk premium rises/falls based on Iranian retaliation* → *Physical loss due to war/conflict*. Timeline along the bottom: 8 May 2018 → 4 November 2018, then Q1 2019 → Q3/Q4 2019, then Q3/Q4 onward.

- **Step I: the US reimposes sanctions against Iran.**
  - **80%**: compliance with US sanctions reduces Iranian oil exports significantly → **Step II: Iran retaliates to the reimposition of sanctions.**
    - **45%**: Iran retaliates rhetorically → **Scenario 1** (conditional probability 36%): an uneasy status quo. Iran gains from a geopolitical risk premium on oil but loses exports.
    - **30%**: Iran retaliates through concrete action across the Middle East → Step III.
    - **25%**: Iran retaliates *and* restarts the nuclear program → Step III.
    - **Step III: the US retaliates to Iranian retaliation.**
      - **Scenario 2** (conditional probability 24%): the US ratchets up military and economic pressure on Iran.
      - **Scenario 3** (conditional probability 20%): Scenario 2 fails to move Iran. The US and/or Israel attack its nuclear facilities.
  - **5%**: the EU and other US allies refuse to comply → **Scenario 4**: trade war between the US and EU.
  - **15%**: Iran opens back channels ahead of implementation → **Scenario 5**: a new US-Iran détente is negotiated.

Arithmetic check: 0.80 x 0.45 = 36%. The two kinetic branches carry 0.80 x 0.55 = 44%, which the figure splits 24% and 20%. Total: 36 + 24 + 20 + 5 + 15 = 100%.

**Result as the text reports it**: the outcome "was somewhere between the first two scenarios". An uneasy status quo emerged and the US did raise pressure. Oil spiked 15% after the September 2019 Aramco attack and 4.6% after the January 2020 Soleimani killing. "Both times, the correct call was to short the spike in oil prices." Subdued oil demand also helped.

### The constraints behind the tree (net assessment written 2020)
| Constraint | Content |
|---|---|
| Politics | In mid-2019, 82% of Americans viewed Iran unfavorably, yet only 18% supported military action and 65% feared the US would be "too quick to use military force". Iran ranked third among perceived enemies after Russia and China. In 2012 it was first at 32% (Figure 9.15). |
| Geopolitics | Too few US ground troops in Iraq, "the geopolitical epicenter of the region". Iran gained most from the fall of Saddam, and its influence extends through Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Yemen and Qatar. The US priority is China and Russia. |
| Retaliation | Iran can hit Saudi facilities (shown in 2019) and US troops in Iraq and Syria (shown in January 2020). |
| Allies' capability | Israel has no strategic bombers. A strike needs about 50-70 fighters and refueling, probably in Saudi airspace. Saudi Arabia has only struck the Houthis. Iran has had the S-300 since 2017 and 20 MiG-29s. |

**Forecast**: "in the long term, the US has to come back to a détente with Iran", ceding Iran some regional influence. The JCPOA (2015) was "the optimal path of least resistance". **Stated risk**: tit-for-tat that "crosses some undefined red line", helped by terrorism's power over the median voter (Ch 8).

## Worked Example: India cyclical net assessment (early 2020)
1. **Consensus**: India is "the next big thing". Modi's 2019 win gave him more political capital.
2. **Reject the standard narrative**: heterogeneity does not explain India's poverty. Imperialism and a command economy kept until the early 1990s do. So the bull case is mean reversion.
3. **Run the fundamentals check (Ch 5)**: too many markers are off, so dig for the root cause.
4. **Fulcrum**: "Underinvestment is the fulcrum constraint." Gross fixed capital formation and savings peaked in 2007. All three ways to raise investment (state, domestic firms, foreigners) are blocked. See the table.
5. **Why reform will not come**: the GST (2017) used up first-term capital. COVID-19 makes economic performance "a prohibitive constraint" to painful tax and labor reform. The median voter "would not support painful reforms amid a global recession". Modi turned to nationalism and citizenship law, which drew protests from late 2019.
6. **Bull-case fail-safes rejected**: "trade war insurance" and "Chinese reform insurance" no longer hold in 2020. Post-stimulus commodity inflation, especially food, would hurt India.
7. **Geopolitics**: mildly positive. India is a valued US ally and Pakistan no longer matches its hard power. Climate change is the long-run risk.
8. **Verdict**: "As of early 2020, investors should not invest in India yet. Regardless of valuations." Consumption can still grow, but not "high-quality growth", and it would widen the current account deficit or raise inflation.
9. **What would change the view**: labor and land reform.

## Key Takeaways
1. Build net assessments before events. A reactive one is too late.
2. The deliverable is a prior plus a watch-list tied to one fulcrum constraint.
3. Assign specific subjective probabilities. Citing Tetlock, "the more specific the subjective probability, the better the forecast is likely to be".
4. Use a decision tree to discipline the number. In late 2019 the author's gut estimate of no-deal Brexit was 15%. The tree gave 4%.
5. Update when the data streams move. The prior is a starting point.
