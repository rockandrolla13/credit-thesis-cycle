# Chapter 1: We're Not in Kansas Anymore

## Core Idea
From 1985 to about 2008-2010 politics and geopolitics were tailwinds, so investors could ignore them. Four paradigm shifts turned them into headwinds. Political analysis is now part of the investment process and cannot be outsourced.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Goldilocks Era (1985-2008/2010)**: "twin tailwinds" of *laissez-faire* economics and American geopolitical hegemony. The author dates its start to Gorbachev's 1985 "Leningrad speech" launching *perestroika*.
  - Causal chain as stated: Soviet ideological capitulation → Third Wave of democratization and free-market adoption → within two decades "90% of the global population was living in capitalism" (Figure 1.1). US as sole superpower enforces norms and backs the Washington Consensus. Thatcher/Reagan supply-side policy beats Mitterrand's *dirigisme*.
- **The four paradigm shifts of the 2010s**: (1) the end of American hegemony, (2) multipolarity, (3) the apex of globalization, (4) the erosion of laissez-faire economics.
  - Mechanism for (1)-(2): "Hegemony sows the seeds for its own decline." US policing let challengers focus on development until they could challenge the US (cites Gilpin).
  - Mechanism for (3): globalization needs one or more states to pay for global public goods. The author lists defense of trade routes, global economic policy coordination, a consumer of last resort, and defense of rules such as sovereignty. No state fills the role, so "deglobalization is structural and thus difficult to reverse".
  - Mechanism for (4): the 2008 Great Recession plus rising inequality. Globalization expanded global labor supply and weighed on developed-world middle-class wages (Figure 1.3, Lakner and Milanović).
- **Three weaknesses of the 2020 financial industry**:
  1. *The Quant Obsession*: economics "won the war of social sciences". Finance modeled macro and market inputs because elections and wars had stopped mattering for liquid markets.
  2. *Self-selection*: the rise of the "Newtonian Investor", who wins by manipulating mathematical rules. The laws of economics are not fixed and change in a paradigm shift.
  3. *Ideology*: laissez-faire belief came with disdain for government, which denigrates political analysis itself.
- **Two weaknesses of the "intelligence model"** of political consultancy (beyond ex-officials being cut off from classified flow and overweighting their own expertise):
  1. *Statistical significance problem*: governments build a mosaic of many signals. A for-profit consultancy cannot. The investor gets one "over-the-hill technocrat".
  2. *Complex systems*: in the most liquid markets (US, China, EU, India, large EMs) power is diffuse and checked by constitutional and bureaucratic constraints, so "access to power does not guarantee insight".

## Key Concepts
- **Multipolarity**: a world where no single entity (unipolarity) and no two entities (bipolarity) hold a preponderance of power to impose order. It produces high geopolitical volatility (Figure 1.2).
- **Hegemonic stability**: Kindleberger's term for order supplied by one dominant state.
- **Washington Consensus**: the macroeconomic reform package promoted through international institutions. Its pillars came from Thatcher's tax cuts, privatization and deregulation.
- **Buenos Aires Consensus**: the author's name for what the Washington Consensus may mutate into (developed in Ch 4).
- **Great Moderation (author's reading)**: tamed inflation and low volatility came from globalization, expanded global labor supply, laissez-faire policy and US-enforced stability, not from "a committee of academics setting interest rates". He calls this a heretical view.
- **Newtonian Investor**: see above.

## Mental Models
- Treat the supply-side victory as contextual, not absolute (footnote 14). Supply-side fixes were right for an era of runaway inflation and capacity constraints. In "today's secular stagnation era" demand may be what is lacking.
- Read COVID-19 as an accelerant, not a cause. The author "vehemently" disagrees that the pandemic was the turning point. It sped up shifts that began in the 2010s.
- Think of political analysis as endogenous to the investment process, not an exogenous input to buy.

## Anti-patterns
- **Outsourcing geopolitics to ex-officials**: they give context, not forecasts.
- **Crediting central banks alone for the Great Moderation**: it hides the political drivers that are now reversing.
- **Blaming the 1973 oil shock for 1970s stagflation**: footnote 13 says ex-oil commodity prices were already rising before the Yom Kippur War.
- **Studying preferences**: "The world of constraints is measurable and quantifiable whereas the world of preferences is not."

## Worked Example: the COVID-19 stimulus call (February-March 2020)
- **Setting**: when the sell-off began in February 2020 the author was at a Miami gathering of macro investors.
- **Consensus there**: Speaker Nancy Pelosi would play politics and delay stimulus to hurt President Trump. Policymakers would be "behind the curve".
- **What happened, per the text**: unlimited credit lines to businesses, massive QE, and "helicopter money".
- **Author's explanation**: the response had "little to do with the nature of the crisis". Policymakers were already primed by the decade's shift away from the Washington Consensus. The US had already "blown the budget deficit" under Trump at the top of the cycle, so nobody would balk at stimulus size.
- **Lesson**: map the prevailing paradigm before forecasting a crisis response. Ch 8 gives the fuller account.

## Worked Example: Genex (Yugoslavia, 1986-1994)
- **The edge**: the author's father joined General Export (Genex) in 1986. Genex manufactured nothing. The USSR refused to run a trade deficit and bartered. Non-aligned Yugoslavia could sell goods to the Soviets for commodities, then sell those commodities on the world market for hard currency. The author calls this "pure geopolitical alpha".
- **Scale**: at end-1989 Genex controlled 12-13% of Yugoslav foreign trade and nearly a third of Serbian trade.
- **The shift**: the Berlin Wall fell on 9 November 1989 and the USSR disintegrated two years later. "The firm's entire corporate strategy was leveraged to the geopolitical status quo."
- **Outcome as stated**: the dinar was devalued 18 times in seven years with 22 zeros erased. Monthly inflation peaked at 313,000,000% in January 1994. Between February 1992 and January 1994 the price level rose by a factor of 3.6 x 10^22. The family left Belgrade in May 1994.
- **Lesson**: a business or portfolio whose edge depends on a geopolitical status quo carries a hidden paradigm-shift exposure. Training built over "the past 35 years" does not prepare investors for it.

## Key Takeaways
1. "Getting the market right, from here on out, is as much about the politics and geopolitics as it is about valuations, interest rates, and earnings."
2. Deglobalization and multipolarity are structural. Do not model them as a one-politician phenomenon.
3. The populist backlash was strongest in the US and UK because they adopted laissez-faire most enthusiastically, leaving their middle classes most exposed after 2008.
4. In complex political systems access is not insight. Diffuse power is itself a constraint.
5. The last market-relevant developed-market election in 1980-2010 was, in the author's view, France 1981 (footnote 10). Mitterrand's win led to two major runs on the franc within a year.
