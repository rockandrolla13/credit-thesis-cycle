# Chapter 11: Geopolitical Alpha

## Core Idea
Geopolitical alpha comes from "betting against the spread, not trying to predict who wins the game". Compare your constraint-based probability or bias with what the market prices. The gap is the trade. The market's result then checks the forecast.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Bet the spread, not the winner**: the sports bettor is "indifferent toward the winner". Success is beating the line the bookie sets. Here "the bookie" is the market. "Some of my worst calls as an investment strategist have been when I forgot this point and tried to bet on who wins the election."
- **Why the edge exists**: a "fundamental skills mismatch". Investors are over-professionalized and overquantified, with no training in politics, so "the market consistently over-reacts to geopolitical events". Different time horizons add to the mispricing.
- **The full process, as the takeaway states it**:
  1. Produce a constraint-based **net assessment** (Ch 9).
  2. Extract the **fulcrum constraint(s)** and the associated **data streams**. "If they change, the view needs to change."
  3. Set a **prior probability** for the event or its scenarios (example: US-Iran kinetic conflict at 20%), or choose a deliberate **bias** (bearish Brazil 2018, bullish Mexico 2018, bearish India 2020).
  4. **Compare with market pricing and action.** "What do the valuations say about assets that would be impacted?"
  5. Act only if the view is **out of consensus**. "Your assessment has to be out of consensus, or the market may have already priced it in."
  6. Let the **market check the work**. "If I'm in green, my forecast was sound."
- **Relative sentiment trade**: when two leaders each face constraints opposite to their stated agenda, and the market has already priced the agendas, trade the gap between them. Each leader must "prove that he could overcome his constraints in order to justify" a move "that had already happened".
- **Constraints apply to the analyst**: "all preferences are subject to constraints - not just those of the actors, but those of the analyst as well."
- **Strategist versus tactician**: the framework is not "a trading rule". The strategist plots the course. Trade structure belongs to someone else. The author credits Mathieu Savary for the trade design in the France case.

## Key Concepts
- **Geopolitical alpha**: return against a benchmark earned by pricing geopolitical risk or opportunity better than the market.
- **The spread / the line**: the market-implied probability or valuation.
- **Deliberate bias**: a directional lean "grounded in research", unlike "an implicit bias rooted in stereotypes".
- **Catastrophizing**: assigning a feared outcome more probability than the data supports.
- **"The broad set of the possible" versus "the constrained set of the probable"**: investors err by pricing the first.
- **Revisionist performance review**: political forecasts are vague enough to be called right afterwards. Markets give "brutal, immediate feedback".

## Mental Models
- Think of yourself as a gambler, not a fan. You "cannot simultaneously be a gambler and a fan".
- Treat a missed binary call as a success if you were on the right side of the price. The author forecast Remain would win narrowly and still made money, because he put Brexit near 50% against a market at 30%.
- Hunt where "investors confuse stereotyping for analysis". Anti-French bias and anti-"communist" ideology supplied two of the three trades.
- Do not transfer a result across countries without checking the local median voter. Brexit and Trump did not raise Le Pen's odds.
- Use carry as a tie-breaker. In the Mexico-Brazil case investors were "leaving positive carry on the table purely because of ideological preference".

## Anti-patterns
- **Predicting winners** for their own sake.
- **Contagion by analogy**: "investors were assigning a higher probability to the election of Marine Le Pen because of the anti-establishment victories in the UK and the US."
- **Investing with stereotypes** instead of a net assessment.
- **Ideology as kryptonite**: the "Legend" who would not "go long communism and short capitalism".
- **Geopolitics in a vacuum**: "In each case of a successful investment call, I've also relied on valuations and market sentiment."
- **Following a stale media narrative**: the press kept the migration story after the data had turned (Figure 11.1).

## Worked Example: long euro before the 2017 French election
1. **Market mood, late 2016**: after Brexit and Trump, investors raised Le Pen's odds.
2. **Net assessment of France**: the Anglo-Saxon median voter was moving away from laissez-faire. That says nothing about France.
3. **Fulcrum constraint**: "the French median voter was moving away from Euroscepticism." By late 2016 the migration crisis was over (Figure 11.1). Le Pen's long-run support was "almost the perfect inverse" of French support for the euro (Figure 11.2).
4. **Second finding**: a "silent majority" wanted reform. Even Le Pen promised to cut the state and lower the retirement age. The next government would close the unit labor cost gap with Germany (Figure 11.3).
5. **Le Pen's error**: she stayed "intellectually wedded" to leaving the Euro Area, a preference that ran into the median voter.
6. **Compare with the line**: investors bet "a 20%-30% electoral lead ... would evaporate in three months". Trump's polling gap had been far smaller (Figure 11.5).
7. **Trades**: long EURUSD call, and long French / short German industrials.
8. **Check**: the euro's 21% rally from 20 December 2016 to 25 January 2018.

## Key Takeaways
1. You are paid for the gap between your probability and the market's, not for being right about the event.
2. A view that matches consensus has no alpha, however well built.
3. Write the trade next to the forecast. The P&L is the audit.
4. Add valuation, sentiment and carry to the geopolitical view before acting.
5. Audit your own preferences with the same framework. Ideological investors are the counterparties you want.
6. Leave structuring to a tactician if that is not your skill.
