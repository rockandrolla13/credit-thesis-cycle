# Glossary: Geopolitical Alpha (Papic)

**Around the Curve** — the long-term, theme-based lens of forecasting; test assumptions peers take as gospel (Ch 9)
**Bayesian prior** — the subjective starting probability or bias a net assessment produces, updated as data streams change (Ch 9)
**Betting the spread** — trading the gap between your probability and the market's, not predicting the winner (Ch 11)
**Buenos Aires Consensus** — the author's name for the populist, "growth above all else" successor to the Washington Consensus; democracy returns to economic policy (Ch 4, Ch 8)
**Byrd rule** — Senate rule that strikes non-budget provisions from reconciliation bills and forces deficit-raising provisions to sunset; 60 votes to waive (Ch 7)
**Capitulation point** — the time at which accumulated pain reaches an actor's pain threshold (Ch 10)
**Competing hypotheses analysis** — Heuer's method of eliminating hypotheses with diagnostic evidence (Ch 2)
**Constraint framework** — forecast from what policymakers must do given material reality, not from what they want (Intro, Ch 2)
**Constraint-preference hybrid** — legal and constitutional rules, which act as constraint or preference depending on the environment (Ch 7)
**Cyclical lens** — net assessments over the next 12-18 months, built around a geopolitical calendar (Ch 9)
**Danger zone** — the trough of the reform J curve, where the public may reject the policy (Ch 4)
**Data streams** — the observable series tied to the fulcrum constraint; kept on a checklist (Ch 9)
**Debt Supercycle** — BCA Research's term for post-war reliance on private relevering; ended 2008-2010 per the author (Ch 2, Ch 4)
**Decision tree** — scenario diagram with conditional probabilities showing the constraint-defined path of least resistance (Ch 9)
**Desensitization** — a public's adaptation to a repeated, bounded threat (Ch 8, Ch 12)
**Diagnosticity** — "the extent to which any item of evidence helps the analyst determine the relative likelihood of alternative hypotheses"; second pillar (Ch 2)
**Discrete Event lens** — reactive, tactical net assessment of a single event (Ch 9)
**Fortuna / Virtù** — Machiavelli's fate versus the Prince's skill; the forecast starts with Fortuna (Ch 2)
**Fulcrum constraint** — a constraint "so powerful that it bends all other factors to it"; the most diagnostic variable (Ch 4, Ch 9)
**Fundamental attribution error** — attributing outcomes to actors' character instead of their situation; third pillar (Ch 2)
**Funneling** — oversimplified mass distribution of insight from a handful of sources (Ch 3)
**Game of chicken** — two players drive at each other; mutual crash is likely unless payoffs are asymmetric (Ch 10)
**Geopolitical alpha** — return against a benchmark from pricing geopolitics better than the market (Intro, Ch 11)
**Goldilocks Era** — 1985 to 2008/2010, when laissez-faire and US hegemony were tailwinds (Ch 1)
**Grand strategy** — geography-rooted imperatives from which daily foreign policy derives (Ch 6)
**Hierarchy of constraints** — political, then economic and financial, then geopolitical, then constitutional/legal on the bottom rung (Ch 2, Ch 6, Ch 7)
**"Hold me back"** — aggressive rhetoric made safe by a known restraint; preference anticorrelated with outcome (Ch 2)
**Intelligence model** — forecasting from insider access; five stated limitations (Ch 1, Ch 3)
**J curve of structural reform** — reform drains political capital before it pays back (Ch 4)
**Lost trade** — imports forgone under a tariff, captured by third powers in a multipolar world (Ch 5)
**Material balance of power** — relative economic and military exposure; one of three capability factors (Ch 10)
**Materialist dialectic** — material conditions come first and ideas derive from them; first pillar (Ch 2)
**Median citizen** — the median-voter proxy in a one-party state, read from middle-class support (Ch 4)
**Median Voter Theorem (MVT)** — parties approximate the median voter's position to win or keep power (Ch 4)
**Middle-income trap** — growth stall at middle income; the CCP's central fear (Ch 4)
**Misery index** — unemployment plus inflation; a crude political-risk gauge (Ch 3)
**Multipolarity** — no one or two powers hold a preponderance of power; the least ordered system (Ch 1, Ch 5)
**Net assessment** — netting all constraints into a prior, a fulcrum and data streams (Ch 9)
**Nondiagnostic** — evidence that eliminates no hypothesis; preferences and rhetoric (Ch 2, Ch 3)
**Pain threshold / tolerance curve** — how much pain an actor can bear, and how fast domestic politics turns pain into surrender (Ch 10)
**Path of least resistance** — the course constraints force on policymakers (Ch 5, Ch 9)
**Political capital** — a leader's stock of power to enact unpopular policy; gauged by six factors (Ch 4)
**Professional nihilism** — indifference to what should happen (Intro, Ch 12)
**Reconciliation** — US budget procedure that bars the Senate filibuster for revenue and spending bills (Ch 7)
**Reflexivity** — perceptions alter fundamentals (Soros); also policymakers using markets to force action (Ch 3, Ch 5)
**Relative vs absolute gains** — realist distinction that explains trade behaviour by system polarity (Ch 5)
**Talking their book** — stating a preference because the statement itself pays (Ch 3)
**TARGET2** — Euro Area settlement system whose imbalances bind creditor to debtor (Ch 5)
**Thucydides Trap** — conflict between a status quo power and a challenger (Ch 5)
**Time constraint** — collective psychology can delay constraint-based outcomes; the framework's blind spot (Ch 8)
**Trump Doctrine** — transactionalism, mercantilism, nationalism (Ch 6)
**Two-level game** — domestic win-sets bound international deals (Putnam); extended to three levels for Johnson (Ch 3, Ch 10)
**Unit Cost of Fear** — the rising socioeconomic price of fear-led behaviour (Ch 8, Ch 12)
**Washington Consensus** — 1980-2010s policy set that removed elected officials from economic policy (Ch 1, Ch 4)
**Win-set** — international outcomes a leader's domestic politics will accept (Ch 10)
**World Island** — Mackinder's Eurasia; no single power may dominate it (Ch 6)
