# Chapter 1, Section 7: How Political Chaos Is Created from Runaway Greed

Source section: HOW POLITICAL CHAOS IS CREATED FROM RUNAWAY GREED. Includes Figure 1.2.

## Core Idea
An overheated banking system ends governments. Once the LDR peaks, it takes about seven years to fall back to a safe level near 0.75. Asset prices accelerate between an LDR of 1.0 and 1.2–1.3 and then collapse.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Banking collapse to political change chain**: Schulte lays out the transmission in steps.
  1. The banking system turns insolvent.
  2. Economic stagnation follows.
  3. Budget deficits rise to support the financial system.
  4. Unemployment rises. Governments lose room for subsidies.
  5. Quality of life falls and people become angry.
  6. Unemployed young people turn to religion and abandon political parties.
  7. The government is voted out, or holds power through violent tactics and forms of martial law.
  8. Chaotic political change tends to slip into civil war or war with neighbours.
  - When to use: to derive political and social second-order effects from a banking-system reading.
  - Trigger level stated: governments whose banking systems overheat with LDRs of 1.4 "almost invariably are kicked out of power". The text says the book "will show" this.
- **The seven-year deleveraging clock**: after the peak, the LDR needs "many years" to return to safe levels of around 0.75. Asia took about seven years. The United States also took about seven years.
  - How: date the LDR peak, then expect the trough about seven years on. Figure 1.2's annotation says six to seven years.
  - Mechanism in the figure's annotation: "Loans down and savings up—no other way."
- **Lender versus borrower asset prices**: wholesale banking redistributes money from low-LDR countries to high-LDR countries. Asset prices of the lenders "will almost always do better" than those of the borrowers, most of all when the borrower's LDR is 1.2 to 1.4.
  - Example pairing: China in 2005 as lender. The United States or Spain in 2006 as borrower.
- **The late-cycle acceleration**: asset prices rise faster between an LDR of about 1 and 1.3.
  - The ride from 1.0 to 1.2 "tends to be glorious".
  - The ride from 1.2 to 1.4 "is disastrous".
  - Investors who understand credit become alarmed in this range and sell, "probably too early".

## Key Concepts
- **Safe level**: an LDR of around 0.75, the level the text treats as the end of deleveraging.
- **Catastrophic level**: an LDR of 1.3–1.4 in this section's wording.
- **GIIPS**: Greece, Italy, Portugal, Spain and Ireland. Figure 1.2 labels the group "PIIGS".
- **"No credit left to distribute"**: the condition the text assigns to Brazil at an LDR of 1.30.
- **Canyon of exhausted credit**: Schulte's image for the point where wholesale funding stops.
- **Simple rules of the road**: the LDR-style limits that politicians, bankers and central banks decline to adopt.

## Mental Models
- Use the LDR as a political-risk indicator. A reading near 1.4 predicts a change of government after the system breaks.
- Think of the LDR peak as the start of a clock, not the end of the story. The adjustment runs for years.
- Think of a wholesale-funded boom as two trades. The lender country's assets are the stronger side. The borrower country's assets are the weaker side.
- Expect the best-looking price action just before the break. Rising prices between 1.0 and 1.3 are a warning on this view.

## Anti-patterns
- **Reading late-cycle price acceleration as health**: it comes from fickle overseas funding.
- **Expecting a quick repair**: the text gives roughly seven years for both Asia and the United States.
- **Ignoring the political leg**: the text ties religious and party realignment to credit exhaustion.
- **Waiting for governments to restrain wholesale borrowing**: Schulte calls their failure to act "a staggering mystery".

## Worked Example: Brazil as a live case
1. The LDR is 1.30, inside the range the text calls catastrophic.
2. The financial system is "stuck in recession" because no credit is left to distribute.
3. Young people give up hope of progress. The text reports a large evangelical Christian movement.
4. Petrobras shares fell more than 75% in 2013–14, which makes conditions worse.
5. ch02 adds the funding detail: Petrobras carries USD 91 bn of dollar debt.

Schulte gives two parallel cases. He cites David Shambaugh for the view that the collapse of the financial system was a major reason for the fall of the Soviet Union. He calls the rise of the Religious Right in the United States "a very understandable phenomenon" under the same chain.

## Key Takeaways
1. Add government-change risk to any theme where the LDR nears 1.4.
2. Budget for a deleveraging of about seven years after an LDR peak.
3. Favour the lender country's assets over the borrower's while the borrower's LDR is 1.2–1.4.
4. Treat price acceleration between LDR 1.0 and 1.3 as late-cycle.
5. Selling at the first alarm is likely to be early. The text says so directly.
