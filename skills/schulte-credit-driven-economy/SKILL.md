## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. Credit-first causality (ch01)
The capacity of a country's banking system to create credit is the cause. Income, output, inflation, earnings and asset prices are outcomes. Use this ordering before treating any macro series as a driver. Schulte asserts it from 25 years of sell-side work. This excerpt gives no formal test.

### 2. The four properties of "black swan" credit events (ch01)
Schulte says the book will show that these events:
1. are predictable from definite, repeatable circumstances,
2. "happen like clockwork", on a timeline set by banker behaviour, with similar patterns across asset prices,
3. are a function of the credit cycle and have little to do with an economic cycle,
4. are visible early if credit conditions rank "primus inter pares" with traditional indicators.

### 3. Loan/deposit ratio (LDR) and its cycle map (ch02, ch03)
LDR = bank loans ÷ bank deposits. Savings create credit, and credit creates money supply. Count the whole system, including lenders that deposits do not fund (the US GSEs).

| LDR | Reading |
|---|---|
| ~0.5 | Cycle start. Asset appreciation for four to six years. |
| ~0.75 | Safe level where deleveraging ends. |
| 1.0 | Natural resting place. Current account near zero. Policy cap in South Korea, Hong Kong, Singapore, China. |
| 1.0–1.2 | Foreign-funded late boom. "Glorious" ride. |
| 1.2–1.3 | "Usual danger zone". |
| 1.2–1.4 | Endgame: downgrades, scandals, joint foreign withdrawal. "Disastrous" ride. |
| 1.4 | Governments "almost invariably" lose power. |
| 1.7–1.8 | Spain's peak (1.7). Southern Europe at worst (1.8). |

### 4. LDR and current account link (ch02)
Across countries in 2010, Schulte's fit of LDR against current account/GDP passes through LDR 1 at a zero balance. A system at that point funds itself. Above 1, foreign money funds the margin.

### 5. Pro-cyclical wholesale funding (ch02, ch03)
Foreign bank inflows arrive late, extend the cycle and leave together. Domestic bankers treat them as "other people's money". Foreign bankers see "easy pickings". Prefer the lender country's assets over the borrower's, most of all when the borrower's LDR is 1.2–1.4. Expect borrower asset prices to accelerate between 1.0 and 1.3 first.

### 6. Tangible leverage (ch04)
Tangible leverage = (assets − intangibles) ÷ (capital − intangibles). Markers: 10× healthy, 20× resting place, about 30× dangerous, 30–40× trigger zone, 50× "suicidal".

### 7. The combined trigger (ch04)
When LDR is about 1.3 and tangible leverage is 30–40×:
- corporate earnings "do not matter one whit",
- all stocks fall, with better companies falling less,
- the macro break is "only a few months away",
- the government is out within about 24 months.

### 8. Two exits from each ratio (ch04)
- Lower the LDR: loans down or deposits up. Households bear it.
- Lower tangible leverage: assets down or capital up. "There is no other way." Managers, shareholders and the state bear it.
- The trap: 8–10 percent of the balance sheet is written off at the height of most crises, so capital shrinks while the bank seeks more.
- 2009 arithmetic from about 30×: 20× needed about $600bn of capital. 15× needed about $1.5tn of capital or about $22tn of asset removal.

### 9. Three crisis routes (ch04, ch05)
- United States: recapitalise (TARP) and write off. Leverage fell below 15× quickly.
- Europe: guarantee and keep assets on bank balance sheets (ECB). High leverage persisted seven years on.
- United Kingdom: midpoint. Nationalisation (80 percent of RBS), moral suasion, more public debt.

### 10. Debt-deflation loop (ch04)
Shrinking debt lowers asset values and wages and raises real debt burdens. Spending falls and purchases are delayed. The government becomes the only actor, builds debt, needs low rates, and gains an incentive toward low growth. Japan is the named example.

### 11. Rule of 1 and 20 (ch05)
Maximum LDR of 1 and tangible leverage of 20. Bank share prices tend to peak and fall once tangible leverage passes about 20×. The supporting chart is a 2012 cross-section of 11 institutions. Its fitted curve peaks nearer 25–30 by eye.

### 12. Crisis costs and clocks (ch03, ch05)
- LDR peaks near 1.2 and returns to about 0.75 in roughly seven years (Asia from 1997, the United States from 2007).
- Government debt/GDP rises about 50 points per crisis (attributed to Rogoff). It rises far more where the LDR reached 1.5–1.7.
- Northern Rock alone cost 6 points of UK government debt to GDP.

### 13. Credit-to-politics chain (ch03, ch05)
Insolvent banks lead to stagnation, deficits, unemployment and anger. Voters leave established parties. Governments fall or turn to repression. Bailouts that spare bankers speed the process.

### 14. Early-warning tells (ch01)
- Bank share prices: Citi peaked in 2005, Lehman in 2006.
- Credit spreads and CDS prices at records while the official forecast held (Greenbook, September 2008: 2 percent growth for 2009. December 2008: −4.7 percent).
- Mortgage growth funded by offshore money that can leave at short notice.
