# Patterns

Techniques from Part One, Chapter 1 of Schulte's book. Each names its chapter file.

## System-wide LDR reading
**When to use**: placing a country in its credit cycle (ch02).
**How**: divide total loans by total deposits. Add lenders that deposits do not fund, such as the US GSEs. Compare with 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.2–1.3 and 1.2–1.4.
**Trade-offs**: the source's threshold bands overlap and vary by paragraph. A deposit-only count hides the stealth rise.

## LDR plus current account cross-check
**When to use**: testing whether credit is funded at home (ch02).
**How**: plot LDR against current account as a percent of GDP. Schulte's fit crosses LDR 1 at a zero balance. Above 1, look for the foreign lender.
**Trade-offs**: the evidence shown is one 2010 cross-section of 12 points.

## Tangible leverage calculation
**When to use**: sizing a bank's real loss buffer (ch04).
**How**: (assets − intangibles) ÷ (capital − intangibles). Compare with 10× (healthy), 20× (resting place), 30–40× (trigger zone), 50× ("suicidal").
**Trade-offs**: values intangibles at zero, which is Schulte's stated view.

## Two-gauge crisis trigger
**When to use**: deciding whether fundamentals still matter (ch04).
**How**: check LDR near 1.3 and tangible leverage at 30–40×. If both hold, Schulte expects a break within months, broad equity falls, and a change of government within about 24 months.
**Trade-offs**: asserted with proof deferred to later chapters, which this excerpt does not include.

## Rule of 1 and 20 screen
**When to use**: a first-pass prudential screen for banks and systems (ch05).
**How**: flag an LDR above 1 or tangible leverage above 20.
**Trade-offs**: the supporting chart's fitted curve peaks nearer 25–30 by eye.

## Deleveraging route classification
**When to use**: forecasting the speed and form of a post-crisis repair (ch04, ch05).
**How**: sort the response into recapitalise (US, TARP), shrink or guarantee assets (Europe, ECB), or midpoint (UK, nationalisation and moral suasion). Capital is fast. Assets are slow and deflationary.
**Trade-offs**: the fast route carried the electoral cost in the text's account. So did the slow one.

## Buffer versus write-off test
**When to use**: judging whether a system needs a public backstop (ch04).
**How**: compare capital per $100 of assets with the 8–10 percent write-off seen at the height of most crises.
**Trade-offs**: the 8–10 percent figure is stated without a source.

## Seven-year deleveraging clock
**When to use**: setting the horizon after an LDR peak (ch03).
**How**: date the peak. Expect a return to about 0.75 roughly seven years later (Asia from 1997, the United States from 2007).
**Trade-offs**: two cases. Southern Europe is described as more extreme with no duration given.

## Lender versus borrower positioning (directional only)
**When to use**: ranking country assets inside a wholesale-funding relationship (ch03).
**How**: identify the low-LDR lender and the high-LDR borrower. Schulte says lender assets "almost always do better", most of all with the borrower at 1.2–1.4.
**Trade-offs**: borrower assets accelerate between 1.0 and 1.3 first, so an early exit loses money for a while.

## Bank equity as early warning
**When to use**: checking a benign macro forecast (ch01).
**How**: watch for major bank shares peaking and falling. Citi led by two years and Lehman by 18 months.
**Trade-offs**: two examples, chosen after the event.

## Credit-to-politics chain
**When to use**: deriving political risk from a banking reading (ch03, ch05).
**How**: insolvency, stagnation, deficits, unemployment, anger, flight from parties, then government fall or repression. Bailouts speed it up.
**Trade-offs**: built from narrative cases (Soviet Union, Brazil, United States, United Kingdom, Europe).

## Public-debt add-on for a banking crisis
**When to use**: scenario work on sovereign debt (ch05).
**How**: add about 50 points of GDP to government debt for an average crisis (LDR to 1.2, back to 0.75). Add more where the LDR reached 1.5–1.7.
**Trade-offs**: the "more" is not quantified in this excerpt.
