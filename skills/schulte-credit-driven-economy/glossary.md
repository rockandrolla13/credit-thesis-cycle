# Glossary

Terms from Part One, Chapter 1 of Schulte's book. "Ch" numbers refer to this skill's chapter files.

**Asset/liability mismatch** — long-dated lending funded by sources that can leave, as with GSE mortgages funded by securities sold to foreign institutions (ch02)

**Backstop** — public support for a banking system whose capital buffer is smaller than its coming losses (ch04)

**Black swan event** — a crisis said to "come out of nowhere". Schulte says these are predictable products of the credit cycle (ch01)

**Combined trigger** — an LDR of about 1.3 together with tangible leverage of 30–40×, after which earnings stop mattering (ch04)

**Credit cycle** — the path of a banking system's LDR from about 0.5 up through 1.0 to an overshoot and back to about 0.75 (ch02, ch03)

**Danger zone** — an LDR of 1.2–1.3 (ch02)

**Debt-deflation loop** — shrinking debt lowers asset values and wages, raises real debt burdens and cuts spending, in a self-reinforcing sequence (ch04)

**Deleveraging** — the unwinding of leverage, by lower loans and higher savings (LDR) or lower assets and higher capital (tangible leverage) (ch01, ch03, ch04)

**DSGE model** — dynamic stochastic general equilibrium model, the framework Schulte says trained most economists and has no room for banks (ch01)

**Endgame** — an LDR of 1.2–1.4, where downgrades, scandals and joint foreign withdrawal tend to appear (ch02)

**Endogenous money demand** — a modelling choice that rules out any causal role for reserves and money, per the IMF paper quoted (ch01)

**Ever-greening** — repaying old loans with new debt (ch04)

**GIIPS / PIIGS** — Greece, Italy, Portugal, Spain and Ireland (ch03)

**Great Moderation** — the phrase attributed to Greenspan for the pre-crisis calm (ch01)

**Greenbook** — the Federal Reserve staff forecast (ch01)

**GSEs** — government-sponsored enterprises, here Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. Schulte adds them to the US LDR (ch02)

**Intangibles** — acquisition value above cash price carried on a bank's balance sheet. Schulte values them at zero (ch04)

**Loan/deposit ratio (LDR)** — bank loans divided by bank deposits, the gauge of how much savings has become credit (ch02)

**Moral suasion** — government pressure on a bank without ownership (ch05)

**Natural resting place** — the level a ratio returns to: LDR 1.0 with a zero current account, and tangible leverage near 20× (ch02, ch05)

**"Outcome" statement** — Schulte's name for the income statement, a derivative of credit trends (ch02)

**"The plumbing"** — Rajan's term for the lending machinery economists ignored (ch01)

**Pro-cyclical capital inflows** — foreign bank funding that amplifies booms and busts and does not balance them (ch02)

**Rule of 1 and 20** — maximum LDR of 1 and tangible leverage of 20 (ch05)

**Safe level** — an LDR of around 0.75, where deleveraging ends (ch03)

**Stealth rise in the LDR** — an LDR increase hidden in lenders that deposits do not fund (ch02)

**Subsidiarization** — limiting a bank's lending to the deposits it can raise in the same country (ch02)

**Systemically important institution** — a bank judged too important to fail, and so bailed out with public funds (ch04)

**Tangible leverage** — (assets − intangibles) ÷ (capital − intangibles) (ch04)

**TARP** — the US federal program used to recapitalise banks (ch01, ch04)

**Virtuous cycle** — at a low LDR, savings become loans and loans become deposits (ch02)

**Wholesale banking / wholesale funding** — domestic banks borrowing from foreign banks and institutions in place of local deposits (ch02, ch03, ch05)
