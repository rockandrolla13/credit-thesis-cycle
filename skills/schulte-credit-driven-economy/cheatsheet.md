# Cheatsheet

Decision aids from Part One, Chapter 1 only. Thresholds are the author's, quoted as stated.

## LDR decision table (ch02, ch03)

| LDR | Read it as | Author's expectation |
|---|---|---|
| ~0.5 | Cycle start | Four to six years of asset appreciation |
| ~0.75 | Safe level after a bust | Deleveraging is done |
| → 1.0 | Savings nearly used | Rates tighten slightly |
| 1.0 | Resting place and policy cap | Current account near zero |
| 1.0–1.2 | Foreign-funded late boom | "Glorious" ride, prices accelerate |
| 1.2–1.3 | Danger zone | Growth trouble, savings tapped out |
| 1.2–1.4 | Endgame | Downgrades, scandals, foreign exit, "disastrous" ride |
| 1.4 | Political trigger | Government "almost invariably" removed |
| 1.5–1.8 | Extreme (Spain 1.7, southern Europe 1.8) | Depression, outsized public-debt rise |

## Tangible leverage decision table (ch04, ch05)

| Leverage | Read it as |
|---|---|
| 10× | Healthy ($10 capital per $100 assets) |
| <15× | Where US banks ended after recapitalisation |
| ~20× | Resting place. Bank share prices peak and fall above it. |
| ~30× | Dangerous. $3 buffer against 8–10% write-offs. |
| 30–40× | Trigger zone when LDR is ~1.3 |
| 50× | "Suicidal" ($2 per $100) |

## Decision rules
- When LDR is ~1.3 and tangible leverage is 30–40×, ignore earnings. Expect a break within months and a new government within ~24 months (ch04).
- When LDR is above 1, find the foreign funder and ask how fast it can leave (ch02).
- When a lender is funded by securities or wholesale markets, add it to the LDR (ch02).
- When a country's rate structure drops suddenly, expect the LDR to overshoot (ch02).
- When official forecasts and credit-market prices disagree, weight the credit prices (ch01).
- When major bank shares fall while forecasts stay benign, treat it as an early warning (ch01).
- When an LDR peaks, plan for about seven years of deleveraging (ch03).
- When a banking crisis hits, add ~50 points of GDP to public debt (ch05).
- When capital per $100 of assets is below 8–10, expect a public backstop (ch04).

## Which way does the system deleverage? (ch04, ch05)

| Route | Speed | Pain falls on | Case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raise capital | Fast | Shareholders, taxpayers, the government in office | US, TARP, below 15× |
| Cut or guarantee assets | Slow | Borrowers, growth, via deflation | Europe, ECB guarantees |
| Midpoint | Medium | Taxpayers and the bank | UK, RBS 80% stake |
| Lower the LDR (loans down, savings up) | ~7 years | Households | Asia after 1997 |

2009 arithmetic: from ~30×, reaching 20× needed ~$600bn of capital. Reaching 15× needed ~$1.5tn of capital or ~$22tn of asset removal.

## Tells
- Mortgage growth funded offshore (ch01).
- Inflation worries at the central bank while credit spreads are at records (ch01).
- Cement use per person far above peers (ch02).
- Foreign banks arriving late in the cycle (ch02).
- A national champion with heavy dollar debt (ch02).
- Asset prices accelerating with the LDR between 1.0 and 1.3 (ch03).
- Large intangibles from acquisitions on a bank balance sheet (ch04).
- Rights issues, business exits and regulatory fines at a universal bank (ch05).

## Caveats to carry
- The bands overlap: 1.2–1.3, 1.2–1.4, 1.3–1.4 and 1.4 all appear.
- Most claims are stated with proof deferred to chapters outside this excerpt.
