# Glossary: Capital Flows and Crises (Eichengreen)

Terms are defined as the book uses them. (Ch N) gives the chapter.

**Acceleration provisions** — bond clauses that let creditors demand immediate repayment after a rating downgrade (Ch 9)
**Adverse selection** — lenders who cannot judge quality pay an average price, so good borrowers withdraw and bad ones remain (Ch 11)
**Asset grab race** — creditors without faith in insolvency law rush to liquidate claims even when forbearance would serve them jointly (Ch 9)
**Baker Plan** — 1985 plan for $20 billion of new bank lending to 15 debtors, doubled multilateral lending and structural reform (Ch 2)
**Bangkok International Banking Facility (BIBF)** — Thai scheme giving tax and licensing favors to banks borrowing offshore and on-lending in foreign currency (Ch 9, Ch 11)
**Baring crisis** — the 1890 near-failure of Baring Brothers after an Argentine loan could not be placed (Ch 2, Ch 7)
**Bath formula** — a general ERM realignment against the Deutschmark in exchange for a German rate cut, proposed in September 1992 (Ch 8)
**Black market premium** — percent premium over the official exchange rate, used as the indicator of macroeconomic imbalance (Ch 4)
**Brady Plan** — 1989 plan using official funds to collateralize conversion bonds, which reopened bond-market lending (Ch 2)
**Capital account crisis** — a crisis in which a reversal of capital flows forces deficits to close at once (Ch 8)
**Carry trade** — borrowing in a low-rate center to hold higher-yielding assets in a pegged currency (Ch 2, Ch 8, Ch 9, Ch 11)
**Cedulas** — bearer mortgage bonds issued by Argentine national and provincial mortgage banks in the 1880s (Ch 7)
**Collective action clauses** — loan contract provisions that ease restructuring (Ch 11)
**Common-pool problem** — spending ministries ignore the effect of their own spending on the total deficit (Ch 10)
**Concerted (involuntary) lending** — lending just enough to keep a debtor out of arrears, organized by officials (Ch 2)
**Contagion** — a rise in the probability of a crisis at home caused by a crisis elsewhere, after controlling for fundamentals (Ch 1, Ch 6)
**Contractionary devaluation** — depreciation that lowers output because foreign-currency debt service rises (Ch 9)
**Convergence play** — the ERM version of the carry trade, betting that high-rate currencies converge on German rates (Ch 8)
**Corporation of Foreign Bondholders** — British creditors' committee that could bar defaulters from the London market (Ch 2)
**Countercyclical capital flows** — the pre-1914 pattern in which lending abroad rose when the lender's economy slowed (Ch 2)
**Crisis (currency)** — a quarter in which the exchange market pressure index exceeds its mean by a set number of standard deviations (Ch 5, Ch 6)
**Current account crisis** — a crisis caused by deficits that can no longer be financed (Ch 8)
**Disaster myopia** — Guttentag and Herring's term for 1970s bank overlending (Ch 2)
**Event (exchange market)** — an officially declared devaluation, revaluation, flotation, fixing or other regime change (Ch 5)
**Exchange market pressure index (EMP)** — weighted sum of exchange rate change, interest differential change and reserve loss, relative to Germany (Ch 5, Ch 6)
**Exchange rate based stabilization** — ending inflation by pegging, which builds in overvaluation (Ch 8)
**Exit strategy problem** — the difficulty of leaving a peg that carries anti-inflation credibility (Ch 8)
**Failed attack (successful defense)** — a crisis that does not coincide with an event (Ch 5)
**Fail-safe econometrics** — Frankel's phrase for hypotheses framed so that insignificance counts as support (Ch 10)
**First-generation model** — Krugman (1979): fiscal and monetary excess exhausts reserves (Ch 5, Ch 8)
**Focal point** — a prominent event that coordinates speculators (Ch 5)
**Free Banking Law (1887)** — Argentine law letting banks issue notes against government bonds bought with foreign loans (Ch 7)
**Gold premium** — the price of gold in paper pesos (Ch 7)
**Guilt by association** — contagion to countries that look similar, such as Portugal with Spain (Ch 1, Ch 8)
**Holding-period tax** — Chile's unremunerated deposit on capital inflows (Ch 3, Ch 11)
**Hot money** — interwar flight capital (Ch 2)
**Importer of last resort** — Kindleberger's term for a creditor that keeps its market open to distress goods (Ch 2)
**Inflation targeting** — the monetary anchor adopted by the United Kingdom and Sweden after floating (Ch 8, Ch 11)
**Information cascade** — investors inferring value from each other's trades (Ch 6, Ch 10, Ch 11)
**Kuznets Cycle** — long swing in which migration, infrastructure investment and capital inflow reinforce each other (Ch 2, Ch 7)
**Liquidity transformation** — funding illiquid assets with liquid liabilities (Ch 9, Ch 10)
**MERM weights** — IMF Multilateral Exchange Rate Model trade weights (Ch 6)
**Messy middle** — the position that liberalization is neither always good nor always bad (Ch 11)
**Monsoonal effects** — Masson's term for common global shocks (Ch 1)
**Moral hazard** — risk-taking induced by guarantees or by the lender bearing losses (Ch 2, Ch 7, Ch 9, Ch 11)
**Old EMS / New EMS** — the flexible system before 1987 and the rigid one after (Ch 8)
**One-way bet** — a peg or narrow band that lets a speculator risk little (Ch 8, Ch 11)
**Policy contagion** — countries liberalize when their peers do (Ch 3)
**Push factors** — center-country conditions that drive flows to the periphery (Ch 7)
**Quinn index** — coded intensity of capital account restrictions, 0 to 4 (Ch 3, Ch 4)
**Sachs-Warner openness** — dummy equal to one if none of five trade and macro distortions is present (Ch 4)
**Scramble for cover** — unhedged borrowers buying foreign exchange as the rate falls (Ch 9)
**Second-generation model** — the attack itself induces the policy change that validates it (Ch 5, Ch 8)
**Shadow exchange rate** — the rate that would prevail if the peg were abandoned (Ch 5)
**Sovereign ceiling** — no issuer rated above its sovereign (Ch 9)
**Sterilized intervention** — reserve sales offset so that money growth is unchanged (Ch 5)
**Tequila Effect** — spillover from Mexico's 1994-1995 crisis (Ch 6, Ch 7)
**Tesobonos** — Mexican dollar-indexed short-term government debt (Ch 7, Ch 9)
**Tranquility** — periods with no event and no crisis, the control group (Ch 5, Ch 6)
**Twin crisis** — a banking crisis and a currency crisis together (Ch 1, Ch 2)
**Wake-up call hypothesis** — Goldstein's term for a crisis that alerts investors to problems elsewhere (Ch 9)
**Zone of vulnerability** — the state in which a government cannot bear the interest rates a currency defense needs (Ch 8, Ch 9)
