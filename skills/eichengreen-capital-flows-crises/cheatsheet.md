# Cheatsheet: Capital Flows and Crises (Eichengreen)

## Decision rules
- When a lending boom is under way, ask if trade is growing faster than output and if exports can reach the creditor's market. If not, expect servicing trouble, because borrowers cannot earn the foreign exchange (Ch 2).
- When center-country interest rates turn up, expect inflows to the periphery to fall first. Every boom in the book ended this way (Ch 2, Ch 7).
- When a country defends a peg with sterilized intervention alone, expect a full crisis. Unsterilized tightening is what separates an orderly devaluation or a successful defense (Ch 5).
- When judging a peg, ask if the government can bear high interest rates. High unemployment, weak banks, short-term debt or indexed mortgages mean it cannot (Ch 8, Ch 9).
- When a government converts local debt into foreign-currency-linked debt under pressure, raise the worst case from devaluation to default (Ch 7).
- When someone cites a growth effect of capital account opening, ask which openness measure, which period and which estimator. The result is robust only for 1988-1992 and only with the Quinn index (Ch 4).
- When macro imbalances remain (large black market premium), treat opening the capital account as likely to hurt as to help (Ch 4).
- When a crisis hits a neighbor, weight the spillover by trade and third-market competition first, then by similarity (Ch 6).
- When fiscal stimulus is proposed in a crisis, ask if investors expect monetization. If yes, stimulus weakens the currency (Ch 10).

## Which regime with an open capital account
| Option | Verdict | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Adjustable peg, band, crawling peg, target zone | Not viable. One-way bet (Ch 5, Ch 8, Ch 11) | Capital controls to survive |
| Hard peg, currency board, dollarization, monetary union | Viable | Fiscal discipline, strong banks, legal entrenchment |
| Float | Viable | Independent central bank, inflation target, sound fiscal institutions |

## Liberalization order (Ch 11)
1. Stabilize macro policy and reform domestic finance (recapitalize, supervise, end blanket guarantees).
2. FDI, including foreign banks.
3. Stock and bond markets.
4. Offshore bank borrowing, last.
Throughout: price-based inflow taxes, not administrative controls.

## Controls: which kind
| Type | Signal | Evidence cited |
|---|---|---|
| Inflow tax (Chile) | Prudential | Shifts maturity, not volume. Lowers currency crisis risk in Rossi 1999 (Ch 3) |
| Outflow controls | Desperation | Raise currency and banking crisis risk in Rossi 1999. Leak in a panic (Ch 3, Ch 11) |

## Thresholds and numbers the book commits to
- Crisis flag: EMP above mean by 1.5 sd (Ch 6) or 2 sd (Ch 5).
- Contagion: a crisis elsewhere adds about 8 percentage points to crisis probability. EMS sub-sample: 19.9 (Ch 6).
- Sachs-Warner cut-offs: tariffs 40%, nontariff barrier coverage 40%, black market premium 20% (Ch 4).
- Chile: 30% non-interest-bearing deposit for one year (Ch 11).
- Basel 1988: 8% weighted capital (Ch 11).
- Growth change around a currency crisis, t-1 to t+1: emerging markets -3.34 points, OECD 0.00, EMS 1992 -1.63 (Ch 8).
- Crisis frequency: about twice the pre-1914 rate. Severity: similar (Ch 2).
- Short-term debt to reserves, Q2 1997: Korea 198%, Indonesia 168%, Thailand 145% (Ch 9).
- US bank claims on major debtors, 1982: 255% of capital (Ch 2).

## Tells
- Small investors arrive as institutions leave (Ch 7).
- New borrowing falls below debt service (Ch 7).
- Bank foreign liabilities jump as a share of GDP (Thailand 5.9% to 28.4%, 1992 to 1995) (Ch 9).
- State or development banks borrow abroad off the budget (Ch 7).
- A long-stable peg with unhedged private foreign debt (Ch 9).
- Official reviewers "pass" loans and the public reads that as endorsement (Ch 2).
- No warning in spreads, ratings or IMF outlooks does not mean no risk (Ch 8, Ch 9).

## What not to rely on
- Leading indicators for timing (Ch 5).
- Regime-change prediction: flotations look like tranquility (Ch 5).
- Reserves as insurance against a run on M2 (Ch 11).
- IMF on/off control dummies as an openness measure (Ch 3, Ch 4).
