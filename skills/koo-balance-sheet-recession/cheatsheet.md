# Cheatsheet

Decision aid for the excerpt (Chapter 7 of Koo, *The Holy Grail of Macroeconomics*). Chapter references are to this skill's files.

## Decision rules

| When | Do | Because | Ch |
|---|---|---|---|
| A banking crisis is localized and loan demand is normal | Dispose of bad loans quickly, pursue accountability | Buyers exist; this is the only case that rewards speed | 1 |
| It is systemic and loan demand is normal | Dispose slowly, give banks a fat spread | Fire sales destroy value; margin rebuilds capital without public funds | 1 |
| It is localized and loan demand is absent | Dispose at a normal pace, no taxpayer money | Bad loans are not the main brake on growth | 1 |
| It is systemic and loan demand is absent | Dispose slowly, inject government capital | Fat spread cannot work without borrowers | 1 |
| No private bidder appears for a failed bank | Treat the crisis as systemic; expect nationalisation | All institutions are impaired | 1 |
| Designing a capital injection | Keep conditions light; leave reform to regulators | Strict terms: zero applicants in Japan, early 1998 | 1 |
| Rate cuts follow a burst bubble | Check prices against DCF value first | Prices ignore cuts until they reach DCF value | 1 |
| Choosing a fiscal tool in a balance sheet recession | Prefer government spending to tax cuts | Tax cuts are saved or repay debt | 1 |
| Timing fiscal stimulus | Run it seamlessly until balance sheets are repaired | On-off stimulus: at least ¥100 trillion extra deficit, up to five extra years | 1 |
| Money-to-GDP looks stable in a boom | Check deposit turnover | Asset trades are outside GDP | 2 |
| Sizing a bubble's aftermath | Ask who financed it | Cash-funded: little damage. Bank or corporate-funded: balance sheet recession | 2 |
| A member of a currency union is in a balance sheet recession | Exempt it from the deficit cap and require stimulus | Otherwise it exports the adjustment | 3 |
| Several countries are in balance sheet recessions | Each absorbs its own excess saving fiscally; no devaluation race | Avoids a global fallacy of composition | 4 |

## Staging a post-bubble economy (Ch 1)

- Is weakness still described as temporary and local? → **Stage 1, denial**
- Are households saving more and banks foreclosing, with prices snowballing? → **Stage 2**
- Are banks cutting lending to lift capital ratios? → **Stage 3**
- Has public pain overcome bailout aversion?
  - Yes → **Stage 4**: capital injection plus fiscal stimulus; economy stabilises
  - No → the credit crunch continues
- After stage 4: recovery is not self-sustaining until balance sheets are repaired and debt rejection ends.

## Tells and smells

- Long rates flat while the central bank hikes → repaired borrowers are not borrowing. (Ch 1)
- Lenders chasing yield into a small, untested borrower pool → standards are about to collapse. (Ch 1)
- Top-tier banks paying more for capital than weak borrowers pay for loans → severe systemic stress. (Ch 1)
- Central bank says "neutral bias" just after cutting → stage 1 denial. (Ch 1)
- New loan products with low initial payments → demand pulled forward from future years. (Ch 1)
- Futures price a multi-year decline → buyers wait, stretched owners walk away. (Ch 1)
- Stable velocity and stable consumer prices during an asset boom → false comfort. (Ch 2)
- High turnover read as "liquid, I can always get out" → churn illusion. (Ch 2)
- Record trade surplus with weak domestic demand, low wages and a deficit cap → adjustment exported to neighbours. (Ch 3)
- Economists urging a weaker currency for a balance sheet recession → global fallacy of composition risk. (Ch 4)
