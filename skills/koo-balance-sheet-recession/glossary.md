# Glossary

Terms from the excerpt "Ongoing Bubbles and Balance Sheet Recessions" (Chapter 7 of Koo, *The Holy Grail of Macroeconomics*). Chapter numbers refer to this skill's chapter files, which follow the excerpt's four numbered sections.

**Balance sheet recession** — the recession that follows a burst asset-price bubble, when damaged balance sheets make borrowers pay down debt and refuse new borrowing; the full definition sits in book chapters outside this excerpt (Ch 1)

**Balance sheet scare syndrome** — the 1993-era Japanese term for the defensive turn once losses reach balance sheets; referenced from the book's Chapter 1 (Ch 1)

**Behind the curve** — stimulus applied only after the previous dose expired and deflationary pressure had already weakened the economy (Ch 1)

**Beggar-thy-neighbour policy** — gaining demand at neighbours' expense; the author says Germany's intra-eurozone export drive is not strictly this because the currency is shared (Ch 3)

**Capital injection** — government funds placed in undercapitalised banks; the prescribed tool for a type (IV) crisis and the only thing that ends a capital-driven credit crunch (Ch 1)

**Churning** — the rapid rise in asset trading volume that accompanies a bubble and gives a false sense of liquidity (Ch 2)

**Code of behaviour** — the author's proposed agreed rule set for countries in a yin phase, to be administered by the IMF (Ch 4)

**Conditionality** — strings attached to a capital injection; if strict, banks refuse the money and cut lending instead (Ch 1)

**Corporate hesokuri** — undisclosed corporate funds played in the stock market (Ch 2)

**Credit crunch** — banks cutting lending to restore capital adequacy ratios after losses (Ch 1)

**DCF value** — discounted present value of an asset's future cash flows; the level post-bubble buyers wait for (Ch 1)

**Debt rejection syndrome** — borrowers staying unwilling to borrow even after balance sheets are repaired; stage 4 of the yin-yang cycle (Ch 1)

**Deposit turnover** — total withdrawals from the banking system divided by average demand deposits; the exhibits label it total debits over demand deposits (Ch 2)

**Exporting unemployment** — the author's description of Germany's export-led exit from its balance sheet recession (Ch 3)

**Fallacy of composition** — behaviour that is right for each actor and harmful in aggregate; applied to households, banks, eurozone members and nations (Ch 1, Ch 3, Ch 4)

**Fat spread** — cheap central bank funding with lending rates left high, so banks rebuild capital from margin; the type (II) remedy (Ch 1)

**Four types of banking crises** — the Exhibit 7-1 grid: localized or systemic, crossed with demand for funds present or absent (Ch 1)

**Hesokuri** — Japanese word for savings kept secret from family; losses on it go unspoken (Ch 2)

**Maastricht Treaty deficit cap** — budget deficits limited to 3 percent of GDP regardless of private saving (Ch 3)

**Market solution** — relying on private buyers for failed banks or bad assets; works only in type I and III crises (Ch 1)

**Micro-level tightening** — targeted curbs such as higher mortgage downpayments (Ch 2)

**Neg-am loan** — negative-amortization loan with low initial payments and sharply higher later ones (Ch 1)

**NPL** — nonperforming loan (Ch 1)

**Speed limits** — Volcker's 2001 proposal to cap the pace of Japanese banks' NPL write-offs (Ch 1)

**SWF** — sovereign wealth fund; source of capital for Citibank, UBS and others in 2007–08 (Ch 1)

**Teaser rate** — low initial rate on an adjustable-rate mortgage; its expiry triggered subprime defaults (Ch 1)

**Tobin tax** — the author's label for China's June 2007 transaction tax on share trading (Ch 2)

**Velocity of money** — nominal GDP divided by demand deposits, as defined in Exhibit 7-3 (Ch 2)

**Yang phase** — the ordinary phase with normal private demand for funds (Ch 1, Ch 3, Ch 4)

**Yin phase** — the phase with weak or absent private demand for funds; the balance sheet recession phase (Ch 1, Ch 3, Ch 4)

**Yin-yang cycle** — the book's cycle of bubble and balance sheet recession; this excerpt names only its stage 4 (Ch 1)
