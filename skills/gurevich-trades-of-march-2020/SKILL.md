## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

The book is a discretionary global macro manager's diary of March 2020 at HonTe Investments, with the positioning history from 2018. Use it for decision rules, regime tells and worked trades. The author says the result came from strategic positioning, not from a pandemic forecast.

### 1. Shield against Uncertainty (Intro)
Use when a crisis has variables you cannot judge. Exclude them. Keep the facts that hold on any path. His two for 2020: "Eventually, the pandemic would pass" and the policy response "would continue building until it overwhelmed any lack of liquidity". Hold only trades that pay on those facts, and size to survive the wait. Prefer "eventually" trades over trades with a deadline: his only clear losses in March were short-dated funding bets (EDH0, EDJ0).

### 2. Regime and cycle indicators (Ch 1, 2)
- **Inflation fear regime**: stocks start to respond to rates, falling on higher yields and on strong data. Then "a protracted sell-off in the bonds is self-limiting".
- **Late-cycle indicators** (4 of his 13 are in the book): shift from recovery skepticism to inflation fear; belief that the secular bond bull market is over; simultaneous weakness, then bottoming, in dollar and bonds; oil rallying after a dip. Low unemployment and rising wages "portend lower interest rates".
- **Fed script**: "The Fed always tightens till they break something." Six steps: overheating, hike; stocks aren't the economy; stocks are the economy; pause and resume; just one cut; low for longer.
- **Short hike-to-ease gap**: 1 to 18 months across eight cycles. Markets read it as a pause and are slow to price the cut.
- **Economic gravity**: extreme rate differentials between developed markets close. Trade the mean reversion and the currency that the differential supports.
- **ZIRP singularity**: any crisis or ordinary recession sends short rates toward zero.

### 3. Fed asymmetry trades (Ch 2, 4)
- The Fed surprises with eases, never hikes (last surprise hike 1994). Buy Fed Funds futures that price a 5% to 10% hike chance, or no ease: a **free lunch**. Black swan odds: 1% to 2% a month, about 100 bp.
- **Intermeeting-cut rule**: every intermeeting cut in the table (six) was followed by easing at the next meeting. After an emergency cut, buy the next contract immediately.
- The market "invariably underpriced the scope of the central bank's response".

### 4. Trade selection (Ch 1, 2, 5)
- **Dominance**: prefer the trade that wins in every scenario where the alternative wins, plus more (short AUD over short RMB). "If the dominant way to express a particular economic view appears to be an inferior trade, the underlying view should be reexamined."
- **Positive risk symmetry**: at least as much upside as downside. Never become a wing seller.
- **Options are the exception**: use them when the outcome range is wide and flat with a boundary (zero rates) and survival is at stake. Pair with a smaller outright core. If bought for the expiry level, hold to expiry. Known cost: right on direction, no profit.
- **Nascent dislocation**: sell what trades as if all is well, buy what is priced for Armageddon.
- **Bottom-fishing**: buy what will be higher in five years. A negatively correlated core book pays for this.

### 5. Sizing and execution (Ch 4, 5, 6)
- **Psychologically neutral positioning**: core size is where flat, up and down days are all fine. Core is well below maximum, "the most you can hold without fear of being stopped out by a violent correction".
- Do not overlay a long-horizon call with tight stops and technical entries. It "muddies" the risk profile and misses the move.
- Set add and profit levels in advance. Execute them, even through the night.
- **Forget the past**: only the price in front of you matters. A missed winner "is no better than entering a losing position".
- **Incremental reductions** at preset targets, with stops on the residual. Choose the exit method that stresses you least.
- **Weak opinions, strongly held**. A trade is "a bullet out of a gun". "Make a plan and stick to it."

### 6. Risk and drawdown (Ch 6, 7, 8)
- Portfolio risk management is rule-bound and "paramount". Position losses are "a matter of strategy". Stops suit trending assets only.
- In a drawdown, cut what is causing the loss. Selective cuts of minor positions backfire. Do not go flat by reflex.
- **Portfolio paralysis**: no capacity at the best prices. Portfolio stops are "perfectly mistimed" because everyone stops together.
- **Safe-asset leverage trap**: 10:1 on an assumed 5% loss becomes insolvency at 12%. Disruptions in safe instruments do the most damage.
- **Funding singularity**: after the day liquidity vanishes and policy answers (October 3, 2008; March 18, 2020), funding stress does not return even if stocks keep falling.

### 7. Crisis mechanics (Ch 4, 5, Concl)
- "Every crisis starts with fear and ends with necessity." Forced flows break normal causality.
- **Dollar in early easing**: it rises. Causality (foreign bond inflows) and concurrency (easing begins in a liquidity shortage). Gold is an excess-liquidity hedge, not a risk hedge; it gains from later Fed action.
- **Swap spreads are not apples to apples**: bond is funded principal lending; swap is a string of short rates. Inversion is a term-premium spike. Carry: receive bond yield and LIBOR, pay repo and swap.
- **A price is flow**: a 1% thirty-year breakeven or a record bond crash on dovish news signals forced sellers. Run the carry test and buy.
- **Beanie Baby Theory**: cash is an asset that can be expensive. "The origin of market fluctuations is liquidity." A short is a purchase of cash from a costless producer.

### Scorecard, in his words
Hits: FFH0, EDM0 and EDH0 calls, AUD short, TIPS asset swap, silver and gold, MXN, deferred oil, EURCHF (the "unsung hero"), swap spreads. Misses: April Fed Funds (too slow), late EDH0 and EDJ0 additions, Australian bonds, junior gold miners, the March municipal-swap spike, paying 30-year swaps near 0.40%. Details: ch04 to ch09.
