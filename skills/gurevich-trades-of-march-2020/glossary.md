# Glossary: The Trades of March 2020

Format: **Term** — definition (chapter). "Intro" = ch00, "Concl" = ch09.

**Accepting the noise** — taking a trade with a very good return profile plus some randomness: "Lose a little, or win a little, or win a lot." (Ch 2)
**Asset swap** — long a bond against paying fixed on a swap; isolates the bond's spread to swaps from rate direction. (Ch 4, 5, 8)
**AUD short** — his dominant expression of long USD; covered in increments in March 2020. (Ch 5, 6, 8)
**Beanie Baby Theory** — cash is an asset that can be cheap or expensive; moving to cash for safety is a fallacy for a manager. (Concl)
**Black swan carry** — his estimate of a 1% to 2% monthly chance of a surprise ease worth about 100 bp, giving at least 1 bp of value to a Fed Funds future. (Ch 2)
**Bottom-fishing** — buying panic-priced assets he expects to be higher in five years, without regard to near-term price. (Ch 4)
**Concurrent necessity** — B must happen whenever A happens; the test behind trade dominance. (Ch 1, 5)
**Core position** — the psychologically neutral size, well below maximum. (Ch 5)
**DEDZ** — European (Euro Stoxx 50) dividend futures. (Ch 4, 5)
**Dollar shortage** — March 2020 episode in which dollars "left circulation" and USD rose against all currencies. (Ch 5)
**Dominance** — one trade wins in every scenario where another wins, plus others; prefer it. (Ch 1, 5)
**Double whammy** — lose on the move against you, then lose to option decay when proven right. (Ch 1)
**EDH0 / EDJ0 / EDM0** — March, April and June 2020 Eurodollar futures. EDM0 is "the most fateful futures contract" of his career. (Ch 1, 2, 4 to 7, Concl)
**Economic gravity** — rate differentials between developed markets pull capital and currencies until they close. (Ch 1, 4)
**Eurodollar future** — $1mm contract on a three-month deposit rate; price = 100 minus rate; 1 bp = $25. (Ch 1)
**FFH0** — March 2020 Fed Funds future; "the second most fateful contract". (Ch 2, 4, 5)
**Fade the tightening** — buy Fed Funds futures that price a small chance of a hike before a meeting. (Ch 2)
**Fear, then necessity** — "Every crisis starts with fear and ends with necessity": voluntary selling becomes forced selling. (Ch 4)
**Free lunch** — big upside if an unlikely event occurs, at no price for the option. (Ch 2)
**Funding singularity** — the day liquidity vanishes, after which funding stress does not return: October 3, 2008; March 18, 2020. (Ch 6)
**HonTe** — "true move" in Go; a patient strategy that looks slow and wins long term. The firm's name. (Intro)
**Incremental reductions** — taking profit in pieces at preset levels. (Ch 6)
**Inflation breakeven** — nominal yield minus TIPS real yield. (Ch 5, 8)
**Inflation fear regime** — late-cycle state in which stocks fall on higher rates and strong data. (Ch 1)
**Intermeeting cut rule** — every intermeeting cut in his career was followed by easing at the next meeting. (Ch 4)
**Invoice spread** — futures-based proxy for the swap spread. (Ch 4)
**Involuntary flows** — forced transactions that break efficient-market and behavioral assumptions. (Ch 4, 5)
**LIBOR-OIS spread** — term premium of three-month LIBOR over projected overnight rates; normally about 20 bp. (Ch 4)
**Long gamma** — option exposure that grows as the market moves in your favor. (Ch 3)
**Magic Sword of Necessity** — his trade-selection method from *The Next Perfect Trade*. (Intro, Concl)
**Maximum position** — the most you can hold without fear of being stopped out by a violent correction. (Ch 5)
**MUNIPSA (BMA)** — municipal swap index; ratio to LIBOR implies a marginal tax rate. (Ch 5, 8)
**Nascent dislocation** — the gap between assets priced for normality and assets priced for Armageddon. (Ch 4)
**On-the-run / off-the-run** — newest versus older Treasury issues. (Ch 5)
**The One Trade** — long US long bonds. (Ch 1, 4)
**Payer swaption** — option that gains if swap rates rise. (Ch 4, 5)
**Portfolio paralysis** — no risk capacity while opportunities appear; usually precedes a portfolio stop. (Ch 6)
**Portfolio risk management** — rules on total risk and drawdown; "paramount". (Ch 7)
**Portfolio stop** — forced liquidation at the portfolio level; "perfectly mistimed". (Ch 6)
**Position risk management** — how much to lose on one trade; a matter of strategy. (Ch 7)
**Positive risk symmetry** — at least as much upside as downside on every trade. (Ch 2)
**Psychologically neutral positioning** — size at which any day's price action is acceptable. (Ch 5)
**Repo** — overnight collateralized borrowing for bonds; cost unbounded in a panic. (Ch 5)
**Roll-adjusted chart** — futures chart including roll costs and carry; the true economics. (Ch 1, 6)
**Shield against Uncertainty** — trade only what must be true eventually; size to survive the wait. (Intro)
**Swap spread** — swap rate minus Treasury yield; negative at the long end since 2015. (Ch 4, 5)
**TIPS** — Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities. (Ch 5, 7, 8)
**TNPT** — *The Next Perfect Trade: A Magic Sword of Necessity*, his first book. (Intro)
**Weak opinions, strongly held** — no forecast conviction, firm adherence to a well-chosen trade. (Ch 6)
**White hole** — central bank liquidity after the singularity. (Ch 6, 7, Concl)
**Wing selling** — collecting premium on rare outcomes at the risk of ruin. (Ch 2)
**XMA / XMM0** — Australian ten-year bond future. (Ch 6, 8)
**ZIRP singularity** — the end state of any crisis or recession for short rates. (Ch 4)
