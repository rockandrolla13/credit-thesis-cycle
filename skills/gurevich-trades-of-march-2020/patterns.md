# Patterns: The Trades of March 2020

Techniques as the author states and uses them. Chapter in brackets.

## Two-Fact Reduction (Shield against Uncertainty) [Intro]
**When to use**: a crisis with many variables you cannot judge.
**How**: strike every variable you lack expert knowledge of. Keep what must be true eventually. Hold only trades that pay on those facts. Size to survive the wait.
**Trade-offs**: no timing. The wait is "long and painful". Fails if the trade has an expiry or the size cannot survive.

## Late-Cycle Regime Read [Ch 1]
**When to use**: dating the end of an expansion.
**How**: look for stocks falling on higher rates and on strong data; a belief that the bond bull market is over; dollar and bonds weak together and then bottoming; oil rallying after a dip. Add the Fed's six-step script (Ch 2).
**Trade-offs**: direction only. He sat in a severe drawdown in 2018 before it paid. Only 4 of his 13 indicators are in the book.

## Flat-Distribution Long-Dated Option [Ch 1, 2, Concl]
**When to use**: a wide, roughly uniform range of outcomes with a boundary on one side (zero rates), priced off a bell curve, and a portfolio that must survive being early.
**How**: buy far out-of-the-money options at the boundary side (EDM0 98.375 calls at 6 bp). Keep a smaller outright core. Hold to expiry if bought on a view of the expiry level.
**Trade-offs**: decay; you can be right on direction and lose. If you foresee the price freezing just past the strike, exit vega then.

## Free Option on a Surprise Ease [Ch 2]
**When to use**: a Fed Funds future pricing no easing, or pricing a 5% to 10% chance of a hike just before a meeting.
**How**: buy the future. Stay on the bid as technical noise (IOER) moves it. The Fed surprises with eases, never hikes.
**Trade-offs**: small mark-to-market noise. Payoff needs a shock that arrives 1% to 2% of months.

## Follow-Up Ease After an Intermeeting Cut [Ch 4]
**When to use**: minutes after an emergency cut, when the next contract prices out the scheduled meeting.
**How**: buy the next-month Fed Funds future at once. Ignore where it traded seconds ago.
**Trade-offs**: the window was minutes. He missed it by waiting about fifteen.

## Nascent Dislocation Scan [Ch 4]
**When to use**: rapid, uneven repricing.
**How**: sort assets into those trading "as if everything were going great" and those "priced for Armageddon". Sell the first, buy the second. Example: US Treasuries rich, Canadian bonds lagging.
**Trade-offs**: needs trading access in the right time zone and capacity.

## Outright to Spread Rotation [Ch 4]
**When to use**: a long bond position is extended in price but cheap to swaps.
**How**: pay fixed on swaps instead of selling the bonds. One trade cuts rate risk and adds spread risk.
**Trade-offs**: operationally heavy (margin, swap futures liquidity). Spreads went more negative before recovering.

## Take the Offset Leg First [Ch 4]
**When to use**: the main leg has paid and its natural offset has not yet lost ("having my cake and eating it too").
**How**: take profit on the offset before the market catches up. He cut USD longs after rates paid.
**Trade-offs**: gives up further gains if the offset keeps working.

## Core and Maximum Sizing [Ch 5]
**When to use**: accumulating a long-horizon position in a falling market.
**How**: hold core, the size at which flat, up and down days are all acceptable. Keep it well below maximum. Add on weakness at preset levels. Do not wait for a catalyst or a pullback.
**Trade-offs**: mark-to-market pain; ties up capital and margin. He ran out of capacity on March 18.

## Dominant Expression Test [Ch 1, 5]
**When to use**: several ways to express one view.
**How**: pick the trade that wins in every scenario where the alternative wins and in some others. Short AUD over short RMB. If the dominant expression looks like a bad trade, re-examine the view.
**Trade-offs**: he missed Australian bonds in 2018 by applying it incompletely.

## Incremental Exit with Residual Stops [Ch 6]
**When to use**: a trend trade reaching targets fast.
**How**: place unwind orders at each level (AUD at every figure). Keep a residual. Put stop-buys on the residual in tranches (0.60 for half, 0.65 for the rest).
**Trade-offs**: always some regret in one direction. He holds that reasonable exit methods have similar expectation and picks the least stressful.

## Cut What Is Hurting [Ch 6]
**When to use**: an untenable drawdown under risk limits.
**How**: reduce the positions causing the loss, not the minor ones. Give priority to short-dated bets that can lose permanently.
**Trade-offs**: you sell what you believe in most. Do not go flat by reflex.

## Carry Test on a Dislocated Safe Asset [Ch 5, 8]
**When to use**: a government bond trading far from swaps or from its own curve.
**How**: write out what you pay and receive. TIPS asset swap: pay repo + 0.92%, receive LIBOR + inflation. State the outcome that loses money (inflation far below 1%). Pick the issue cheapest to its curve.
**Trade-offs**: needs funding and balance sheet. The price can fall much further first (28 bp low against his 92 bp entry).

## Deferred Contract for Normalization [Ch 7, 8]
**When to use**: a spot shock is priced far out the curve.
**How**: buy the long-dated contract (WTI below $40, June 2021) against an equilibrium view (at least $60).
**Trade-offs**: avoids spot delivery mechanics, as in May 2020 WTI. Requires patience.

## Buy the Scare in Backwardated Dividends [Ch 5]
**When to use**: a crisis pushes deferred dividend futures to "Forever Armageddon" prices.
**How**: wait for the scare, load up, then earn carry and roll.
**Trade-offs**: thin liquidity and one-way flows; vicious corrections; policy risk to dividends.

## Cash-Is-an-Asset Test [Concl]
**When to use**: judging a move to cash or a short.
**How**: replace "cash" with "beanie babies" in the statement. Ask whether cash is cheap or expensive now, and whether the liquidity shortage is transitory.
**Trade-offs**: a view on liquidity, not on fundamentals. Timing of the supply response still matters.
