# Chapter 6: Week 3: The Dark Days (March 15 to 20, 2020)

## Core Idea
A 100 bp emergency cut on Sunday March 15 did not stop the funding squeeze. LIBOR kept rising, Treasuries and stocks fell together, margin calls forced collateral sales, and on Wednesday March 18 the fund hit its risk limits and could not buy the best prices of the year. This is the chapter on drawdown rules: what to cut, when not to get flat, and why to follow a plan made in calm conditions.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Weak opinions, strongly held**: his inversion of "strong opinions, lightly held" (Paul Saffo). He is "agnostic about specific market or economic forecasts" and has "no conviction or even opinion on the time horizon or circumstances" of a trade's success. He holds the trade because he judges "the odds to be in favor of its eventual success".
- **Bullet out of a gun**: "You think, evaluate, and aim very carefully before taking the shot, but once you've pulled the trigger, the trajectory is no longer within your control."
- **Make a plan and stick to it**: "The victory is forged behind the lines." The thought process during research "is inherently superior to what you may come up with in the middle of a market commotion".
  - Applies to winners too. A stock bought at $100 with a $150 target reaches $150 on good earnings. Do not stretch to $180: "you no longer have the advantage of your original analysis".
- **Incremental reductions at preset targets**: reduce in pieces as levels are reached, in both directions of regret.
  - Why: "all reasonable exit strategies have similar mathematical expectation, so I apply the one most suited to my psychological makeup."
  - Principle: "all else being equal, I choose the approach that stresses me least." It "may be worth sacrificing incremental value in the market for the option value of guaranteeing your clear thinking through future market events".
- **Cut what is giving you trouble**: in an untenable drawdown, selective reduction "typically backfired". The positions he believed in most and held largest were the ones hurting, and they looked better value as they fell ("if you like a stock at $30, you would love it at $20, right?"). The minor positions he cut went on to perform.
- **Critique of the maxim to cut your losses and let your wins run**: the maxim rests on two separate ideas, trend persistence and the poker point that you decide better when winning. Both have wisdom; neither is universal.
  - When the overall portfolio is doing well: cutting a loser "is not a requirement"; decide trade by trade.
  - In a drawdown: risk parameters "may mandate broad risk reductions", but "getting flat to regroup doesn't have to be warranted". Getting flat is itself a decision, made when you are impaired.
  - "Often, reducing positions that go against you is the opposite of what you should be doing", because some asset classes do not trend.
- **Portfolio paralysis and portfolio stops** (TNPT): paralysis is "the anticipation leading up to a portfolio stop", when opportunities appear and you have no capacity. Stops are "always, in my experience, perfectly mistimed": forced stops hit many managers at once, and "when everyone is done stopping out, the market starts to normalize".
- **Funding singularity**: the day liquidity vanishes and after which funding pressure does not return, though stocks may fall much further. GFC precedent: Friday October 3, 2008, then TARP; stocks bottomed March 2009. In 2020 he places it on Wednesday March 18.
  - Use: once the liquidity problem is resolved, build risk positions as entry points improve.
- **Collateral in medium-term notes**: he holds margin reserves in six-to-seven-year Treasury notes, not bills, and manages duration with swaps. This expresses long bonds versus swaps with better cash flow. Cost: the notes must be sold in a hurry when margin rises.

## Key Concepts
- **EDH0 settlement mismatch**: the contract settled on LIBOR published Monday March 16 at 11:55 a.m. London time. The scheduled FOMC was Wednesday March 18. His options would expire before a scheduled ease could be priced.
- **Portfolio collateral**: investor cash is mostly margin for leveraged instruments; most of it sits as reserve.
- **Risk parity defiance**: bonds and stocks falling together, so bonds do not cushion equity losses.
- **Yield curve control**: the RBA capped the three-year sovereign yield at 0.25% on March 18.
- **XMM0**: Australian bond future (front contract then). **CN / TY**: Canadian and US ten-year note futures.
- **Roll-adjusted (true economic) price**: includes carry and cross-currency basis; used to judge the yen trade.
- **Unsung hero**: a position that fails to lose when it should have. "Not enough attention is given to failures to make money, nor to narrow escapes from losses."

## Mental Models
- Think of an emergency cut as psychology first: "it takes time for liquidity to 'seep' into the market and even longer to affect the real economy."
- Think of a record low in a sovereign bond on good policy news as order flow, not information: "a couple of forced sellers and all buyers out of oxygen".
- If you are out of risk capacity, assume most other risk players are too.
- "Hope" is "an ugly word in the world of investments".
- Trade in a style that fits your own psychology. "It is useless to deny your own emotional biases."

## Anti-patterns
- **Missing a contract technicality**: he had not thought through the EDH0 settlement date when buying the options.
- **Fading a funding move with short-dated contracts**: buying March and April Eurodollars "were only causing more aggravation".
- **Selective reduction in a drawdown**: keeping the largest, most-loved losers.
- **Stretching a target after the news confirms the thesis**.
- **Getting flat by reflex**: a possible "source of many macro portfolios' underperformance".
- **"Be nimble" as a default**: re-deciding mid-commotion replaces a good analysis with a worse one.

## Worked Example
**A. The AUD short: "the true move of our most successful repositioning of 2020".**
- Built in 2019 as the dominant expression of long USD (Ch 5).
- March 18: AUD fell so fast he placed unwind orders "at every figure (59, 58, 57, 56, 55…)".
- He covered most of the short when AUD was down about 20% on the year. A single profit level would have taken him out too early.
- Residual: held a few more weeks. When the dollar reversed he set stop-buys at 0.60 for 50% of the residual and 0.65 for the rest. Both triggered quickly.
- Aftermath: AUD "ended positively for the year".

**B. EDH0 and EDJ0: the deadline trade.**
- The Sunday cut arrived "only hours ahead of the LIBOR reset". On paper it was probably "the greatest gain in my career in absolute dollars"; it faded by Monday, mainly on precious metals.
- LIBOR did not come down. By Tuesday EDJ0 "was starting to give us trouble".
- Thursday March 19: "I sounded full-on retreat in April Eurodollars." Reasons: 2007 experience; a short-term bet threatens "unrecoverable loss, even if I would eventually be proven correct about funding"; and the rule to cut what is giving trouble.
- Friday March 20: used the relief bounce in EDJ0 to reduce further, on Qin's point that risk measures were still high.

**C. Ending the yen trade.**
- Long USDJPY since 2010; "USDJPY will touch 125 before 75" when it was near 81-82. Most profit taken in 2015 at the target; a small residual kept; rebuilt modestly near 100 after Brexit and the 2016 election.
- March 19, 2020: closed. With dollar rates collapsing, "the dollar rally was transient". USDJPY never revisited 120, but on a roll-adjusted basis "the true economic price was, in fact, very close" to the 2015 peak.

**D. Other actions.**
- Missed: Australian bond futures on March 15 (RBA verbal easing moved the price first) and again on March 18 ("tapped out on risk").
- Took profit on long USDCAD after the Bank of Canada moved; bought Canadian bond futures in thin markets.
- Bought BRL, TRY and MXN as long-term holds; took profit on short KRW. He wishes he had unwound Asian shorts "more decisively".
- March 18 plan: use the steepening to take profit on shorter Treasuries and shift risk into long-dated TIPS.
- EURCHF long (accumulated between 1.06 and 1.07): lost "less than 1 percent" by late March. "A huge success" for a risk-on position in a crisis.
- Not being long the long bond any more "helped us survive" March 17.

**State**: still up on the year, after "our possibly largest ever intra-month swing", from a record month to "one of my worst months ever".

## Key Takeaways
1. Check settlement mechanics against the event you are betting on.
2. Reserve capacity is the scarce asset at the bottom. He was right on Australian bonds and could not buy them.
3. In a drawdown, cut the position causing the loss, not the ones you like least.
4. Do not get flat by reflex. Follow risk limits, then keep the plan.
5. Take profit in increments at preset levels and place stops on the residual.
6. A funding bet with an expiry can lose permanently even when the thesis is right.
7. Identify the funding singularity. After it, liquidity risk is gone even if equities keep falling.
8. Stress is a risk factor. Choose methods that protect clear thinking.
