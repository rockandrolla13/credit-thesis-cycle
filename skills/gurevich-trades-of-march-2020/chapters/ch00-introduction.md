# Introduction: A Shield against Uncertainty

## Core Idea
In a crisis with many unknowable variables, do not bet on the variables you cannot judge. Reduce the thesis to the few facts you can hold with near certainty, hold trades that pay "eventually", and make survival the first objective. The chapter is short on trade detail; it sets the book's method and its evidence base (a trading log plus a Slack trading channel).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Shield against Uncertainty**: the author's name for his crisis method. He excluded every variable "of which I didn't have expert understanding" and built the book on two facts only.
  - When to use: a regime break where short-term outcomes are "virtually impossible" to bet on.
  - How:
    1. List the variables driving the event. In March 2020 these were the epidemiology of COVID-19, the effectiveness of mitigation, vaccine timelines and the details of the policy response.
    2. Strike out every variable you have no expert edge on. He struck out all four.
    3. Keep the facts that survive. His two: (a) "Eventually, the pandemic would pass." (b) "The monetary and fiscal policy response would continue building until it overwhelmed any lack of liquidity."
    4. Hold only trades that capitalize on those facts, and size them so that price swings cannot remove you before "eventually" arrives.
  - Why it works / failure mode: it removes the need to forecast timing. Its cost is that "the wait was often long and painful". The method gives no protection if the position is too large to survive the wait.
- **Magic Sword of Necessity** (carried over from his first book, *The Next Perfect Trade*, "TNPT"): the tool for selecting trades "with odds skewed in my favor". The sword picks the trade; the shield keeps the portfolio alive while the trade works.
- **HonTe ("true move")**: the firm is named after a term from the game of Go. *Hon* = truth, *Te* = move. A true move "applies a patient strategy, which may appear slow but is the best at delivering long-term results".
- **Battle preparation** (quoted from TNPT): prepare the armor, shield and training first. "A magic formula ... will be wasted if you get killed by the market's first arrow."

## Key Concepts
- **HonTe Investments, LLC**: the author's firm, San Francisco. Several accounts are managed; the narrative reads as if it were one portfolio.
- **Trading channel transcript**: the firm's internal Slack trading channel for March 2020, published in full in Chapters 4 to 7. It is internal chatter, not official orders, and it is "neither official nor complete".
- **Trading log / daily P/L**: the record he reconciles his memory against. "The trading records and the daily P/L don't lie."
- **CIO burden**: the CIO's distinction is not more work or knowledge but that he "bear[s] the final burden of decision making".
- **New normal**: he expects the pandemic, like 9/11 and the GFC, to be "a brief but intense episode, followed by normalization to a somewhat altered world".

## Mental Models
- Use the two-fact reduction when you cannot rank short-term outcomes: ask what must be true eventually, whatever path is taken.
- Think of the portfolio as an army: the best weapon is worth nothing if the holder does not survive the first exchange.
- Treat your own memory of a crisis as unreliable. He prides himself on recall of price action, yet "the memories of March are not clear" and "my memories did turn out to be faulty". Reconstruct from logs and charts.
- A slow, honest move beats a clever fast one over a long horizon.

## Anti-patterns
- **Speculating outside your expertise**: he refused to bet on epidemiology, mitigation, vaccines or policy detail.
- **Betting on short-term outcomes in chaos**: called "virtually impossible" under unprecedented developments.
- **Self-flattering recall**: "I can no longer deceive myself with stories of being more prudent or more prescient than I actually was."

## Worked Example
The author's own stress episode is the book's opening datum. A few days after shelter-in-place was ordered in the Bay Area in March 2020, he collapsed on the bathroom floor with shortness of breath, palpitations, chills and sweating. He states it was stress, not COVID. He uses it to frame the book as both a strategy guide and a psychological one. He also discloses the outcome up front: "this is not a story of how we failed and lost a fortune"; "from a financial perspective, this thriller has a happy ending".

## Key Takeaways
1. Reduce a crisis thesis to the facts you can hold whatever the path. Trade those and nothing else.
2. Survival precedes edge. A shield is the precondition for using the sword.
3. A trade that "will work eventually" still needs sizing that survives a long, painful wait.
4. Keep a contemporaneous record of decisions. It is the only check on hindsight.
5. The book's evidence is one firm's internal chat and log. It is a single, narrow viewpoint, as the author says.
