# Chapter 8: Hits and Misses

## Core Idea
The author audits March 2020 asset class by asset class. "I got several right, but none perfectly right. There are many I missed entirely." The structural lesson is that the instruments deemed safest (Treasuries, TIPS, municipal paper, Australian government bonds) showed stock-like volatility, and that leverage built on an assumed small loss is what turns a price dislocation into insolvency.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three kinds of miss**: (1) "not within my mandate or expertise"; (2) "escaped my notice in the commotion"; (3) no chance to act "because my risk capital was tied up in other transactions". He does not blame himself for the first kind.
- **Safe-asset leverage trap**:
  - A long-only asset has limited downside: "Price can only go down to zero."
  - Assume bonds cannot lose more than 5%. Lever 10:1 (borrow nine times cash). Expected worst case: 10 × 5% = 50% of cash.
  - If bonds fall 12%: 10 × 12% = 120%. "You are insolvent."
  - Conclusion: "disruptions in instruments deemed safe are more detrimental to the stability of the financial market than disruptions in instruments already considered volatile." Bond portfolios built on an expectation of low volatility "can be the most vulnerable".
- **Price is flow**: "assets go down in price when there are more sellers than buyers. You can stand on the sidewalk shouting, 'The price is wrong!' all you want. The price will do what it will do."
- **TIPS asset swap carry test**: look at TIPS as the spread between the matching swap rate and the TIPS real yield.
  - Position: pay repo + 0.92%, receive LIBOR + inflation.
  - Loss condition: repo is almost always below LIBOR, so "average inflation would have to stay way below 1%".
  - Thesis link: policymakers "would keep adding liquidity until liquidity became excessive", so that outcome was "extremely unlikely".
  - Target: "what I considered to be the minimum economic value of 2.50%".
  - Scale: a 2% a year change over thirty years is "30 × 2% = 60% variance in cash flows", and near-zero rates give distant payments heavy weight.
- **Operating leverage in miners**: if it costs $1,000 to mine an ounce, gold moving from $1,200 to $2,000 moves profit per ounce from $200 to $1,000.
- **Pascal's wager on Bitcoin**: a very small allocation that "can easily be written off" protects against a collapse of fiat. His advice to investors who think that way: "do it yourself". A buy-and-hold position does not merit management and incentive fees.
- **Chasing a missed entry**: "Having missed the great entry and then chasing the bonds up led to a risk of being in a losing trade." This occurred twice (Australian bonds, junior gold miners).
- **All one can ask of a trend trade**: ride a significant position "without taking too much profit prematurely" and cover on the turn "without giving back too much profit".

## Key Concepts
- **XMA**: Australian ten-year bond future.
- **CLM1**: June 2021 WTI crude oil future.
- **GDXJ**: VanEck Vectors Junior Gold Miners ETF.
- **Real yield / breakeven / asset swap**: three ways to value TIPS; he prefers the asset swap for portfolio purposes.
- **Fastest (and shortest) bear market in history**: the equity sell-off into March 23.
- **Hard assets**: precious metals, supply "limited by the laws of physics". Digital assets "are assets and thus are subject to liquidity".
- **Second chance**: the 30-year municipal swap ratio re-spiked in May 2020 after the March shock.

## Mental Models
- Think of a "safe" asset's risk as duration times leverage. "It all depends on duration and leverage."
- Use liquidity, not fundamentals, to explain the joint crash and joint recovery of gold, silver, Bitcoin and equities.
- Treat precious metals "as a form of currency ... subject to the vagaries of dollar funding".
- Stay out of single stocks without expertise. He once considered Amazon puts as a recession hedge. The recession turned out to be "a tremendous windfall for the company". "Good thing I ... stayed away from such amateurish bets."
- A correction after a first leg is consistent with past gold bull markets "such as the one that started in 2003".

## Anti-patterns
- **Leverage sized to an assumed maximum loss** on a safe asset.
- **Arguing with the price** in a forced-flow market.
- **Entering late after missing the dislocation, in size** (XMA, GDXJ).
- **Amateur single-stock bets** outside a macro mandate.
- **Paying fees for buy-and-hold** (his point about Bitcoin).

## Key Takeaways
1. Rank the worst case of a "safe" asset by what leverage does to it, not by its normal volatility.
2. In a liquidity event, the cheapest prices appear in the safest instruments, because that is where the leverage is.
3. Value TIPS by the carry test: what you pay, what you receive, and the inflation rate at which you lose.
4. You do not need the low. An entry at 92 bp against a 28 bp low was still, in his words, a gift.
5. If you miss the dislocation, do not rebuild the trade in size at worse levels.
6. Classify misses. Only the ones inside your mandate deserve self-criticism.
7. Deferred contracts isolate the normalization view from spot delivery mechanics. The May 2020 WTI collapse "had nothing to do with deferred contracts".
