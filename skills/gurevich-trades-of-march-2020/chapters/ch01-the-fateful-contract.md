# Chapter 1: The Fateful Contract

## Core Idea
The March 2020 book was built in 2018. The author read early-2018 price action as a late-cycle "inflation fear regime", concluded that the bond sell-off was self-limiting, and converted part of his long-rates risk into deep out-of-the-money calls on the June 2020 Eurodollar future (EDM0) so that he could survive being early. Stocks are "only the tip of the iceberg"; the rates market underneath is where the size and the danger sit.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Inflation fear regime** (from TNPT, restated in the January 2018 Investor Letter): "When, due to inflation fears, rising rates become a concurrent necessity with respect to a strong stock market performance, the growth outlook becomes dim."
  - When to use: to date the end of an expansion.
  - How: watch whether stocks start to RESPOND to rates. The tell is stocks "going down when interest rates rise and even going down on stronger economic data, as it implies further rate hikes".
  - Implication: "a protracted sell-off in the bonds is self-limiting", because the stock weakness brings a slowdown and then "the prospect for much lower rates".
  - Why it works / failure mode: it is a reflexive loop from rates to equities to growth to rates. It gives direction, not timing. He was in "a very severe drawdown" before it paid.
- **Dominance corollary** ("chicken and egg logic", TNPT): "If the dominant way to express a particular economic view appears to be an inferior trade, the underlying view should be reexamined." In 2000, betting on higher stocks was dominated by betting on higher rates, and higher rates was inferior on the secular trend. So "betting on a strong economy was no longer a good idea".
- **Late business cycle indicators**: a market note from April 2018 cites "thirteen historical indicators (often counterintuitive)". The book reproduces four (see Reference Tables).
- **Economic gravity (rate differential)**: US yields cannot be judged alone. A wide rate differential makes USD cash attractive, pulls foreign money into the United States, lifts the dollar, puts "deflationary pressure on the economy" and forces rates down.
  - How he used it: long USD against three currencies, where ten-year forwards discounted the dollar heavily, giving "a large margin of error working in our favor over long-horizons".
- **Flat-distribution option mispricing**: when the plausible range of outcomes is wide and roughly uniform, with mass piling up at a boundary, deep out-of-the-money options priced off a bell curve are too cheap.
  - How: (1) take the market forward (2.86%); (2) compare the model's normal or lognormal curve with your own picture; (3) his picture: "about a 400-bp (4%) range in which all outcomes were roughly equally weighted", with "a probability bump when rates got close to zero and a sharp drop-off afterward" because the Fed was unlikely to breach the zero lower bound; (4) buy the far strike where the two pictures differ most.
- **The One Trade**: his name for the core position in classic bond futures. He kept a smaller core in outright futures next to the options, to earn on any path to lower rates.

## Key Concepts
- **Long-horizon, discretionary, global macro**: opportunities in every market category worldwide; trades "often take years to pay off"; the human decides, not an automated system.
- **Eurodollar future**: $1mm notional contract on a three-month deposit rate. Price = 100 minus rate. 1 bp = $25 per contract.
- **EDM0**: June 2020 Eurodollar future, "the most fateful futures contract in my career".
- **Roll-adjusted chart**: incorporates the gains and costs of rolling the futures position at each expiry; the true profile of holding it.
- **Double whammy**: lose on the downswing, then lose on the way up through option decay despite the right directional view.
- **The two "perfect" trades**: TNPT detailed two. The second was long US dollar plus long US Treasury bonds, which "dominated my performance in the 2010s".
- **Balancing pair**: dollar and bonds usually moved in opposite directions day to day, cushioning P/L volatility. In early 2018 both fell together.

## Mental Models
- Think of the stock market as the tip of an iceberg "carried by the currents of more powerful and fundamental financial flows".
- Use a wide-range, boundary-capped distribution when policy could plausibly tighten, hold or ease and a floor (zero) truncates one side.
- Treat low unemployment and accelerating wage growth as top-of-cycle signs that "counterintuitively, but consistently, portend lower interest rates".
- Hold a strong view as "just an opinion". Smart players held the opposite, well-argued view. Structure for being wrong on timing.

## Anti-patterns
- **The seductive lure of optionality** (TNPT): buying options to express direction is like trading with stops and pyramiding; you pay for whipsaw up front. Two inherent problems: you may not make money even when the market moves your way, and the option may expire before the move.
- **Equating "market" with "stock market"**: misses the rates, currency and commodity flows that drive it.
- **Treating rate futures as tame**: daily moves are a few bp, but size makes them large (see Worked Example).
- **Calling the secular bond bull market over at a cyclical low**: he lists this perception itself as a late-cycle indicator.

## Worked Example
**Trade: deep out-of-the-money calls on EDM0, begun February 2018.**
- Context: Fed target range on February 15, 2018 was 1.25% to 1.50%. EDM0 traded at 97.14, an implied June 2020 LIBOR of 2.86%.
- Problem: the portfolio was "severely down on the year". Some expected the Fed to "normalize" to 4%-4.5%, which made an outright long "very substantial" in downside.
- Structure: 98.375 calls on EDM0. They pay if LIBOR sets below 1.625% in June 2020. Premium about 6 bp (0.06%).
- Payoff he computed: if LIBOR set near 0.25% (99.75), the calls expire worth 1.375%, "about twenty-three-fold return on the initial premium".
- Why an option, against his own rule: the range of outcomes 2.5 years out was "very wide", and "the long-term option provides better risk control to manage the possibility of larger, short-term swings in the meantime". The priority was "to ensure the survival of my ailing portfolio" while keeping the upside.
- Stated cost: the market could turn his way too slowly and the options decay (the double whammy). Remedy: keep a smaller core in outright bond futures.
- Outcome within this chapter: by end-2018 the options "hadn't gotten much traction"; strikes were far from the money and decay worked against him. The plain bond position ran in his favor after bonds made a cyclical low on October 9, 2018. US equities made a low on Christmas Eve 2018. The Fed got in "one last hike". He ended 2018 "very close to flat on the year". The options' eventual result is in later chapters.

## Key Takeaways
1. Date the cycle by cross-asset behavior: stocks falling on higher rates and on strong data marks the inflation fear regime.
2. Dollar and bonds falling together, then bottoming together, preceded "two previous recessions" in his reading.
3. If the best expression of a view is itself a poor trade, question the view.
4. Use options only as an exception: when the outcome range is wide and flat, a boundary caps one side, and survival is at stake.
5. Pair long-dated options with a smaller outright core so a slow path still pays.
6. Risk rules set in good times make drawdown trading mechanical; the hard part in 2018 was keeping a positive attitude and investor confidence. "Huge profits can be achieved by having unencumbered capital and clarity of mind during the times of challenge."
7. "The protracted strife of 2017-2018 ended up laying the foundation for our outsized returns of 2019 and 2020."
