# Chapter 5: Week 2: The Loss of Innocence (March 8 to 13, 2020)

## Core Idea
The week starts with a record P/L and ends with two of the worst days since 2018. Three things broke: Treasuries stopped working as a risk hedge, the dollar rose on a global shortage rather than on rates, and term funding (LIBOR) went up while the Fed eased. The author kept accumulating cheap assets under his sizing rule, found TIPS and off-the-run Treasuries at distressed prices, and lost on one trade that had a deadline: March Eurodollars.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Psychologically neutral positioning** (TNPT): the core position is the size at which you do not worry about the day's price action.
  - If the market does little: capital is not tied up; you can wait.
  - If it moves for you: you make money and move on sooner.
  - If it moves against you: "even better", a chance to add.
  - Core must be "considerably smaller" than the **maximum position**, defined as "the most you can hold without fear of being stopped out by a violent correction". No formula; judgment. At maximum there is no room to add, so you are reduced to hoping.
- **Do not muddy a long-horizon call** (TNPT): "overlaying a rare and valuable long-horizon macro call with tight stops and technical entry points will only 'muddy-up' your risk profile and create a potential for missing your move." Waiting for "more of a pullback" makes managers miss trades they identified correctly.
- **Pre-set levels, executed faithfully**: he decides in advance the levels at which he adds or takes profit. In March those levels arrived in hours, so he traded through the night. "In the long run, I rarely regret adhering to my strategy rigorously."
- **Dominance applied to pairs**: short AUD was "strictly dominant" to short RMB, because AUD falls in every scenario in which RMB devalues ("concurrent necessity") and can also fall on its own. By the same logic he concedes Australian bonds were dominant to US bonds in 2018 and "should have replaced them in the portfolio".
- **Trend versus pattern**: "the ability to draw the distinction between trends, which may continue, and patterns, which have no choice but to shift, is paramount for macro trading." The beach rule: start running when the water is still high and just beginning to recede.
- **Dollar in an easing cycle: two paradigms** that override the "obvious" causality of lower rates leading to a weaker dollar:
  - *Causality*: rate cuts make Treasuries attractive to foreigners for price appreciation; cross-border bond flows lift the dollar through the capital account.
  - *Concurrency*: first cuts respond to tightening financial conditions, so easing begins with a liquidity shortage. "Until this shortage is fully alleviated, the dollar goes up."
- **Gold as an excess-liquidity gauge**: nobody needs to own gold; its worth is its "consensus rating as a store of value". It is "an inflation hedge or, if you wish, an excess liquidity hedge, not a risk hedge". Hence gold does not benefit from the start of a crisis; "it benefits from later Fed actions". In past cycles initial easing was not gold's strongest period.
- **Portfolio Construction Pyramid** (GMI conference, 2017): long gold, long bonds, long dollar via short Swiss franc as "a balanced combination able to withstand most economic scenarios". Gold plugs the hole of an "inflationary collapse of the USD".
- **Eventually versus on a deadline**: "Counting on the Fed to get things under control eventually was correct, but counting on it to get things under control on a deadline was unreasonable."
- **Swap spread carry, four components**: receive the bond yield and LIBOR; pay the repo rate and the swap yield.
- **Riding the coattails of a bailout**: after the September 2019 repo spike, policymakers "had no choice but to provide a flood of liquidity". He positions for that as someone who does not himself need the rescue.
- **Sit it out**: "if you can't wrap your head around a certain transaction, you always have the option of sitting it out."

## Key Concepts
- **Dollar shortage**: "For a dozen days or so in March, dollars left circulation." The USD rose against risky and safe-haven currencies alike.
- **Cash/futures basis**: a Treasury trading cheap to the futures contract for its delivery. Profit needs financing for the bond.
- **On-the-run / off-the-run**: most recent issues versus less active ones. A price gap opened as buyers took liquidity.
- **Repo**: overnight collateralized borrowing, rolled daily. "No theoretical limit" on its cost if cash is scarce. September 2019: 2.25% to 6%.
- **TIPS breakeven**: nominal yield minus TIPS yield; the market's inflation projection "by definition", which need not make sense.
- **MUNIPSA (BMA/3ML)**: municipal index swap. If it trades at 70% of LIBOR, the implied marginal tax rate is 30%.
- **Dividend-curve backwardation**: deferred dividend futures chronically cheap because of hedgers; buy deferred and earn "positive slide".
- **High-beta currency**: AUD; a developed country with a commodity and trade dependent economy.
- **Risk-barometer currencies**: CHF and JPY gain in panic; MXN and other EM do well in good times.
- **EDJ0**: April 2020 Eurodollar future.

## Mental Models
- Think of gold through 2001-2002 and 2007-2012: weak early in the easing cycle, a multiyear bull market after liquidity arrives.
- Think of the 1998, 2008 and 2020 spread regimes as different. In 1998 swap spreads exploded wider and kept rising to a March 2000 peak. In the GFC, LIBOR-OIS widened most but the 30-year spread spike was mild. In 2020 long spreads went more negative on involuntary flows.
- When assets are "ripped away from actual cash flows", carry improves while price worsens. That is the opportunity and the funding risk at once.
- A veteran trader's sequence: the price goes from "cheap" to "cheaper" to "really cheap" to "unbelievable value" to "GET ME OUT!!!!"
- It is easier to be in a position you trust for the long run than to time the entry. He walks through gold in 2020: a January 8 spike, a March crash, a July breakout, each a trap for the waiting buyer.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ignoring a funding warning**: on March 10 LIBOR set higher. "To my detriment ... I ignored that warning and kept betting that the liquidity would normalize."
- **Doubling down at the final stretch**: the late EDH0 additions "backfired".
- **"Very little downside" said casually**: he calls his March 11 comment "cavalier".
- **Hedging rate risk and pocketing the spread** (1998): long spread product, short Treasuries. "Their 'hedge' only made things worse."
- **Straying to the less dominant "big short"**: the RMB bet behind his 2017-2018 setbacks.
- **Loading dividend futures at the top**: low liquidity and one-way flows make corrections "vicious".
- **Betting that lower rates mean a weaker dollar** at the start of easing.

## Key Takeaways
1. Size to core, well under maximum, so a rout is an opportunity to add and not a threat.
2. Never let a trade depend on normalization by a date. Funding stress can outlast a contract.
3. Listen to the risk officer's asymmetry call. Qin flagged "sticky" LIBOR twice.
4. In the first phase of a crisis expect a dollar shortage. Gold, EM currencies and everything priced in dollars fall together.
5. A breakeven or a spread is a price, not a forecast. When it implies something absurd, look for forced selling.
6. Funding and leverage are "the bedrock of our operations". Cheap assets financed overnight are only as safe as the repo market.
7. Past crises are a guide to mechanisms, not to sign. Swap spreads moved the opposite way in 1998.
