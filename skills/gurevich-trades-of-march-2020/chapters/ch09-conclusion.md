# Conclusion (including The Beanie Baby Theory)

## Core Idea
The March result came from strategic positioning built years earlier, not from a pandemic call, tactical timing, tail-hedging or luck. Starting the crisis ahead gave the author the capacity to keep deciding well. His closing thesis, the Beanie Baby Theory, treats cash as an asset that can be expensive: market swings are liquidity events, and when the liquidity problem is transitory the old recession-equals-bear-market metric should be discarded.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The poker night (two seats)**:
  - *Stuck*: losing, hoping for "one or two lucky hands to break even", unable to leave the table, committing the remaining stack on a hand that is probably beaten.
  - *Up*: a large stack, no rush. "When a hand starts breaking against you, you fold and minimize your losses." When you "smell an opportunity, you have no fear of committing your chips". "The easy money is at the table, and you must keep playing."
  - Application: "A poker player on a winning streak is more likely to make good decisions and pyramid their gains." "Having started the year strong gave me the leverage to weather the volatility." Even so he was "stretched pretty thin around the Ides of March".
- **Four misconceptions about the result, and his rebuttals** (Q1 2020 investor report):

| Misconception | His answer |
|---|---|
| A "perpetually long vol, tail-hedging fund" that usually loses and wins on black swans | No. The program performed through years of expansion and through low and high volatility. Objective: "on average no correlation or negative correlation to the stock market". Not invulnerable to correlation breakdowns |
| The team identified the pandemic risk early | No. "We erred on the side of being too optimistic/complacent." They relied "not on our laypersons' speculations, but on our professional approach to portfolio construction" |
| The team pinpointed every shift and traded it tactically | No. "A lot of poorly timed trades were executed along with the good ones." "The main source of P/L was strategic positioning, not tactical trading." |
| Pure luck | Partly. They "always strived to position to benefit from a sudden crisis" and "on average ... expect to make money on risk-off events" |

- **Risk-off event base rate**: such events "do happen every few years (1998: Russian debt crisis, 2001: 9/11, 2008: GFC, 2011 European debt crisis, 2016: Brexit)". Performance in them "is an important contribution to the long-term track record", and he accounts for "continuous risk of 'black swans'" in portfolio construction.
- **The Beanie Baby Theory**: for a money manager "to move into cash for safety during the times of uncertainty is a PROFOUND and CATASTROPHIC fallacy". Cash "must be viewed as an asset which can be cheap or expensive at a given time".
  - His evidence: "the greatest bubble I have witnessed in my career was ... USD cash in March 2009". Everything was cheap in cash terms: foreclosed homes, shares of debt-free companies, bank bonds, foreign currencies.
  - Test: replace the word "cash" with "beanie babies" in a manager's statement and see how it reads. Example: "We have strengthened our balance sheet by converting some of our risky bonds into beanie babies."
  - Short-selling corollary: "if you are tempted to sell a stock short - remember - you are buying cash" from "a monopolistic and costless producer". Short sellers of Tesla and GameStop focused on "the numerator", intrinsic value, and were hit by "a localized collapse of the Beanie Babies in the denominator".
  - Scope: cash is safe only "for a consumer who has immediate cash needs". Over longer periods it is "demonstrably less safe because of persistent inflation".
- **Liquidity origin of market fluctuations**: "The origin of market fluctuations is liquidity." In a slowdown "cash becomes scarce. Some investors have to sell to raise cash; others fail to raise the cash needed to buy. This has nothing to do with rational long-term outlook."
  - Consequence: the link between recessions and bear markets is a liquidity pattern, not "some sort of divine valuation metric".
  - Rule he applied in 2020: when "the liquidity problem would be very transitory", discard the old stock metrics. He "went from hoarding the Beanie Babies ... when they were in demand to selling them when the supply exploded".
- **Central bank as a market player**: "Their product is domestic currency, and they provide it when there is a demand." In the last two decades they "became more adroit at producing when the demand increased". The Fed "turned the black hole into a white hole".
- **Shield, then sword**: "The Shield against Uncertainty had helped me survive, and now the Magic Sword of Necessity was unsheathed."

## Key Concepts
- **Strategic positioning versus tactical trading**: positions chosen by long-horizon analysis versus the month's rapid adjustments. He credits the first.
- **Correlation breakdown**: the admitted weak point of a negatively correlated program.
- **Numerator and denominator**: the asset's intrinsic value and the cash it is priced in. A price can explode because the denominator collapses.
- **Five-day rolling volatility**: his chart of annualized portfolio volatility since the fund's inception on May 1, 2019 shows a spike in the week he calls The Dark Days.
- **Economic gravity**: the new, cash-flooded universe "is still governed by economic gravity".

## Mental Models
- Before a decision under stress, ask which poker seat you are in. If stuck, your judgment is impaired and your options are narrow.
- Read a move to cash as the purchase of an asset at today's price. Then ask whether that asset is cheap or expensive.
- Think of a short as two trades: short the asset, long cash. The cash leg can lose.
- Treat the central bank as the producer with unlimited capacity in the product everyone is short of.

## Anti-patterns
- **Moving to cash for safety as a manager**.
- **Holding short risk-asset positions after the liquidity turn** "because the pandemic recession was so bad", and giving back first-quarter profits.
- **Using recession-to-bear-market history as a valuation rule**.
- **Explaining a good crisis result by foresight**: he denies it for himself.
- **Casting the central bank as villain** in place of modeling it as a supplier.

## Worked Example
**The EDM0 call position from first purchase to expiry, read from the log.**
1. February 15, 2018: first 98.375 calls bought at 0.06, the 6 bp premium of Ch 1.
2. January 2019: after the hike-to-ease discussion (Ch 2), adds at 0.05 to 0.065 on the 98.375 strike and 0.06 to 0.08 on the new 98.25 strike. The largest single ticket is 10,000 of the 98.25 calls at 0.06 on February 5, 2019.
3. June 3, 2019: small sales of both strikes at 0.32, about five times cost.
4. October 14, 2019: 900 of the 98.25 calls sold at 0.295. This is small against the position; Ch 2 records his regret at not exiting more of the vega exposure in this period.
5. March 4, 2020: 11,751 of the 98.25 calls exercised early at 1.04, deep in the money and already offset by futures.
6. March 13, 2020: 617 of the 98.375 calls sold at 1.105.
7. June 15, 2020: the remainder closed at 1.326 (98.375 strike; 10,783 contracts) and 1.451 (98.25 strike; 5,749 contracts).

Reading: premiums paid of 0.05 to 0.08 against exits of 1.04 to 1.451. He presents this as "the scope of our gains on just one aspect of our portfolio". The bulk was held to expiry, in line with his hold-to-expiry rule (Ch 2), through the 2019 period in which he regretted owning options at all.

## Key Takeaways
1. Build the crisis book before the crisis. Strategic positioning was "the main source of P/L".
2. Being ahead is itself an edge. It buys the capacity to fold bad hands cheaply and press good ones.
3. Risk-off events arrive every few years. Budget for them in construction, without becoming a tail fund that bleeds in normal markets.
4. Cash is an asset with a price. In a liquidity panic it is the bubble.
5. A short is a long cash position against a producer who can make cash at no cost.
6. Decide whether a liquidity shortage is transitory. If it is, do not apply recession bear-market templates.
7. Model the central bank's reaction as supply meeting demand, then ride it.
