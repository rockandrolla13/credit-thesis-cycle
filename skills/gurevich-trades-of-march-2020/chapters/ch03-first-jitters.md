# Chapter 3: First Jitters

## Core Idea
From late January to the end of February 2020 the market priced no immediate crisis, and the author himself did not expect a global pandemic. He was long rates for other reasons, was right for a partly wrong reason, and had one of his best months. The chapter is thin on trades; it records what was and was not priced, and how he thinks about profiting from bad events.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Unpriced-risk check**: compare what forecasters and option prices assume with a visible developing event.
  - Evidence he gives: at a Florida investment convention on January 27 and 28, 2020, a panel of economists and strategists gave cautious 2020 growth projections and "none of them was factoring the budding epidemic in Wuhan". By end of January "the market still had not priced in any chance of immediate crisis". His March options traded at 2¼ bp (0.0225%), below his November 2019 cost.
- **China-skeptic deflation chain** (his causal reasoning at the time): shutdown of travel and trade → accelerated economic crisis in mainland China and Hong Kong → private debt unwind and "catastrophic currency devaluation in the region" → "a deflationary shock wave around the world" → lower interest rates, in his favor.
  - Failure mode he records: the chain rested on an "erroneous view" that the infection weakened with each transmission, as he believed of SARS in 2003. The alternative, a long incubation period, "eluded" him.
- **Strategic space**: he treats the market as a game he studies as a professional. He views the world "as a global economic causality flux, which I strive to transform into portfolio dominance."
- **Red button test** (ethics of profiting from pain): version 1, push to kill a stranger and make a billion; he says few traders would push. Version 2, do nothing and a stranger dies, or push to save them at catastrophic portfolio loss; "many of us would find a way to talk ourselves into inaction", and he is "honestly not sure" what he would do.

## Key Concepts
- **Long gamma**: positions "keep growing when the market moves in your favor". Profit-taking is then needed to stay under risk limits.
- **The Last Party**: a friend's birthday party on Saturday, February 29, 2020, the last normal social event. The market mood shifted over that weekend.
- **Risk limits under gamma**: his unposted joke was that the market moved in his favor so fast he could not take profits fast enough to stay under limits.

## Mental Models
- Use option prices as a consensus gauge: a hedge that is "dirt" cheap while an event develops means the event is not in the price.
- Think of an equity high next to falling yields as a divergence. The S&P 500 made a new high on February 19, 2020 while the ten-year yield declined through February.
- A correct position can rest on a wrong mechanism. Record the mechanism so you can audit it later.
- Personal behavior lags the portfolio view. He bet a friend on February 29 that closure of all international travel was imminent, and still attended a party.

## Anti-patterns
- **Forecasting growth without the visible shock**: the convention panel.
- **Extrapolating from the last analogue (SARS 2003)** to dismiss a new event.
- **Confusing "aware" with "acting"**: "a keen awareness of the impending global disaster but still with the sense that it was not happening to us".

## Worked Example
**Position path, January to February 2020.**
- End of January: the March options (the EDH0 98.50 calls of Ch 2) trade at 2¼ bp, cheaper than the November 2019 purchase.
- January 2020 overall: "one of my best months ever, thanks to the surge in the US fixed-income market". He "took profits here and there" as long gamma grew the positions.
- February: a prospective investor noted the fund performed in January when risk markets were choppy and did not give ground in early February when the risk rally resumed.
- February 19: S&P 500 new high. End of February: "the stock market at last caught the virus"; the drop "would prove to be just the few first sniffles".
- Verdict stated: he was "not really concerned" about a global pandemic. The gains came from the existing long-rates book, not from a pandemic call.

## Key Takeaways
1. Check whether a developing event is in forecasts and in option prices. In late January 2020 it was in neither.
2. Being long convexity ahead of an unpriced event means the position grows as it works. Plan the profit-taking and the limits in advance.
3. Write down the causal chain behind a position. His chain to lower rates ran through China, not through a pandemic.
4. Rates led equities: yields fell through February while stocks made a new high.
5. He does not claim foresight. He says he was, if anything, too optimistic about COVID (Ch 4).
