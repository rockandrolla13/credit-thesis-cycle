# Section 6: Conclusion

## Core Idea
Estimate multipliers with the right substitute set. Bond-level demand is "quite elastic". Portfolio-level demand is more inelastic, and more so as the portfolio widens. The price effect "almost disappears after a quarter".

## Frameworks Introduced
None new. The section restates the aggregation rule and the omitted-variable argument.

## Key Concepts
- **Latent substitutability**: most asset classes lack salient characteristics like rating and maturity. The authors point to Gabaix et al. (2023) for measuring it from holdings data.
- **Horizon-dependent elasticity**: the authors suggest linking "demand elasticities at different horizons to different arbitrage theories" as future work.

## Mental Models
- Corporate bonds are a convenient laboratory because rating and maturity make close substitutes easy to name. The lesson about substitutes is general. The numbers are specific to this market.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ignoring cross-elasticity heterogeneity.** It "leads to overestimation of the multiplier".

## Stated Limits (collected from the whole paper)
1. The shock is flow-induced mutual fund trading. Two appendix checks use PIMCO outflows and index rebalancing. No estimate uses issuance (Sections 3, Appendix H).
2. The framework holds net supply constant. With elastic supply the multiplier is "the inverse of a weighted average of supply and demand elasticities" (footnote 9).
3. No asset-class multiplier. The method "does not allow us to uncover the macro elasticity for the corporate bond market as an asset class" (footnote 30).
4. Crisis quarters are dropped. Multipliers may differ in crises (footnote 25).
5. One-to-one passthrough from flows to demand is assumed. The bond-level upper bound is about 0.08 (Appendix D.1).
6. Cross-elasticity with Treasuries, MBS and other asset classes is assumed small (footnote 15).
7. Arbitrage risk evidence is "suggestive". Quantification is left to future research (Sections 1, 6).
8. The quantitative QE analysis is left to future research (Section 4.2).
9. Persistence is estimated only for the baseline portfolio (Section 4.3).
10. The sample is quarterly, 2002Q1 to 2021Q3, US dollar bonds rated CCC- or better (Section 3.1).

## Key Takeaways
1. Name the substitute set before quoting a multiplier.
2. Bond level: elastic. Rating-bucket level: inelastic.
3. Temporary: about a quarter at the baseline level.
