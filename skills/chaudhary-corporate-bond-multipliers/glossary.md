# Glossary: Corporate Bond Multipliers (Chaudhary, Fu, Li)

**Aggregation rule** — the more aggregated the asset, the higher its multiplier and the lower its substitute passthrough (Section 1, Section 4.2, Table 5)
**Arbitrage risk (ArbRisk)** — `std(ν̃ + M u)`: the volatility of trading an asset against its substitute portfolio, the part of risk the substitute cannot hedge (Section 5, eq. 19)
**Assumption 1** — share exogeneity: a fund's lagged holding share in a bond is orthogonal to the bond's unobserved demand shock, given controls (Section 3.3)
**Assumption 1'** — the same condition at the close-substitute group level, needed to identify the passthrough (Section 3.3)
**Bartik instrument** — a shift-share instrument. Here shares are funds' lagged holdings and shifts are fund flow innovations. Validity comes from the shares (Section 3.2, 3.3)
**Baseline portfolio** — bonds with the same detailed rating and the same quarter to maturity, value weighted (Section 4.1)
**Close substitutes** — in the baseline, all other bonds in the same detailed rating category (Section 2.2)
**Coarse rating category** — AAA, AA, A, BBB and so on (footnote 4)
**Crisis periods** — 2008Q3 to 2008Q4 and 2020Q1 to 2020Q2, dropped in most specifications (Table 7 note)
**Cross-elasticity** — the response of demand for bond j to the price of bond k, `Γ_{j,k}` (Section 2.1)
**Davis-Haltiwanger change** — `2(Q_t - Q_{t-1})/(Q_t + Q_{t-1})`, used to measure holding changes and flows in the passthrough test (footnote 34)
**Demand shock (u)** — the bond-level sum of fund-level flow shocks weighted by each fund's lagged share of the bond. In percent of amount outstanding (eq. 15)
**Detailed rating category** — AAA, AA+, AA, AA- and so on. 21 groups (footnote 4, Appendix IA.D)
**Distant substitutes** — all bonds outside the close-substitute group, proxied by the market portfolio (Section 2.2)
**Elasticity (implied)** — the inverse of the multiplier. 16.6 at the bond level from M = 0.06 (footnote 2, Section 4.1)
**FE specification** — returns on the shock with group-by-time fixed effects. Identifies M only (eq. 11)
**Flow innovation (f⊥)** — the fund flow left after removing three own lags and a fund trend (eq. 14)
**Flow-induced trading (FIT)** — the trade implied when a fund invests its flow at existing portfolio weights, from Lou (2012) (Section 3.2)
**γ^o, γ^c, γ^d** — own, close-substitute and distant-substitute elasticity parameters of the two-layer demand system (eq. 8)
**Heterogeneous substitution** — close substitutes have a larger cross-elasticity than distant ones (Section 2.2)
**Homogeneous substitution** — one common cross-elasticity for all pairs of bonds, as in logit demand (eq. 5)
**IV specification** — returns on the shock and the close-substitute return, with the latter instrumented by the group shock. Identifies M and M̃ (eq. 10, eq. 16)
**Macro multiplier** — the multiplier when a whole asset class is treated as one asset. Not identified in this paper (Section 4.2, footnote 30)
**Micro multiplier** — the multiplier on a single security (Section 4.2)
**Multiplier (M)** — the percent price change from a 1 percent demand shock to the asset, holding all other asset prices fixed. About the inverse of the own-price demand elasticity (footnote 1, Section 2.1)
**Omitted variable bias (OVB)** — the upward bias in M from not controlling for close-substitute price changes (Section 2.2)
**Passthrough from flows to demand (β)** — the share of a fund flow that becomes demand for each held bond. Assumed to be one (Section 3.2, Appendix D.1)
**Pooled exposure design** — reading the shift-share setup as many exposure experiments, one per fund flow (Section 3.3)
**Portfolio multiplier (M^G)** — `1/(γ^o - γ^c)` for a portfolio made of a whole close-substitute group (eq. 12)
**Sharpe ratio of the convergence trade** — `M x Mean(|u|) / Mean(ArbRisk)`, annualized (eq. 20, Table 7)
**Shock propagation matrix** — `-Γ^{-1}`, the price effect of a demand shock on every bond (eq. 13)
**Substitute passthrough (M̃)** — the percent rise in an asset's price when its close substitutes' prices rise by 1 percent (Section 1, Section 2.1)
**Substitute portfolio** — the cross-elasticity-weighted portfolio of other assets. In practice the value-weighted close-substitute group, excluding the asset itself (Section 2.1, footnote 21)
