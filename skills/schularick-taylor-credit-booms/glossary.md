# Glossary — Credit Booms Gone Bust (Schularick and Taylor)

Chapter numbers refer to this skill's chapter files. ch01 = introduction and Section I. ch02 = Sections II-III. ch03 = Section IV. ch04 = Section V. ch05 = Sections VI-VII.

**Added-variable test** — χ² test that all five lags of an added control are jointly zero (Ch 5)
**Age of Credit** — the authors' name for the postwar world, where financial innovation and regulatory ease broke the money-credit link (Ch 5)
**Age of Money** — the earlier world, where credit was tied to money and money could proxy for credit (Ch 5)
**AUROC** — area under the ROC curve. 0.5 is a coin toss, 1 is a perfect classifier. Independent of the chosen cutoff (Ch 4)
**Bank assets (total)** — year-end sum of all balance sheet assets of banks with national residency, excluding foreign currency assets (Ch 2)
**Bank loans (total lending)** — end-of-year domestic-currency lending by domestic banks to domestic households and nonfinancial corporations, excluding lending within the financial system (Ch 2)
**Banks** — monetary financial institutions, including savings banks, postal banks, credit unions, mortgage associations and building societies. Excludes brokerage houses, finance companies and insurers (Ch 2)
**Baseline specification** — logit with country fixed effects, no time effects, five annual lags of real loan growth. Specification 5, repeated as 6 (Ch 4)
**Broad money** — typically M2 or M3 (Ch 2)
**Common global time component** — the highly significant year effects in crisis incidence. Not usable for forecasting because not known ex ante (Ch 4)
**CREDIT** — the model's credit variable: total bank loans deflated by the CPI (Ch 4)
**Credit booms gone wrong** — the hypothesis, tied to Minsky and Kindleberger, that financial crises are the end state of lending booms (Ch 1, Ch 4)
**Credit view** — the mechanisms and quantities of bank credit matter above and beyond the level of bank money (Ch 1)
**Cumulative log level effect** — gap between a variable's level after years 0-5 of a crisis and its noncrisis trend (Ch 3)
**Deposit insurance** — post-Depression institution the authors link to stable monetary aggregates in postwar crises (Ch 3)
**Early-warning approach** — predicting crises from a lagged macro information set (Ch 1, Ch 4)
**Event analysis** — tracking variables in the five-year window after a crisis against the noncrisis trend (Ch 3)
**Financial accelerator** — credit as an amplifier of monetary transmission and shocks, not an independent source (Ch 1)
**Financial crisis** — bank runs, sharp increases in default rates and large capital losses leading to public intervention, bankruptcy or forced merger (Ch 3)
**Financial depth** — the level of credit to GDP. With country fixed effects it is measured relative to the country's own mean (Ch 5)
**Fisherian debt-deflation** — deflation raising real debt burdens after a crisis. Operated prewar, avoided postwar (Ch 1, Ch 3)
**Five-year moving average** — mean of the five preceding years, excluding the current observation. Used in the interaction models (Ch 5)
**Incidental parameters problem** — reason logit with year effects is infeasible here. Small N, large T leaves only 140 usable observations (Ch 4)
**Irrelevance view** — real decisions are independent of financial structure (Modigliani and Miller) (Ch 1)
**Kolmogorov-Smirnov test** — test that the model's signal distribution differs between crisis and noncrisis states (Ch 4)
**Lawson doctrine / Pitchford critique / consenting adults view** — the claim that capital flowing into investment booms does not matter (Ch 5)
**Lender of Last Resort** — postwar backstop that now has to confront wholesale bank runs (Ch 2)
**Leverage cycle** — Geanakoplos (2009) concept the authors group with feedback models (Ch 1)
**Linear Probability model** — OLS on the crisis dummy. Fitted values are not confined to the unit interval (Ch 4)
**Loan-money ratio** — bank loans over broad money, the paper's key leverage measure (Ch 2)
**Marginal effect at the means** — change in predicted crisis probability per unit of a regressor, evaluated at variable means (Ch 4)
**Mission creep** — the central bank being forced to underwrite the whole funding market in distress (Ch 2)
**Money view** — control of aggregate bank liabilities is what matters (Friedman and Schwartz) (Ch 1)
**Narrow money** — M0 or M1 (Ch 2)
**Nonmonetary liabilities** — bank funding other than money, mainly debt securities, used increasingly after the 1970s (Ch 2)
**Normal** — noncrisis-year growth, the benchmark in the event study (Ch 3)
**Pillar** — a quantitative indicator used in monetary policymaking. Money for price stability, credit for financial stability (Ch 4)
**Procyclical balance sheets** — nonloan bank assets falling more than loans after postwar crises (Ch 3)
**ROC curve** — true positive rate against false positive rate across all thresholds (Ch 4)
**Second derivative of credit** — the change in credit growth. Its sign change, not negative credit growth, marks crisis risk (Ch 3, Ch 4)
**Sum of lag coefficients** — total effect of five years of credit growth. About 0.40 in OLS, about 10 in logit (Ch 4)
**Two eras of finance capitalism** — 1870-1939 and 1945-2008 (Ch 1, Ch 2)
**Wholesale bank run** — a run on nondeposit funding (Ch 2)
**Year effects regression** — `x_it = a_i + b_t + e_it`. The year effects give the average global trend (Ch 2)
