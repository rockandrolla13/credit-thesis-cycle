# Chapter 10: How to Trade Equities: Globalizing

## Core Idea
Non-US equity markets are highly correlated with the SPX, so they diversify little on a down week. Use country selection to express a macro view instead: cyclicality (Japan), rates (Japan), FX (Japan and the UK with a strong USD, EM with a weak USD), semiconductors (Taiwan, Korea), oil (CEEMEA) and China's own drivers.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Correlation base rate**: weekly-return correlation with the SPX ranges from 0.28 (Chinese A-shares) to 0.79 (Canada). Eurozone 0.74. The chance that local and US weekly returns have the same sign runs from the mid-50% to the high 70%. The US is almost 75% of global market capitalization and about 50% of global earnings at the time of writing.
- **Cyclicality ranking** (Sharpe ratio when the global manufacturing PMI, proxied by the US manufacturing ISM, is above its 6-month average, from 2000):
  - Best absolute: Japan and Hong Kong, then SPX and Italy. Underperformers: UK and Brazil.
  - Local data momentum (Citi indexes) works slightly better than the global PMI for the US, Mexico, most of Europe and the UK. The global PMI works better for Hong Kong, Switzerland, Italy and Canada.
  - Rule: "go with the highest Sharpe ratio on offer, and use both local and global momentum indicators".
- **Rate sensitivity** (monthly US rate moves in five buckets): when rates fall more than 20bp all markets do poorly. The -10bp to +10bp bucket is one of the strongest. The above +20bp bucket is the most bullish. Japan likes rising rates the most. "When expecting higher rates, consider going long Topix."
- **FX sensitivity (FX-hedged indexes)**: Japan and the UK do better when DXY strengthens and their currencies weaken. Europe benefits slightly from a weaker EUR. EM trades much better when its currency appreciates. The US is slightly negatively correlated with a stronger USD. "When bullish USD, buy Japan and the United Kingdom, when bearish USD, buy EM."
- **Japan (Topix) rules**:
  - ISM rule (from 1999): long only when the US manufacturing ISM is above its 3-month average. Active IR 0.73 vs 0.33 unconditional.
  - Global ERI in its top 75th percentile (from 2000): active IR 1.09 vs 0.28. The global index works much better than the Japanese one.
  - Foreign flows: when weekly foreign inflows are in the upper 50th percentile (two-year lookback), buy the day after publication for the following week. Active IR 0.95 vs 0.45.
  - JPY rule: Topix when USDJPY is above its 55dma and the SPX is above its 55dma, lagged one day. Active IR 1.83 vs 0.76 buy-and-hold. USDJPY alone: 1.1.
  - Mechanism: a weaker JPY helps exporters and also banks, through reflation.
- **Germany (DAX) rules**: the IR of long DAX is about twice as high when the IFO survey is above its 3-month average, buying the day after the release. Lever up when the signal is bullish. Do not own it when bearish.
  - DAX vs SPX: relative CESI above its 55d MA gives IR 0.1. Adding the 55dma of relative performance gives 0.21. Buy-and-hold is slightly negative. Relative momentum alone does worse than buy-and-hold.
- **UK (FTSE 100) rules**: the most defensive large market. Preferred underweight in a global bull market and overweight in a bear market. Long the FTSE when GBP is below its 55dma (one-day lag) moves the IR from 0.29 to 0.45. High beta to oil, larger on the downside.
- **China rules**:
  - Li Keqiang index (rail freight, electricity consumption, bank loan growth): buy after a bottom, defined as the 3-month average falling below 3.5% and turning up. Double-digit returns 50 trading days after all three bottoms (publication lag not included). Better than buying the credit impulse bottom, which turns too early for equities.
  - Chinese ERI as countersignal: below -0.4, buy and hold three months. IR 1.4 vs 0.4 unconditional (total return index).
  - Chinese ERI as trend: above its 75th percentile, active IR 1.16. The global ERI gives only 0.5 here.
  - M1 rules on CSI 300 (M1 lagged 40 business days): long when yoy M1 growth is above its 3-month average, IR 0.91 vs 0.4. Long when M1 growth is above its expanding median, IR 0.72.
  - Momentum: days when the HSCEI outperforms the SPX by 2.5 standard deviations (post-COVID sample) have follow-through. "Shooting first and asking questions later."
  - Flows: use flows into Asia ex Japan funds, not pure China flows (which may be the national team). Flows in the 75th percentile: active IR 1.08 vs 0.32. Below the 25th percentile: -0.30.
  - Seasonals: Q4 strong, Q2 and Q3 often weak. Not a standalone strategy.
  - "CSRC indicator": removal of the securities regulator's chairman after a fall in equities marked a bounce (tiny sample).
- **Rest of EM**:
  - Taiwan and Korea: long when the SOX is above its 55dma, two-day lag. TWSE IR 0.86 vs 0.39. Korea 0.41 vs 0.24.
  - Kospi bottoms about two months before the ISM bottom (illustrative, uses hindsight to define the bottom).
  - Brazil: long Bovespa when USDBRL is below its 55dma, IR 0.43 vs 0.18. Adding Bovespa above its 55dma gives 0.60.
  - Latam and commodities: tight coincident correlation with oil. No strong trading rule.
  - CEEMEA: long vs flat by WTI above its 200dma (currency hedged, last 10 years). Active IR 0.54 in the prose and 0.62 in the chart table, vs -0.19 unconditional. The 55dma works less well (-0.11).
- **VRP model globally**: Sharpe ratios beat buy-and-hold in every market tested. Outside the US it works best in India and the UK. In Eurostoxx and France the simple price rule works better. Trigger dates are highly correlated across countries, so diversification is limited.

## Key Concepts
- **A-shares / H-shares / ADRs**: onshore retail-dominated, SOE-heavy shares. Hong Kong-listed shares with more foreign ownership and tech. US-listed receipts with delisting and VIE legal risk.
- **VIE**: variable interest entity. A claim on earnings, not on assets.
- **Li Keqiang indicator**: alternative monthly gauge of Chinese activity.
- **National team**: state-directed buyers of Chinese equities.
- **SOX**: Philadelphia semiconductor index.
- **IFO**: German business survey.
- **Credit impulse**: change in new credit relative to GDP. Leads the Li Keqiang index.

## Mental Models
- Treat the FX trade and the equity trade as two decisions. Compare markets FX-hedged.
- Think of EM as driven by one overriding macro risk factor that lifts currency and equities together.
- Think of Japan as a geared play on the global cycle, higher rates and a weaker JPY.
- In China, follow the first momentum signal. Do not wait to judge whether a stimulus package is sufficient.
- Extreme official concern about weak equities is a buy signal.

## Anti-patterns
- **Buying non-US equities to offset SPX losses**: when the SPX is down, most markets are down.
- **Trading DAX vs SPX on relative momentum alone**: worse than buy-and-hold.
- **Bottom fishing China on the credit impulse**: too early.
- **Following pure China fund flows**: state orders can change overnight.
- **Expecting VRP across countries to diversify**: the triggers coincide.

## Worked Example
*China, September 2024 and February 2024.*
- September 26, 2024: authorities announced measures that looked subpar, "adding liquidity into a liquidity trap". The market rose anyway and kept rising as more measures followed. The 2.5 standard deviation outperformance rule said to buy on the first move, without judging the package. The authors add that even in earlier episodes where follow-up stimulus never came, jumping on the first momentum signal paid.
- February 6, 2024: the rule triggered after President Xi met regulators and the sovereign wealth fund said it would buy more stock. On February 7 the CSRC chairman was replaced. Equities rallied as they had the two previous times this happened.
- Reading: in a retail-driven momentum market the policy signal and the price signal arrive together, and the price signal is enough.

## Key Takeaways
1. Pick countries to express a macro view, not to diversify.
2. Own Japan for a rising global cycle, rising rates and a weaker JPY.
3. With a strong USD prefer Japan and the UK. With a weak USD prefer EM.
4. Use the global ERI for Japan and the local ERI for China.
5. In China buy extreme ERI weakness, otherwise buy strength, and follow large relative up-days.
6. Trade Taiwan with the SOX, Brazil with USDBRL, CEEMEA with oil's 200dma.
