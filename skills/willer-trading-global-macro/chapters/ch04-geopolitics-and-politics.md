# Chapter 4: Temporary Turmoil: Markets and (Geo)Politics

## Core Idea
Geopolitics rarely moves macro markets for long. The analytical question is how quickly the news can be faded, and the answer depends mainly on whether oil is affected. Elections follow a risk-premium pattern: weakness before, relief just before and after, then follow-through of the election-day move.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **GPR spike event study**: uses the Caldara and Iacoviello (2022) geopolitical risk index (share of news articles on geopolitical risk). Day zero is a 100% jump in a month or a move to the 99th percentile.
  - Finding: spikes mean-revert quickly. The SPX reacts to major shocks (Gulf War, 9/11, Russian invasion) and barely to small ones. It recovers quickly and at times leads the index. After 9/11 the market sold off for 5 days and 10%, then regained pre-9/11 levels by October 11.
- **The oil test**: "We therefore fade geopolitical events quickly if there is no oil impact." Most large GPR spikes had an oil angle. 9/11 was the exception.
  - Even with oil: spikes are sharp and short. Median oil rally lasted 38 days (Table 4.1). After Russia attacked Ukraine WTI rose a bit more than 40% and peaked 12 calendar days later.
  - Extension: "semiconductors are the oil of the twenty-first century". A Taiwan conflict would qualify as a shock that should hit markets significantly.
- **Oil spike studies**:
  - Oil up 30% or more within three months (WTI, holds for Brent): US equities rise into day zero and keep rising in the median case. Bonds go sideways to down before day zero, then perform in line with unconditional returns.
  - Oil up more than 15% combined with GPR at the 95th percentile (a proxy for a supply shock): some SPX weakness going in, little impact from the event or after.
  - FX in the 10 episodes since 2000 where oil rose 30% and the SPX fell: commodity currencies (especially EM), EUR and EUR proxies do well. JPY and CHF weaken. TWD, KRW and THB have negative returns. INR and CNY hover around zero.
- **Oil spike and recession**: spike defined as +75% yoy holding for at least five days. Such spikes preceded or coincided with five of the last six recessions (COVID excluded), with many false alarms and unstable lags. Verdict: "oil spikes causing recessions is somewhat of an urban myth."
- **Hedging geopolitical risk** (median paths around large GPR spikes):
  - Energy: best hedge. Up 10% over the 10 days before day zero, peaks on the spike, then retraces.
  - Gold: trades well in the 10 to 20 trading days before, with less upside, and rallies a few days longer.
  - US bonds: sideways just before, up a day or two, then back. In the bigger picture they do well into day zero and for the next 50 trading days.
  - FX: not the go-to hedge. JPY trades in a volatile range (Japan imports oil). CHF works better but the move is small. NOK and MXN trade well after, not into, the event.
- **Tariff playbook**: treat tariff action as initially equity negative, do not stay negative for long, and do not be dogmatic about which country outperforms.
  - Trump 1.0 (11 tariff dates in 2018 and 2019 from Amiti et al., 2021): equities weak on the day and for 10 days. Chinese equities worst, US outperforming in a negative context. Bonds did well, including US bonds. Base metals weak. Gold a hedge only on the day. USD up against CNY.
  - Trump 2.0 (liberation day, April 2, 2025): equity negative, but US equities in line with Europe. US bonds rallied for a few days then sold off hard, underperforming Bunds. The USD weakened.
  - Authors' explanation: a trade war with one country hurt that country. A trade war with the rest of the world hurt the US economy more than its partners.
- **Two ways elections matter**:
  1. Candidates with very different platforms and a close race. Risk premium builds, then unwinds shortly before and after the vote. Implied volatility normalizes quickly. Tradable.
  2. The incumbent boosts the economy before the vote. Hard to trade directly. For the US the authors find little evidence: the median path shows no Fed cuts into elections, though rates tend to rise afterwards. Median election-year deficit increase (1.18% of GDP) matches the long-run median (1.16%).
- **US election tactical rules** (elections from 1952):
  - Equities underperform from about 40 trading days before the election. VIX starts rising about two months before.
  - Around six trading days before the election the SPX starts to outperform, and this continues after. VIX peaks a few days before and returns to pre-election levels about two months later.
  - Rule of thumb: "fade election-driven weakness in equities shortly before the election takes place."
  - **Election-day momentum**: go with the election-day price move, judged at the close. Hit ratios in Table 4.5.
  - VRP trigger: if VIX rises into the election while realized volatility does not, the authors' volatility risk premium buying model can trigger without a dip. It triggered into the 2024 election and worked.
- **Unified government rule for bonds**: Treasuries underperform into elections that produce a unified government and keep performing poorly afterwards. The gap widens after the first 150 days in office. The USD strengthens into such elections for a few days, then the pattern fades.

## Key Concepts
- **GPR index**: news-based geopolitical risk index. The authors plot its 30-day moving average.
- **Supply vs demand oil shock**: one proposed split (Groen et al., 2013) labels oil rallies on days risky assets fall as supply-driven. The authors find it unhelpful because it assumes the answer.
- **Unified government**: one party holds the White House and both chambers of Congress.
- **Election risk premium**: discount built into prices before a close election with divergent platforms.
- **Presidential cycle**: election, post-election, mid-term and pre-election years.

## Mental Models
- Think of geopolitics as a supply shock: slower growth and, at first, higher inflation and commodity prices (Citi economists' view cited by the authors).
- Ask "do the earnings of most companies change?" If not, markets will not pay attention for long.
- Use the left side of the event window to judge a hedge, because markets anticipate headlines.
- Think of a newly elected market-unfriendly government as likely to move to the centre under its constraints.

## Anti-patterns
- **Staying bearish after a geopolitical shock**: even large events had short-lived impact.
- **Using equity moves to classify oil shocks**: circular.
- **Hedging conflict with JPY**: not supported.
- **Acting on the election-day move intraday**: 2016 saw a vicious intraday reversal. Wait for the close.
- **Positioning on USD election patterns alone**: "not strong enough to justify a position".
- **Assuming the Trump 1.0 tariff reaction repeats**: the USD and Treasury reactions were almost opposite in 2025.

## Worked Example
*The 2016 US election.* Polls favoured Clinton but were tight. The SPX fell about 4.5% in the two months before the vote and troughed one day before it, later than normal. On the Trump win the SPX dipped, then rallied and closed higher, and kept rallying for weeks. VIX peaked just before the vote. Rates and USDMXN behaved differently: both had some premium priced, yet rates "exploded higher" and rose for 1.5 months, and USDMXN peaked two months later. The follow-through rule worked in every asset. The premium rule did not hold for USDMXN, which the authors attribute to Trump being a "truly unknown quantity". Volatility still followed the rule: USDMXN 3m implied volatility peaked four trading days before the election. On Trump's second win USDMXN "did not budge", because enough premium was already priced.

## Key Takeaways
1. Fade geopolitical events fast when oil is not affected.
2. Hedge geopolitical risk with energy first, gold and Treasuries second, not FX.
3. Large oil spikes hurt bonds more than equities.
4. Mid-term years are the weak equity years (50% positive). Pre-election years are the strong ones (94% positive from 1952).
5. Buy election-driven equity weakness a few days before the vote.
6. Follow the election-day closing move, especially in DXY (93% hit ratio after 10 days) and SPX (86%). US rates show little follow-through.
7. Sell Treasuries into a likely unified government.
