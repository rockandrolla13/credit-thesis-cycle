# Chapter 3: It's the Business Cycle, Stupid

## Core Idea
Macro investing is mostly business-cycle investing, and the cycle that matters is the US one. Markets lead the data, equities look ahead only about three months, bonds look ahead a year or more when inflation is low, and the famous labour-market recession signals arrive after the damage is done.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Five-regime cycle**: growth x inflation gives Goldilocks (high growth, low inflation), overheating (high, high), stagflation (low growth, high inflation) and recession (low, low). The authors split recession into **early** and **late**, because risky assets perform well late in recessions.
  - Rule: risky-asset returns are worst in early recession and in stagflation. Identifying very weak growth is the key task.
- **Leading vs lagging indicator hierarchy**:
  - Leading (fundamental): manufacturing ISM, housing starts.
  - Leading (market): equity prices, the consumption composite, yield-curve inversion. Market prices are "the only thing clearly leading PMIs".
  - Lagging: initial claims, the Sahm rule, negative NFP prints, the NBER recession label (comes 6 to 12 months late or later).
- **ISM level rules**: manufacturing ISM below 45 is a recession. Between 45 and 50 is typically a less serious inventory correction. Past hard landings had the ISM fall below 45. Soft landings bottomed in the 45 to 50 zone. Whenever manufacturing ISM fell below 45, the services PMI also fell below 45 and the US had a recession.
  - Lead on the headline: new orders minus inventories leads, at the cost of more false signals.
  - Trading use: "best used as a counter-indicator at extremes, especially when momentum is changing" (Table 3.4).
- **Housing rule**: new housing starts below 1.1 million SAAR "fairly reliably coincided with a recession". Main exception: 2000 (Nasdaq bust).
- **Consumption leading indicator** (authors' composite): sum of five-year rolling z-scores of gasoline prices, mortgage rates and SPX returns. It leads the weighted ISM (manufacturing and services from 1997, manufacturing only before) by about 10 months. No direct trading rule. It informs the view of the next big ISM move.
- **Yield-curve inversion = easing-cycle forecast**: the curve forecasts Fed cuts, not a recession as such. Because soft landings are rare, inversion usually coincides with recession.
  - How: receive rates into the first cut. Median lag from 3m/10y inversion to first cut is 18 months (range 1 to 23). 10-year rates fall significantly after inversion in the median case.
  - Modifier: the lag to the first cut correlates with inflation at inversion. Cuts come quicker when inflation is lower.
  - Failure mode: 2022. Inversion came with CPI at 7.8% and the hiking cycle still running. Receiving on the signal first made money, then gave back all gains and more.
- **Recession playbook (equities)**:
  1. SPX range-trades six months before the recession starts. It sells off sharply only two to three months before.
  2. Valuations compress first. Forward EPS then falls (at least 10% typically, 35% in the GFC) while valuation still compresses. Valuation recovers only late in the recession while EPS still falls.
  3. Do not buy back too early. Consider bottom fishing from two months in.
  4. Fed cuts, or expectation of imminent cuts, are a necessary condition for a bottom in a recession. The first cut was usually not enough.
  5. Bear markets without a recession do not need a cut (2022 bottomed on October 13, 2022, the day of the cycle's highest CPI print, with hikes still to come).
- **Recession playbook (rates)**: yields peak much earlier than equities, often more than a year before the recession, but only in low-inflation recessions. In high-inflation cases (inflation above 7% at the start) rates peaked well after the recession began. In every recession inflation was still above 2% at the start.
- **Soft vs hard data rule**: when soft-data momentum (surveys) falls below hard-data momentum, measured by the Citi data change indexes, reduce risky assets. Re-enter when soft is back above hard.
  - Result: SPX information ratio improves from 0.55 to 1.04. Data start in 2004. Works in both recessions in the sample. Does not maximize total return. Many false alarms.
  - Weighting: take it more seriously when a recession is expected for other reasons. Take it less seriously when the SPX is above its 200dma.
- **Financial accident trigger**: banks underperform the SPX by three standard deviations over three months, in an SPX down-market.
  - Typical path: banks keep underperforming. The SPX usually recovers all lost ground within three months (GFC is the exception). Credit spreads take much longer. Two-year yields keep falling because the Fed stays cautious.
  - Trade: receive rates and, with a slight delay, buy the equity dip (the authors point to the volatility risk premium model in the equity chapter). Rule of thumb: the larger the SPX downside, the stronger the USD.
- **Inflation chain**: oil leads PPI, which leads headline CPI, which leads core CPI. Focus more on commodity prices than on the labour market for the inflation outlook.
- **Where to look for the next recession cause**: Fed overtightening, large oil moves, asset bubbles. Start with the assets that boomed most in the preceding good times, then ask how much leverage financed them and where it sits.

## Key Concepts
- **Diffusion index**: a survey measure of how many respondents report rises vs falls. It often leads level data.
- **Sahm rule**: recession signal when the 3m average unemployment rate rises 0.5 points or more above its 12-month low.
- **Claims rule of thumb**: by recession onset claims are up at least 50k from the low and typically above 350k. They peak above 500k.
- **Disinversion**: the curve returning to positive slope after inverting.
- **Second-round effects**: price pressure spreading beyond the shocked goods, or destabilized inflation expectations. The orthodox trigger for central bank action.
- **Third mandate**: financial stability, read by markets as a put for equities.
- **Detrending by 200dma deviation**: the authors' tradable alternative to yoy changes.
- **Global growth contribution**: since 2001 the US contributed about 35%, China close to 30%, the EU 15%, India just above 5%.

## Mental Models
- Use the 200dma as the risk-management tool, not claims or the Sahm rule. On the claims trigger (50k above the prior-year low and above 350k) the SPX had already fallen through its 200dma in every case with a subsequent recession.
- Think of every recession as starting out looking like a soft landing. Soft-landing news stories spiked into each of the last four hard landings.
- Think of Europe as a follower. A European shock must be enormous to matter globally (reunification, the euro crisis), and even then US GDP was barely affected.
- Think of GDP growth as a poor predictor of equity and FX returns across countries.
- Inflation "contains the seeds of its own demise" unless a central bank or finance ministry blunts the adjustment.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trading the Sahm rule in equities**: on average equities peak 126 trading days before it triggers. Median SPX returns after the trigger are flattish. It triggered in July 2024 and untriggered in October with no recession. "If you have to use the Sahm rule, use it for bonds."
- **Selling equities on the first negative NFP**: the SPX lost almost 10% in the 75 trading days before such prints and "typically bottomed when the first negative NFP print was in". In 6 of 13 events NFP turned positive on the next print.
- **Detrending with yoy changes**: SPX yoy is not tradable because of base effects.
- **Trusting majority soft/hard-landing sentiment**: not predictive.
- **Buying a country for its GDP growth**: Shanghai Composite returned about 5% a year from WTO entry to December 2024 with nominal USD GDP growth of 12%. The SPX returned slightly above 9% with about 4% nominal growth.
- **Using inversion to time equities**: lags are too long and variable.

## Worked Example
*The 2022 inversion that cried wolf.* The 3m/10y curve inverted in October 2022 with CPI at 7.8%. The rule said receive rates. The trade made money at first, then lost it all as hikes continued. The first cut came in September 2024, 23 months later, with no recession. The authors give two explanations. First, high inflation at inversion lengthens the lag to the first cut, and the market ignored this. Second, too much hedge fund money was front-running the easing cycle: cuts priced for the next 12 months at the day of the first cut were larger than in any previous cycle. They reject the idea that the Fed overhiked more than usual, because housing starts and the manufacturing PMI never reached recession levels. Lesson: condition the inversion signal on the inflation level. The authors expect cycles with smaller inflation spikes to trade like the older pattern again.

## Key Takeaways
1. Avoid equities two to three months before a recession and stay bearish for the first half of it.
2. In a recession do not buy before Fed cuts are very close. The first cut alone rarely marks the low.
3. Receive rates after a 3m/10y inversion, but expect a long lag when inflation is high.
4. Use ISM extremes with a momentum turn as a contrarian equity signal. The sample is small.
5. After a bank-led accident: receive first, buy equities second, expect banks and credit to lag.
6. Watch financial conditions in real time during accidents. In the tightest quintile (Chicago index) the one-month forward active IR is close to zero.
7. 2s/10s disinversion is a slow warning: equities trade in line at first and turn lower on 3m and 6m horizons. 3m/10y disinversion gives negative 1m and positive 3m SPX returns.
