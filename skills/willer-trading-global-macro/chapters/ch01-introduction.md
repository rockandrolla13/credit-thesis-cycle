# Chapter 1: Introduction

## Core Idea
Conviction comes from a method, and data can substitute for personal trading experience. The book backtests rules across countries, instruments and periods to give "empirically validated rules of thumb", and it reports what does NOT work as carefully as what does. This chapter is thin: it states goals, caveats and the chapter map.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Methodology creates conviction**: success needs (1) a solid methodology and (2) the conviction to press bets "when the stars align". The two are linked, because the method is what creates the conviction.
  - When to use: before sizing any macro view.
  - How: replace gut feel with event studies and rule backtests across many markets and periods. Treat "what does not work" as noise reduction.
- **Markets are not very forward-looking** (organizing premise of the book): empirical evidence on elections and recessions shows markets rarely price an event more than a few months ahead. Markets can rarely focus on more than a couple of narratives at once. Geopolitical events fade quickly. The largest impact of a monetary policy cycle comes around its beginning or toward its end.
  - How: start from the growth and inflation cycle and its knock-on effect on monetary policy, then geopolitics, then each asset class (strategic view, then tactics), then non-US markets, then allocation.

## Key Concepts
- **Rules of thumb**: backtested regularities meant as frameworks, not as off-the-shelf strategies.
- **"Deep" fundamentals**: human psychology and behavioural patterns, which the authors expect to change less than economic fundamentals.
- **US first**: the US economy, the Fed and the USD "set the stage" for other countries, so each asset class starts with the US.
- **Decay**: the authors expect the half-life of the principles to be "reasonably high" but say the information content will decay.

## Mental Models
- Use the data-derived base rate first, then ask what is different this time.
- Think of policymakers as the source of many market opportunities: their rate and FX actions create the trades.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating the backtests as rigorous quant work**: the authors say they accept small samples and simplifying assumptions, "typically assume zero transaction costs", and ignore risk-management overlays.
- **Relying on experience alone**: one career covers too few instruments and too short a time span.

## Key Takeaways
1. Read every result in the book as a base rate with no transaction costs and often a small sample.
2. The authors' discretionary recommendations realized an information ratio of 1.1 from January 2023 to October 2025 (their own claim, time of writing).
3. EM frameworks are mostly left to the authors' earlier book (Willer et al., 2020).
4. The views are the authors' own, not those of Citi Research.
