# Cheatsheet: How Markets Fail

## Decision rules
- When every participant has a sound private reason and the total is harmful, call it rational irrationality. Look for the incentive, not the villain (Ch 11, 21).
- When restraint by one firm would hand share to rivals, expect no restraint. Only a rule binding all firms works (Ch 11, Conclusion).
- When prices rise and demand rises with them, treat the market as unstable (Ch 18, 22).
- When a calm period has lasted long, raise your estimate of fragility. "Stability is destabilizing" (Ch 16).
- When the originator of a loan does not keep it, assume underwriting is weaker than stated (Ch 16, 18, 19).
- When a rating is paid for by the issuer and the yield beats peers at the same rating, discount the rating (Ch 19).
- When a pool is called diversified, ask about correlation with the common factor (Ch 20).
- When a risk figure comes from a calm data window, treat it as a floor (Ch 20).
- When an insider says the market is a bubble and stays invested, expect a crowded exit (Ch 14).
- When officials cite "fundamentals" or say an asset has never fallen nationally, treat it as late-cycle talk (Ch 1, 18).
- When a firm funds long-term assets overnight, model a run, whatever its label (Ch 22, 23).
- When credit markets stay shut for months, treat the problem as solvency (Ch 23).

## Bubble checklist (Cassidy's three features, Ch 18)
| Feature | Evidence in 2002-2006 |
|---|---|
| Policymakers under the illusion of stability | Funds rate at 1%, deregulation, "no national bubble" |
| Financial innovation that eases speculation | Interest-only loans, option ARMs, liar loans, Alt-A securitization |
| New Era psychology | Buyers expecting 14% a year, "it can't go to zero" |

Kindleberger stages: displacement, boom, euphoria, peak, bust (Ch 17).

## Minsky regime tree (Ch 16)
- Cash flow covers interest and principal → **hedge finance** → stable.
- Covers interest only → **speculative finance** → needs rollover.
- Covers neither → **Ponzi finance** → needs rising asset prices or a new income source.
- Share of the last two rising → the system is moving to an unstable regime.

## Coase vs Pigou (Ch 9)
| Condition | Use |
|---|---|
| Few parties, measurable harm, clear rights, low transaction costs | Private bargaining |
| Many parties, lasting harm, future generations | Tax, auctioned permits, regulation, public provision |

## Which herding mechanism (Ch 13-15, 18)
| Tell | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Managers fired for deviating, whatever the result | Reputational herding |
| Shorts squeezed, redemptions, margin calls | Noise trader risk, limits to arbitrage |
| Weak private information, visible public price | Information cascade |
| Long gap since last crisis | Disaster myopia |

## Crisis response template (Ch 23)
1. Acknowledge the scale of losses.
2. Remove some of the bad debts.
3. Recapitalize the banks that can survive.
4. Pledge not to let systemic firms fail, and keep the pledge consistent.
5. Afterwards, tighten leverage, liquidity, disclosure and pay rules to offset the moral hazard.

## Smells
- "Private regulation" or "counterparties will police each other" (Ch 1, 17, 20).
- Rising profits with rising leverage and flat capital (Ch 16, 20).
- Issuers keeping the tranches they cannot sell (Ch 20).
- Narrowing spreads on riskier paper (Ch 20).
- A public denial of liquidity trouble (Ch 22).
- Reform that exempts "customized" products (Conclusion).
