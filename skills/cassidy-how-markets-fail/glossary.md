# Glossary: How Markets Fail

**Adverse selection** — hidden information before a deal, so the worst risks are the keenest to trade; Cassidy prefers "hidden information" (Ch 12, 19)
**Availability heuristic** — overweighting dramatic or personally experienced events (Ch 15)
**Beauty contest** — Keynes's model of investing as guessing what average opinion expects average opinion to be (Ch 13)
**CEO's option / trader's option** — pay with unlimited upside and capped downside, equal to a nonrecourse loan from the firm (Ch 21)
**Coase theorem** — with clear property rights and negligible transaction costs, private bargaining gives an efficient outcome (Ch 9)
**Collateralized debt obligation (CDO)** — a "fund of funds" combining pieces of many RMBSs (Ch 19, 20)
**Conventional wisdom** — Galbraith's term for the unquestioned assumptions that frame debate (Ch 1)
**Credit default swap (CDS)** — unfunded credit insurance contract (Ch 17, 20)
**Disaster myopia** — the falling perceived probability of a systemic shock as good times last (Ch 15, 17)
**Displacement** — the event that starts a bubble in Kindleberger's five stages (Ch 17)
**Dominant strategy** — the best choice whatever the opponent does (Ch 11)
**Efficient market hypothesis** — Fama's claim that prices reflect all available information; weak, semistrong and strong forms (Ch 7)
**Externality / spillover** — a cost or benefit falling on people outside the transaction (Ch 9, 10)
**Fat tails** — big moves occurring more often than the bell curve predicts (Ch 7, 20)
**Financial instability hypothesis** — Minsky: prolonged prosperity moves the economy from stable to unstable financing regimes (Ch 16)
**First / second welfare theorems** — competitive equilibria are Pareto-efficient; with lump-sum redistribution any efficient outcome can be reached (Ch 4, 5)
**General equilibrium** — Walras's term for a set of prices clearing all markets at once (Ch 4, 5)
**Greenspan doctrine** — welcome for new financial products and reliance on "private regulation"; later, the refusal to act against bubbles (Ch 1, 17, Conclusion)
**Hedge / speculative / Ponzi finance** — Minsky's regimes: cash flow covers interest and principal, interest only, or neither (Ch 16, 18)
**Illusion of harmony** — the claim that free markets always generate good outcomes (Intro, Ch 19)
**Illusion of predictability** — the premise that the distribution of market moves can be foreseen (Intro, Ch 7, 20)
**Illusion of stability** — the idea that a market economy is sturdy and self-correcting (Intro, Ch 5, 17)
**Information cascade** — people follow others' actions and ignore private signals, so public information stops accumulating (Ch 14, 18)
**Invisible hand** — Smith's metaphor for self-interest led to serve the public; a negative-feedback claim (Ch 2)
**Issuer-pays model** — rating agencies paid by the firms whose products they rate (Ch 19)
**Liquidity trap** — extra money is hoarded and does not move rates or spending (Ch 13)
**Lock-in** — a product keeping dominance through network externalities, even if inferior (Ch 10)
**Loss spiral** — leverage targets plus mark-to-market force sales that cut prices and force more sales (Ch 22)
**Market for lemons** — Akerlof: bad quality drives out good when buyers cannot see quality (Ch 12)
**Minsky moment** — the point where lenders call in loans and restrict new credit (Ch 16)
**Moral hazard** — insurance or a safety net changing the behaviour of the protected party (Ch 12, 18, 21, 23)
**Natural rate of unemployment** — Friedman and Phelps: the unavoidable rate, below which inflation accelerates (Ch 6)
**Negative / positive feedback** — offsetting versus amplifying responses to a disturbance (Ch 2, 13, 18, 22)
**Network externality** — the benefit each new user gives all other users (Ch 10)
**Noise trader** — a naive investor reacting to irrelevant information (Ch 14)
**Paradox of thrift** — everyone saving more can lower total income and saving (Ch 8, 13)
**Pareto efficiency** — nobody can be made better off without making someone worse off (Ch 4)
**Pigovian tax** — a tax that brings private cost up to social cost (Ch 9)
**Policy ineffectiveness proposition** — anticipated monetary policy does not affect output (Ch 8)
**Principal-agent problem** — an agent's effort is hidden from the principal who employs the agent (Ch 21)
**Prisoner's dilemma** — a game in which each player's dominant strategy gives both a worse result (Ch 11)
**Public good** — a nonrival and nonexcludable good (Ch 9, 10)
**Rational expectations hypothesis** — everyone holds the same correct model of the economy (Ch 8)
**Rational herding** — copying others because of career risk or inference (Ch 14)
**Rational irrationality** — Cassidy's term: rational self-interest in the marketplace leading to "an inferior and socially irrational outcome" (Intro, Ch 11, throughout)
**Reality-based economics** — Cassidy's name for the economics of market failure (Intro, Part II, Conclusion)
**Representativeness heuristic** — treating a small sample as representative; the "law of small numbers" (Ch 15)
**Residential mortgage-backed security (RMBS)** — a bond backed by a pool of home loans (Ch 16, 19)
**Risk vs uncertainty** — known distribution versus "we simply do not know" (Ch 13)
**Shadow banking system** — SIVs, conduits and other off-balance-sheet vehicles (Ch 16, 20, 22)
**Sonnenschein-Mantel-Debreu theorem** — rational agents and perfect competition do not determine aggregate behaviour or stability (Ch 5)
**Subordination** — ordering tranches so junior holders take losses first (Ch 19, 20)
**Synchronous lateral excitation** — the Millennium Bridge wobble; Shin's analogy for banks reacting together to prices (Ch 22)
**Systematic risk** — a common factor that diversification cannot remove (Ch 20)
**Tail risk** — a very unlikely event with disastrous consequences (Ch 1, 21)
**Taylor rule** — a benchmark policy rate based on inflation and unemployment (Ch 17)
**Too interconnected to fail** — a firm whose failure would disrupt funding and derivatives networks (Ch 23)
**Tragedy of the commons** — Hardin: each user overuses a shared resource (Ch 11)
**Utopian economics** — Cassidy's name for free-market doctrine from Smith to Greenspan (Intro, Part I)
**Value-at-risk (VAR)** — a dollar loss estimate at a confidence level from recent volatility (Ch 7, 20)
**Volatility clustering** — large changes follow large changes (Ch 7)
