## Decision rules (the author's)
- When you see announced GW, apply realisation haircuts before you size anything. (App B)
- When you see hyperscaler capex, remember it excludes leased data centres. (Sec III, Sec VI)
- When you see low reported leverage, add leases and look for residual value guarantees. (Sec IV)
- When you see an aggregate equity-debt split, ask what the leverage is on the shell and power assets. (Sec III)
- When debt is long and the collateral is IT, compare maturity with a three-to-six-year economic life. (Sec V, App C)
- When a supplier guarantees residual value on its own product, treat the exposures as circular. (Sec V fn 9)
- When demand weakens, expect tenants to protect owned capacity and pull workloads from leased capacity first. (Sec V)
- When you judge systemic risk, ask about a joint fall in utilisation, collateral values and refinancing capacity. (Sec VI)

## Three vulnerabilities, one amplifier (Sec V)
1. Tenant and end-demand concentration
2. Technological obsolescence
3. Execution: power, permits, hardware
4. Amplifier: fixed debt service on high asset-level leverage, with layered and opaque claims

## Three conditions that made past booms financially consequential (Sec V)
1. Demand falls short
2. Financing conditions tighten
3. Technology adoption does not match the maturity of the debt

## What the paper does not give
No spread model. No default or loss estimate. No own forecast of debt issuance. No view on inflation or the Fed.
