# Appendix C: Historical Investment Booms

**Type**: CASE (past US episodes, used as a size benchmark) | **Source pages**: 25-26

## Core Idea
Measure each boom as average annual capex over contemporaneous GDP. The AI scenario is larger than every earlier episode on that measure. The comparison is approximate, and gross investment overstates AI's lasting capital.

## Episodes (App C)
| Episode | Period | Average % of GDP | Peak | Source and scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canals | 1836-1841 | 0.66 | 0.90 in 1840 | Cranmer (1960) construction spending. $4.4m rising to $14.3m in 1840, $11.7m in 1841. GDP from Johnston and Williamson (2026) |
| Railroads | 1870-1890 | 2.24 | Above 4 in 1870 and 1881 | Gallman's series in Rhode (2002), constant 1860 dollars. Construction only. Excludes at least some locomotives and rolling stock |
| Electrification | 1905-1925 | 0.50 | 0.96 in 1924 | Ulmer (1960), electric light and power. Excludes land and users' electrical machinery |
| Highways | 1956-1973 | 1.13 | 1.32 in 1958 | Federal Highway Administration (2014), government capital outlays |
| Telecom and fiber | 1996-2003 | 1.10 | 1.39 in 2000 | BEA private fixed investment in communications equipment and structures. Consistent with Couper et al. (2003) |
| AI buildout | 2025-2032 | 3.22 | 4.96 in 2028 | Author's scenario (App B). $8.973tn, 3.17% of cumulative GDP |

## Two caveats the author states (App C)
1. The sources "differ in scope and accounting conventions". Treat the numbers as approximate, not as harmonised national accounts.
2. **Asset life.** Canals, railroads and fiber "often provided services for decades, whereas GPUs may become economically obsolete within three to six years". So the AI buildout "generates less net capital formation per dollar of gross investment". Buildings and power connections stay long-lived.

## What this appendix does not give
It gives no financing data, default rates or credit losses for the earlier booms. Section V only says that railroads and telecom became "financially consequential" under the three conditions listed there.
