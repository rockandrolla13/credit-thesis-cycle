# Section II: AI as a Physical Capital Boom

**Type**: METHOD (measurement) | **Source pages**: 3-6

## Core Idea
Size the buildout from the rack up. Then turn an announced pipeline into a realised-capacity scenario. Then turn capacity into dollars and a share of GDP.

## Physical facts (Sec II)
- Traditional CPU racks drew 5-10 kW. Current rack-scale AI systems draw "well above 100 kW" and increasingly use direct-to-chip liquid cooling.
- A 200 MW campus supports about 166.7 MW of critical IT capacity at a power usage effectiveness (PUE) of 1.2.
- At about 142 kW per NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 rack, that is about 1,170 racks.
- A continuous 200 MW load uses about as much electricity per year as 170,000 average US households.

## Cost per MW (Sec II, detail in App A). Author's estimates, 2025 dollars
| Component | $m per MW of total facility power |
|---|---|
| Real estate asset, including cooling and backup generation | 11 |
| Power infrastructure | 2 |
| Integrated GPU racks ($4m per rack), network fabric, storage, integration | 28 |
| **Total** | **41** |

A 200 MW campus costs about $8.2bn. About one-third is facility and power. About two-thirds is compute and IT.

## Pipeline (Sec II, App B). Cleanview project data, accessed September 12, 2026. US only
- Operating capacity: about 63 GW.
- Development pipeline: about 494 GW. Of that, 47 GW is under construction and 447 GW is planned.
- The author warns the pipeline "is not a completion forecast".

## Central scenario (Sec II). Author's scenario, "a scenario rather than a forecast"
- 188.3 GW becomes operational in 2025-32. That includes 22.5 GW already operational in 2025 or 2026.
- 114.9 GW completes after 2032.
- 213.5 GW is never completed.
- Implied project-count cancellation rate: 32.6%. The author compares it with the 33% of Brandsaas et al. (2025, Federal Reserve Board), from Dodge Construction Network history.
- Capacity-weighted non-realisation: 43.2%. This includes downsizing.
- The scenario is "particularly sensitive" to large multiphase projects and projects with no completion date.

## Investment (Sec II). Author's estimates
- Method: $41m per MW, cost growing 4% a year by completion cohort, spending spread over the completion year and the three years before.
- Result: $8.973tn in 2025-32. This includes pre-completion spending on projects that open after 2032.
- With 4% nominal GDP growth: 3.22% of GDP on average per year, 3.17% of cumulative GDP.
- With 6% nominal GDP growth: 2.95% of cumulative GDP.

## Shared features with earlier booms (Sec II)
A general-purpose technology induces complementary investment in a large physical network. Returns depend on uncertain future demand. Financing needs are large.

## Geography (Sec II)
- Training is batch-style and latency-insensitive. It can sit far from users.
- Inference often needs proximity to population centres and network hubs.
- For large training sites the main constraint is "reliable, scalable, and inexpensive power". Grid capacity, transmission and permits can take years.
- State and local subsidies, often sales-tax exemptions on equipment, also shape location (Gargano and Giacoletti 2025).
- The author cites Soto et al. (2026, FEDS Notes): AI infrastructure investment "contributed meaningfully" to US GDP growth in 2025 and Q1 2026. The paper gives no figure.
