# Section I: Introduction

**Type**: METHOD (framing) | **Source pages**: 1-3

## Core Idea
AI is a physical investment cycle, not only a software story. The buildout now needs more money than the firms that want the compute generate. Financing moves to leases, joint ventures, project finance, private credit and structured vehicles. These structures "transform rather than eliminate risk".

## The three-part argument (Sec I)
1. **Scale.** Quantify the buildout and compare it with past US infrastructure booms. (Sec II, App A-C)
2. **Capital stack.** Describe third-party ownership, leases, project finance, private credit and structured vehicles. Use Meta's Hyperion deal as the worked example. (Sec III-IV)
3. **Risk.** Show how the architecture allocates and can amplify technology, operating and demand risk. Focus on asset-level leverage, contingent obligations and exposures that sit outside hyperscaler balance sheets. (Sec V)

## Key mechanism (Sec I)
- The structures separate **users of compute** from **owners of the assets**.
- Equity comes from developers, infrastructure funds, REITs and private equity. Debt comes from banks, private credit funds and securitisation vehicles.
- Investment-grade hyperscaler tenants make the structures financeable. Frontier AI firms reach infrastructure "through the hyperscalers' balance sheets and creditworthiness".
- Moving assets into separate vehicles can raise leverage on the infrastructure "even when reported hyperscaler leverage remains low".
- Result: exposures become "more layered, correlated, and difficult to observe".
