# Glossary: Balanced Asset Allocation (Shahidi)

**60/40** — the conventional mix of 60% stocks and 40% bonds; 99% correlated to equities since 1927 (Ch 2, Ch 4)
**Active risk** — the risk taken by deviating from the neutral allocation; a zero sum game that needs "exceptional skill" (Ch 12)
**All Weather** — Dalio's name for the approach, launched by Bridgewater as a strategy in 1996; the book draws on it but does not claim to match it (Ch 3, Ch 9)
**Arrows method** — box diagram in which each asset gets one, two or three arrows per bias box according to low, medium or high volatility (Ch 9)
**Balance, good** — a return stream that is as steady as possible over very long periods; success not overly dependent on one economic outcome (Ch 1, Ch 2)
**Balanced Portfolio, the** — 20% equities, 20% commodities, 30% long-term Treasuries, 30% long-term TIPS (Introduction, Ch 10)
**Balanced portfolio lens** — viewing an asset class by economic bias and volatility, not by expected return (Ch 2, Ch 4)
**Break-even inflation rate** — nominal Treasury yield minus the TIPS yield at the same maturity; the inflation the market discounts (Ch 6)
**Business cycle, short-term** — the 3 to 7 year cycle driven by Fed rate moves; self-correcting (Ch 1)
**Cash** — the risk-free asset; its rate is set largely by the Fed and averaged 3.8% a year from 1927 to 2013 (Ch 3)
**Constant duration** — holding a rolled long-maturity bond position, not a bond to maturity (Ch 5)
**Core bonds** — the intermediate bonds in 60/40; volatility 4.8% (Ch 3, Ch 10)
**Credit spread** — corporate yield minus Treasury yield; the excess return for risk minus the expected default rate (Ch 8)
**Debt limits** — the point where interest cannot be paid and assets are impaired; where the long-term debt cycle turns (Ch 1)
**Deflation** — falling prices; treated as its own case outside the four-environment grid (Ch 5)
**Deleveraging** — economy-wide debt reduction; self-reinforcing until money is printed (Ch 1)
**Discounting machine** — the market; current prices reflect expectations of the future (Introduction)
**Disinflation** — falling inflation with prices still rising (Ch 5)
**Diversifiable risk** — unexpected shifts in the economic environment; the only one of the three return drivers that a mix can neutralize (Ch 3)
**Duration** — the lever that turns low-volatility bonds into medium-volatility ones; "Duration is key" (Ch 5)
**Economic bias** — the growth and inflation environments in which an asset class beats or misses its average excess return (Ch 2, Ch 3)
**Economic environment** — unexpected changes in growth and inflation (Ch 3)
**Economic portfolio** — the ideal two-asset portfolio for one of the four economic climates (Ch 10)
**Efficient starting point** — the neutral allocation held when you have no view (Ch 12)
**Excess return** — return above cash; the pay for taking risk (Ch 2, Ch 3)
**Exposure** — the return impact from an environment; a product of weight and volatility (Ch 9)
**Flight to quality** — the move into assets with no credit risk in weak economies (Ch 5)
**Futures price / spot price** — the agreed future price and the current price of a commodity (Ch 7)
**Golden rule** — "Diversification always trumps conviction" (Ch 12)
**Good / bad environment** — where an asset beats or misses its own long-run average, not zero (Ch 4, Ch 9)
**High yield** — bonds rated BB and below; credit-sensitive and equity-like in return pattern (Ch 5, Ch 8)
**Long-term debt cycle** — the 50 to 75 year leveraging and deleveraging cycle; self-reinforcing (Ch 1)
**Measurement shortfall** — low reported volatility from infrequent pricing of private assets (Ch 8)
**Neutral allocation** — the mix with no bias to any economic climate; "know where neutral is" (Ch 9)
**No-risk commodities** — hypothetical asset with the commodity average return and zero volatility; it lowers the balanced portfolio's return (Ch 7)
**Peak-to-trough drawdown** — loss from the worst entry to the lowest point, measured in excess return (Ch 11)
**Prepackaged** — the standard form of an asset class with a fixed risk level; it can be changed with leverage, cash or duration (Ch 2, Ch 4)
**Quantitative easing** — the Fed prints money and buys bonds to lower long rates (Ch 5)
**Real yield** — nominal yield minus inflation; can be negative (Ch 6)
**Real yield curve** — the curve of real yields by maturity; TIPS returns depend on outcomes against it (Ch 6)
**Rebalancing advantage** — about 1% a year of return above the weighted average of the components (Ch 11)
**Return-to-risk ratio** — average excess return divided by volatility (Ch 4)
**Rising / falling (data definition)** — current growth or inflation rate above or below its trailing 12-month average (Ch 4)
**Risk appetite** — the excess return investors demand to hold risk; an undiversifiable driver (Ch 3)
**Risk parity** — weighting to equalize asset class risk; the author says the aim should be equal economic exposure (Ch 10)
**Risk premium** — the excess return above cash (Ch 3)
**Rolling returns** — returns for every start date over a fixed window (Ch 3, Ch 11)
**Secular bear market** — a long period of near-zero or negative equity excess returns; 57% of 1927-2013 (Ch 4)
**Smooth deleveraging** — the debt ratio falls while growth stays positive (Ch 1)
**Spending = money + credit** — the identity behind the economic machine (Ch 1)
**Timing mismatch (TIPS)** — TIPS react to actual inflation while other assets react to expected inflation (Ch 6)
**TIPS** — Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities; indexed to CPI; first issued 1997 (Ch 6)
**Volatility buckets** — low 4-6%, medium 8-12%, high 14-20% (Ch 8, Ch 9)
**Weighted volatility** — allocation times volatility; the measure equalized in the two-step method (Ch 10)
**Yield curve** — the market's expected path of cash rates; steepness shows the expected speed of change (Ch 3, Ch 5)
**Zero sum game** — trading and active risk; holding the balanced portfolio is not one (Introduction, Ch 12)
