# Cheatsheet: Balanced Asset Allocation (Shahidi)

**Core claim**: each asset class is "a package of economic biases". Balance the biases, not the dollars. (Ch 1, Ch 3)

## Bias grid
| | Rising inflation | Falling inflation |
|---|---|---|
| **Rising growth** | Commodities, EM bonds | Equities, private equity, corporate bonds |
| **Falling growth** | Long-term TIPS | Long-term Treasuries, high-quality municipals |

Deflation: Treasuries only. (Ch 5, Ch 8)

## Decision rules
- When someone says a yield is too low, ask what the curve already prices. Level alone "does not matter". (Ch 5)
- When rates sit at zero, ask how long they stay there. Do not assume they must rise. (Ch 5)
- When you fear an asset, add its offset. Do not cut it. (Ch 5, Ch 12)
- When you want yield, do not swap Treasuries for credit. Credit has the equity bias. (Ch 5, Ch 8)
- When your view matches consensus, do nothing. It is in the price. (Introduction, Ch 12)
- When you tilt, name the growth or inflation surprise you are betting on. (Ch 12)
- When an added asset falls, check whether the fall fits its bias before judging it. (Ch 12)
- When private asset volatility looks low, raise it before weighting. (Ch 8)
- When a portfolio has many holdings, count biases, not holdings. (Ch 9, Ch 10)

## Which rate rise is it? (Ch 11, Ch 12)
- Rates rise because of **growth above expectations** → equities and commodities are biased to offset.
- Rates rise because of **inflation above expectations** → TIPS and commodities are biased to offset.
- Rates rise **less than the curve priced**, or fall → Treasuries pay.
- Cash rates jump **far above what was discounted** → everything loses to cash for a short time. No offset exists.

## Tells and smells
- A "conservative" 30/70 stock and core-bond mix: still 88% correlated to equities. (Ch 4)
- No TIPS, no commodities: the rising-inflation box is empty. (Ch 6, Ch 10)
- Short-duration bonds as the hedge: the bias is right and the exposure is missing. (Ch 5)
- A risk parity fund heavy in equities and credit: risk balanced, not economically balanced. (Ch 10)
- Commodity stocks or REITs standing in for the real asset: extra equity risk. (Ch 7, Ch 8)
- Widespread overconfidence, heavy risk taking, excess leverage, "this time is different": the author's late-bull signs. (Ch 4)
- Rate cuts that no longer raise borrowing: deleveraging, not recession. (Ch 1)
- A strong conviction: the author treats it as the danger sign. "Diversification always trumps conviction." (Ch 12)
