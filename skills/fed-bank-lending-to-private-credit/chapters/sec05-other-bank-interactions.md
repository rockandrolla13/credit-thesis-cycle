# Section 5: Other Interactions of Banks with Private Credit (main note)

Source: main note, Section 5, footnotes 11 to 13.

## Core Idea
Direct loans are one link. Banks also own stakes in private credit vehicles, form partnerships with private credit managers, and shed loan risk to them. The section is descriptive. It contains no data series.

## The four channels the note lists

**1. Affiliated vehicles and minority stakes**
- Banks originate their own private credit deals "by using minority stakes in PD funds and BDCs".
- Chernenko, Ialenti, Scharfstein (2025): banks may prefer to lend to middle-market firms through affiliated BDCs or private credit funds. This avoids regulatory and supervisory costs of holding the loans on balance sheet and gives a higher return on equity.
- Examples named: Goldman Sachs runs GS BDC. Morgan Stanley runs Morgan Stanley Direct Lending Fund.

**2. Partnerships**
- A Morgan Stanley and Oliver Wyman report (2024) describes new revenue for banks from supporting the private credit industry.
- Private credit lenders are expanding into "asset-based financing and infrastructure financing", areas historically dominated by banks.
- Partnerships named in fn 11: Wells Fargo and Centerbridge Partners. Citigroup and Apollo Global Management. PNC and TCW. Webster Bank and Marathon Asset Management. Barclays and AGL Private Credit.

**3. Warehouse financing and originate-to-distribute**
- The note expects banks to raise exposure through "warehouse financing, and originating and distributing loans to private credit managers".
- Private credit managers "will hold an increasing proportion of these loans".
- Through partnerships banks "extend their originate-to-distribute models and risk-transfer deals".

**4. First-lien and second-lien split, and synthetic risk transfer**
- Banks provide first-lien financing for private transactions. They source the second lien, the "riskier and wider-spread yielding part", to a private credit manager.
- This can be structured as a **synthetic risk transfer**. The bank competes in direct lending and unloads the riskiest part of the notes to private credit funds and other investors.

## The key caveat (Sec 5, fn 13)
"banks are lending to the same NBFIs including private credit vehicles that are providing the credit insurance". So "in aggregate, some of the risk is actually not leaving the banking system as it appears". The note says this exposes banks "to new vulnerabilities". It gives no size estimate. Its cite for this point is a Bloomberg opinion article (fn 13), not data.

## What the section predicts
"it is expected that banks will continue to increase their exposures and interconnectedness with private credit lenders". This is a forecast in a 2025 staff note. It is not a measured result.

## Anti-pattern
Do not treat the Section 4 stress result as covering these channels. Section 4 tests credit line drawdowns only.
