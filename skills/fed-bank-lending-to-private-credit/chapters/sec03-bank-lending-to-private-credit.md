# Section 3: Bank Lending to Private Credit (main note)

Source: main note, Section 3 (3.1, 3.2, 3.3), Tables 1 and 2, Figures 2 to 4.

## Core Idea
Large US banks had about $95 billion committed and $56 billion drawn to BDCs and PD funds at 2024-Q4. The loans are mostly revolving credit lines. They carry higher utilization and interest rates than loans to other NBFIs, with lower default probabilities and delinquency.

## 3.2 The all-lender estimate for BDCs, from 10-Q data

This is the note's only estimate that reaches beyond the largest US banks. It is as of 2024-Q1 and covers BDCs only.

**Lead arranger mix on loans to the 74 BDCs (revolving facilities, 2024-Q1)**
| Lead arranger type | Share of loans |
|---|---|
| US banks | 50 percent |
| Foreign banks | around 30 percent |
| Foreign non-banks | about 2 percent |
| Unknown lenders, "which may include other NBFIs" | 18 percent |

- Largest US lead arrangers, in the note's order: J.P. Morgan Chase, Citigroup, Wells Fargo, Bank of America.
- Largest foreign bank lenders: BNP, SMBC, Barclays.
- Largest borrowers: Blackstone, Ares Capital, FS KKR.
- Utilization: 67 percent on foreign bank loans, 54 percent on domestic bank loans.

**The scale-up method, step by step**
1. Sum outstanding bank credit to the 74 BDCs: $43.6 billion (2024-Q1). Some BDCs report only outstanding debt, not commitments.
2. For the 86 percent of that outstanding amount where commitments are reported, compute utilization: 57.6 percent.
3. Assume that utilization holds for all BDCs.
4. Assume committed amounts per dollar of assets are the same for all BDCs.
5. Result: total committed credit to the whole BDC sector "is around $87 billion".
6. Compare with Y-14: $56 billion of commitments to BDCs. So the largest US banks hold "around 60 percent of total credit commitments to BDCs as of 2024-Q1".

Both step 3 and step 4 are assumptions the authors state. The $87 billion is an estimate, not a count.

## 3.3 Characteristics of the loans

**Table 1: bank loans to NBFIs in FR Y-14Q, 2024-Q4**
| | Commitment ($ bn) | Utilization (%) | Avg interest rate (%) | Time to maturity (yrs) | Avg rating | Delinquency (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BDCs | 56 | 56.8 | 6.4 | 4.1 | BBB | 0.5 |
| Private debt funds | 40 | 55.0 | 6.6 | 2.6 | BBB | 0.7 |
| Other NBFIs | 2193 | 48.7 | 6.2 | 3.0 | BBB | 0.7 |

Ratings are banks' internal ratings (Sec 2.1). The note says the bulk of bank loans to NBFIs "are rated investment grade, exhibit very low delinquency rates and tend to be of shorter maturity than loans to nonfinancial corporations".

**Table 2: bank-estimated default probabilities (%) by rating, 2024-Q4**
| | Other NBFIs mean / median / % obs | PD funds mean / median / % obs | BDCs mean / median / % obs |
|---|---|---|---|
| All firms | 1.65 / 0.37 / 100 | 2.07 / 0.22 / 100 | 0.71 / 0.30 / 100 |
| Inv. grade excl. BBB | 0.38 / 0.06 / 16 | 0.06 / 0.05 / 29 | 0.06 / 0.07 / 10 |
| BBB | 0.37 / 0.23 / 37 | 0.60 / 0.20 / 40 | 0.44 / 0.24 / 52 |
| Non-inv. grade | 3.07 / 1.03 / 47 | 5.76 / 1.03 / 32 | 1.26 / 0.85 / 38 |

How the authors read Table 2:
- Loans to private credit vehicles have the smallest non-investment-grade share: 32 percent for PD funds and 38 percent for BDCs, against 47 percent for other NBFIs.
- BDC default probabilities are "the smallest ones across almost all different credit ratings".
- PD funds have a higher MEAN default probability than other NBFIs (2.07 against 1.65) but a lower MEDIAN (0.22 against 0.37). The mean is pulled up by the non-investment-grade bucket (5.76).
- Conclusion: "banks take low credit risk in their lending to private credit".

## The section's one-sentence summary (Sec 3.3)
Bank credit lines to private credit vehicles have "higher utilization rates, higher interest rates, lower probability of default, and lower delinquency rates" than those to other NBFIs. Public BDCs have been raising leverage.

## Inconsistencies in the source (recorded, not corrected)
- The text says loans to BDCs and PD funds "have an average interest rate of 6.6 percent respectively". Table 1 shows 6.4 for BDCs and 6.6 for PD funds.
- Sec 3.1 components give PD fund commitments of $30 billion plus $9 billion, so $39 billion. Table 1 shows $40 billion. Figure 2 shows $39.7 billion. Rounding.
- Sec 3.2 compares the 2024-Q1 estimate of $87 billion with "$56 billion in credit commitments reported in the Y-14 data". Figure 2 shows BDC commitments of $53.3 billion at 2024-Q1 and $55.6 billion at 2024-Q4. The $56 billion looks like the 2024-Q4 figure.
- "$56 billion" appears twice with different meanings: drawn amounts to ALL private credit vehicles (Sec 3.1, fn 4) and COMMITTED amounts to BDCs alone (Table 1, Sec 3.2). Check which one a sentence means.
