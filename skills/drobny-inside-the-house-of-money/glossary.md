# Glossary: Inside the House of Money (Drobny)

Each term carries the chapter and, for interview chapters, the speaker.

**Absolute return bucket** — Leitner's sixth category for rare mispricings, sized up to 10% of the fund on one idea (Ch 4)
**Accrual accounting** — bank treatment that books current income on a position, not its mark-to-market. The basis of Porter's staying power (Ch 7)
**Beauty contest** — Keynes's picture of markets as guessing what average opinion expects average opinion to be (Ch 8, boxed text)
**Bias and trade** — Andres Drobny's split between the view he talks about and the risk/reward bet consistent with it (Ch 6)
**Big bet approach** — Thomas's one-bet-a-year case: 50% position, 60% hit rate, Sharpe 0.1 (Appendix A, ch17)
**Brady bond** — sovereign bond whose principal is backed by a U.S. Treasury zero, leaving the coupon stream as sovereign risk (Ch 14, Dimitrijević)
**Brain damage** — Andres Drobny's term for the state after a run of stopped-out losses. Remedy is a month away (Ch 6)
**Carry trade** — earning a yield gap. Called positive risk premium by Leitner (Ch 4) and "synthetic short gamma" by Siva-Jothy (Ch 5)
**Clean sheet test** — Dimitrijević's question: would we buy this today at this price, forgetting our history in it (Ch 14)
**Commodity stream / equity stream** — Drobny's two lineages of macro managers after 1971 (Ch 2)
**Concept exhaustion** — Porter: a market idea loses force once it reaches magazine covers (Ch 7)
**Conditional steepener** — long a receiver swaption on the one-year rate one year forward, short the same on the 10-year rate (Ch 15, Gorton)
**Convergence trade** — bet that currencies and bonds converge toward one currency and rate (Ch 2, Ch 5)
**Country as a company** — Dimitrijević: in a crisis every instrument of a country falls together, like Enron's securities (Ch 14)
**Discretionary / systematic** — judgment-led or rule-led approach to finding trades (Ch 1, Nicholas)
**Doubly right** — Leitner: a short pays away risk premium, so it must be right twice (Ch 4)
**DV01** — value change for a one basis point move (Ch 7)
**Fan** — the anonymous manager's image of FX spreading out at the end of the credit curve (Ch 16)
**Far-from-equilibrium condition** — Nicholas: a price more than one standard deviation from its mean (Ch 1)
**Fat tails** — extreme outcomes more frequent than a normal distribution implies (Ch 2)
**Favorite trade** — Andres Drobny's interview question: the one trade to get rich today (Ch 6)
**Flight to quality** — capital moving into on-the-run U.S. government bonds, widening other spreads (Ch 2)
**Forward rate bias** — forwards overestimate later spot moves, so discount forwards have earned positive returns (Ch 4, boxed text)
**Frozen ice theory** — the anonymous manager: crowds drift from the liquid original trade to illiquid proxies, the thin ice (Ch 16)
**Gamma** — rate of change of an option's delta. Owning options is long gamma (Ch 5, boxed text)
**Gap risk** — lending long and borrowing short. Porter's mandate (Ch 7)
**Gestalt shift** — the anonymous manager: a sudden change in the market's whole view of something (Ch 16)
**Global micro** — macro themes checked and expressed through micro detail (Preface, Ch 3, Ch 4, Ch 12)
**Greenspan put** — "an implicit option that the Fed writes anytime equity markets stumble" (Ch 2)
**Home country bias** — investors hold most assets at home (Ch 14, boxed text)
**Insecure confidence** — Anderson's name for constantly asking what he has wrong (Ch 12)
**LTCM liquidity premium** — positions correlated only because one fund held them all (Ch 2)
**Matrix** — Harris's mental picture of global money flows seeking the best risk-adjusted return (Ch 10)
**Minimum Funding Requirement** — UK pension rule that forced buying of long Gilts (Ch 7, boxed text)
**Multibet approach** — Thomas's five independent bets at 10% each: Sharpe 0.25 (Appendix A, ch17)
**Narrow end of global macro** — Wadhwani: directional bets in indexes, rates and currencies (Ch 3, Ch 8)
**Nonprice indicator** — Thiel: an observable that should move a set way as a trade progresses (Ch 9)
**One-year, one-year forward** — the market's forecast of the one-year rate in a year. Porter's core area (Ch 7)
**Pain trade** — the move that hurts the crowded position (Ch 13, Bessent)
**Paradox of perfection** — central bank success on inflation breeds risk taking and asset imbalances (Ch 3, Ch 8)
**Political business cycle** — rate policy bent by elections, before central bank independence (Ch 8, Wadhwani)
**Prayer trade** — Harris: holding a loser to get back to even (Ch 10)
**Price of money** — the anonymous manager: the one true macro trade. In practice the 18-month to two-year rate (Ch 16)
**RAROC** — risk-adjusted return on capital at Bankers Trust, an early form of VAR (Ch 4)
**Real money** — unlevered long-only managers such as pensions and endowments (Ch 4, boxed text)
**Relative value** — pairing similar assets long and short (Ch 1). "a trade that exploits an inefficiency, typically in the yield curve or the volatility surface" (Ch 15, Gorton)
**Reset** — Siva-Jothy's six-monthly return of the trader's P&L to zero (Ch 5)
**Risk curve** — the anonymous manager: two-year rate, government bonds, corporate bonds, equities, then FX. He equates it with the credit curve (Ch 16)
**Secular trend** — Rogers's term for a multi-year macro trend (Ch 11)
**Sharpe ratio** — return less the risk-free rate, divided by volatility (Appendix A, ch17)
**Slower Fool Theory** — Richard Dennis, 1987: hold bad paper and plan to sell before the other fools (Ch 10, boxed text)
**Staying power** — ability to hold through adverse marks. Praised by Porter (Ch 7), lacked by Rogers in 1970 (Ch 11)
**Style drift** — leaving a stated style. Built into macro (Preface, Appendix A)
**Synthetic call** — Thiel: a high-cost producer whose cash flow surges above a price point (Ch 9)
**Tequila effect** — fear of further devaluations after Mexico, end of 1994 (Ch 14)
**Time horizon arbitrage** — Porter: holding what short-horizon investors must sell (Ch 7)
**TIPS** — Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities. Read as a real-yield signal by Thiel (Ch 9) and traded by Gorton (Ch 15)
**Trade your P&L** — Siva-Jothy: scale risk to profit in hand (Ch 5)
**Twin surplus / twin deficit** — Leitner's first sort of good and bad countries (Ch 4)
**VAR** — value at risk. Criticized as backward-looking by Porter (Ch 7), as dampening by Anderson (Ch 12), and as a trap in low volatility by Gorton (Ch 15)
**Whale watching** — Bessent: the boat tips because everyone runs to one side (Ch 13)
**Winner's curse** — the winning bidder tends to overpay (Ch 16, boxed text)
