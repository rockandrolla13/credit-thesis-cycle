# Patterns: Inside the House of Money (Drobny)

Each pattern is one interviewee's stated technique. The book does not test any of them. Report them as that person's.

## View-to-trade conversion (Andres Drobny)
**When to use**: you hold a macro view and need to decide whether to act. (Ch 6)
**How**: (1) State the fundamental process. (2) State what the market believes and where it is positioned, from talk and from multi-year flows. (3) Name what is missed or misread. (4) Set target and stop and require at least 4 to 1. (5) After each stop, add the realized loss to the risk side. Drop the trade near 4 to 3.
**Trade-offs**: he says he finds trades better than he runs them. Positioning is judged by ear.

## Peg-break sequence (Drobny's history, Bessent, Andres Drobny)
**When to use**: a fixed or banded exchange rate is under pressure. (Ch 2, Ch 6, Ch 13)
**How**: (1) Check whether the rate policy needed to hold the peg fights the domestic economy. (2) Find the transmission, such as UK floating mortgages. (3) Watch locals and companies hedge. (4) Treat a defensive rate hike in a recession as the signal that the defense cannot last. (5) Size at the band edge so the loss is capped at profit in hand.
**Trade-offs**: Andres Drobny was a year early in 1991. Buying the currency that already devalued failed (Finnish markka).

## Carry risk/reward check (Andres Drobny, Leitner)
**When to use**: holding or entering a carry position. (Ch 4, Ch 6)
**How**: compare the remaining yield gap with the build-up of system risk and holder leverage. Leitner stepped aside when central banks signalled a devaluation, then returned. Andres Drobny left when carry became "almost insignificant" with holders leveraged.
**Trade-offs**: Siva-Jothy (Ch 5) and the anonymous manager (Ch 16) reject carry outright as short gamma.

## Long-dated option with unknown timing (Leitner, anonymous manager, Wadhwani)
**When to use**: you expect a move and cannot time it. (Ch 4, Ch 8, Ch 16)
**How**: buy options with long maturity when volatility is cheap or when "everyone" calls an outcome inevitable. Keep dead options to expiry. Wadhwani pairs spot with stops and options he keeps. Leitner buys one-year FX straddles weekly with 6.5% of NAV a year.
**Trade-offs**: you overpay against realized volatility. Leitner wanted a trigger before paying decay. Thiel (Ch 9) and Harris (Ch 10) do not use options.

## Dislocation trade in the most liquid market (Siva-Jothy, Gorton)
**When to use**: a shock that hurts the U.S. economy or forces deleveraging. (Ch 5, Ch 15)
**How**: buy Eurodollar futures or calls, or front-end Treasuries. Do not diversify into less liquid expressions.
**Trade-offs**: needs speed. Siva-Jothy says fixed income rallies in over 90% of major dislocations, which leaves the rest.

## Trade your P&L (Siva-Jothy, Druckenmiller via Bessent)
**When to use**: sizing through a year. (Ch 5, Ch 13)
**How**: start small. Build a cushion. Lever up only winners with a high-confidence view. Cap the worst case at the year's profit. Reset every six months.
**Trade-offs**: returns are volatile when ahead. Investors must accept that.

## Time horizon arbitrage (Porter)
**When to use**: you have funding and accounting that survive adverse marks. (Ch 7)
**How**: (1) Identify why an anomaly exists. (2) Require positive carry. (3) Enter or add when forced sellers blow out. (4) Lock in funding. (5) Hold to convergence or maturity.
**Trade-offs**: "The road to ruin is paved with positive carry." His Bund/Gilt trade was down £60m before it made £180m.

## Central bank path against the forwards (Porter, Wadhwani)
**When to use**: front-end rate positions. (Ch 7, Ch 8)
**How**: compare one-year, one-year forwards with the path the central bank is likely to take. Expect the market to extrapolate. From Wadhwani: discount single data outliers, because the committee smooths. Watch for guidance that pins the price while data moves.
**Trade-offs**: Porter's cycle length of 16 to 24 months is a rule of thumb with no data shown.

## Micro research with adverse macro assumptions (Anderson)
**When to use**: commodity and basic industry positions. (Ch 12)
**How**: (1) Build supply, demand and cost curve from primary sources. (2) Assume strong growth when short and weak growth when long. (3) Trade only if it still works. (4) Prefer low-cost assets and competent management. (5) Size on worst case and add positions without correlation offsets.
**Trade-offs**: heavy travel and data work. Tiger lost for three years on palladium before it paid.

## Contagion sort (Dimitrijević)
**When to use**: after a sovereign or sector crisis drags down look-alikes. (Ch 14)
**How**: (1) List issuers sold in sympathy. (2) Check each one's own trade, bank and company links to the crisis. (3) For secured bonds, strip out the safe part and price the rest. (4) Cap any one country at 10% to 15%.
**Trade-offs**: he says contagion has weakened as local investors grew. Russia 1998 showed a "decent" analysis can still be wrong on the policy choice.

## Debt against equity inside one country or issuer (Dimitrijević)
**When to use**: debt prices the good outcome and equity does not. (Ch 14)
**How**: long equity, short debt. Good outcome: equity outruns the tightening. Bad outcome: debt falls and hedges.
**Trade-offs**: fails when bonds are near investment grade and do not fall.

## RV with a macro hedge (Gorton and Standing)
**When to use**: running spread or curve positions. (Ch 15)
**How**: (1) Start with RV structures. (2) Net the volatility exposure. (3) Add long-volatility macro positions. (4) Cap any strategy at 20% of capital. (5) Put two-thirds of a theme's risk in the best instrument.
**Trade-offs**: limits cost them about ten points of annual return by their own estimate.

## Frozen ice check (anonymous manager)
**When to use**: late in a trend. (Ch 16)
**How**: ask whether holders still own the liquid original trade or only its proxies. Ask whether the weakest credits are now leading. Compare exit liquidity with entry liquidity.
**Trade-offs**: a description, not a timing tool.

## Cheap plus change (Rogers) and industry bottom
**When to use**: long-horizon ideas. (Ch 11)
**How**: look for an asset far below its high, hated, with a real change under way. For an industry, look for losses everywhere and a few bankruptcies.
**Trade-offs**: he says he times badly. He was wiped out short in 1970 on names that later failed.

## Five premia plus home runs (Leitner)
**When to use**: a long-horizon pool with no redemptions. (Ch 4)
**How**: 20% of risk each to equities, fixed income, FX, commodities, real estate. Earn each premium with stops and suitable leverage. Add up to 10% on a rare asymmetric idea.
**Trade-offs**: he says the fund was not fully built this way.

## Breadth arithmetic (Thomas)
**When to use**: judging a manager or a book. (Appendix A, ch17)
**How**: count independent bets. One bet at 60% odds gives Sharpe 0.1. Five give 0.25 at the same return.
**Trade-offs**: independence is the hard part. Thiel (Ch 9) says correlations are too high for it.
