# Chapter 3: The Future of Global Macro Hedge Funds (Steven Drobny)

Type: METHOD (industry structure). Thin chapter of four pages.

## Core Idea
The era of a few giant funds ended in 2000. Macro became many managers with specialties, which Drobny calls "global micro".

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Diseconomies of scale**: when a fund gets too big, it can take meaningful positions only "in the most liquid instruments and deepest markets". Scope and diversification shrink. Drobny puts Soros and Tiger of the late 1990s in this camp.
- **Global micro**: macro themes checked and expressed through micro detail.
  - Soros and Druckenmiller believed micro evidence gives insight into the macro picture (per Bessent).
  - Tiger did "mountains of primary, on-the-ground micro research" before a position (per Anderson).
  - Their biggest wins and losses still came from classic macro instruments. Wadhwani calls that "the narrow end of global macro".
- **Multi-team structure**: Tudor and Caxton allocate capital to internal teams with specific skills.
  - Tudor in 2004: a little more than half of risk with Jones and in-house macro traders, a third in global equities, the rest in distressed and risk arbitrage.
  - Caxton: from $7.6m in 1983 to over $12bn, with about 30 teams.
  - Tudor Jones on why: many traders let each one "practice patience". "If you don't see anything, you don't trade."
- **The two explanations for fewer opportunities** (Drobny reports both and does not choose):
  1. Past macro profits came from policy errors, such as fixed nominal exchange rates and micromanaged economies. Policy makers learned.
  2. A "paradox of perfection": beating inflation and exchange rate volatility may have created bigger problems, "global trade imbalances and asset bubbles".

## Key Concepts
- **Leitner's definition**: "Macro themes expressed in a micro style. Global macro only means that you start at the top and work your way down."
- **Deeper field**: no single fund drives macro markets now (Siva-Jothy). Emerging markets have local institutions (Dimitrijević).

## Key Takeaways
1. Size narrows the opportunity set. Check a fund's assets against the depth of the markets it claims to trade.
2. The book's interviews are the evidence for "global micro". Each interviewee has a specialty.
3. The 2005 worry list in this chapter is imbalances and asset bubbles, not inflation.
