# Conclusion and Appendices A and B

Type: METHOD. Appendix A (Dr. Lee R. Thomas III) is the only formal argument in the book. Appendix B (Steven Drobny) is a note to investors.

## Core Idea
With a fixed level of skill, more independent bets give the same return with less volatility. Thomas argues that the wide macro mandate is the easiest place to find independent bets. Drobny adds that calm periods build the leveraged positions that cause the next dislocation.

## The Conclusion
The book's Conclusion is one line, a Keynes quote: "In the long run, we're all dead."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Big bet versus multibet** (Thomas, Appendix A). Assumptions: a target of 10% a year, a 60% chance that any bet is right, and a 5% risk-free rate.
  1. *One bet a year.* To average 10% it must return +50% when right and −50% when wrong. Expected gain 0.50 × 0.60 = 30%. Expected loss 0.50 × 0.40 = 20%. Net 10%.
  2. Volatility is about 50%. Sharpe ratio = (10 − 5) / 50 = 0.1.
  3. *Five independent bets a year.* Average bet size falls from 50% to 10% for the same 10% target.
  4. Chance of +50% in a year: 0.60^5 = 0.0776, about 8%. Chance of −50%: 0.40^5 = 0.0102, about 1%.
  5. Volatility is about 20%. Sharpe ratio 0.25.
  - Thomas's reading: the one-bet manager "may prove lucky for a spell" and is then forced out, because investors do not stay through the −50% years.
  - Why 60%: darts hit about 50%. A manager under 50% loses investors. 60% puts a manager "well inside the upper half of the league tables".
- **The independence condition** (Thomas): bets must be "both better-than-average and independent". Long Thai equities and long Indonesian equities are one bet, "long Southeast Asian equities", when the two markets are highly correlated.
- **Three ways to diversify under a macro mandate** (Thomas):
  1. *Asset classes and products*: rates, equities, currencies, commodities, real estate, in cash, futures, derivatives or direct investment.
  2. *Geography*: doubling the countries more than doubles the independent opportunities.
  3. *Strategies*: fundamental, technical, relative value, arbitrage, top-down, bottom-up, quantitative, long/short. Other styles are penalized for "style drift". Macro is encouraged to move.
- **Stability breeds dislocation** (Drobny, Appendix B): "long periods of stability are often followed by extreme dislocations as leveraged positions built up during the 'safe' environment cause significant bottlenecks when there is a sudden shift in the investment climate and a rush for the exit."
- **Allocate when it is quiet** (Drobny): allocations to macro have been cut in stable periods. He says those "have often proven to be the best times to be invested".
- **Diversify across macro managers** (Drobny): apply Thomas's logic to manager selection. Combine managers with different skills, products, regions and time frames. Worry less about one manager's volatility.

## Key Concepts
- **Sharpe ratio**: return minus the risk-free rate, divided by the volatility of returns. Thomas says funds strive for at least 1.0.
- **Nearly efficient markets**: the margin between winning and losing is small and "often resolved only by chance".
- **Style drift**: leaving a stated investing style.
- **Global micro**: Drobny says it needs "multiple better-than-average, independent bets" and is "extremely difficult to put into practice".

## Numbers Stated
| Item | One bet | Five bets |
|---|---|---|
| Average annual return | 10% | 10% |
| Bet size | 50% | 10% |
| Probability of +50% year | 60% | about 8% |
| Probability of −50% year | 40% | about 1% |
| Volatility | about 50% | about 20% |
| Sharpe ratio (5% risk-free) | 0.1 | 0.25 |

Other numbers: macro fund returns in 2005 ranged from +57% to −8% (Appendix B).

## Drobny's List of Pending Shocks (Appendix B, written January 2006)
He calls these "dream scenarios for global macro managers". The book does not report outcomes.
- The large imbalances unwind.
- The housing bubble pops.
- Equities revert to their historical mean.
- The U.S. deficit becomes a real problem.
- Existing currency regimes come unglued.
- Global markets become uncorrelated.
- The new Fed chairman, Ben Bernanke, "lifts the Greenspan put".
Conditions he notes: flat yield curves, volatilities "at all-time lows", and uncorrelated markets hard to find.

## Anti-patterns
- **Counting correlated positions as separate bets** (Thomas).
- **Trying to lift the hit rate** or the win/loss ratio in nearly efficient markets. Thomas calls both difficult.
- **Cutting macro allocations because markets are calm** (Drobny).
- **Reading the appendices as neutral**: Thomas runs macro products for Allianz. Drobny's firm advises macro funds.

## Worked Example: the arithmetic of breadth
1. Fix the skill: each bet is right 60% of the time.
2. Fix the promise: 10% a year.
3. One bet must be sized at 50%. Four years in ten lose half the fund.
4. Five independent bets are sized at 10% each. The loss of half the fund needs all five wrong, about once in a hundred years.
5. Same return. Volatility falls from about 50% to about 20%. Sharpe rises from 0.1 to 0.25.
6. Thomas extends the logic without further numbers: more independent bets push the ratio "toward the magical 1.0".
Note the tension with the interviews. Siva-Jothy, Andres Drobny and the anonymous manager each say only three or four real macro trades exist in a year.

## Key Takeaways
1. Count independent bets, not positions.
2. Test independence by correlation today, not by label.
3. Breadth raises the quality of returns. It does not raise the average return in Thomas's example.
4. Low volatility and heavy leverage together are Drobny's warning sign.
