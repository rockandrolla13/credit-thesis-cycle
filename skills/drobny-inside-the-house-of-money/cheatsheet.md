# Cheatsheet: Inside the House of Money (Drobny)

Every rule below is one interviewee's. The book gives no tests. Speakers disagree, and the table of disagreements matters as much as the rules.

## Decision rules
- When you have a view, ask for the trade, the stop and a ratio of at least 4 to 1. (Andres Drobny, Ch 6)
- When a stop has hit twice, count the losses. At 4 to 3, move on. (Andres Drobny, Ch 6)
- When your view is everyone's talk or on the front page, stand aside. (Andres Drobny, Ch 6. Rogers, Ch 11. Anonymous, Ch 16)
- When a peg is defended by rate hikes in a recession, treat the defense as finished. (Ch 2. Bessent, Ch 13)
- When carry is thin and holders are leveraged, leave. (Andres Drobny, Ch 6)
- When a dislocation hurts the U.S. economy, buy the front end in the most liquid form. (Siva-Jothy, Ch 5)
- When you are ahead on the year, size up. When at zero, protect the first 10%. (Siva-Jothy, Ch 5)
- When a trade wins for the wrong reason, exit. (Siva-Jothy, Ch 5)
- When forced sellers appear and you can hold, take the other side for the carry. (Porter, Ch 7)
- When a trade goes wrong for reasons you do not understand, cut it through a broker who does not know you. (Porter, Ch 7)
- When a move runs in a straight line and politicians object, pay attention. (Wadhwani, Ch 8)
- When there is a large misalignment, wait for a catalyst. (Wadhwani, Ch 8. Thiel, Ch 9)
- When you size, assume everything correlates. (Thiel, Ch 9) Or add up worst cases with no offsets. (Anderson, Ch 12)
- When the market disagrees, do not muscle it. Take the loss and re-enter later. (Harris, Ch 10)
- When a whole industry loses money and some firms fail, look for the bottom. (Rogers, Ch 11)
- When long, assume weak growth. When short, assume strong growth. (Anderson, Ch 12)
- When an asset is high-cost, do not buy it at any price. (Anderson, Ch 12)
- When shorting a mania, wait for the catalyst and short on the way down. (Bessent, Ch 13)
- When a country cracks, expect every instrument tied to it to fall. (Dimitrijević, Ch 14)
- When look-alikes sell off with a crisis country, check their real links and buy the unrelated ones. (Dimitrijević, Ch 14)
- When VAR falls because markets are calm, do not add to keep it level. (Gorton, Ch 15)
- When a disaster is under way, "chop all ropes". (Gorton, Ch 15)
- When the weakest credits lead the rally, the bull market is near its end. (Anonymous, Ch 16)

## Where the interviewees disagree
| Question | One side | Other side |
|---|---|---|
| Carry | A premium to earn (Leitner, Ch 4. Porter, Ch 7) | Synthetic short gamma, never (Siva-Jothy, Ch 5. Anonymous, Ch 16) |
| Options | Core tool (Leitner, Siva-Jothy, Wadhwani, Anonymous) | Lazy or slow (Thiel, Ch 9. Harris, Ch 10) |
| Stops | Essential (Porter, Wadhwani, A. Drobny) | Rarely used, entry matters (Bessent, Ch 13). From entry, not trailing (Thiel). Unsafe in non-continuous markets (Dimitrijević) |
| Price against you | Market is right, get out (Harris, Ch 10) | Irrelevant if fundamentals hold, be bigger (Anderson and Robertson, Ch 12) |
| After a drawdown | Cut risk and step away (A. Drobny, Siva-Jothy) | Keep the trade and the leverage (Anonymous, Ch 16) |
| Travel | Vital (Leitner, Rogers, Anderson, Dimitrijević) | Distraction (Siva-Jothy, A. Drobny, Harris, Bessent, Gorton) |
| Diversify | Yes, many premia or bets (Leitner. Thomas. Wadhwani) | No, few themes (Thiel. Porter. Anonymous) |
| Correlation | Assume perfect (Thiel) or ignore offsets (Anderson) | Use it to build the book (Thomas. Gorton and Standing) |
| Relative value | Run it with a macro hedge (Gorton and Standing, Ch 15) | Negative gamma, never (Anonymous, Ch 16). Source of systemic risk (Thiel, Ch 9) |
| Risk moved to funds | Makes the system absorb shocks (Standing, Ch 15) | Socialized, too big to fail (Harris, Ch 10). Leverage at every level (Bessent, Ch 13) |
| Central banks | Better, less volatility (Wadhwani. Gorton) | Buying time only (Siva-Jothy). Always wrong (Rogers) |
| Outcome of the debt overhang | Deflation (Thiel. A. Drobny's bias) | Printing press, dollar collapse (Harris. Rogers) |

## Tells and smells (2005 list, by speaker)
- High yield spreads very tight, volatility very low, managers needing 50 bp a month. (Porter, Ch 7)
- "Junk spreads have collapsed." No pay for the risk. (Anderson, Ch 12)
- Credit risk "cheap". CDS and yield-reaching through sold optionality. (Leitner, Ch 4)
- Credit markets running on the Slower Fool Theory. (Harris, Ch 10)
- Funds of funds leveraged five to one on leveraged funds. Prime brokers selling leverage. (Bessent, Ch 13)
- The same trades put on bigger each quarter as inflows arrive. (Bessent, Ch 13)
- Money wired with no documents. Money far above good ideas. (Thiel, Ch 9)
- MBAs rushing to one sector. (Thiel, Ch 9)
- Fees of 2% to 3% and 30% to 50%. (Rogers, Ch 11)
- Seventeen makers adding capacity for market share with no profit. (Dimitrijević on Chinese autos, Ch 14)
- Smooth 1% a month for years. (Anonymous, Ch 16. Gorton, Ch 15)
- Phrases such as "this has never happened before". (Dimitrijević, Ch 14)

## Process for a crowded-position question (assembled from the book's speakers, each step cited)
1. Who holds it, and with what leverage and redemption terms? (Bessent, Ch 13)
2. Can they hold through marks? (Porter, Ch 7)
3. What is exit liquidity against entry liquidity? (Anonymous, Ch 16)
4. Is the holder short volatility in effect? (Siva-Jothy, Ch 5. Standing, Ch 15)
5. What is the catalyst? Without one, wait. (Wadhwani, Ch 8. Bessent, Ch 13)
6. What survives the stop? (Wadhwani, Ch 8. Leitner, Ch 4)
