## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. View is not trade (Andres Drobny, Ch 6)
"Talk is cheap." Build the view. Compare it with what the market believes and how it is positioned. Name what is missed. Then set target and stop and require at least 4 to 1. Add each stopped-out loss to the risk side. At 4 to 3, move on. Be confident in a trade, never in a view.

### 2. Short gamma, stated and hidden (Siva-Jothy Ch 5, Anonymous Ch 16, Standing Ch 15)
Siva-Jothy: "Never, ever be short gamma." He counts carry trades as "essentially synthetic short gamma trades". The anonymous manager adds relative value: "Negative gamma is implicit in relative value trading because it works on regression to the mean." Standing agrees that "a lot of RV trades are inherently short volatility" and offsets it. Leitner (Ch 4) and Porter (Ch 7) take the other side and earn carry on purpose.

### 3. Liquidity is the risk (Ch 2, Ch 5, Ch 16)
- Drobny on LTCM: positions correlate "for no other reason than the fact that they were in LTCM's portfolio". "The problem with liquidity is that it is never there when really needed."
- Siva-Jothy: "liquidity comes at a cost, and a lack of liquidity comes at an even greater cost!"
- Anonymous: "The hedge funds that blow up are always the ones who misestimate how much liquidity is in the marketplace." In his 1998 loss, "the liquidity going in was a lot deeper than the liquidity getting out".

### 4. The forced seller and who can hold (Porter Ch 7, Bessent Ch 13)
Porter calls his edge "time horizon arbitrage". A bank book on accrual accounting takes "the other side of market distress". His Bund/Gilt trade was down £60m and later made about £180m. Bessent's family office kept "a segregated pile of money" and was never a forced seller in 1987. Druckenmiller's rule: never get backed into a corner.

### 5. The price of money and the risk curve (Anonymous, Ch 16)
"There is only one true macro trade and that's the price of money." Policy works "by encouraging people to move out along the risk curve". "The risk curve, in essence, is the credit curve": the 18-month to two-year rate, government bonds, corporate bonds, equities, then FX as "beta on beta". "The weaker credits always have the least liquidity and the so-called highest returns." "All the crap rallies at the end of a bull market."

### 6. Frozen ice theory (Anonymous, Ch 16)
A crowd starts in the sound, liquid trade (thick ice). It takes profit early, cannot buy back higher, and buys proxies further out in "liquidity and credit" (thin ice). When the liquid leg turns, all ask for prices at once.

### 7. Peg-break sequence (Ch 2, Ch 6, Ch 13)
A peg forces a rate policy that fights the domestic economy. Find the transmission. Bessent's was UK mortgages floating off the overnight rate. A defensive hike in a recession signals the end. Druckenmiller sized the 1992 trade to risk the year's 12% profit "but not more".

### 8. Trade your P&L (Siva-Jothy Ch 5, Bessent Ch 13)
Start small, build a cushion, lever up winners only. "There's a big difference between going from 30 percent to 20 percent, and going from zero to –10 percent." Siva-Jothy expects three or four real opportunities a year.

### 9. Central bank path against the forwards (Porter Ch 7, Wadhwani Ch 8)
Porter trades "the probability that a central bank will move interest rates in line with the market", mainly in one-year, one-year forwards, where the market extrapolates. Wadhwani, from inside the Bank of England: single data points matter less than markets think, because the committee smooths.

### 10. Paradox of perfection (Ch 3, Wadhwani Ch 8)
Independent central banks cut inflation and rate volatility. A focus on consumer prices let "huge asset market distortions" build. Wadhwani's policy view: do not target asset prices, but hold rates higher than the two-year inflation forecast needs when a bubble forms.

### 11. Global micro and adverse assumptions (Anderson, Ch 12)
"Pure Microeconomics 101." Assume strong growth when short and weak growth when long. Trade only what still works. "It is margins that revert to the mean", not prices. "You can't buy a high-cost asset cheap enough." Add up worst cases with no correlation offset.

### 12. Country as a company (Dimitrijević, Ch 14)
"When a country implodes, it acts like a company where management is discovered to have committed fraud." Cap each country at 10% to 15%. After a crisis, buy the look-alikes with no real link to it.

### 13. Risk premia plus home runs (Leitner, Ch 4)
Five buckets at 20% of risk each, stops in each, and up to 10% on a rare asymmetric idea. "To fight risk premia, you have to be doubly right."

### 14. Breadth arithmetic (Thomas, Appendix A)
At a 60% hit rate and a 10% target, one bet a year gives a Sharpe ratio of 0.1. Five independent bets give 0.25. Bets must be truly independent.

### 15. Stability breeds dislocation (Drobny, Appendix B)
"Long periods of stability are often followed by extreme dislocations as leveraged positions built up during the 'safe' environment cause significant bottlenecks."

The interviewees disagree on carry, options, stops, travel, diversification and whether risk held by funds is safer. See the disagreement table in [cheatsheet.md](cheatsheet.md).

## Use for Credit and Macro Questions

- **Credit spreads**: no model and no spread data. It gives a positioning lens. In 2005 Porter (Ch 7), Anderson (Ch 12), Leitner (Ch 4), Harris (Ch 10) and Bessent (Ch 13) each said credit was tight because of crowding and leverage. Bessent named corporate spreads as where he would be short in an unwind, and said he was not short because "you get killed while waiting". Standing (Ch 15) held the opposite view on systemic risk.
- **Debt-financed investment booms**: little. Thiel (Ch 9) gives bubble tells and a post-bubble chain. Dimitrijević (Ch 14) describes Chinese autos: 17 makers adding capacity for share with no profit. Anderson (Ch 12) gives the cost-curve test. Nothing on financing structures.
- **Inflation and Fed regimes**: useful. Wadhwani (Ch 8) on independence, anchoring and asset prices. Porter (Ch 7) on forwards against the policy path. Ch 2 on the Greenspan put, 1994 and 1998. The anonymous manager (Ch 16) on the Fed as the one central bank.
- **Issuer screening**: almost nothing. Anderson's low-cost and management tests and Rogers's industry-bottom signal (Ch 11) are the only items.
