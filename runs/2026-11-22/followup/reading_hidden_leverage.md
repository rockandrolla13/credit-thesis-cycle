# Reading list: hidden leverage and unidentified borrowers

Run date: 2026-11-23. Topic: debt that public bond prices do not show, and how to find the borrowers and lenders the market has not yet identified.

**How I verified.** A web search returned every item below, with the URL shown. I read search results and abstracts only. I did not open the full texts, so check page counts and exact figures against the source before you cite them. Research sources only. No trade ideas.

Ranked best first.

## 1. Building AI on credit: structures and spillovers (BIS Bulletin No 140)

- **Authors:** Mathias Drehmann, Egemen Eren
- **Year:** 2026 (27 October)
- **Type:** report (short central-bank bulletin, with an online annex)
- **Length:** not confirmed. BIS Bulletins are short.
- **Access:** free
- **URL:** https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-140-building-ai-credit-structures-and-spillovers.pdf
- **Method or case:** both
- **What it adds:** It maps the off-balance-sheet data-centre vehicle. A special purpose vehicle owns the data centre, earns contract income, and sells its debt to private credit funds, with insurers, funds and their bank lenders bearing the losses. That gives you the chain of holders to trace for the live 2026 case.
- **Weakness:** It measures spillovers on news days in public markets, so it says little about losses inside the private holders.
- **Companions (same URL family, verified):** BIS Quarterly Review box "Financing the AI infrastructure boom: on- and off-balance sheet borrowing", March 2026, https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2603u.htm . BIS Bulletin No 120 "Financing the AI boom: from cash flows to debt" (Aldasoro, Doerr, Rees, January 2026), https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull120.htm . BIS Bulletin No 128 on BDC exposure to software firms, https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull128.htm .

## 2. Bank Lending to Private Credit: Size, Characteristics, and Financial Stability Implications

- **Authors:** Berrospide, Cai, Lewis-Hayre, Zikes (Federal Reserve Board)
- **Year:** 2025 (23 May)
- **Type:** report (FEDS Note)
- **Length:** not confirmed
- **Access:** free
- **URL:** https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/bank-lending-to-private-credit-size-characteristics-and-financial-stability-implications-20250523.html
- **Method or case:** method
- **What it adds:** It shows how to match BDCs and private debt funds to bank loan records in the Y-14Q Schedule H.1 data. The match gives committed and drawn credit lines per vehicle, which is the bank-to-fund link that bond prices do not show.
- **Weakness:** The Y-14 data are confidential, so an outside investor can copy the logic but not the dataset.
- **Companions:** FEDS Note "The Price of Bank Funding Behind Private Credit: Evidence from Business Development Companies" (7 August 2026), https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/the-price-of-bank-funding-behind-private-credit-evidence-from-business-development-companies-20260807.html . Haque, Jang and Wang, FEDS 2025-059, https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2025059pap.pdf .

## 3. Where Do Banks End and NBFIs Begin?

- **Authors:** Viral V. Acharya, Nicola Cetorelli, Bruce Tuckman
- **Year:** 2024 (NBER Working Paper 32316; also New York Fed Staff Report 1119)
- **Type:** paper
- **Length:** not confirmed
- **Access:** free via the New York Fed
- **URL:** https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr1119 (NBER: https://www.nber.org/papers/w32316)
- **Method or case:** both
- **What it adds:** It uses the Fed "From Whom to Whom" accounts to show that banks and non-banks fund each other, with non-banks dependent on bank credit lines. It gives the frame for the whole search: risk did not leave the banks, it changed form into contingent credit lines.
- **Weakness:** The evidence is at sector level, so it does not name individual exposed lenders.

## 4. Introducing Private Credit into the Financial Accounts of the United States

- **Authors:** Katie Brinkman and seven co-authors (Federal Reserve Board)
- **Year:** 2026 (16 October)
- **Type:** report (FEDS Note, data documentation)
- **Length:** not confirmed
- **Access:** free
- **URL:** https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/introducing-private-credit-into-the-financial-accounts-of-the-united-states-20261016.html
- **Method or case:** method (data source)
- **What it adds:** From the September 2026 Z.1 release, the Financial Accounts carry a separate sector for each private credit vehicle type, with data from 2012:Q4. This is a public, quarterly series for who holds private credit and who funds the vehicles.
- **Weakness:** The Fed labels the integration preliminary, and the series are aggregates with no borrower names.
- **Companion:** FEDS Note "U.S. Private Credit Vehicles: Institutional Structure and Aggregate Balance Sheets" (9 November 2026), https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/u-s-private-credit-vehicles-institutional-structure-and-aggregate-balance-sheets-20261109.html .

## 5. Private Debt versus Bank Debt in Corporate Borrowing

- **Authors:** Sharjil Haque, Simon Mayer, Irina Stefanescu
- **Year:** 2025 (working paper, several versions)
- **Type:** paper
- **Length:** not confirmed
- **Access:** free
- **URL:** https://www.fdic.gov/center-financial-research/private-debt-versus-bank-debt-corporate-borrowing.pdf (summary: https://www.hoover.org/research/private-debt-versus-bank-debt-corporate-borrowing)
- **Method or case:** method
- **What it adds:** It joins Y-14 bank loans to PitchBook private debt deals and finds "dual borrowers" that owe both. About half of private debt borrowers also hold bank credit lines, and they draw more bank credit after taking private debt, so one borrower's stress reaches banks and funds together.
- **Weakness:** About 80% of the private loans are sponsor-backed buyout loans, so the sample is far from data-centre project debt.

## 6. Securitization without risk transfer

- **Authors:** Viral V. Acharya, Philipp Schnabl, Gustavo Suarez
- **Year:** 2013 (Journal of Financial Economics 107(3), pages 515-536)
- **Type:** paper
- **Length:** 22 journal pages
- **Access:** free as NBER Working Paper 15730 and on the author page
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w15730 (author copy: https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/pschnabl/research/AcharyaSchnablSuarez2013.pdf)
- **Method or case:** both
- **What it adds:** It is the standard case of 2007 conduits. Banks guaranteed off-balance-sheet vehicles, and the losses came back to the sponsor banks. The method transfers directly: find who gave the liquidity or credit guarantee, because that party owns the risk.
- **Weakness:** The data are a hand-collected panel that ends in 2008, and today's vehicles use different guarantee forms.
- **Companions:** Pozsar, Adrian, Ashcraft, Boesky, "Shadow Banking", New York Fed Staff Report 458 (2010, revised 2012), https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr458.pdf . Covitz, Liang, Suarez, "The Evolution of a Financial Crisis: Collapse of the Asset-Backed Commercial Paper Market" (Journal of Finance 2013), free Fed version at https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/the-evolution-of-a-financial-crisis-panic-in-the-asset-backed-commercial-paper-market.htm .

## 7. Contagion in Financial Networks

- **Authors:** Paul Glasserman, H. Peyton Young
- **Year:** 2016 (Journal of Economic Literature 54(3), pages 779-831)
- **Type:** paper (survey)
- **Length:** 53 journal pages. The 2015 OFR working paper version has 76 pages.
- **Access:** free via LSE Research Online
- **URL:** https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/68681 (OFR version: https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ofr:wpaper:15-21)
- **Method or case:** method
- **What it adds:** It is the standard survey of network contagion models, built on the Eisenberg-Noe clearing framework. It gives metrics for how leverage, size, common exposures and short-term funding turn one default into many, including when you do not know the full network.
- **Weakness:** It covers interbank networks, and it predates private credit funds.
- **Companion:** Acemoglu, Ozdaglar, Tahbaz-Salehi, "Systemic Risk and Stability in Financial Networks" (American Economic Review 2015), free at https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/100979 . It shows dense links help with small shocks and spread large ones.

## 8. Credit Shock Propagation Along Supply Chains: Evidence from the CDS Market

- **Authors:** Senay Agca, Volodymyr Babich, John Birge, Jing Wu
- **Year:** 2022 (Management Science 68(9), pages 6506-6538)
- **Type:** paper
- **Length:** 33 journal pages
- **Access:** journal is paid. Free working paper version at GWU.
- **URL:** https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2021.4174 (free: https://www2.gwu.edu/~iiep/assets/docs/papers/2021WP/AgcaIIEP2021-18.pdf)
- **Method or case:** method
- **What it adds:** It measures how a credit shock at one firm moves the CDS spreads of its suppliers and customers. Trade credit and large sales exposure amplify the effect, and adverse shocks reach the second and third tier, which is the template for chip, power and construction suppliers to data-centre builders.
- **Weakness:** It needs firms with traded CDS, so private suppliers fall outside the sample.
- **Companion:** Barrot and Sauvagnat, "Input Specificity and the Propagation of Idiosyncratic Shocks in Production Networks" (Quarterly Journal of Economics 2016), free at https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/111134 .

## 9. Non-Traded Business Development Companies

- **Authors:** Anadu, Wasner (Federal Reserve Bank of Boston)
- **Year:** 2026 (November)
- **Type:** paper (Boston Fed Supervisory Research and Analysis series)
- **Length:** not confirmed
- **Access:** free
- **URL:** https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/risk-and-policy-analysis/2026/non-traded-business-development-companies.aspx (PDF: https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/Workingpapers/PDF/2026/SRA2603.pdf)
- **Method or case:** method
- **What it adds:** It compares the mark on the same loan across BDCs at the same date. The gap between marks links to fund leverage, more so for non-traded BDCs, so cross-holder mark dispersion becomes a usable signal of hidden stress.
- **Weakness:** It covers BDCs only, which file public schedules of investments. Closed private funds stay dark.
- **Companion (industry, not academic):** FTI Consulting, "Private Credit's Valuation Problem" (2024), https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/private-credit-valuation-problem . It compares marks on 34 loans held by two or more large BDCs.

## 10. The Rise and Risks of Private Credit (Global Financial Stability Report, April 2024, Chapter 2)

- **Authors:** International Monetary Fund staff
- **Year:** 2024
- **Type:** report (chapter)
- **Length:** not confirmed
- **Access:** free
- **URL:** https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/GFSR/Issues/2024/04/16/global-financial-stability-report-april-2024
- **Method or case:** both
- **What it adds:** It is the standard single map of private credit vulnerabilities: borrower quality, stale valuations, layers of leverage, and links to insurers and pension funds. It lists which data are missing, which tells you where unidentified exposure is most likely to sit.
- **Weakness:** It predates the AI data-centre build, and it admits that data limits block a firm estimate of total risk.
- **Companion:** FSB "Global Monitoring Report on Nonbank Financial Intermediation 2025" (16 December 2025), https://www.fsb.org/2025/12/global-monitoring-report-on-nonbank-financial-intermediation-2025/ .

## 11. Shadow Insurance

- **Authors:** Ralph S. J. Koijen, Motohiro Yogo
- **Year:** 2016 (Econometrica 84(3), pages 1265-1287)
- **Type:** paper
- **Length:** 23 journal pages
- **Access:** free as NBER Working Paper 19568 and Minneapolis Fed staff report
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w19568 (Minneapolis Fed: https://www.minneapolisfed.org/research/staff-reports/shadow-insurance)
- **Method or case:** both
- **What it adds:** It uses statutory filings to trace liabilities that life insurers moved to unrated, off-balance-sheet reinsurers in the same group. It then recomputes capital with those liabilities added back, which is the method to apply to today's offshore reinsurance.
- **Weakness:** The data end in 2012, before private-equity-owned insurers and Bermuda sidecars grew large.
- **Companions:** Foley-Fisher, Heinrich, Verani, "Capturing the Illiquidity Premium", later titled "Are Life Insurers the New Shadow Banks?" (2020, revised 2023); copy seen at https://retirementincomejournal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/SSRN-Foley-Fisher-2023-shadow-banks.pdf . BIS Quarterly Review, "Shifting landscapes: life insurance and financial stability" (September 2024), https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2409b.htm .

## 12. Do operating leases expand credit capacity? Evidence from borrowing costs and credit ratings

- **Authors:** Lim, Mann, Mihov
- **Year:** 2017 (Journal of Corporate Finance 42, pages 100-114)
- **Type:** paper
- **Length:** 15 journal pages
- **Access:** paid (journal)
- **URL:** https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:corfin:v:42:y:2017:i:c:p:100-114
- **Method or case:** method
- **What it adds:** It shows that loan pricing and credit ratings respond less to off-balance-sheet lease debt than to balance-sheet debt. Firms near the investment-grade line lease more, so heavy lease use is a marker for leverage the market undercounts.
- **Weakness:** The sample predates the 2019 lease accounting change, and it does not cover data-centre leases or vendor financing.
- **Companion:** Federal Reserve IFDP 1441 on leverage after the 2019 lease accounting change, https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/ifdp/files/ifdp1441.pdf .

## Considered and left out of the top 12

- **Cordell, Roberts, Schwert, "CLO Performance"** (Journal of Finance 2023; NBER w29410, https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29410/w29410.pdf). It is the standard CLO reference, but it studies returns, not who holds the tranches.
- **Chernenko, Ialenti, Scharfstein, "Bank Capital and the Growth of Private Credit"** (2024-2026 drafts, https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/mrcbg/programs/growthpolicy/bank-capital-and-growth-private-credit). It computes bank-style capital ratios for BDCs. Useful, but it argues BDCs are well capitalised and does not map exposures.
- **Weiss, "Private Credit and Systemic Risk"** (Harvard M-RCBG, June 2025, https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/mrcbg/programs/growthpolicy/private-credit-systemic-risk). I could not confirm the full author list, so I left it out of the ranking.
- **Matvos, Piskorski, Seru** NBER Working Paper 34991 on private credit fund balance sheets (https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34991/w34991.pdf). The search gave the finding (equity of 65-80% of assets) but not the title, so I did not rank it. It is the main counter-view.

## Not found

1. **No book.** I found no book that gives a method for hidden leverage. Every strong item is a paper or an official report.
2. **Vendor financing and chip-supplier credit.** I found nothing rigorous on vendor financing or "circular" supplier-customer funding in AI. The BIS items touch it only in passing.
3. **Data-centre lease obligations.** The lease literature is general corporate finance. I found no study that measures hyperscaler lease commitments as debt.
4. **Private fund marks outside BDCs.** I found no academic paper on valuation marks in closed private credit funds. The evidence is BDC-only or industry commentary.
5. **CLO and insurer holder maps.** I found no recent public paper that maps which insurers and banks hold which CLO or asset-based finance tranches. NAIC filings are the raw source, but I found no method paper on using them.
6. **SNC review as a data source.** I found no research paper that uses the Shared National Credit review to trace non-bank exposure.
7. **Asset-based finance.** I found no standard reference for asset-based finance held by insurers.
