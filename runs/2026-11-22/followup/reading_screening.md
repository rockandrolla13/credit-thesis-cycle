# Reading list: issuer screening under high inflation and slow growth

Date: 2026-11-23. Scope: sources for the 11-variable issuer screen and for the regime-probability question.

How I checked: every item below appeared in a web search result on 2026-11-23. I read abstracts and summaries, not full texts. Verify any number against the original before you use it as a threshold.

Ranking rule: an item ranks higher when it gives a tested method or a number that maps directly onto a screen variable or onto the regime probability.

---

## 1. In Search of Distress Risk

- **Authors:** John Y. Campbell, Jens Hilscher, Jan Szilagyi
- **Year:** 2008 (Journal of Finance 63(6), pp. 2899-2939). Working paper version: NBER 12362, 2006.
- **Type:** Journal paper. About 40 pages.
- **Access:** Free as NBER working paper and through Harvard DASH. Journal version is paid.
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w12362
- **Method or case:** Method.
- **Informs:** Net leverage, equity drawdown, margin change (profitability). It also gives the back-test design.
- **What it adds:** The paper fits a failure-hazard model on US firms from 1963 to 2003. The predictors are leverage, profitability, market capitalisation, past stock return, equity volatility, cash, market-to-book and share price. This is the closest published template for a multi-variable issuer screen with out-of-sample testing.
- **Weakness:** The sample ends before 2008 and has few high-inflation years after the early 1980s, and the model has no interest-coverage or refinancing variable.

## 2. The Information in Interest Coverage Ratios of the US Nonfinancial Corporate Sector

- **Authors:** Francisco Palomino, Stephen Paolillo, Ander Perez-Orive, Gerardo Sanz-Maldonado
- **Year:** 2019 (FEDS Notes, 10 January 2019)
- **Type:** Federal Reserve staff note. Short.
- **Access:** Free.
- **URL:** https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/information-in-interest-coverage-ratios-of-the-us-nonfinancial-corporate-sector-20190110.html
- **Method or case:** Both.
- **Informs:** Interest coverage now. Cyclicality (the note shows thresholds differ by sector).
- **What it adds:** The note builds coverage ratios from Compustat for 1970 to 2017, so it covers the 1970s. The search summary reports a link from aggregate coverage to default rates: coverage near 3.4 goes with a 1.5% default rate, and coverage near 1.7 goes with about 3%.
- **Weakness:** The mapping is aggregate, and the authors say the distress level depends on the firm and the sector, so one threshold does not fit all issuers.
- **Companion (same series, verified):** "Interest Coverage Ratios: Assessing Vulnerabilities in Nonfinancial Corporate Credit", FEDS Notes, 3 December 2020. https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/interest-coverage-ratios-assessing-vulnerabilities-in-nonfinancial-corporate-credit-20201203.htm

## 3. Interest Expenses, Coverage Ratio, and Firm Distress

- **Authors:** Federal Reserve Bank of Boston staff (Current Policy Perspectives series). I did not confirm the author names.
- **Year:** 2023 (August)
- **Type:** Reserve Bank policy paper.
- **Access:** Free.
- **URL:** https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/current-policy-perspectives/2023/interest-expenses-coverage-ratio-and-firm-distress
- **Method or case:** Both.
- **Informs:** Interest coverage at refinancing rates, floating-rate share, refinancing need.
- **What it adds:** The paper measures how fast policy-rate rises reach corporate interest expense, using Compustat for 1995 to 2019. The search summary reports that a 1-point rise in the policy rate lifts the average interest expense ratio by about 0.5 point, with the peak five quarters later. This gives a tested lag and size for the "coverage at refinancing rates" variable.
- **Weakness:** The estimate comes from a low-inflation sample and from listed firms only.

## 4. Inflation and Growth Risk: Balancing the Scales with Surveys

- **Authors:** Jean-Paul Renne, Sarah Mouabbi, Adrien Tschopp
- **Year:** 2026 (Banque de France Working Paper 1036, 25 February 2026)
- **Type:** Central bank working paper.
- **Access:** Free.
- **URL:** https://www.banque-france.fr/en/publications-and-statistics/publications/inflation-and-growth-risk-balancing-scales-surveys
- **Method or case:** Method.
- **Informs:** The regime-probability question.
- **What it adds:** The paper outputs a stagflation probability for the US. It defines stagflation as inflation above 4% and GDP growth below 0%, year over year, and it uses survey forecasts in a dynamic factor model with stochastic volatility. The companion note reports the probability rose in 2022 after three decades near zero.
- **Weakness:** The model depends on survey data that start in 1981, so it has no fit to the 1970s, and the thresholds are the authors' choice.
- **Companion (verified):** "US inflation and growth risk through expert eyes", Banque de France Eco Notepad 450, May 2026. https://www.banque-france.fr/en/publications-and-statistics/publications/us-inflation-and-growth-risk-through-expert-eyes

## 5. Issuer Quality and Corporate Bond Returns

- **Authors:** Robin Greenwood, Samuel G. Hanson
- **Year:** 2013 (Review of Financial Studies 26(6), pp. 1483-1525). Earlier version: NBER 17197, "Issuer Quality and the Credit Cycle".
- **Type:** Journal paper. About 43 pages.
- **Access:** Free through Harvard DASH and NBER. Journal version is paid.
- **URL:** https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-d54b-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b
- **Method or case:** Method.
- **Informs:** Spread per turn of leverage, and the timing layer of the screen (when weak issuers are overpriced).
- **What it adds:** The paper shows issuer quality falls in credit booms, and that the fall predicts low excess returns on corporate bonds. The working-paper version reports R-squared as high as 30% at a three-year horizon, and the authors find issuer quality is a more reliable overheating signal than aggregate credit growth.
- **Weakness:** The signal is aggregate and slow, so it ranks periods and does not rank issuers.

## 6. Credit Spreads and Business Cycle Fluctuations

- **Authors:** Simon Gilchrist, Egon Zakrajsek
- **Year:** 2012 (American Economic Review 102(4), pp. 1692-1720). Working paper: NBER 17021.
- **Type:** Journal paper. About 29 pages.
- **Access:** Free as NBER working paper PDF. Journal version is paid.
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w17021
- **Method or case:** Method.
- **Informs:** Spread per turn of leverage, and the growth leg of the regime probability.
- **What it adds:** The paper splits the credit spread into an expected-default part and a residual, the excess bond premium. The residual carries the power to predict downturns. The same split tells the screen how much of an issuer's spread pays for default risk and how much is market risk appetite.
- **Weakness:** The premium predicts growth, not inflation, so it covers only one half of a stagflation call.

## 7. Monitoring Joint Tail Risks: An Application to Growth and Inflation

- **Authors:** Valentina Corradi, Jordi Llorens-Terrazas
- **Year:** 2024 on SSRN. The University of Surrey repository lists it in the Journal of Econometrics, vol. 255, 2026.
- **Type:** Journal paper.
- **Access:** Free on SSRN. Journal version is paid.
- **URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4977011
- **Method or case:** Method.
- **Informs:** The regime-probability question.
- **What it adds:** The paper joins growth-at-risk and inflation-at-risk into one "frontier" and estimates the conditional probability of weak growth with high inflation. It also tests how tighter financial conditions move that joint risk.
- **Weakness:** The method is econometrically heavy, and I did not confirm the sample period or the out-of-sample record.

## 8. Stress Testing the Corporate Debt Servicing Capacity: A Scenario Analysis

- **Authors:** Federal Reserve Board staff (FEDS Notes). I did not confirm the author names.
- **Year:** 2024 (9 May 2024)
- **Type:** Federal Reserve staff note.
- **Access:** Free.
- **URL:** https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/stress-testing-the-corporate-debt-servicing-capacity-a-scenario-analysis-20240509.html
- **Method or case:** Both.
- **Informs:** Interest coverage at refinancing rates, refinancing need, floating-rate share.
- **What it adds:** The note projects coverage ratios for US public nonfinancial firms as maturing debt reprices at higher rates. It credits the large fixed-rate share for the slow pass-through in 2022-2023. That is the documented case for the maturity-wall variables.
- **Weakness:** The projection covers public firms, and it is a scenario exercise, not a back-test against realised defaults.
- **Companion (verified):** "Private Firm Repayment Vulnerabilities and Adverse Economic Conditions", FEDS Notes, 16 May 2023. The search summary says it projects coverage for private firms under a stagflation scenario. https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/private-firm-repayment-vulnerabilities-and-adverse-economic-conditions-20230516.html

## 9. Corporate Financial Distress, Restructuring, and Bankruptcy (4th edition)

- **Authors:** Edward I. Altman, Edith Hotchkiss, Wei Wang
- **Year:** 2019 (Wiley)
- **Type:** Book. 368 pages. ISBN 978-1-119-48180-5.
- **Access:** Paid.
- **URL:** https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/corporate-financial-distress/9781119481805/
- **Method or case:** Both.
- **Informs:** Net leverage, interest coverage, margin change. It also covers the high-yield market and recovery.
- **What it adds:** The book holds the Z-score family of distress models in one place, with their applications. It also covers the high-yield bond market, restructuring outcomes and distressed investing, which gives practitioner rules to sit beside the academic models.
- **Weakness:** The Z-score coefficients come from old samples, and the book is not about inflation regimes.

## 10. 2024 Annual Global Corporate Default and Rating Transition Study

- **Authors:** S&P Global Ratings
- **Year:** 2025 (27 March 2025). A US edition followed on 29 April 2025.
- **Type:** Rating agency default study. Published every year.
- **Access:** Free to read on the S&P site. Some content may need a free registration, and I did not test that.
- **URL:** https://www.spglobal.com/ratings/en/research/articles/250327-default-transition-and-recovery-2024-annual-global-corporate-default-and-rating-transition-study-13452126
- **Method or case:** Case (base rates).
- **Informs:** Honest back-testing. It gives default base rates by rating and year against which to score the screen.
- **What it adds:** The study gives realised default and transition rates by rating grade over a long history. The search summary reports 145 global defaults in 2024 against 153 in 2023. A screen that cannot beat the rating-implied base rate adds nothing.
- **Weakness:** The data cover rated issuers only, and the S&P series does not reach back through the full 1970s episode in depth.
- **Note:** The edition for 2025 data exists. I saw an S&P webinar page for it but not the report URL.

## 11. The Rise of Zombie Firms: Causes and Consequences

- **Authors:** Ryan Banerjee, Boris Hofmann
- **Year:** 2018 (BIS Quarterly Review, September 2018)
- **Type:** BIS special feature. Short.
- **Access:** Free.
- **URL:** https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1809g.htm
- **Method or case:** Both.
- **Informs:** Interest coverage now.
- **What it adds:** The feature gives a published, reusable rule: coverage below 1 for three years in a row, and firm age of at least 10 years. It applies the rule to listed firms in 14 advanced economies from 1980 to 2016. A later working paper by the same authors (BIS Working Paper 882) puts the zombie share at 15% in 2017.
- **Weakness:** The rule flags firms that survived because rates were low, so its hit rate in a high-rate regime is untested in this paper.

## 12. High Inflation: Low Default Risk and Low Equity Valuations

- **Authors:** Harjoat S. Bhamra, Christian Dorion, Alexandre Jeanneret, Michael Weber
- **Year:** 2023 (Review of Financial Studies 36(3), pp. 1192-1252). Earlier version: NBER 25317.
- **Type:** Journal paper. About 60 pages.
- **Access:** Free through the University of Chicago repository. Journal version is paid.
- **URL:** https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/5098/files/High-Inflation-Low-Default-Risk-and-Low-Equity-Valuations.pdf
- **Method or case:** Method.
- **Informs:** Net leverage, floating-rate share, equity drawdown. It challenges the premise of the screen.
- **What it adds:** The paper argues that higher expected inflation lowers credit spreads for firms with fixed nominal coupons, because inflation erodes real debt. It also predicts the equity hit is larger for low-leverage firms. The screen needs this counter-argument, because it says fixed-rate leveraged issuers can gain from inflation while floating-rate issuers do not.
- **Weakness:** The result concerns expected inflation with sticky cash flows, and it does not model a growth slowdown at the same time.

## Not found

- **Moody's annual default study.** The search confirmed the series exists and reaches back to 1920. It did not return a working Moody's URL, so I dropped it. Moody's data would cover the 1970s better than S&P.
- **Issuer-level credit case for the 1970s.** I found no paper that tracks which US corporate issuers defaulted or widened in 1973-1982 by leverage or coverage. Items 2 and the Giesecke paper are the nearest.
- **Evidence for four screen variables.** I found no tested source for coupon step-up, customer concentration, cost intensity, or spread per turn of leverage as default predictors. Their thresholds stay judgment calls for now.
- **Peer-reviewed work on margins and pricing power under inflation.** I found only the Kansas City Fed article and an advocacy brief. Neither links margins to default.
- **A Fed, ECB or IMF paper that outputs a stagflation probability.** The two that do (items 4 and 7) come from the Banque de France and from academics.
- **IMF GFSR 2026 corporate chapters.** The search returned editions up to October 2025 only.
- **Full texts.** I read no full paper. Every number above comes from an abstract or a search summary.
