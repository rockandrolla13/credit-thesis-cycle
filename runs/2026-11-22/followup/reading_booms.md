# Reading list: debt-financed technology booms and how they ended

Scout run: 2026-11-23. Topic: analogues and methods for the 2026 AI data-centre debt build.

Every item below appeared in a web search result during this run. I read search summaries only. I did not open or download the full texts. Check any figure against the original before you use it.

## Ranked items

### 1. Financing the AI Buildout
- **Author:** Stijn Van Nieuwerburgh
- **Year:** 2026 (October), NBER Working Paper 35865
- **Type:** paper. Length not confirmed.
- **Access:** NBER page is free to view. The PDF may need a subscription.
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w35865
- **Method or case:** both
- **What it adds:** It maps the move from cash-flow funding to off-balance-sheet structures and prices a 1 GW campus at about $41 billion. It names the core mismatch: long-term debt against assets, chips above all, that may lose value fast.
- **Weakness:** It is a working paper on a live episode, so it has no outcome data.

### 2. BIS series on AI financing (Bulletins 120 and 140, plus the March 2026 Quarterly Review box)
- **Authors:** Bulletin 140 is by Drehmann and Eren. I did not verify the authors of Bulletin 120 or the Quarterly Review box.
- **Year:** 2026 (7 January, 16 March, 27 October)
- **Type:** short policy papers. Lengths not confirmed.
- **Access:** free
- **URLs:**
  - https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull120.htm ("Financing the AI boom: from cash flows to debt")
  - https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2603u.htm ("Financing the AI infrastructure boom: on- and off-balance sheet borrowing")
  - https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-140-building-ai-credit-structures-and-spillovers.pdf (Bulletin 140)
- **Method or case:** both
- **What it adds:** Bulletin 140 asks who bears the loss if returns disappoint, structure by structure. It also measures spillovers from AI-financing news days to Treasury and investment-grade markets, which is a reusable event-study method.
- **Weakness:** The notes are short and descriptive, and the spillover sample covers only 2026.

### 3. Collective hallucinations and inefficient markets: The British Railway Mania of the 1840s
- **Author:** Andrew Odlyzko
- **Year:** 2010 (preliminary working paper)
- **Type:** paper. 187 pages of main text, plus 63 of appendices and 54 of endnotes.
- **Access:** free
- **URL:** https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/hallucinations.pdf
- **Method or case:** case, with a method inside it
- **What it adds:** It documents a boom where vocal critics existed and investors still ignored the warnings until the lines opened. The lesson for AI is timing: the damage arrived when the assets started operating, not when doubts first appeared.
- **Weakness:** Gareth Campbell's econometric work disputes the bubble reading, and the paper is long and unrefereed.

### 4. Capital Returns: Investing Through the Capital Cycle. A Money Manager's Reports 2002-15
- **Author:** Edward Chancellor (editor), reports by Marathon Asset Management
- **Year:** 2015 hardback (Palgrave Macmillan). The Springer e-book carries 2016.
- **Type:** book. Length not confirmed.
- **Access:** paid
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-137-57165-6
- **Method or case:** method
- **What it adds:** It gives the capital-cycle method: track capital flowing into an industry, because heavy inflows depress future returns. That is the direct supply-side test to apply to data-centre capacity.
- **Weakness:** A CFA Institute review found the compiled reports uneven, and the book is written for equity holders, not creditors.

### 5. Real and Financial Industry Booms and Busts
- **Authors:** Gerard Hoberg and Gordon Phillips
- **Year:** 2010, Journal of Finance 65(1), pages 45-86. NBER Working Paper 14290 (2008).
- **Type:** paper. 42 journal pages.
- **Access:** NBER page is free to view. The journal version is paid.
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w14290
- **Method or case:** method
- **What it adds:** It shows that in competitive industries, high valuation, investment and financing predict weaker cash flows and negative abnormal returns. In concentrated industries the pattern is weak, which matters because hyperscalers and neoclouds sit in different competitive positions.
- **Weakness:** The outcome variables are equity returns and cash flows, not defaults or credit losses.

### 6. Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital: The Dynamics of Bubbles and Golden Ages
- **Author:** Carlota Perez
- **Year:** 2002 (Edward Elgar). Some listings say 2003.
- **Type:** book. xix + 198 pages.
- **Access:** paid
- **URL:** https://e-elgar.com/shop/technological-revolutions-and-financial-capital
- **Method or case:** method
- **What it adds:** It links technology and finance in one long-run cycle of boom, crash and later build-out. It gives a frame for asking which phase the AI build is in.
- **Weakness:** The frame is qualitative and it gives no measurable threshold or date.

### 7. Corporate Bond Default Risk: A 150-Year Perspective
- **Authors:** Kay Giesecke, Francis Longstaff, Stephen Schaefer, Ilya Strebulaev
- **Year:** 2011, Journal of Financial Economics 102(2), pages 233-250. NBER Working Paper 15848 (2010).
- **Type:** paper. 18 journal pages.
- **Access:** NBER page is free to view. The journal version is paid.
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w15848
- **Method or case:** both
- **What it adds:** It puts a number on the US railroad bust: defaults in 1873-1875 reached about 36 percent of the par value of the whole corporate bond market. It also tests which variables forecast default rates and finds that credit spreads do not adjust to realized defaults.
- **Weakness:** It covers the whole bond market and says little about how railroad debt was structured.

### 8. Bubbles for Fama
- **Authors:** Robin Greenwood, Andrei Shleifer, Yang You
- **Year:** 2019, Journal of Financial Economics 131(1), pages 20-43. NBER Working Paper 23191 (2017).
- **Type:** paper. 24 journal pages.
- **Access:** free copy on the author page
- **URL:** https://shleifer.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/bubbles-fama
- **Method or case:** method
- **What it adds:** It shows that a sharp industry price run-up raises crash probability, using US industries from 1926 to 2014. It names run-up attributes that help forecast the crash: volatility, turnover, issuance and the price path.
- **Weakness:** It studies equity prices only and it does not cover debt.

### 9. Doing Capitalism in the Innovation Economy (second edition)
- **Author:** William H. Janeway
- **Year:** 2018 (Cambridge University Press). First edition 2012.
- **Type:** book. Length not confirmed.
- **Access:** paid
- **URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/9781108558440/type/book
- **Method or case:** method
- **What it adds:** It frames innovation funding as a three-player game between markets, speculators and the state. It helps separate a boom that leaves useful infrastructure from one that only destroys capital.
- **Weakness:** It is a practitioner memoir and essay, and I could not confirm that it treats debt finance in depth.

### 10. Oil and debt
- **Authors:** Dietrich Domanski, Jonathan Kearns, Marco Lombardi, Hyun Song Shin
- **Year:** 2015, BIS Quarterly Review, March
- **Type:** paper. 11 pages.
- **Access:** free
- **URL:** https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1503f.htm
- **Method or case:** case
- **What it adds:** It documents the shale analogue: oil and gas sector debt rose from about $1 trillion in 2006 to about $2.5 trillion in 2014, much of it at smaller shale firms. It argues that leverage amplified the price fall and the cut in investment.
- **Weakness:** The authors wrote it early in the bust, so it does not cover the 2015-2016 defaults.

### 11. Beyond Valuations: Long-Term Financial Risks from the AI Build-Out
- **Authors:** Padma Sharma and Pierce George
- **Year:** 2026 (5 October), Kansas City Fed Economic Bulletin
- **Type:** short paper. Length not confirmed.
- **Access:** free
- **URL:** https://www.kansascityfed.org/research/economic-bulletin/beyond-valuations-long-term-financial-risks-from-the-ai-build-out/
- **Method or case:** case
- **What it adds:** It compares leverage across the AI value chain, including neoclouds, data-centre operators and utilities. It traces where the debt sits: bank commercial real estate loans, REITs, CMBS and ABS.
- **Weakness:** It is brief, and its private credit data stop at 2025.

### 12. Internet growth: Myth and reality, use and abuse
- **Author:** Andrew Odlyzko
- **Year:** 2000
- **Type:** paper, described on the author's reading list as brief
- **Access:** free
- **URL:** https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/internet.growth.myth2.pdf
- **Method or case:** both
- **What it adds:** It tests the demand claim behind the fibre boom against measured traffic. The method transfers directly: check the growth story that justifies the capital spending against real usage data.
- **Weakness:** The author wrote it before the crash, and it says nothing about debt or vendor financing.

## Verified, but did not make the top 12

- Quinn and Turner, *Boom and Bust: A Global History of Financial Bubbles* (Cambridge, 2020). The "bubble triangle" of marketability, credit and speculation. https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/D09C2E3BEA798F6EDC9D3880FC0300ED
- Goldfarb and Kirsch, *Bubbles and Crashes: The Boom and Bust of Technological Innovation* (Stanford, 2019, 264 pages). It compares technologies that did and did not produce bubbles. https://sup.org/books/title/?id=24950
- Minsky, *Stabilizing an Unstable Economy* (1986, reissued McGraw-Hill 2008). Hedge, speculative and Ponzi finance. https://www.levyinstitute.org/publications/stabilizing-an-unstable-economy/
- Cudahy and Henderson, "From Insull to Enron" (Energy Law Journal 26, 2005). The only verified item on the 1920s utility holding companies. https://www.repository.law.indiana.edu/facpub/308
- Campbell and Turner, "Dispelling the Myth of the Naive Investor during the British Railway Mania, 1845-1846" (Business History Review, 2012). The counter-view to Odlyzko. https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/dispelling-the-myth-of-the-naive-investor-during-the-british-rail/
- Nairn, *Engines That Move Markets* (second edition, Harriman House, 2018, 570 pages). https://www.harriman-house.com/enginesthatmovemarkets
