# Reading list: bond supply, what is priced, and balance-sheet reduction

Scout run: 2026-11-23. Question served: "how much of next year's AI-related corporate debt supply is already priced into US IG and HY spreads".

**How I verified.** Every item below appeared in a web search result that I saw in this run. I read search summaries and abstracts only. I did not open or download the full texts. Check any number against the paper before you use it.

---

## Ranked list (best first)

### 1. Institutional Corporate Bond Pricing
- **Authors:** Lorenzo Bretscher, Lukas Schmid, Ishita Sen, Varun Sharma
- **Year:** 2026 (Review of Financial Studies 39(3), 605-660). Earlier drafts carried the title "Institutional Corporate Bond Demand".
- **Type:** journal article. Length: about 56 journal pages.
- **Access:** journal is paid. Free drafts exist (NBER conference draft, Swiss Finance Institute paper 21-07).
- **URL:** https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f163197/f163197.pdf (draft) and https://sfi.ch/en/publications/institutional-corporate-bond-pricing
- **Method or case:** method.
- **What it adds:** It estimates how much price each investor type (insurers, pension funds, mutual funds) needs to absorb more bonds. That gives a direct way to convert a supply forecast into a spread move, split by rating group.
- **Weakness:** The market-wide elasticity I saw (about 3.7) comes from the 2022 draft, and the published number may differ.

### 2. Corporate Bond Multipliers: Substitutes Matter
- **Authors:** Manav Chaudhary, Julie Zhiyu Fu, Jian Li
- **Year:** 2022 first draft, latest version June 2026 (LSE Financial Markets Group DP 955).
- **Type:** working paper. Length unknown.
- **Access:** free.
- **URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4292266 and https://www.fmg.ac.uk/publications/discussion-papers/corporate-bond-multipliers-substitutes-matter
- **Method or case:** method.
- **What it adds:** It shows that price impact is near zero for one bond but large for a whole rating-and-maturity bucket, because buckets lack close substitutes. This matters for the AA-to-BBB ratio: supply concentrated in one rating bucket moves that bucket, not single names.
- **Weakness:** The shocks come from fund flows, not from issuance, and the price effect reverses after about a quarter.

### 3. Building AI on credit: structures and spillovers (BIS Bulletin No 140)
- **Authors:** Mathias Drehmann, Egemen Eren
- **Year:** 27 October 2026.
- **Type:** central-bank bulletin, short. An online annex exists.
- **Access:** free.
- **URL:** https://www.bis.org/publications/bulletin-140-building-ai-credit-structures-and-spillovers.pdf
- **Method or case:** both. It is an event study on 2026 AI-financing news days.
- **What it adds:** It is the only central-bank source I found that measures AI debt news against Treasury and IG markets. It reports small, short-lived spillovers so far, and it fills the "after March 2025" library gap.
- **Weakness:** It is short, and it warns that its own result may not hold if issuance continues at the current pace.
- **Companions (same team, also free):** BIS Bulletin No 120, "Financing the AI boom: from cash flows to debt", Aldasoro, Doerr, Rees, 7 January 2026, https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull120.htm. BIS Quarterly Review March 2026 box, "Financing the AI infrastructure boom: on- and off-balance sheet borrowing", Eren, Krohn, Todorov, https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2603u.htm.

### 4. Raising Bond Capital in Segmented Markets
- **Author:** Kerry Yang Siani
- **Year:** 2022 job-market paper. Later work cites it as Siani (2025).
- **Type:** working paper. Length unknown.
- **Access:** free.
- **URL:** https://economics.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Siani-JMP.pdf
- **Method or case:** method.
- **What it adds:** It shows that a firm's spread at issue rises when other firms issue more on the same day. It defines the "issuance premium" (primary yield minus secondary yield), which is a cleaner supply gauge than the index spread.
- **Weakness:** It measures same-day crowding, not a year-long supply wave.

### 5. Illiquidity Spillovers: Theory and Evidence from European Telecom Bond Issuance
- **Authors:** Yigal S. Newman, Michael A. Rierson
- **Year:** 2004.
- **Type:** job-market paper. I found no journal version. Length unknown.
- **Access:** free.
- **URL:** https://finance.wharton.upenn.edu/department/Seminar/2004SpringRecruiting/Micro/Newman-micro020504.pdf
- **Method or case:** both.
- **What it adds:** It is the closest documented case to today: one sector (telecom, 2000) issued jumbo debt to fund a capital-spending race. It estimates that a single EUR 15.5bn Deutsche Telekom deal cut the value of about EUR 100bn of other telecom debt by roughly 0.28 percent, and the effect was temporary.
- **Weakness:** It is old, European, and unpublished.

### 6. Corporate Credit Conditions Around the World: Novel Facts Through Holistic Data (NY Fed Staff Report 1074)
- **Authors:** Nina Boyarchenko, Leonardo Elias
- **Year:** 2023, revised 2024. Earlier title: "The Good, the Bad, and the Ugly of International Debt Market Data".
- **Type:** Fed staff report. Length unknown.
- **Access:** free.
- **URL:** https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr1074
- **Method or case:** method.
- **What it adds:** It argues that secondary-market spreads are a poor proxy for the cost of new debt. That is a direct warning for a measure built on secondary OAS, such as the AA-to-BBB ratio.
- **Weakness:** Its scope is global and broad. It does not study a supply shock.

### 7. Quantitative Tightening Around the Globe: What Have We Learned?
- **Authors:** Wenxin Du, Kristin Forbes, Matthew N. Luzzetti
- **Year:** 2024 (NBER WP 32321, CEPR DP 19626).
- **Type:** working paper. Length unknown.
- **Access:** NBER page is free to read for many users. A free conference draft exists.
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w32321
- **Method or case:** both. Event study on 38 QT announcements in seven economies.
- **What it adds:** It fills the QT gap with data. It finds that QT raised government yields modestly and that domestic nonbanks absorbed the bonds, which tells you who must also absorb corporate supply.
- **Weakness:** The sample ends before the last stage of Fed runoff, and the authors say frictions may rise later.

### 8. Liquidity Dependence and the Waxing and Waning of Central Bank Balance Sheets
- **Authors:** Viral Acharya, Rahul Chauhan, Raghuram Rajan, Sascha Steffen
- **Year:** 2023 (NBER WP 31050).
- **Type:** working paper. Length unknown.
- **Access:** free drafts on the authors' pages and NBER.
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w31050
- **Method or case:** both.
- **What it adds:** It shows that QE and QT are not mirror images. Banks took on deposits and credit lines during QE and did not shed them in QT, so funding stress can appear before reserves look scarce.
- **Weakness:** It covers banks, not credit spreads. The link to corporate bonds is your inference.

### 9. Treasury Duration Supply and Term Premiums (CBO Working Paper 2026-11)
- **Author:** Michael McGrane
- **Year:** October 2026.
- **Type:** government working paper. Length unknown.
- **Access:** free.
- **URL:** https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62759
- **Method or case:** method.
- **What it adds:** It gives a recent, tested supply-to-price conversion for Treasuries: about 2.8 basis points on the 10-year term premium for a 1-point rise in debt-to-GDP. It is a template for the same calculation in credit, and it is post-March-2025.
- **Weakness:** The search snippets for its numbers were partly garbled. Other 2025-2026 estimates range from about 1 to 3 basis points.
- **Companion:** "Treasury Supply Shocks: Propagation Through Debt Expansion and Maturity Adjustment", NBER WP 35098, https://www.nber.org/papers/w35098 (authors per a Kansas City Fed version: Bi, Phillot, Zubairy).

### 10. Option-Based Credit Spreads
- **Authors:** Christopher Culp, Yoshio Nozawa, Pietro Veronesi
- **Year:** 2018 (American Economic Review 108(2), 454-488). NBER WP 20776.
- **Type:** journal article, 35 pages. Replication data is public.
- **Access:** NBER draft is free.
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w20776.pdf
- **Method or case:** method.
- **What it adds:** It builds "pseudo bonds" from Treasuries and equity index options, so you get a credit spread with no supply or liquidity effect in it. The gap between that spread and the real one is a candidate measure of what supply adds.
- **Weakness:** The authors find that credit-supply constraints do not explain spreads, which cuts against the supply thesis.

### 11. The Financing of Local Government in China: Stimulus Loan Wanes and Shadow Banking Waxes
- **Authors:** Zhuo Chen, Zhiguo He, Chun Liu
- **Year:** 2020 (Journal of Financial Economics 137(1), 42-71). NBER WP 23598.
- **Type:** journal article, 30 pages.
- **Access:** NBER draft is free.
- **URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w23598
- **Method or case:** case.
- **What it adds:** It fills the China gap with one clean mechanism: 2009 stimulus loans came due and pushed local governments into bonds and shadow credit after 2012. It is also a documented case of a refinancing wall that created forced supply.
- **Weakness:** The data end in 2015, before the property downturn.

### 12. Surviving the AI Capex Boom
- **Author:** Kai Wu (Sparkline Capital)
- **Year:** October 2025.
- **Type:** practitioner research paper. Length unknown.
- **Access:** free.
- **URL:** https://theideafarm.com/portfolio-management/surviving-the-ai-capex-boom/ (summary page with a link to the paper)
- **Method or case:** both. Uses 1963-2025 data plus the railroad and telecom buildouts.
- **What it adds:** It fills the equity-valuation gap for a capital-spending boom. It reports that firms that raise capital spending fast earn lower stock returns later.
- **Weakness:** It is not peer reviewed, and I saw only secondary coverage of it. The peer-reviewed base it rests on is Arif and Lee (2011, Review of Financial Studies), which I did not verify directly.

## Not found

- **No academic study of the 2015-2016 or 2019-2020 M&A jumbo deals as supply shocks.** One search returned trade press only (IFR, Morningstar). Newman and Rierson (item 5) is the nearest tested case.
- **No Fed or NBER paper on AI debt supply and credit spreads.** The BIS items are the only official sources I found. The rest is bank and asset-manager commentary.
- **No source that tests the AA-to-BBB spread ratio as a "priced-in" measure.** Items 2 and 6 bear on it, but neither uses it.
- **No Minneapolis Fed market-based-probabilities methodology page.** The search returned the Malz paper instead.
- **No direct check of Koijen and Yogo (2019), Vayanos and Vila, Collin-Dufresne-Goldstein-Martin, Huang and Huang, or Arif and Lee (2011).** They surfaced only as citations inside other results, so I left them off the list.
- **No full texts read.** All claims above rest on abstracts and search summaries.
