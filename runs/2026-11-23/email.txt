Subject: Credit thesis update 2026-11-23: spreads wider on 20 Nov prints, thesis stands

When the top tier leads the fall

Spreads are wider and the thesis stands. FRED published the 20 Nov prints, so our data now include that day's equity fall. IG is 98bp from 94bp, HY is 376bp from 352bp and CCC is 1,402bp from 1,361bp. The AA to BBB spread ratio, our axis, rose to 0.590 from 0.581. No falsifier triggered and no scenario probability moved. One fact qualifies this. Two X accounts describe a sharp equity rebound on 23 Nov, so spreads dated 20 Nov may now be too wide. In our view the thesis holds. The bond market prices about two thirds of next year's AI debt supply in top-rated spreads and has repriced the weak AI borrowers, but it does not yet price the AI debt that sits in bank loans, private credit and off-balance-sheet vehicles. A second bank also met weak demand for a data-centre loan. Two reports that could matter failed a web check. One says the Nvidia board reinstated its former chief executive. The other says Google cut a 2027 chip order.

The causal chain

A few AA and A rated companies sell between 14% and 20% of 2026 IG issuance. They borrow because capex exceeds operating cash flow, $230bn against $199bn in the third quarter. Alphabet sold $4.2bn of bonds in Canada in the week of the fall, so the supply continued.

That supply lifts spreads in the tier, meaning the AA and A rating group, against lower-rated spreads. The pressure stays there because rating groups are weak substitutes. Chaudhary finds that a fund flow equal to 1% of a group moved its price 3.94%. That study measures flows, not issuance. Last week added a test. In the two days to 20 Nov, AA widened 5bp and BBB widened 6bp. AA moved more in proportion, and the ratio rose from 0.578.

Part of the supply lands in Treasury yields. Long AI bonds go to holders of long Treasuries, so they lift the term premium, the extra yield for holding long bonds. The BIS found it about 1.5bp higher on 18 AI news days, while the IG index spread moved 0.13bp. The index is therefore a weak gauge of supply. The 2-year yield is 4.76% against a 4.00% policy ceiling, so the curve holds about three hikes. Only a surprise in the path is new (Shahidi). Markets priced a December hike near 96% before the fall, and no source gives the odds after it.

The market sorts AI borrowers by the support of a big-tech customer, whose lease pays the debt. Oracle CDS is 341bp. Borrowers also move debt into vehicles, meaning separate companies that own the site and issue the debt. They use bank loans and private credit as well. One vehicle's A-rated notes priced about 80bp wider than its parent's BBB bonds. This debt leaves the bond indices and is marked late. HSBC sold a $300m loan piece at a mid-80s price. ING has now met muted demand for a $500m sale, although that report is second-hand and gives no price. Against that, the BIS found in January that private lenders priced AI loans like other loans.

One link works against the first. Wider spreads slow projects, and slower projects need less debt (Cowen). We have the least evidence here. One account reports that Google cut a 2027 chip order by about 20%, and a web search did not find it. No large spender cut 2027 capex guidance. A capex cut would not remove signed leases, and about $1.3tn of them have not started. Debt service is fixed, so a modest shortfall becomes a large loss for junior lenders (Van Nieuwerburgh). Less bond supply can therefore arrive with more stress at vehicles and lenders.

What would be true if we were wrong

If supply did not matter, AA spreads would move with the rest of IG. The ratio would sit near its mean of 0.476. In a flight from risk, BBB would widen more than AA in proportion. Our data show the ratio at 0.590, AA at its 100th percentile and BBB at its 87th. The gap between A and AA fell from 18bp to 9bp in a year. In our view supply explains that pattern best. The 0.13bp index result fits the opposite view.

The strongest objection

Willer says supply is endogenous, meaning companies issue when demand is strong. Supply then does not forecast returns. In our view this weakens the second link and our two-thirds estimate. It rests on backtests of monthly supply and does not compare tiers. A second objection is new. The top tier now holds the AI names. If they widened because their equity fell, the ratio measures AI credit risk. Our data cannot separate the two.

The axis and what it prices

We read supply in the AA to BBB spread ratio. A high ratio means the market charges top-rated issuers more than usual against BBB issuers. The ratio is 0.590, with AA at 72bp and BBB at 122bp. The three-year mean is 0.476. Today's reading is at the 94th percentile, 2.25 standard deviations above that mean.

What is priced differs by market, and the estimates are our judgement. Top ratings price about two thirds. About 14bp of the 72bp AA spread is supply premium, and the grid puts the ratio near 0.65 if 2027 supply arrives in full. Weak AI borrowers are repriced, with CCC spreads at a new three-year high. The HY index is not cheap on a long view, because 376bp is below its long-run average of about 520bp. The IG index prices little. IG at 98bp is fair only if Supply absorbed has a 58% chance, and the grid gives it 28%. Treasury yields price an unknown part. Bank loans, private credit and vehicles are not priced in bonds. Two loan sales are two marks. They do not make a market.

Falsifiers

None of the four triggered, and none is close.
F1. AA to BBB ratio below 0.52 before 2027-05-23 while none of the five largest spenders has cut 2027 capex guidance. Today 0.590, up from 0.581. Not triggered.
F2. Five business days with no IG deals outside holidays, or a new Fed emergency facility. No such report. Not triggered.
F3. CCC spread above 1,700bp. Today 1,402bp, dated 20 Nov, up 49bp in two days. Not triggered.
F4. Fed funds upper bound still 4.00% after the January 2027 meeting. Today 4.00%, next meeting 9 Dec. Not triggered.

Where sources disagree (both sides reported, none settled)

1. Does supply matter? Willer says no for monthly issuance. Dalio and Cassidy say a debt-financed build is the core risk.
2. Does supply show in spreads? The BIS finds no IG index move on AI news days. JunkBondInvestor and the FRED ratio show AA pressed against A and BBB.
3. Is HY wide? The three-year percentile says yes. Ilmanen's long-run average says no.
4. Is a capex cut good for credit? JunkBondInvestor says yes for hyperscaler bonds, if all cut together. The same letter says signed leases keep fixed claims rising.
5. How large is hyperscaler supply this year? An LSEG count gives $370.7bn. The X account of JunkBondInvestor gives $620bn, with no definition.
6. How risky are bank lines to lending funds? Fed staff call the direct risk limited. Schulte says hidden leverage sits there.
7. Will the Fed hike in December? Markets priced about 96% before the fall. One table shows no hike since 1990 with the VIX above 30, and the VIX closed at 35.89.
8. How large is 2027 supply? Bank forecasts give $420bn to $450bn of hyperscaler IG debt. Wider definitions give much larger counts.
9. Did the Nvidia board reinstate its former chief executive on 23 Nov? One X account says yes. A web search found a report that the board does not intend to.
10. How large is the SpaceX chip financing, and can the chips leave Earth? One newsletter says $44bn and that lenders barred it. Another says $43bn and that the final terms are unclear.

Open questions for the PM

1. How do the banks, insurers and private funds that hold AI loans fund themselves?
2. Should the funding-accident test change shape? The BIS expects a valuation freeze and late loss recognition, not a run.
3. Are the hard-landing moves too small?
4. Do the largest spenders earn enough from AI to service the debt?
5. Does the AA premium need continuing supply to last? If so, F1 would need a second condition.
6. Does the ratio measure supply, or fear about the AI names in the top tier?
7. Should the scenario moves be re-based? The month to 20 Nov delivered about half of the AI digestion move.
