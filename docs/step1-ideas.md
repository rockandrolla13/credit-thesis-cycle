# Ideas from the skill library, by the four inputs of step 1

Written on 2026-11-23, from the skill sweep of that day's run (`runs/2026-11-23/sweep.md`).

Step 1 of the four-step investment process is "development of views". It has four inputs and one output, a list of specific market themes. The inputs are:

1. Macroeconomic fundamentals.
2. Business cycle: which part are we in, and who will outperform.
3. Monetary and fiscal conditions, and regulation.
4. Drivers of asset class performance: cash flow, default rates, industry trends.

The ingestion of 35 skills gave about a dozen ideas. The most useful one: each input can become a fixed test with a skill behind it. These are ideas for views. None is a tested view yet.

## 1. Macroeconomic fundamentals

| Idea | Source skill | Reading in our files |
|---|---|---|
| Test whether the debt pays for itself. Does borrowed money earn enough to service the debt? | `dalio-big-debt-crises` | AI capex of $230bn in one quarter against $199bn of operating cash flow. |
| Look where the total hides it. A boom in one sector can hide in the aggregate. Leverage in vehicles and non-bank lenders is a "stealth rise". | `claessens-financial-globalization`, `schulte-credit-driven-economy`, `van-nieuwerburgh-financing-ai-buildout` | Aggregate leverage hides asset-level leverage in vehicles. |
| Watch the slowdown, not the boom. The danger sign is a credit boom that then slows. | `schularick-taylor-credit-booms` | Limit: the measure is bank loans. |

## 2. Business cycle

| Idea | Source skill | Reading in our files |
|---|---|---|
| Two roads to a downturn. Cheap risk builds long projects slowly. Volatile funding cuts long projects at once, with no boom before it. | `cowen-risk-and-business-cycles` | The VIX was at its 99th percentile of three years on 20 Nov. |
| The weakest borrowers lead. The riskiest debtors miss payments about six months before the peak. The mix of hedge, speculative and Ponzi borrowers measures the stage. | `dalio-big-debt-crises`, `cassidy-how-markets-fail` | CCC spreads at a three-year high. |
| Ask about vulnerability, not triggers. Risk is highest when assets are rich and volatility is low. | `rozanov-global-macro-theory-practice` | Not today's state. |

## 3. Monetary and fiscal conditions, and regulation

| Idea | Source skill | Reading in our files |
|---|---|---|
| Only the surprise matters. A rate path that follows the curve does not move returns. | `shahidi-balanced-asset-allocation` | The 2-year yield is 4.76% against a 4.00% policy ceiling, so the hikes are priced. |
| Private supply can tighten conditions without the Fed. Long corporate bonds lift the term premium. The Fed must then choose between more tightening and growing imbalances. | `bis-building-ai-on-credit`, `axilrod-inside-the-fed` | Term premium about 1.5bp higher on 18 AI news days. The effect was gone in two days. |
| The regulator's blind spot. Banks lend to private credit vehicles through credit lines. | `fed-bank-lending-to-private-credit` | $349bn of bank loans. A full drawdown costs about 2bp of capital ratio. Correlated drawdowns are the open risk. |

## 4. Drivers of asset class performance

| Idea | Source skill | Reading in our files |
|---|---|---|
| Think in rating tiers, not indices. Rating groups are weak substitutes, so supply and flows move one tier and leave the index calm. | `chaudhary-corporate-bond-multipliers` | A demand shock of 1% of a rating group moved its price 3.94%. It measures fund flows, not issuance. |
| A spread is not a return. Start from the spread minus expected loss. | `ilmanen-investing-amid-low-expected-returns` | IG earned 75bp of a 132bp average spread from 1989 to 2020. |
| Announced is not built. Fixed debt service turns a small shortfall into a large loss for junior lenders. | `van-nieuwerburgh-financing-ai-buildout` | In one scenario, 43.2% of announced capacity never completes. |
