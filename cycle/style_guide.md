# House style for the credit thesis memo and email

Derived from one 2021 sell-side quantamental rates note. The note is licensed research. This guide describes its style and copies none of its sentences. Every count below was measured on the note's text.

## Measurements

- The file has 9 pages and 5,197 words. The research body is 3 pages and 768 words. The rest is disclosures.
- The body has 7 paragraphs, 33 sentences, 3 figures, 1 title and 1 section heading.
- Sentences: median 24 words, mean 23.3, range 4 to 49.
- Paragraphs: median 101 words, range 44 to 167, with 2 to 7 sentences each.
- One figure for every 256 words. No tables, footnotes or bullet lists.

## 1. Structure

- **Title.** A short phrase that names the puzzle. No colon, no number. Example: "When shocks no longer shock".
- **Opening paragraph.** It is the longest (167 words, 22% of the body) and it is a full summary. The order is fixed: the puzzle, then the one fact that shows it with a pointer to Figure 1, then the authors' reading, then what that implies for pricing.
- **Order.** Puzzle, history and method, result, interpretation. The one section heading is a plain noun phrase that names the test.
- **Ending.** No conclusion and no summary. The last paragraph states the view and points to earlier work.

## 2. Content

- **Claims, in order:** a puzzle, a history, a test, a result, an interpretation, an implication.
- **Evidence.** One test carries the note. One sentence reports that the same test on two other data series agreed. The note does not show that work.
- **Figures.** The text names each figure before it appears. A 44-word paragraph says what each line is. The next paragraph says what a reader should expect to see in the normal case, then what the figure shows. Captions are noun phrases with a source line.
- **Counter-case.** The note states the opposing view, gives its two supports and grants them. Then it says where the authors differ.
- **Left out:** literature, formulas, data tables, caveat lists, and any magnitude a figure already shows.

## 3. Way of writing

- **Person.** First person plural. "We" or "our" appears 10 times. "In our view" marks opinion (3 times). Facts carry no marker.
- **Tense.** Present for the current state, simple past for history, present perfect for a continuing trend.
- **Hedges.** About 8 in 33 sentences. The words are: in our view, appears, suggests, does not seem, somewhat, plausible. A hedge sits on an interpretation, never on a measurement.
- **Connectives.** "However", "on the other hand", "therefore" and "while" link the sentences.
- **Numbers.** Sparse: 13 numeric tokens in the body. Three are years, two are method parameters, one is a data value. The figures hold the levels.
- **Jargon.** Market terms appear undefined. The reader is a practitioner.

## 4. Succinctness

- Words by part: opening 167 (22%), method 145 (19%), result 255 (33%), interpretation 201 (26%).
- The method gets one paragraph, and one 27-word sentence holds its parameters. The result gets almost twice the space of the method.
- Quantities go to a figure. The prose says what the figure means.

## 5. What we must NOT copy

- The note takes a directional view and says which way two market rates should move. We state what is priced and stop.
- No trade recommendation, positioning, sizing, hedge ratio, stop, or level to enter or exit. Strategy families at most, and only if the input memo names them. Our memo is research only.
- Do not copy its sentences, its 49-word sentences or its sparse numbers. Our memo must state the axis level, every probability and every falsifier threshold.

## 6. Where we depart on purpose

- **Sentence length.** The reader's standing rule caps a sentence at 25 words. Split anything longer. The one-sentence thesis may run over.
- **Lists.** The note has none. We number the falsifiers, the source disagreements and the open questions.
- **Headings.** The note has one. We use one plain heading per required part.
- **Exhibits.** In the memo, a table replaces a figure. In the email, one or two sentences give the reading with its numbers.

## Do

1. Open with the state of the thesis and the one fact that qualifies it.
2. Put the full argument in the first paragraph.
3. Name an exhibit in the text before it appears, and say how to read it.
4. Mark judgement with "in our view". Leave facts unmarked.
5. State the opposing evidence, then say where we differ.

## Do not

1. Do not add a conclusion that repeats the opening.
2. Do not give a method more space than its result.
3. Do not list evidence item by item. Keep the few facts that carry the argument.
4. Do not settle a disagreement between sources. Report both sides.
5. Do not name a trade, a size or a level. Do not use em dashes or semicolons.

## Skeleton for our memo

| Part | Target words |
|---|---|
| Title that names the puzzle | 4 to 8 |
| Status line: date, horizon, research only | 25 |
| Opening: what changed, the qualifying fact, the thesis | 130 to 170 |
| Exhibit 1: readings against the previous memo | table |
| The causal chain, as prose | 120 to 150 |
| The axis and what is priced | 200 to 250 |
| Scenarios as proposals, with Exhibit 2 | 80 to 120 |
| Falsifiers: two sentences, then Exhibit 3 | 30 |
| Where sources disagree: numbered, both sides | 130 to 160 |
| Open questions for the PM: numbered | 60 to 100 |
| Limits and the research-only line | 40 to 60 |

Length rule. The note argues one point in 768 words. Our argument (opening to scenarios) must fit in the same 768 words. The three lists and the limits may add about 350. That gives about 1,100 words of prose plus 3 exhibits for the memo. The email has no exhibits and less detail: hold it near 900 words.
